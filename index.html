About · English

MathGalaxy is an interactive knowledge graph of mathematics. Every definition, theorem and lemma is a node; every arrow is a derivation. Zoom out and you see the branches of the subject; zoom in and you read a single statement together with its remarks, its references, and the proofs that tie it to what it rests on.

The graph is organised at three scales — a galaxy (a branch of mathematics), a cluster (a self-contained apparatus built to make one thing work), and a star (one result). It runs from the ZFC axioms and the equivalence chain around the axiom of choice, through algebra, topology, measure theory and homological algebra, up to category theory and condensed mathematics — the parts of the subject a student actually has to walk through in dependency order.

It doubles as lecture notes, a study reference and a course companion: read a topic along its prerequisite chain instead of in the order a textbook prints it, and when a proof cites something you have not met yet, follow the edge. Each concept has its own page with statement, notes and references; the whole graph is exported as graph.json, graph.md and llms.txt as well, so an AI agent can look a result up without first guessing the Chinese terminology.

Content is licensed CC BY 4.0; the engine is MIT and has no dependencies. Interface and prose are Chinese-first, with English names on the concepts that have them.
