## 2. One statement is recorded once

Mathematically **equivalent** statements are not split into two nodes (duplicates have
been merged). Identical terminology does not establish equivalence.

## 3. Valuable special cases and different proofs are kept

Classical, commonly used, educationally important or independently valuable special cases
are kept and linked to the general result; a special case is not deleted merely because a
more general theorem exists. Several genuinely different valid proofs of one theorem may coexist.

## 4. Clusters are divided by *apparatus*, not by course

A cluster is the whole set of results that exists to produce one usable thing. Cluster size
and connectivity are diagnostic information only, not limits.
