What this is

MathGalaxy organises mathematics as a graph rather than as a shelf of books. A node is one concept — a definition, an axiom, a theorem, a lemma, a proposition, a corollary, an example. An edge is a relationship: one statement implies another, two statements are equivalent, a result depends on a definition, or two ideas are analogous across branches. Proofs live on the edges, so a node stays small enough to read in one screen.

Zoom out and you see the branches of the subject; zoom in and you read a single statement together with its remarks, its references and the proofs that connect it to what it rests on. The graph runs from the ZFC axioms and the equivalence chain around the axiom of choice, through algebra, topology, measure theory and homological algebra, up to category theory and condensed mathematics — in dependency order, so you can read a topic along its prerequisite chain instead of in the order a textbook prints it.

The working language is Chinese and the Chinese text is authoritative. English names exist on most concepts, and a growing set of entries has an English abstract written for this site; those are collected below. Where an English entry exists, it is linked from its Chinese page, and both pages declare each other with hreflang.

For machine readers the whole graph is exported as graph.json, graph.md and llms.txt — see the llms.txt protocol.
