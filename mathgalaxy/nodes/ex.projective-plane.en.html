Statement

RP2:=S2/(x∼−x),\mathbb{RP}^{2} := S^{2} \big/ (x \sim -x) ,RP2:=S2/(x∼−x),

With the quotient topology from antipodal identification, this is a closed nonorientable surface of nonorientable genus k=1k = 1k=1 and Euler characteristic χ(RP2)=1\chi(\mathbb{RP}^{2}) = 1χ(RP2)=1.

Notes

⭐ Three equivalent descriptions: (1) A sphere with antipodal points identified. (2) A disk with antipodal boundary points identified. (3) A square with opposite sides glued in reverse orientation (T2T^{2}T2 has same-orientation gluing, so reversing orientation makes the difference).

⭐ Computing χ=1\chi = 1χ=1: Start with a disk (χ=1\chi = 1χ=1). Identifying antipodal boundary points amounts to attaching a half-twisted band, which eliminates the boundary. The full circle of VVV becomes one point, and EEE becomes one closed curve. Thus χ=1−1+1=1\chi = 1 - 1 + 1 = 1χ=1−1+1=1, in agreement with 2−k=12 - k = 12−k=1.

⭐ Why it is nonorientable: After going once around a line in RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2, the front becomes the back. It is a capped Möbius band: glue a disk to the boundary circle of a Möbius band to obtain RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2.

⚠ RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 cannot embed in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 (no compact nonorientable surface without boundary embeds in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3; an embedding requires going to R4\mathbb{R}^{4}R4). This is a topological obstruction. The familiar crosscap picture shows an immersion.
