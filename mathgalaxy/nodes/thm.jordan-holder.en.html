Notes

⭐ This theorem makes the phrase “the composition factors of GGG” legitimate — otherwise it would be a property of one chain.

It occupies in group theory exactly the place of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic in the integers: split an object into indecomposable pieces and guarantee the splitting is unique.

⚠ The converse fails: equal composition factors do not imply isomorphic.

Example: Z/12\mathbb{Z}/12Z/12 and Z/2×Z/6\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/6Z/2×Z/6 both have composition factors Z/2,Z/2,Z/3\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/3Z/2,Z/2,Z/3, but the former has an element of order 121212 and the latter does not, so they are not isomorphic.

That is, composition factors are an invariant, not a complete invariant.

⭐ The proof proceeds by refinement: first show that any two normal series have equivalent refinements (Schreier's refinement theorem), then use the Zassenhaus (butterfly) lemma to make the refinements.

A composition series is already finest (the factors are simple and admit no further refinement), so two composition series must be equivalent.
