def.free-abelian-sheaf · Z[X]

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let T\mathcal{T}T be a topos. The forgetful functor

T(Ab)⟶T\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Ab}) \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}T(Ab)⟶T

(forgetting an abelian sheaf to its underlying sheaf of sets) has a left adjoint, written

Z⋅(−):T⟶T(Ab),X⟼Z⋅X,\mathbb{Z}\cdot(-) : \mathcal{T} \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Ab}), \qquad X \longmapsto \mathbb{Z}\cdot X,Z⋅(−):T⟶T(Ab),X⟼Z⋅X,

also denoted Z[X]\mathbb{Z}[X]Z[X].

It is characterized by the universal property: for X∈TX \in \mathcal{T}X∈T and M∈T(Ab)M \in \mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Ab})M∈T(Ab),

Hom⁡Z(Z⋅X, M)  ≅  Hom⁡T(X, M)=M(X).\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X,\ M\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\ M) = M(X).HomZ​(Z⋅X, M)≅HomT​(X, M)=M(X).

Notes

How to construct it. First free pointwise, then sheafify: regard XXX as a presheaf, take the presheaf U↦Z⋅X(U)U \mapsto \mathbb{Z}\cdot X(U)U↦Z⋅X(U) (free abelian group pointwise), then sheafify:

Z⋅X  :=  (U↦Z⋅X(U))#.\mathbb{Z}\cdot X \;:=\; \bigl(U \mapsto \mathbb{Z}\cdot X(U)\bigr)^{\#}.Z⋅X:=(U↦Z⋅X(U))#.

Why this is right. The presheaf layer is pointwise: adjunctions in a functor category are built pointwise, and the forgetful functor Ab→Set\mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Set}Ab→Set has a left adjoint E↦Z⋅EE \mapsto \mathbb{Z}\cdot EE↦Z⋅E (the free abelian group) ✓, so the left adjoint of C^(Ab)→C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab}) \to \widehat{\mathcal{C}}C(Ab)→C is pointwise free ✓.

At the sheaf layer, use "sheafification ⊣\dashv⊣ inclusion" to pass the adjunction down ✓:

Hom⁡Z(Z⋅X, M)=Hom⁡C^(Ab)(Z⋅X, M)≅Hom⁡C^(X, M)=M(X)✓\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X,\ M\bigr) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab})}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X,\ M\bigr) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(X,\ M) = M(X) ✓HomZ​(Z⋅X, M)=HomC(Ab)​(Z⋅X, M)≅HomC​(X, M)=M(X)✓

(MMM is itself a sheaf, so the Hom on the presheaf side equals the Hom on the sheaf side). ∎

⭐ Use: Z⋅X\mathbb{Z}\cdot XZ⋅X is the device for "regarding a space or sheaf as a free generator".

The Čech complex is Cˇ∙(X,M)=Hom⁡Z(Z⋅X∙,M)\check{C}^{\bullet}(X, M) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X_{\bullet}, M)Cˇ∙(X,M)=HomZ​(Z⋅X∙​,M);

sheaf cohomology is Hn(X,M)=Ext⁡Zn(Z⋅X,M)H^{n}(X, M) = \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X, M)Hn(X,M)=ExtZn​(Z⋅X,M) ✓.

⚠️ It is not computed pointwise in general: sheafification alters values, and "free pointwise, then sheafify" agrees with "free" only when the sheaf condition does not interfere.

📌 Example: Z⋅1≅Z\mathbb{Z}\cdot \mathbf{1} \cong \mathbb{Z}Z⋅1≅Z (the constant sheaf). ⚠️ But it is not a projective object: in a sheaf category Hom⁡(Z⋅X,−)≅M(X)\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X, -) \cong M(X)Hom(Z⋅X,−)≅M(X) is only left exact, and "free   ⟹  \implies⟹ projective" holds only in the presheaf category (@@prop.presheaf-free-projective@@).
