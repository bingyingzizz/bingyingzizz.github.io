thm.classification-surfaces

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let SSS be a closed surface (closed surface). Then SSS is homeomorphic to exactly one of the following, which are pairwise nonhomeomorphic:

  • The sphere S2S^{2}S2;
  • The connected sum of ggg tori, denoted T2#⋯#T2T^{2}\#\cdots\# T^{2}T2#⋯#T2 (g≥1g \ge 1g≥1), an orientable closed surface of genus ggg;
  • The connected sum of kkk projective planes, denoted RP2#⋯#RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}\#\cdots\# \mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2#⋯#RP2 (k≥1k \ge 1k≥1), a nonorientable closed surface of nonorientable genus kkk.

The two families are disjoint: orientable and nonorientable closed surfaces (apart from the sphere) are never homeomorphic.

⚠ This node retains status: skeleton. Steps (1) and (2), Radó's theorem and combinatorial classification, are unproved here. Step (3) has a proof. Note: Euler characteristic and genus now uses the elementary route through Radó's theorem and Lemma D, so this theorem no longer blocks that proposition. In turn, this theorem does not use Euler characteristic and genus as an input.

The three steps. (1) Radó's theorem gives a triangulation of every closed surface (Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable, skeleton). (2) Combinatorial classification reduces any triangulation to standard form in finitely many moves. (3) The invariants computed on the standard forms, orientability and χ\chiχ, determine (g)(g)(g) or (k)(k)(k) and distinguish the homeomorphism types.

Step (1) (cited without proof). Radó's theorem states that every compact surface admits a finite triangulation. Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable is still a skeleton and the graph has no proof. It is used here as an input and does not also establish that any two triangulations are combinatorially equivalent, which is the work of step (2).

Step (2): Statement and incomplete verification (Lemma K, a gap in the graph). Let KKK be a finite simplicial complex and let ∣K∣|K|∣K∣ be a closed connected surface. A finite sequence of operations reduces KKK to a single polygon with paired edges, with pairing word a1b1a1−1b1−1⋯agbgag−1bg−1a_{1}b_{1}a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\cdots a_{g}b_{g}a_{g}^{-1}b_{g}^{-1}a1​b1​a1−1​b1−1​⋯ag​bg​ag−1​bg−1​ (orientable, g≥0g\ge 0g≥0) or c1c1⋯ckckc_{1}c_{1}\cdots c_{k}c_{k}c1​c1​⋯ck​ck​ (nonorientable, k≥1k\ge 1k≥1). The moves are (i) cutting along edges and regluing (cut-and-paste), (ii) 222–222 flips, (iii) removing a 222-simplex attached along just one edge, and (iv) vertex reduction (merging several vertices into one). This lemma is the core of the classification. It has its own node, combinatorial classification lemma (still a skeleton, with gaps in vertex reduction, degenerate cases of 2–2 flips, and the termination measure).

Three checks for step (2) (all incomplete, accounting for the gap). - 222–222 flips: The graph only says that two triangles on opposite sides of an edge are replaced by two other triangles with the same vertex set. This works only when the triangles form a quadrilateral along the shared edge and all four vertices are distinct. If the triangles share another vertex (so the quadrilateral degenerates), or the fourth vertex coincides with the first (giving two triangles instead of a quadrilateral), the case needs separate treatment. The graph gives neither these hypotheses nor the degenerate cases. - Cut-and-paste: The graph only says to cut along a closed curve and reglue. To do this within a triangulation, first make the curve a simple closed curve (subdivision +++ general position, normal isotopy). One must then show that cutting gives a new triangulation and that regluing preserves the homeomorphism type. Neither step is given here. - Termination: The original claim that the number of paired edges strictly decreases is false. A 222–222 flip leaves the edge count unchanged, and cut-and-paste can increase it. What is needed is a well-ordered measure (for instance, induction on the number of sides of the polygon, with finitely many cases as in Munkres's proof, or a lexicographic order on the number of edges +++ the number of vertices) and a check that each move decreases it. The graph gives neither the measure nor the verification. - Missing construction for vertex reduction: Before reducing the triangulation to a single polygon, all vertices must be merged into one (move (iv)). The graph names the move without giving a construction, and this is the step with the most degenerate cases.

Step (3) (complete). For the standard 4g4g4g-gon, the fan triangulation from the center gives V=2V=2V=2 (the center and one boundary vertex class), E=6gE=6gE=6g, and F=4gF=4gF=4g, so χ=2−2g\chi=2-2gχ=2−2g. For the 2k2k2k-gon, the counts are V=2V=2V=2, E=3kE=3kE=3k, and F=2kF=2kF=2k, so χ=2−k\chi=2-kχ=2−k. The two families have distinct homeomorphism types because one is orientable and the other is nonorientable. Within either family, distinct values of χ\chiχ give nonhomeomorphic surfaces by the topological invariance of χ\chiχ (Euler characteristic). The standard forms are the conclusion of Lemma K. Defining closed surfaces as the standard forms would make the argument circular.

Why this theorem cannot take the same route as Euler characteristic and genus. That proposition only needs χ=2−2g\chi=2-2gχ=2−2g and can compute H2H_{2}H2​ directly using Lemma D, without classification. This theorem needs the structure theorem that every triangulation reduces to standard form. That is the classification itself, and numerical homological invariants cannot establish it. Bypassing this step would amount to assuming the classification theorem.

⭐ Criterion (assuming Lemma K): Two closed surfaces are homeomorphic   ⟺  \iff⟺ they have the same orientability and the same χ\chiχ (equivalently, both are S2S^{2}S2, both are connected sums of ggg tori with the same ggg, or both are connected sums of kkk copies of RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 with the same kkk). For S2S^{2}S2 and RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2, the values of χ\chiχ are 222 and 111, respectively, so χ\chiχ alone is insufficient and must be used together with orientability.
