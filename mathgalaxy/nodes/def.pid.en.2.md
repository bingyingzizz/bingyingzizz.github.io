# principal ideal domain　`def.pid`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.pid)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.pid.md)

Counterexamples: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is not a PID — $(2, x)$ is not principal (if $(2,x) = (f)$, then $f \mid 2$ forces $f$ to be a constant $\pm 1$ or $\pm 2$; the former gives $(f) = A$, the latter does not contain $x$, so neither works). Similarly $K[x, y]$, where $(x, y)$ is not principal.

PID $\implies$ UFD (`imp.ring-pid-ufd`) is the central theorem for these rings: existence of factorization rests on "ascending chains of ideals terminate" (Noether), uniqueness on "irreducible = prime" (the Bézout property comes from being a PID).
