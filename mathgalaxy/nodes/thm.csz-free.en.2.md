# C(S,Z) is a free abelian group　`thm.csz-free`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.csz-free)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.csz-free.md)

$$C(S, \mathbb{Z}) = \{\, f : S \to \mathbb{Z}\ \mid f \text{continuous}\, \}$$

is a **free abelian group**.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This is a theorem of Specker (1950, for $S$ of type $\mathbb{Z}_p$) and of Nöbeling (1968, for general profinite sets).

The counterintuitive point: $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ looks like the infinite product "one integer at each point", yet it is **always free** — while its dual $M(S,\mathbb{Z}) = \prod_I \mathbb{Z}$ is the large side.
