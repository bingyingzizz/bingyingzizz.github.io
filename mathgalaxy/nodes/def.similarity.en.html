Statement

Two square matrices A,B∈Mn(K)A, B \in M_n(K)A,B∈Mn​(K) are similar, written A∼BA \sim BA∼B, iff there is an invertible matrix PPP such that

B=P−1A PB = P^{-1} A\, PB=P−1AP

PPP is called the transition matrix. An equivalent way to say it: AAA and BBB are matrices of the same linear map in two bases.

Invariants of similarity: rank, trace, determinant, characteristic polynomial (hence eigenvalues and their algebraic multiplicities), minimal polynomial.

Notes

⭐ Similarity is an equivalence relation (reflexive / symmetric / transitive), so Mn(K)M_n(K)Mn​(K) splits into similarity classes; the classification problem is to describe all similarity classes.

⭐ Two worlds, two ways of saying the same thing: on the matrix side "B=P−1APB = P^{-1}APB=P−1AP", on the map side "the same TTT, two bases".

The two edges change of basis and similarity and dep.changeofbasis-similarity on the graph say exactly this.

⚠ Similarity is stricter than equivalence (B=PAQB = PAQB=PAQ): equivalence only requires equal rank, while similarity also requires equal characteristic polynomial.

(1001)\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}(10​01​) and (1101)\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}(10​11​) are equivalent but not similar.
