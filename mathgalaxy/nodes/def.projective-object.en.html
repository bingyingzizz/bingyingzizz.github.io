Statement

An object PPP of a category C\mathcal{C}C is called projective if the functor hP=Hom⁡C(P,−)h_{P} = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(P, -)hP​=HomC​(P,−) preserves epimorphisms. Dually, III is called injective if hIh^{I}hI sends monomorphisms to epimorphisms.

Expanded as a lifting condition: for every epimorphism A↠BA \twoheadrightarrow BA↠B and every P→BP \to BP→B there exists a lift P→AP \to AP→A making the triangle commute:

P↙↓↘A↠B\begin{array}{ccc} & P & \\ \swarrow & \downarrow & \searrow \\ A & \twoheadrightarrow & B \end{array}↙A​P↓↠​↘B​

Dually, injectivity of III says that every A↣BA \rightarrowtail BA↣B and every A→IA \to IA→I extend to B→IB \to IB→I.

Notes

The name "projective" comes from module theory: the projective objects of R-ModR\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}R-Mod are exactly the projective modules, and the injective objects are exactly the injective modules — the two standard criteria (PPP is a direct summand, III is a direct summand) are this definition specialised to modules.

Two immediate corollaries: if PPP is projective, every epimorphism Y↠PY \twoheadrightarrow PY↠P has a section; if X→YX \to YX→Y has a retraction and YYY is projective, then XXX is projective too (the injective case is dual).

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set every object is projective, and this is precisely the axiom of choice: given a surjection A↠BA \twoheadrightarrow BA↠B and a point of BBB, one can always choose a preimage.
