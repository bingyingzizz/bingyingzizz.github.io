## Statement · **draft**
Let $T : V \to V$ be linear with $V \ne \{0\}$. A scalar $\lambda \in K$ is called an **eigenvalue** of $T$ if and only if there is a **nonzero** vector $v \in V$ such that

$$T(v) = \lambda\, v$$

Such a $v$ is called an **eigenvector** belonging to $\lambda$. The eigenvectors belonging to $\lambda$, together with $0$, form the subspace

$$E_{\lambda} := \ker(T - \lambda\,I) \subseteq V$$
