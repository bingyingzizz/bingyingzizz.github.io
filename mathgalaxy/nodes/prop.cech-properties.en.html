Statement

  1. H^\widehat{H}H is left exact (preserves finite limits);
  2. TTT is separated as a presheaf   ⟹  \implies⟹ H^(T)\widehat{H}(T)H(T); TTT separated   ⟹  \implies⟹ H^(T)\widehat{H}(T)H(T) is a sheaf;
  3. TTT is separated (a sheaf)   ⟺  \iff⟺ T→H^(T)T \to \widehat{H}(T)T→H(T) is a monomorphism (isomorphism).

Notes

The reason for 1 is short: Hom⁡(−,T)\operatorname{Hom}(-, T)Hom(−,T) preserves limits, and filtered colimits are left exact. 3 says that the difference between "separated" and "sheaf" is exactly "whether TTT has already reached the top inside H^\widehat{H}H" — hence "applying H^\widehat{H}H twice" has a definite meaning.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the plus construction for set-valued presheaves):

  • Separated   ⟺  \iff⟺ the canonical map is monic: unfold the definition of local equality, then use that covers have common refinements; this does not hold for arbitrary colimits.
  • Sheaf   ⟹  \implies⟹ the canonical map is an isomorphism: compatible families have a unique gluing, which is a direct unfolding of the sheaf condition; conversely use that the plus construction is always separated, deduce separatedness of the original presheaf from the isomorphism, and recover gluing.
  • Separated   ⟹  \implies⟹ becomes a sheaf after one plus: needs pullbacks of covers and the composition axioms; for an arbitrary presheaf one plus guarantees only separatedness, and one cannot directly claim it is already a sheaf.
  • The cost of left exactness is that filtered colimits in sets commute with finite limits; the functor of compatible families for a fixed cover preserves limits, but products over covers can be infinite products, which must not be mislabelled finite limits.
