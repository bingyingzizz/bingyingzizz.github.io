## Abstract · **draft**
A smooth manifold is a Hausdorff second countable space covered by charts $(U,\varphi)$ into $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ whose transition maps $\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}$ are $C^{\infty}$, equipped with a maximal atlas. Maximising the atlas is what makes smoothness a property of the manifold rather than of a chosen family of charts. Hausdorff separates points, while second countability gives paracompactness and hence partitions of unity, the tool that glues local constructions into global ones. A smooth structure is extra data, not a topological invariant: one topological space can carry inequivalent smooth structures.
