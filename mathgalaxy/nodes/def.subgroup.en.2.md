## Notes · **draft**
The two conditions can be combined into one: $H \ne \emptyset$ and $a, b \in H \implies a b^{-1} \in H$ (without $H \ne \emptyset$ the condition is vacuous: $H = \emptyset \subseteq G$ satisfies the universal statement, yet is not a subgroup).

The intersection of any number of subgroups is again a subgroup, but the **union** in general is not — this is why "the subgroup generated by $S$, namely $\langle S \rangle$" is defined as "the intersection of all subgroups containing $S$".
