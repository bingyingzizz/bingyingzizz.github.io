Statement

What it is about: two vector fields cannot be "composed" into a new vector field, but their commutator can be: subtract the two directional derivatives, the second-order terms cancel automatically, and what remains is still a vector field. This difference is the Lie bracket.

Let X,Y∈X(M)X, Y \in \mathfrak{X}(M)X,Y∈X(M). The Lie bracket [X,Y][X,Y][X,Y] is the operator acting on smooth functions

[X,Y]f:=X(Yf)−Y(Xf),f∈C∞(M).[X,Y]f := X(Yf) - Y(Xf),\qquad f \in C^{\infty}(M).[X,Y]f:=X(Yf)−Y(Xf),f∈C∞(M).

The right-hand side appears to use second derivatives, but second partial derivatives commute, so [X,Y]f[X,Y]f[X,Y]f contains only first derivatives of fff — hence it is still a vector field. Explicitly in a chart,

[X,Y]=∑i=1n(∑j=1n(Xj∂jYi−Yj∂jXi))∂∂xi.[X,Y] = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(X^{j}\partial_{j}Y^{i} - Y^{j}\partial_{j}X^{i}\bigr)\Bigr)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}} .[X,Y]=i=1∑n​(j=1∑n​(Xj∂j​Yi−Yj∂j​Xi))∂xi∂​.

Four properties (f,g∈C∞(M)f,g \in C^{\infty}(M)f,g∈C∞(M)):

  1. Antisymmetry: [X,Y]=−[Y,X][X,Y] = -[Y,X][X,Y]=−[Y,X], in particular [X,X]=0[X,X] = 0[X,X]=0;
  2. R\mathbb{R}R-bilinearity;

Notes

Why the result is still a vector field: X(Yf)X(Yf)X(Yf) and Y(Xf)Y(Xf)Y(Xf) each contain second derivatives of fff, in a chart terms such as Xj∂j(Yi∂if)X^{j}\partial_{j}\bigl(Y^{i}\partial_{i}f\bigr)Xj∂j​(Yi∂i​f);

when subtracting, the coefficients of ∂j∂if\partial_{j}\partial_{i}f∂j​∂i​f are XjYiX^{j}Y^{i}XjYi and YjXiY^{j}X^{i}YjXi respectively, and since it is symmetric in i,ji,ji,j, these terms cancel one by one.

The second-order terms disappear, and what remains is a legitimate derivation.

Item 4 shows it is not a tensor: [X,Y][X,Y][X,Y] is not C∞(M)C^{\infty}(M)C∞(M)-linear in YYY — the extra term f(Xg)Yf(Xg)Yf(Xg)Y is precisely the trace of "differentiating twice". Hence "the bracket of two vector fields cannot be defined pointwise", which is the fundamental difference from every tensor operation (张量场).

Jacobi is the composition of three things: writing it as [X,[Y,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z]+[Y,[X,Z]][X,[Y,Z]] = [[X,Y],Z] + [Y,[X,Z]][X,[Y,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z]+[Y,[X,Z]], the reading is "[X,⋅][X,\cdot][X,⋅] is a derivation". It is not an arbitrary axiom but a necessary consequence of "the bracket comes from first-order differential operators" — in the expansion six terms cancel pairwise, using the product rule.

Geometric meaning: [X,Y][X,Y][X,Y] measures the difference between "walk one step along XXX then one along YYY" and "walk in the reverse order", divided by the square of the step. Thus [X,Y]=0[X,Y] = 0[X,Y]=0 means that the two families of integral curves fit into a coordinate net — provided X,YX, YX,Y are linearly independent at that point (when X=Y=∂xX = Y = \partial_{x}X=Y=∂x​ the bracket vanishes, but the two families coincide and cannot serve as two coordinate directions). This is the starting point of Frobenius integrability, which is not developed here.
