## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **Integrable $\iff$ absolutely integrable**: this is where the Lebesgue integral finally becomes convenient — because $|f| \in L^{+}$, every theorem for the non-negative case applies directly.

(Compare the Riemann improper integral: there $\int f$ may converge while $\int|f|$ diverges; this is called conditional convergence.)

⚠ Strictly speaking, the elements of $L^{1}$ are **equivalence classes of functions** (equal a.e. counts as the same), so "$\int |f| = 0 \implies f = 0$" must be read a.e. This is the key step that later makes Lp spaces normed spaces.
