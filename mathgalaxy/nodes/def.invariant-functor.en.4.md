# Invariant Functor　`def.invariant-functor`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.invariant-functor)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.invariant-functor.md)

Typical counterexample: $G = \mathbb{Z}/2$ acts by sign on $\mathbb{Z}$; take $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\cdot 2} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2 \to 0$. Then $M^{G} = 0$ while $N^{G} = \mathbb{Z}/2$, and $M^{G} \to N^{G}$ is not surjective.

⚠️ **The dual side is not it**: $M \mapsto M_{G} := M\big/\langle g m - m \rangle$ (coinvariants) is right exact and corresponds to group **homology** $H_{n}(G, M)$. This map follows only the **co**homology line.
