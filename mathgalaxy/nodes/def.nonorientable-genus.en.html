Statement

Let MMM be a closed nonorientable surface. Its nonorientable genus k(M)k(M)k(M) is defined by requiring that MMM be homeomorphic to the connected sum of kkk projective planes and taking the least such integer k≥1k\ge 1k≥1. Equivalently, k(M)k(M)k(M) is the number of crosscaps in standard form, and the Euler characteristic satisfies χ(M)=2−k(M)\chi(M)=2-k(M)χ(M)=2−k(M).

Notes

Why not use χ=2−k\chi=2-kχ=2−k directly as the definition: That would presuppose the classification theorem (every closed nonorientable surface is homeomorphic to a standard form). Defining it as the minimum number of projective planes in a connected sum is independent of classification, following the same idea as the homological definition in genus.

Small values: k=1k=1k=1 the projective plane. k=2k=2k=2 the Klein bottle. k=3k=3k=3 is the surface with three crosscaps.

Distinction from orientable genus: In the orientable case, χ=2−2g\chi=2-2gχ=2−2g. In the nonorientable case, χ=2−k\chi=2-kχ=2−k. The same χ\chiχ gives different counts of holes in the two classes of surfaces, so the two notions must be distinguished.
