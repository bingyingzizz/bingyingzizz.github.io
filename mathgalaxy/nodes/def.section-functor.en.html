Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a site, X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C, and C~\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}C the sheaf category. The section functor at XXX is

Notes

It is the Hom functor of hXh_{X}hX​: Γ(X,F)=Hom⁡C~(hX,F)=F(X)\Gamma(X, F) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}(h_{X}, F) = F(X)Γ(X,F)=HomC​(hX​,F)=F(X) (Yoneda's lemma) — so the section functor is nothing new; it is simply "probing FFF with a representable presheaf".

⭐ What it preserves and what it does not:

  • Preserves all limits: limits in a sheaf category are computed pointwise in the presheaf category, and a pointwise limit certainly is still that limit at XXX.
  • In general does not preserve colimits: colimits must first be computed in presheaves and then sheafified, and after sheafification the value at XXX may be strictly larger than the colimit. This is the entire content of "the section functor is not exact".

When it does preserve colimits: when XXX is projective in the sheaf category (Hom⁡(hX,−)=Γ(X,−)\operatorname{Hom}(h_{X}, -) = \Gamma(X,-)Hom(hX​,−)=Γ(X,−) preserves epimorphisms), it preserves colimits.

⭐ An example on the condensed side: for F∈FCHausF \in \mathbf{FCHaus}F∈FCHaus, Z⋅F\mathbb{Z}\cdot FZ⋅F is projective, so Γ(F,−)\Gamma(F, -)Γ(F,−) preserves all limits and all colimits — that proposition is a special case of this one.
