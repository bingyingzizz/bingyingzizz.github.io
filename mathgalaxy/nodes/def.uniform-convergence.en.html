Notes

⭐ The only difference from pointwise convergence is the order of quantifiers: here ∃N\exists N∃N comes before ∀x\forall x∀x — a single NNN works for the whole domain.

⭐ The supremum form is the most-used criterion: uniform convergence says "the supremum of the error function tends to zero", that is, convergence in the sup norm.

This also explains why it is "convergence in a function space" rather than "convergence on a set of points".

⚠ Uniform convergence   ⟹  \implies⟹ pointwise convergence, but not conversely (fn(x)=xnf_n(x) = x^{n}fn​(x)=xn on [0,1][0, 1][0,1]).

⭐ The three exchange theorems in this pack (preserving continuity, integrals, derivatives) all need only uniform convergence — and convergence of the kind fn(x)=xnf_n(x) = x^{n}fn​(x)=xn that "misses one point" is exactly what destroys continuity.
