Statement

What it says: a smooth map gives a linear map at every point, sending "directions along curves" to directions on the image — this is the derivative on manifolds; there is no subtraction on a manifold, and the only thing one can do is follow curves.

Let F:N→MF : N \to MF:N→M be smooth and p∈Np \in Np∈N. Define

dFp:TpN⟶TF(p)M,dFp([γ]):=[F∘γ],\mathrm{d}F_{p} : T_{p}N \longrightarrow T_{F(p)}M,\qquad \mathrm{d}F_{p}([\gamma]) := [F \circ \gamma],dFp​:Tp​N⟶TF(p)​M,dFp​([γ]):=[F∘γ],

i.e. the class of a curve through ppp, say γ\gammaγ, goes to the class through F(p)F(p)F(p) of F∘γF \circ \gammaF∘γ. dFp\mathrm{d}F_{p}dFp​ is the tangent map (also differential) of FFF at ppp.

In charts (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ) of NNN and (V,ψ)(V,\psi)(V,ψ) of MMM, the matrix of dFp\mathrm{d}F_{p}dFp​ is the Jacobian of the local representation of FFF:

[dFp]φ,ψ=(∂(ψ∘F∘φ−1)i∂xj(x))i,j,x=φ(p).\Bigl[\mathrm{d}F_{p}\Bigr]_{\varphi,\psi} = \Bigl(\frac{\partial (\psi \circ F \circ \varphi^{-1})^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}(x)\Bigr)_{i,j},\qquad x = \varphi(p).[dFp​]φ,ψ​=(∂xj∂(ψ∘F∘φ−1)i​(x))i,j​,x=φ(p).

d(G∘F)p=dGF(p)∘dFp,d(id⁡N)p=id⁡TpN.\mathrm{d}(G \circ F)_{p} = \mathrm{d}G_{F(p)} \circ \mathrm{d}F_{p},\qquad \mathrm{d}(\operatorname{id}_{N})_{p} = \operatorname{id}_{T_{p}N}.d(G∘F)p​=dGF(p)​∘dFp​,d(idN​)p​=idTp​N​.

Letting ppp run over NNN gives a map of tangent bundles dF:TN→TM\mathrm{d}F : TN \to TMdF:TN→TM, v↦dFπ(v)(v)v \mapsto \mathrm{d}F_{\pi(v)}(v)v↦dFπ(v)​(v).

Notes

Well-definedness is the chain rule: changing the chart φ\varphiφ to θ\thetaθ, two curves through ppp with the same velocity in the φ\varphiφ-chart have the same velocity in the θ\thetaθ-chart (both equal the same Jacobian matrix times the same vector).

Class is invariant, so dFp\mathrm{d}F_{p}dFp​ is defined — but existence rests entirely on smoothness of transition maps, which is where this node meets the previous two.

The derivation viewpoint is leaner: as a derivation, dFp(v)\mathrm{d}F_{p}(v)dFp​(v) is (dFp(v))(f)=v(f∘F)\bigl(\mathrm{d}F_{p}(v)\bigr)(f) = v(f \circ F)(dFp​(v))(f)=v(f∘F) — one composition, no curves mentioned. Both routes give the same linear map.

Why it is not called "the derivative": in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, dFp\mathrm{d}F_{p}dFp​ is a matrix directly tied to difference quotients; on a manifold there is no subtraction, and dFp\mathrm{d}F_{p}dFp​ is merely a linear map between tangent spaces.

It is first-order information; under a change of coordinates one multiplies by a Jacobian on each side (one forward, one backward), so "dF\mathrm{d}FdF is a tensor" is right while "∂iF\partial_{i}F∂i​F is a tensor" is not.

Meaning of the chain rule: M↦TpMM \mapsto T_{p}MM↦Tp​M and F↦dFpF \mapsto \mathrm{d}F_{p}F↦dFp​ form a functor (the tangent functor). Thus on the layer of manifolds and smooth maps, tangent spaces automatically inherit all composition structure — which is also why d2=0\mathrm{d}^{2} = 0d2=0 holds formally.

Notation clash: the same d\mathrm{d}d denotes the tangent map dFp\mathrm{d}F_{p}dFp​ and the exterior derivative dω\mathrm{d}\omegadω.

These are not two things: df\mathrm{d}fdf is the tangent map dfp:TpM→R\mathrm{d}f_{p} : T_{p}M \to \mathbb{R}dfp​:Tp​M→R, i.e. a 111-form (外微分（欧氏开集）).
