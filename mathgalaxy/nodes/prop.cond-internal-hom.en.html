Statement

Let XXX be a condensed set and YYY a compactly generated space (regarded as the condensed set Y‾\underline{Y}Y​). Then there is a natural isomorphism of condensed sets

C(X(∙), Y)≅Hom⁡(X, Y‾),C\bigl(X(\bullet),\ Y\bigr) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(X,\ \underline{Y}),C(X(∙), Y)≅Hom(X, Y​),

where the left-hand side is the condensed set determined by "the test object S↦C(X(S),Y)S \mapsto C\bigl(X(S), Y\bigr)S↦C(X(S),Y)" (the space of continuous maps with the compact-open topology), and the right-hand side is the internal Hom of Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond.

In particular, if XXX and YYY are both compactly generated, then kC(X,Y)≅Hom⁡(X,Y)kC(X, Y) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(X, Y)kC(X,Y)≅Hom(X,Y).

Notes

Why it is needed. Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is a topos, so the internal Hom is automatic from the topos structure — but that is abstract. This item gives its concrete realization: "the internal Hom is the space of continuous functions". With it, the internal Hom becomes something computable and transportable to Top\mathbf{Top}Top.

What the two sides say. X(∙)X(\bullet)X(∙) is the underlying space of XXX (feed XXX to compact Hausdorff test objects SSS to get a set, then equip it with the topology determined by test maps); Hom⁡(X,Y‾)\operatorname{Hom}(X, \underline{Y})Hom(X,Y​) is the object "maps from XXX to YYY".

The equality says: taking the underlying space and then the function space is the same as taking the internal Hom and then the underlying space.
