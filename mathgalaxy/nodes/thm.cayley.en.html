Statement

Let GGG be a group. Left multiplication gives an injective homomorphism

ρ:G↪Sym⁡(G),ρ(g)(h):=gh\rho : G \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}(G), \qquad \rho(g)(h) := ghρ:G↪Sym(G),ρ(g)(h):=gh

In particular, if ∣G∣=n|G| = n∣G∣=n then GGG is isomorphic to a subgroup of SnS_nSn​.

Notes

⭐ The philosophical content of the theorem exceeds its computational content: every abstract group can be realized concretely as a group of permutations.

That is, "group theory" and "permutation group theory" are the same subject — abstraction does not really create new objects, only a change of viewpoint.

⚠ The price is high: for ∣G∣=n|G| = n∣G∣=n one embeds into SnS_nSn​ (of order n!n!n!), so Cayley's theorem is almost never used for computation.

Its real use is as a contrast with representation theory: replacing "acting on a set" by "acting on a vector space" is the beginning of that line.

⭐ Left multiplication is also free (every stabilizer is trivial: gh=h  ⟹  g=egh = h \implies g = egh=h⟹g=e), so it is automatically injective — this is the core sentence of the proof.
