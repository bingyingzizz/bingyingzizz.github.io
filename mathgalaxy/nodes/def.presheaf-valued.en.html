Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C, D\mathcal{D}D be categories.

  1. A presheaf with values in D\mathcal{D}D is a contravariant functor T:Cop→DT : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{D}T:Cop→D; a morphism of presheaves is a natural transformation.
  2. When C\mathcal{C}C is a site, a sheaf F:Cop→DF : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{D}F:Cop→D is a presheaf such that for every Y∈DY \in \mathcal{D}Y∈D, the set-valued presheaf
X⟼Hom⁡D(Y, F(X))X \longmapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl(Y,\ F(X)\bigr)X⟼HomD​(Y, F(X))

is a sheaf.

Notes

Taking D\mathcal{D}D to be Set\mathbf{Set}Set returns the original notion of sheaf; taking it to be Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab gives abelian sheaves.

The trick in the definition is: use "maps into YYY" to reduce the sheaf condition in D\mathcal{D}D to sheaves of sets. Hence every result about sheaves of sets whose statement only uses constructions also available in D\mathcal{D}D (limits, products, …) carries over item by item — this is the whole secret of "sheaves with general values".
