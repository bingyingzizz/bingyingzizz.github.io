Statement

  • Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab: AB6 and AB4*;
  • AbTop\mathbf{AbTop}AbTop (topological abelian groups): satisfies only AB1;
  • AbCHaus\mathbf{AbCHaus}AbCHaus (compact Hausdorff abelian groups): satisfies only AB1 (more precisely AbCHaus≃Abop\mathbf{AbCHaus} \simeq \mathbf{Ab}^{\mathrm{op}}AbCHaus≃Abop, hence satisfies AB4 and AB6*);
  • AbC\mathbf{Ab}^{\mathcal{C}}AbC (presheaves of abelian groups on an arbitrary category C\mathcal{C}C): AB6 and AB4*;
  • Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) (abelian groups on a topos T\mathcal{T}T): AB5 and AB3*;
  • CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb: AB6 and AB4*.

Notes

How to read this table: from bottom to top the axioms get stronger (AB1 < AB2 < AB3 < AB4 < AB5 < AB6). The two most interesting comparisons in the table:

  • AbTop\mathbf{AbTop}AbTop only reaches AB1 — it does not even satisfy AB2. The reason is that "morphisms need not be strict" (there are monomorphisms that are not kernels), so it is not even an abelian category and cannot do homological algebra. This is exactly why topological groups must be moved to the condensed side. - Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) stops at AB5, while CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb reaches AB6 — what is gained is precisely "filtered colimits commute with products" and "products are exact". This is what condensed mathematics obtains over a general topos.

⭐ The AbCHaus≃Abop\mathbf{AbCHaus} \simeq \mathbf{Ab}^{\mathrm{op}}AbCHaus≃Abop line in the table is the result of upgrading Pontryagin duality to an equivalence of categories: the category of compact Hausdorff abelian groups is anti-equivalent to the category of discrete abelian groups, so its axiom level is the dual version of that of Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab.
