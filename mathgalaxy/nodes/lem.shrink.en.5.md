# Shrinking Lemma　`lem.shrink`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=lem.shrink)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./lem.shrink.md)

Take $j := \max J$. Then for every $k > j$ we have $x \notin U_{k}$, so $x \in A_{j} \subseteq V_{j}$.
**Completion**: By $V_{i} \subseteq U_{i}$, an open neighborhood meeting only finitely many $U_{i}$ also meets only finitely many $V_{i}$. Hence $\mathcal{V}$ is locally finite. To check coverage, given $x$, point finiteness gives an index $i$ greater than all indices of members containing $x$ among the $U_{j}$. Applying the covering property to $\mathcal{W}_{i}$ then gives $x \in V_{j}$ (for some $j < i$).

∎
