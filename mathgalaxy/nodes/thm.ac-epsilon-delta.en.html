Statement

Let ν\nuν be a finite signed measure and μ\muμ a measure. Then

ν≪μ  ⟺  ∀ε>0,∃δ>0:μ(E)<δ  ⟹  ∣ν(E)∣≤ε\nu \ll \mu \iff \forall\varepsilon > 0, \exists\delta > 0 : \mu(E) < \delta \implies |\nu(E)| \le \varepsilonν≪μ⟺∀ε>0,∃δ>0:μ(E)<δ⟹∣ν(E)∣≤ε

Notes

It upgrades "zero on null sets" to the quantitative "small values on sets of small measure" — this is precisely where the word "continuous" in absolute continuity comes from.

⚠ "ν\nuν finite" cannot be omitted: otherwise δ\deltaδ cannot be controlled (e.g. the μ=Lebesgue\mu = Lebesgueμ=Lebesgue measure, or ν(E)=∫E1/x\nu(E) = \int_E 1/xν(E)=∫E​1/x).

(The ⟸\Longleftarrow⟸ direction is straightforward: when μ(E)=0\mu (E) = 0μ(E)=0, for every ε\varepsilonε one has ∣ν(E)∣≤ε|\nu (E)| \le \varepsilon∣ν(E)∣≤ε, hence ν(E)=0\nu (E) = 0ν(E)=0.)
