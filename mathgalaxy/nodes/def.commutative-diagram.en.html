Statement

Let III be a small category (its objects and morphisms form a small set). A diagram in C\mathcal{C}C indexed by III is a functor

D:I⟶CD : I \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}D:I⟶C

All such diagrams, with natural transformations as morphisms, form the category

CI:=Hom⁡(I,C)\mathcal{C}^{I} := \operatorname{Hom}(I, \mathcal{C})CI:=Hom(I,C)

If between any two objects of III there is at most one morphism (such an III is called a preorder), then DDD is called a commutative diagram over III.

Notes

Commutativity is a property of the indexing category, not something the functor supplies. “III is a preorder” holds if and only if “every III-shaped diagram commutes”: then two paths in III with the same source and target compose to the unique morphism of Hom⁡(i,j)\operatorname{Hom}(i, j)Hom(i,j), hence are equal.

In general only the “shape” is given. When III is not a preorder, DDD can only be called a III-shaped diagram: a functor is not required to send parallel f≠g:i⇉jf \ne g : i \rightrightarrows jf=g:i⇉j to equal arrows.

The most typical non-commuting diagram is the parallel pair ∙⇉∙\bullet \rightrightarrows \bullet∙⇉∙, whose limit and colimit are exactly the equaliser and coequaliser;

the classical commuting square corresponds to the preorder {A≤B≤D, A≤C≤D}\{A \le B \le D,\ A \le C \le D\}{A≤B≤D, A≤C≤D}.

Reading it on paper. A diagram drawn on paper is only a presentation: objects are assigned to the vertices of a directed graph GGG and morphisms to its arrows. Saying that it commutes means that any two paths with the same source and target have equal composites under the labelling — this is a condition to be checked, equivalent to the labelling descending to a functor on the quotient of the free category on GGG.

III is called the indexing category: it prescribes only the shape of the diagram, not its content.

Precomposing with a functor λ:I→J\lambda : I \to Jλ:I→J gives reindexing λ∗:CJ→CI\lambda^{*} : \mathcal{C}^{J} \to \mathcal{C}^{I}λ∗:CJ→CI: it cuts a JJJ-shaped diagram down to a III-shaped one.

In particular, the unique functor I→1I \to \mathbf{1}I→1 to the terminal category induces the constant-diagram functor Δ:C⟶CI\Delta : \mathcal{C} \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}^{I}Δ:C⟶CI, which sends an object XXX to the constant diagram “XXX at every position and 1X1_X1X​ on every arrow”.
