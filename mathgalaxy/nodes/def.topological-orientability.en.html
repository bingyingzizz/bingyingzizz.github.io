Statement

Let MMM be a nnn-dimensional topological manifold (n≥1n\ge 1n≥1). Call MMM orientable if there is a collection of charts (Uα,φα)(U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})(Uα​,φα​) covering MMM such that the transition maps between any two charts,

φβ∘φα−1: φα(Uα∩Uβ)⟶φβ(Uα∩Uβ)\varphi_{\beta}\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1}:\ \varphi_{\alpha}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})\longrightarrow \varphi_{\beta}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})φβ​∘φα−1​: φα​(Uα​∩Uβ​)⟶φβ​(Uα​∩Uβ​)

are all orientation-preserving (give Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn its standard orientation; see the notes for a precise definition of orientation preservation). Such an atlas is called an oriented atlas. If no oriented atlas exists, MMM is nonorientable.

Notes

1. What orientation preservation means. The standard orientation of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn is given by the standard generator of local homology. For x∈Rnx\in\mathbb{R}^{n}x∈Rn, Hn(Rn,Rn∖{x};Z)≅ZH_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}Hn​(Rn,Rn∖{x};Z)≅Z, choose a generator as the positive orientation. Let h:V→Wh:V\to Wh:V→W be a homeomorphism between two open subsets of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, and let x∈Vx\in Vx∈V. We say hhh is orientation-preserving at xxx if the induced isomorphism Hn(V,V∖{x})→Hn(W,W∖{h(x)})H_{n}(V,V\setminus\{x\})\to H_{n}(W,W\setminus\{h(x)\})Hn​(V,V∖{x})→Hn​(W,W∖{h(x)}) sends the standard generator to the standard generator. Otherwise it is orientation-reversing. If VVV is connected, preserving orientation at one point implies preserving it everywhere (the sign on local homology varies continuously and can take only two values). For n=1n=1n=1 this is the usual notion of being increasing, and for n=2n=2n=2 it is the usual notion of an orientation-preserving homeomorphism.

2. The orientation double cover. Let M^\widehat{M}M be the set of all pairs (x,ox)(x,\mathfrak{o}_{x})(x,ox​), where x∈Mx\in Mx∈M and ox\mathfrak{o}_{x}ox​ is a local orientation of TxMT_{x}MTx​M, obtained by pulling back the standard orientation given by a chart around xxx from Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn. Two charts give the same ox\mathfrak{o}_{x}ox​ if and only if the transition map preserves orientation at xxx. The charts identify M^\widehat{M}M locally with U×{±1}U\times\{\pm1\}U×{±1}, giving its topology. The projection p:M^→Mp:\widehat{M}\to Mp:M→M is a two-sheeted covering called the orientation double cover. MMM is orientable   ⟺  \iff⟺ ppp is trivial   ⟺  \iff⟺ ppp has a continuous section   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat{M}M has exactly two connected components (when MMM is connected). In the nonorientable case, M^\widehat{M}M is connected and Z/2\mathbb{Z}/2Z/2 acts freely on it, without fixed points.

3. Homological characterization. Let MMM be a closed connected nnn-manifold. For any xxx, Hn(M,M∖{x};Z)≅ZH_{n}(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}Hn​(M,M∖{x};Z)≅Z. The image of the natural map Hn(M;Z)→Hn(M,M∖{x};Z)H_{n}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{n}(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb{Z})Hn​(M;Z)→Hn​(M,M∖{x};Z) can only be 000 or Z\mathbb{Z}Z (Hatcher §3.3). MMM is orientable   ⟺  \iff⟺ this image is Z\mathbb{Z}Z (equivalently, there is a fundamental class [M]∈Hn(M;Z)[M]\in H_{n}(M;\mathbb{Z})[M]∈Hn​(M;Z) giving a local orientation at each xxx). In the nonorientable case, Hn(M;Z)=0H_{n}(M;\mathbb{Z})=0Hn​(M;Z)=0. Proof idea: Use the transition functions of the orientation double cover to glue a constant sheaf of local homology coefficients. Orientability is precisely the triviality of this local system. The existence of a fundamental class is dual to the existence of a section.

4. The surface case (the main application here). For a closed surface SSS, SSS is orientable   ⟺  \iff⟺ SSS contains no Möbius band as a subspace   ⟺  \iff⟺ SSS admits a triangulation in which every 222-simplex can be coherently oriented (the two sides of each interior edge induce opposite orientations on it, as used in Lemma D of Euler characteristic and genus)   ⟺  \iff⟺ H2(S;Z)≅ZH_{2}(S;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}H2​(S;Z)≅Z. The equivalence between a coherently oriented triangulation and an oriented atlas is the step before Lemma D: put each simplex in a chart and glue using the orientation-preserving transition maps.

5. Examples. S2S^{2}S2, T2T^{2}T2, and all orientable closed surfaces are orientable. RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2, the Klein bottle, and the Möbius band are nonorientable. The orientation double cover of RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 is S2S^{2}S2 (with the antipodal action), the orientation double cover of the Klein bottle is T2T^{2}T2, and that of the Möbius band is an annulus. These examples show that in the nonorientable case, local orientations exist but cannot be chosen consistently over the whole space.

6. Relation to the smooth definition. Orientability for smooth manifolds is the smooth version, with transition maps of positive Jacobian determinant. Smooth orientability implies topological orientability because the smooth atlas is an oriented atlas. Conversely, for a topologically orientable smooth manifold, a partition of unity glues local nnn-forms into a nowhere-zero nnn-form (see the node on volume forms and orientability in this cluster). The definitions therefore agree on smooth manifolds. The extrinsic characterization of orientability for surfaces is the version in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3, which requires a continuous unit normal field. It applies only to surfaces that embed in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 (for example, RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 does not embed there and is outside its scope). The topological definition applies to all topological surfaces without requiring smoothness.

7. Applications. (1) Lemma D of Euler characteristic and genus uses an oriented atlas to orient the 222-simplices of a triangulation coherently, giving H2≅ZH_{2}\cong\mathbb{Z}H2​≅Z. (2) The nonorientable case of Radó's theorem uses the orientation double cover S^→S\widehat{S}\to SS→S (a free Z/2\mathbb{Z}/2Z/2-action with no branch points). Apply the orientable case to S^\widehat{S}S, then take an equivariant refinement so that the quotient is also a triangulation (the equivariant triangulation lemma has no node here). (3) In the classification theorem for surfaces, orientability is a homeomorphism invariant and distinguishes the standard forms together with χ\chiχ.

⚠ Two common points of confusion. (1) Nonorientable surfaces can be triangulable. Radó's theorem gives triangulations of all compact surfaces, and RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 has one obtained by identifying antipodal points of a regular icosahedron. (2) The orientation double cover is a free two-sheeted covering without branch points. The local model z↦z2z\mapsto z^{2}z↦z2 with branching belongs to the branched covering associated with a meromorphic function (the complex-analytic approach in Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable).
