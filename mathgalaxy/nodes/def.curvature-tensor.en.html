Statement

In plain terms: the curvature tensor measures "how much the result changes when one transports around in two directions and swaps their order". On a flat plane swapping makes no difference, and the difference is zero; on a sphere swapping the order leaves a small discrepancy, and that discrepancy is curvature. It is intrinsic: the formula contains only ggg and its derivatives — no normal, no second fundamental form.

The Levi-Civita connection on a surface defines the curvature operator

R(X,Y)Z=∇X∇YZ−∇Y∇XZ−∇[X,Y]Z,R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z,

in coordinates, R(∂i,∂j)∂k=Rijkℓ∂ℓR(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k} = R^{\ell}_{ijk}\partial_{\ell}R(∂i​,∂j​)∂k​=Rijkℓ​∂ℓ​, where

Rijkℓ=∂iΓjkℓ−∂jΓikℓ+ΓipℓΓjkp−ΓjpℓΓikp,R^{\ell}_{ijk} = \partial_{i}\Gamma^{\ell}_{jk}-\partial_{j}\Gamma^{\ell}_{ik}+\Gamma^{\ell}_{ip}\Gamma^{p}_{jk}-\Gamma^{\ell}_{jp}\Gamma^{p}_{ik},Rijkℓ​=∂i​Γjkℓ​−∂j​Γikℓ​+Γipℓ​Γjkp​−Γjpℓ​Γikp​,

Notes

It comes from integrability conditions.

Finding coordinates that flatten gijg_{ij}gij​ to δij\delta_{ij}δij​ is equivalent to solving gij=δαβ∂yα∂xi∂yβ∂xjg_{ij} = \delta_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial y^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial y^{\beta}}{\partial x^{j}}gij​=δαβ​∂xi∂yα​∂xj∂yβ​;

and the compatibility requirement ∂i∂j=∂j∂i\partial_{i}\partial_{j} = \partial_{j}\partial_{i}∂i​∂j​=∂j​∂i​ is exactly Rijkℓ=0R^{\ell}_{ijk} = 0Rijkℓ​=0.

So from the very beginning RRR is the obstruction to flattening the metric.

Ricci identity: [∇∂i,∇∂j]∂k=Rijkℓ∂ℓ[\nabla_{\partial_{i}},\nabla_{\partial_{j}}]\partial_{k} = R^{\ell}_{ijk}\partial_{\ell}[∇∂i​​,∇∂j​​]∂k​=Rijkℓ​∂ℓ​. Hence RRR measures by how much covariant derivatives fail to commute — this is its most common definition, and the reason this map chose it.

Why it is intrinsic: the formula for RRR contains only Γ\GammaΓ and ∂Γ\partial\Gamma∂Γ, and Γ\GammaΓ contains only ggg and ∂g\partial g∂g. The second fundamental form does not appear.

⚠ In dimension two there is only one independent component (R1212R_{1212}R1212​, the rest being determined by symmetry), so "curvature" on a surface is one number rather than a pile of numbers; only in dimension three and higher does RRR acquire genuine tensor structure (and then KKK gives only part of its trace).

⚠ Sign convention: this pack takes the formula displayed above. The other convention (moving the two Γ\GammaΓ-product terms to the other side) flips every component, and hence every definition of curvature, while the geometry is unchanged. Testing on the unit sphere is the fastest way to tell the conventions apart.
