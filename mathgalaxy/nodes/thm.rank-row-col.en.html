Statement

Let A∈Mm×n(K)A \in M_{m \times n}(K)A∈Mm×n​(K). For AAA, regard its mmm row vectors as vectors in KnK^{n}Kn; the dimension of the subspace they span is called the row rank. The column rank is defined similarly (the AAA's nnn column vectors span a subspace of KmK^{m}Km, whose dimension it is). Then

row rank === column rank =rank⁡A= \operatorname{rank} A=rankA, that is,

rank⁡rowA=rank⁡colA=rank⁡A\operatorname{rank}_{\text{row}} A = \operatorname{rank}_{\text{col}} A = \operatorname{rank} Arankrow​A=rankcol​A=rankA

where rank⁡A\operatorname{rank} ArankA is the rank of the linear map x↦Axx \mapsto Axx↦Ax (see "rank").

Notes

⭐ "Row rank = column rank" is not obvious: it says that in KnK^{n}Kn the mmm row vectors and in KmK^{m}Km the nnn column vectors are exactly equally independent, and this is precisely why "the rank of a matrix" is a single number.

⭐ The proof goes through elementary operations: left multiplication by an invertible matrix does not change the row space (it only changes the basis), so one may first reduce AAA to reduced row echelon form, where the number of nonzero rows is the rank — and the column rank is determined by the same pivots.

⚠ This is the justification for "computing rank by elimination": moving column operations around does not change that number.
