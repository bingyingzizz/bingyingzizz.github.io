Statement

Let AbHaus\mathbf{AbHaus}AbHaus be the category of Hausdorff topological abelian groups (with continuous homomorphisms as morphisms). Then

  1. For a continuous homomorphism f:M→Nf : M \to Nf:M→N, the kernel is ker⁡f=f−1(0)\ker f = f^{-1}(0)kerf=f−1(0) (with the subspace topology) and the cokernel is coker⁡f=N/f(M)‾\operatorname{coker} f = N/\overline{f(M)}cokerf=N/f(M)​ (with the quotient topology);
  2. A continuous homomorphism is a kernel   ⟺  \iff⟺ it is a homeomorphic embedding onto a closed subgroup, and it is a cokernel   ⟺  \iff⟺ it is an open surjection;
  3. The sequence 0→M′→φM→ψM′′→00 \to M' \xrightarrow{\varphi} M \xrightarrow{\psi} M'' \to 00→M′φ​Mψ​M′′→0 is strictly exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ it is exact as a sequence of abstract abelian groups, φ\varphiφ is a closed embedding, and ψ\psiψ is an open surjection.

Notes

⭐ "Strict" is not decoration: for topological abelian groups "exact" and "strictly exact" are genuinely different — topological abelian groups are not an abelian category, and what is missing is exactly "every morphism is determined by its kernel and cokernel". Condensed mathematics repairs this layer by embedding topological groups into CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the universal property of kernel and cokernel also determines the topology):

  • Kernel   ⟹  \implies⟹ closed embedding: the kernel carries the subspace topology, and a Hausdorff target makes the zero point closed; conversely a closed embedding is the kernel of the quotient map M→M/HM\to M/HM→M/H, and it must not be miscalled the equaliser of itself with zero.
  • Cokernel   ⟹  \implies⟹ open surjection: quotient by the closure of the image, and the quotient map of groups is open; conversely an open surjection gives a homeomorphism onto the quotient by the kernel, hence is the cokernel of its kernel — both need the universal property of the quotient topology.
  • Strictly exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ abstract exactness plus closed embeddings and open surjections: forward, expand the kernel–cokernel sequence and use the descriptions above; backward, recover the universal property via the two topological identifications, with no open mapping theorem.
  • Closure cannot be dropped: Q↪R\mathbb Q\hookrightarrow\mathbb RQ↪R has dense but non-closed image in a Hausdorff group. Openness cannot be dropped either: the identity from R\mathbb RR with the discrete topology to the usual topology is a continuous surjection that is not open; the coarse-topology integer group of the original remark is not an object of this category.
