Statement

Let U⊆VU \subseteq VU⊆V be a subspace. The annihilator of UUU is defined as

U0:={ φ∈V∗:φ(u)=0 whenever u∈U }⊆V∗U^{0} := \{\, \varphi \in V^{\ast} : \varphi(u) = 0 \ \text{whenever}\ u \in U \,\} \subseteq V^{\ast}U0:={φ∈V∗:φ(u)=0 whenever u∈U}⊆V∗

It is a subspace of V∗V^{\ast}V∗ and (when dim⁡V<∞\dim V < \inftydimV<∞)

dim⁡U0=dim⁡V−dim⁡U,(U0)0=U\dim U^{0} = \dim V - \dim U, \qquad (U^{0})^{0} = UdimU0=dimV−dimU,(U0)0=U

(the second equality views UUU as a subset of V∗∗V^{\ast\ast}V∗∗ via the natural isomorphism V≅V∗∗V \cong V^{\ast\ast}V≅V∗∗).

Notes

⭐ The annihilator consists of “the functionals that vanish on UUU”, so dim⁡U0=dim⁡V−dim⁡U\dim U^0 = \dim V - \dim UdimU0=dimV−dimU is the dual form of the rank–nullity theorem: dualizing π:V→V/U\pi : V \to V/Uπ:V→V/U gives U0≅(V/U)∗U^{0} \cong (V/U)^{\ast}U0≅(V/U)∗.

⭐ (U0)0=U(U^{0})^{0} = U(U0)0=U says that subspaces and annihilators are dual to each other (in finite dimension a two-way dictionary: a larger UUU corresponds to a smaller U0U^0U0, order-reversing).

⚠ In infinite dimension (U0)0⊇U(U^0)^0 \supseteq U(U0)0⊇U can be strictly larger, and the dictionary breaks.
