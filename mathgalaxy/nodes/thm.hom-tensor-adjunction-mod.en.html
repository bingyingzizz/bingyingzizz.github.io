Statement

Let RRR be a commutative unital ring and M,N,PM, N, PM,N,P RRR-modules. Then there is a natural isomorphism

Hom⁡R(M⊗RN, P)  ≅  Hom⁡R(M, Hom⁡R(N,P)),f⟼(m↦(n↦f(m⊗n))),\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\bigl(M \otimes_{R} N,\ P\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{R}\bigl(M,\ \operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N, P)\bigr), \qquad f \longmapsto \bigl(m \mapsto (n \mapsto f(m \otimes n))\bigr),HomR​(M⊗R​N, P)≅HomR​(M, HomR​(N,P)),f⟼(m↦(n↦f(m⊗n))),

that is, the left adjoint of the functor (−)⊗RN(-) \otimes_{R} N(−)⊗R​N (valued in Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab) is Hom⁡Z(N,−)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, -)HomZ​(N,−) (with its RRR-action); the RRR-linear version below requires NNN to be an (R,R)(R,R)(R,R)-bimodule:

(−)⊗RN  ⊣  Hom⁡R(N,−).(-) \otimes_{R} N \;\dashv\; \operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N, -).(−)⊗R​N⊣HomR​(N,−).

Notes

Proof (write the maps explicitly): set Φ(f):=(m↦(n↦f(m⊗n)))\Phi(f) := \bigl(m \mapsto (n \mapsto f(m \otimes n))\bigr)Φ(f):=(m↦(n↦f(m⊗n))).

The inverse is Ψ(g):=(m⊗n↦g(m)(n))\Psi(g) := \bigl(m \otimes n \mapsto g(m)(n)\bigr)Ψ(g):=(m⊗n↦g(m)(n)): since (m,n)↦g(m)(n)(m, n) \mapsto g(m)(n)(m,n)↦g(m)(n) is balanced and biadditive, the universal property of 张量积 gives Ψ(g)\Psi(g)Ψ(g) uniquely.

ΦΨ\Phi \PsiΦΨ and ΨΦ\Psi \PhiΨΦ are the identity on pure tensors (or on generators of MMM), and the generators span the whole module, so the two maps are mutually inverse.

Naturality: the formula for Φ\PhiΦ involves no choices, only ⊗\otimes⊗ and composition.

∎

⭐ This is currying: something in two variables is the same as something in one variable taking values in a function space.

Two corollaries are immediate: (−)⊗RN(-) \otimes_{R} N(−)⊗R​N is a left adjoint, hence preserves colimits (right exact, preserves direct sums and filtered colimits); Hom⁡R(N,−)\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N, -)HomR​(N,−) is a right adjoint, hence preserves limits (left exact, preserves products).

📌 Non-commutative version: for MMM a right RRR-module, NNN a left RRR-module and PPP an abelian group, the same isomorphism reads

Hom⁡Z(M⊗RN, P)  ≅  Hom⁡R(M, Hom⁡Z(N,P)),\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M \otimes_{R} N,\ P) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{R}\bigl(M,\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)\bigr),HomZ​(M⊗R​N, P)≅HomR​(M, HomZ​(N,P)),

The point is only that Hom⁡Z(N,P)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)HomZ​(N,P) is naturally a right RRR-module.
