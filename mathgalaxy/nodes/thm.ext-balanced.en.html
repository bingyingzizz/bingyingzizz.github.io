Statement

Let P∙↠MP_{\bullet} \twoheadrightarrow MP∙​↠M be a projective resolution of MMM (use the deleted resolution), and let N→I∙N \to I^{\bullet}N→I∙ be an injective resolution of NNN (where NNN is an arbitrary RRR-module). Then for every n≥0n \ge 0n≥0:

Hn(Hom⁡R(P∙,N))  ≅  Ext⁡Rn(M,N)  ≅  Hn(Hom⁡R(M,I∙)).H^{n}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(P_{\bullet}, N)\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(M, N) \;\cong\; H^{n}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M, I^{\bullet})\bigr).Hn(HomR​(P∙​,N))≅ExtRn​(M,N)≅Hn(HomR​(M,I∙)).

In other words, Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext can be computed using either a projective resolution of the first variable (left-hand side) or an injective resolution of the second variable (right-hand side). Both computations give the same group.

Notes

Proof (identify both computations with the same Hom in the derived category). By the definition of Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext, Ext⁡Rn(M,N)=Hom⁡D(R)(M,N[n])\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(M, N) = \operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(M, N[n])ExtRn​(M,N)=HomD(R)​(M,N[n]), where NNN is concentrated in degree 000.

  • Right-hand side: By the identification of Ext with the cohomology of RHom, Hn(Hom⁡(M,I∙))=RnHom⁡(M,N)=Ext⁡n(M,N)H^{n}(\operatorname{Hom}(M, I^{\bullet})) = R^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(M, N) = \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, N)Hn(Hom(M,I∙))=RnHom(M,N)=Extn(M,N) ✓.
  • Left-hand side: By the dual of the description of the derived category via injective complexes, K−(P)→D−(R-Mod)K^{-}(\mathcal{P}) \to D^{-}(R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod})K−(P)→D−(R-Mod) is an equivalence, so P∙P_{\bullet}P∙​ represents MMM in the derived category, and Hom⁡K(P∙,N[n])=Hom⁡D(P∙,N[n])\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(P_{\bullet}, N[n]) = \operatorname{Hom}_{D}(P_{\bullet}, N[n])HomK​(P∙​,N[n])=HomD​(P∙​,N[n]) ✓.

Moreover, Hn(Hom⁡(P∙,N))=Hom⁡K(P∙,N[n])H^{n}(\operatorname{Hom}(P_{\bullet}, N)) = \operatorname{Hom}_{K}(P_{\bullet}, N[n])Hn(Hom(P∙​,N))=HomK​(P∙​,N[n]) (the standard identification of degree-nnn cohomology with homotopy classes) ✓, so the left-hand side also equals Hom⁡D(M,N[n])\operatorname{Hom}_{D}(M, N[n])HomD​(M,N[n]) ✓. Both computations give the same Hom in the derived category, so the two sides are equal ✓. ∎

⭐ Practical value: Choose whichever side is easier to compute. When MMM has a short (possibly two-term) projective resolution, use the left-hand side. This occurs over PIDs, over regular local rings, and in Koszul examples. When NNN has a short injective resolution, use the right-hand side. Examples include divisible groups such as Q/Z\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}Q/Z. Spectral sequences are needed only when both resolutions are long.

📌 General formulation: Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom is a bifunctor balanced with respect to projectives and injectives, so its derived functors can be computed using a resolution in either variable. The same reasoning applies to ⊗\otimes⊗, which explains why the two computations of Tor agree over a commutative ring.

⚠️ The equality assumes that both resolutions exist (there are enough projectives and enough injectives). If this assumption fails, only the Hom in the derived category is available.
