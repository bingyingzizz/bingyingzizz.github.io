Statement

Let ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ be a series of positive terms (an>0a_n > 0an​>0), and {cn}\{c_n\}{cn​} a sequence of positive numbers. Put

Kn:=cn⋅anan+1−cn+1K_n := c_n \cdot \frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}} - c_{n+1}Kn​:=cn​⋅an+1​an​​−cn+1​

Then:

  • Convergence: if lim inf⁡n→∞Kn>0\liminf_{n \to \infty} K_n > 0liminfn→∞​Kn​>0, then ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ converges;
  • Divergence: if ∑1cn\sum \frac{1}{c_n}∑cn​1​ diverges and Kn≤0K_n \le 0Kn​≤0 for all sufficiently large nnn, then ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ diverges.

Notes

⭐ This is the master theorem for positive-term tests: every "ratio-type" test is obtained from it by choosing a different {cn}\{c_n\}{cn​} —

  • cn=1c_n = 1cn​=1: d'Alembert (ratio) test — lim⁡an+1an<1\lim \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} < 1liman​an+1​​<1 means convergence, >1> 1>1 means divergence;
  • cn=nc_n = ncn​=n: Raabe's test — lim⁡n(anan+1−1)>1\lim n\bigl(\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}} - 1\bigr) > 1limn(an+1​an​​−1)>1 gives convergence;
  • cn=nln⁡nc_n = n \ln ncn​=nlnn: Bertrand's test — one finer step than Raabe;
  • cnc_ncn​ given by a Gauss expansion: Gauss's test — handles the class an/an+1=1+μn+O(n−1−δ)a_n/a_{n+1} = 1 + \frac{\mu}{n} + O(n^{-1-\delta})an​/an+1​=1+nμ​+O(n−1−δ).

⚠ The convergence branch requires lim inf⁡Kn>0\liminf K_n > 0liminfKn​>0 (not lim⁡Kn\lim K_nlimKn​, and not merely some Kn>0K_n > 0Kn​>0).

⚠ The divergence branch has the extra hypothesis "∑1/cn\sum 1/c_n∑1/cn​ diverges" — for cn=1c_n = 1cn​=1 this is ∑1\sum 1∑1, for cn=nc_n = ncn​=n it is the harmonic series; neither is free.

⚠ What does not follow from it: the comparison test (more basic), the root test (which goes through lim sup⁡\limsuplimsup and comparison with a geometric series, and is not mutually derivable with Kummer; rather, "existence of the ratio limit   ⟹  \implies⟹ existence of the root limit, and they are equal", so the root test is stronger), the integral test and the condensation test (one replaces the sum by an improper integral, the other groups terms by 2k2^k2k).
