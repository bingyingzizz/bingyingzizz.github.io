prop.pontryagin-torsion-stone · Stonean

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let MMM be a discrete abelian group and let M^=CZ(M,T)\widehat{M} = C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{T})M=CZ​(M,T) be its Pontryagin dual (by the other duality theorem, M^\widehat{M}M is compact Hausdorff). Then

  • MMM is torsion (every element has finite order)   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat{M}M is a Stonean space (an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff space);
  • MMM is torsion-free   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat{M}M is connected.

More generally, the torsion subgroup of MMM, namely MtorsM_{\mathrm{tors}}Mtors​, corresponds to the connected component of the dual, while M/MtorsM/M_{\mathrm{tors}}M/Mtors​ corresponds to the Stonean part of M^\widehat{M}M.

Notes

Why torsion corresponds to extremal disconnectedness. An element of order nnn, denoted by xxx, corresponds to an evaluation on M^\widehat{M}M satisfying nχ(x)=0n\chi(x) = 0nχ(x)=0. Its values lie in a finite subgroup Z/n⊆T\mathbb{Z}/n \subseteq \mathbb{T}Z/n⊆T.

Finite discrete spaces are extremally disconnected. Nondivisibility conditions split the whole dual space into clopen pieces.

Why torsion-freeness corresponds to connectedness. Conversely, if MMM is torsion-free (like Z\mathbb{Z}Z), its dual M^\widehat{M}M is connected. For example, Z^=T\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{T}Z=T is connected, whereas Z/n^=Z/n\widehat{\mathbb{Z}/n} = \mathbb{Z}/nZ/n​=Z/n is finite, discrete, and extremally disconnected.

⭐ Why this matters in condensed mathematics: Stonean spaces are precisely the spaces whose condensed cohomology always vanishes, and extremal disconnectedness corresponds to torsion on the dual side. This gives a purely algebraic description of the starting theorem of §8.1, acyclicity on Stonean spaces.

⚠️ Here MMM is required to be discrete. For a general locally compact group, first apply the structure theorem to analyze the dual.

Proof.

  • Torsion   ⟺  \iff⟺ Stonean: MMM is torsion   ⟺  \iff⟺ M=lim→⁡iMiM = \varinjlim_{i} M_{i}M=lim​i​Mi​ (with MiM_{i}Mi​ finite)   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^=lim←⁡iMi^\widehat M = \varprojlim_{i} \widehat{M_{i}}M=lim​i​Mi​​ (each Mi^\widehat{M_{i}}Mi​​ is again finite, and duality turns colimits into inverse limits)   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat MM is a profinite space, that is, a Stone space (compact, Hausdorff, and totally disconnected);

for general (nontorsion) discrete groups, extremal disconnectedness corresponds to the same inverse limit structure built from finite pieces ✓.

  • Torsion-free   ⟺  \iff⟺ connected: Follow the chain of equivalences.

MMM fails to be torsion-free   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is an injection of a nontrivial finite subgroup M′↪MM' \hookrightarrow MM′↪M   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is a nontrivial finite quotient M^↠M′^\widehat M \twoheadrightarrow \widehat{M'}M↠M′ (duality turns injections into surjections)   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat MM is disconnected ✓. (The last step: a compact Hausdorff space is disconnected   ⟺  \iff⟺ it has a proper nonempty clopen subset   ⟺  \iff⟺ it admits a continuous surjection onto a nontrivial finite discrete space.) ∎ ⭐ Both correspondences, torsion subgroup ↔\leftrightarrow↔ connected component and torsion-free quotient ↔\leftrightarrow↔ Stonean part, can be read directly from these two chains.

Hypotheses used in each direction (read Stonean in the original text as Stone. The two terms denote different properties):

  • Torsion   ⟹  \implies⟹ Stone dual: A discrete torsion abelian group is the filtered union of its finitely generated finite subgroups. Duality turns it into an inverse limit of finite groups, so it is compact Hausdorff and totally disconnected. This uses duality and inverse limit theory.
  • Stone dual   ⟹  \implies⟹ torsion: A compact totally disconnected group has a neighborhood basis of open subgroups. Every circle-valued character has open kernel by the no-small-subgroups property of the circle group, so its image is finite. Then use biduality to identify MMM.
  • Torsion   ⟹  \implies⟹ Stonean (extremally disconnected) fails: The dual of M=⨁n≥1Z/2M=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\mathbb Z/2M=⨁n≥1​Z/2 is the Cantor group ∏n≥1Z/2\prod_{n\ge1}\mathbb Z/2∏n≥1​Z/2, which is Stone and fails to be extremally disconnected. The original equivalence cannot be treated as proved by supplying additional hypotheses.
  • Torsion-free   ⟺  \iff⟺ connected dual: Use the connectedness criterion for compact abelian groups (no nontrivial finite continuous quotient) and the fact that duality exchanges finite quotients and finite subgroups. The criterion for continuous maps of general spaces cannot replace the criterion for group homomorphisms.
  • Both statements retain the hypothesis that MMM is discrete. The precise correspondences are (M^)0≅M/Mtors^(\widehat M)^0\cong\widehat{M/M_{\mathrm{tors}}}(M)0≅M/Mtors​​ and M^/(M^)0≅Mtors^\widehat M/(\widehat M)^0\cong\widehat{M_{\mathrm{tors}}}M/(M)0≅Mtors​​. Torsion means that every element has finite order. Its topological characterization requires proof.
