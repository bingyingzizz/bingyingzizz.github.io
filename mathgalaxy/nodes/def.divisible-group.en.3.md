## Notes · **draft**
**How to read it**: divisible means "division by any positive integer is always possible". $\mathbb{Q}$ has this property; $\mathbb{Z}$ does not ($2x = 1$ has no solution); $\mathbb{Z}/2$ does not either — $2x = 0$ always has a solution, but $2x = 1$ does not. The point is that the $n$-fold map is **surjective** (onto the whole group), not merely that the equation is solvable for $0$.

**Structure theorem**: every divisible group is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}^{(I)} \oplus \bigoplus_{p}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}\bigr)^{(J_{p})}$, so a divisible group is determined entirely by these "ranks".
