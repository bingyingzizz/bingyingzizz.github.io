## Notes · **draft**
The field $F$ itself is a division algebra; the quaternions are a noncommutative example over $\mathbb{R}$. For an irreducible representation $V$, Schur's lemma shows that $\operatorname{End}_A(V)$ is a division algebra. The division algebra $D$ in Wedderburn's theorem is $\operatorname{End}_A(V)$ up to passing to the opposite algebra. Over an algebraically closed field with $\dim V<\infty$, one has $D=F$.
