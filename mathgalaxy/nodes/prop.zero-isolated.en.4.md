# isolated zeros and their order　`prop.zero-isolated`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.zero-isolated)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.zero-isolated.md)

**Accumulation**: if the zeros had an accumulation point $z^{*} \in \Omega$, then $z^{*}$ would itself be a zero (continuity), and every neighbourhood of it would contain zeros, contradicting isolation — so the only possibility is $f \equiv 0$. This is one half of the identity theorem.

⭐ **Where the order is used in the book**: poles, residues and the argument principle (counting zeros with multiplicity) all need it. ⚠️ Here "order" and the "order" of a pole are two sides of one fact: $f$ has a zero of order $m$ at $z_{0}$ $\iff$ $1/f$ has a pole of order $m$ at $z_{0}$.
