Statement

In plain terms, geodesic curvature is the turning rate of the curve minus the turning rate of the frame due to the metric. The latter is determined solely by the partial derivatives of E,GE, GE,G. Thus the metric, that is, III, suffices to compute the geodesic curvature of any curve, independently of how the surface is embedded in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3.

Let (u,v)(u,v)(u,v) be an orthogonal coordinate net on SSS (F=0F = 0F=0, I=E du2+G dv2I = E\,\mathrm{d}u^{2}+G\,\mathrm{d}v^{2}I=Edu2+Gdv2). Let γ\gammaγ be parametrized by arc length, and let θ(s)\theta(s)θ(s) be the angle between γ˙(s)\dot\gamma(s)γ˙​(s) and the uuu coordinate line (in the direction ∂u\partial_{u}∂u​). Then

kg(γ)=dθds−(ln⁡E)v2Gcos⁡θ+(ln⁡G)u2Esin⁡θ.k_{g}(\gamma) = \frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}s}-\frac{(\ln E)_{v}}{2\sqrt{G}}\cos\theta+\frac{(\ln G)_{u}}{2\sqrt{E}}\sin\theta.kg​(γ)=dsdθ​−2G​(lnE)v​​cosθ+2E​(lnG)u​​sinθ.

In particular, γ\gammaγ is a geodesic if and only if θ\thetaθ satisfies this first-order equation. The change in direction of a geodesic is completely determined by the derivatives of the metric coefficients.
