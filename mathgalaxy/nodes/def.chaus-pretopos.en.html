def.chaus-pretopos · CHaus

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

In CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus:

  1. all limits and colimits exist;
  2. finite coproducts are disjoint and universal;
  3. equivalence relations are effective and universal;
  4. epimorphisms are regular and universal.

Hence CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is a pretopos.

Notes

Item 1 is read off directly from "CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is a reflective subcategory of Top\mathbf{Top}Top":

lim←⁡CHausD  ≅  lim←⁡TopD,lim→⁡CHausD  ≅  β(lim→⁡TopD)\varprojlim{}^{\mathbf{CHaus}} D \;\cong\; \varprojlim{}^{\mathbf{Top}} D, \qquad \varinjlim{}^{\mathbf{CHaus}} D \;\cong\; \beta\Bigl(\varinjlim{}^{\mathbf{Top}} D\Bigr)lim​CHausD≅lim​TopD,lim​CHausD≅β(lim​TopD)

Limits are computed as in Top\mathbf{Top}Top (a reflective subcategory is closed under limits), while colimits must first be computed in Top\mathbf{Top}Top and then compactified back by β\betaβ (a left adjoint preserves colimits).

Proof. The key is "which colimits of Top\mathbf{Top}Top are also colimits of CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus":

  • the four properties all hold in Set\mathbf{Set}Set (a topos); and limits in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus are computed pointwise in Top\mathbf{Top}Top (CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is closed under limits), so the part involving limits is inherited directly from Set\mathbf{Set}Set;
  • a colimit of Top\mathbf{Top}Top that is itself still compact Hausdorff is a colimit in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus — finite coproducts, coequalizers of epimorphisms, and coequalizers of closed equivalence relations all belong to this class;
  • hence: finite coproducts are disjoint (first disjoint in Set\mathbf{Set}Set, then note that the coproduct is itself compact Hausdorff), epimorphisms are universal (pullbacks of epimorphisms are epimorphisms, still in the compact Hausdorff world), closed equivalence relations are effective (the quotient is compact Hausdorff);
  • item 4: a continuous surjection between compact Hausdorff spaces is a quotient map — that is exactly "epimorphism   ⟹  \implies⟹ regular". ∎

What each of the four axioms "leans on" in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus (this example shows best what the pretopos axioms amount to):

  • ① all limits and colimits exist: limits come from limits in Top\mathbf{Top}Top (compact Hausdorff spaces are closed under arbitrary products and equalizers, by Tychonoff); colimits require taking "the compact Hausdorffification after quotienting the equivalence relation", i.e. using a Stone–Čech-type reflection to correct a general quotient to a compact Hausdorff one. ⚠️ Colimits are not computed by taking the quotient directly in Top\mathbf{Top}Top; one must pass through a reflection — this is the only technically substantive step of the item.
  • ② finite coproducts are disjoint and universal: disjointness uses "the disjoint union of compact Hausdorff spaces is again compact Hausdorff"; universality is stability under pullback, using that fibred products of compact Hausdorff spaces are again in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus (an argument of the @@prop.cgwh-fiber-product@@ type).
  • ③ equivalence relations are effective and universal: effective = the kernel pair of the quotient map is exactly the original equivalence relation — in compact Hausdorff spaces this is guaranteed by "quotient maps are closed" (the four items of @@prop.chaus-quotient-equiv@@).
  • ④ epimorphisms are regular and universal: epimorphism = surjection (again from the special nature of compact Hausdorff spaces), so regularity is automatic.

⚠️ CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is not a topos: it lacks a small generator + arbitrary colimits (arbitrary colimits need reflection, and there is no representable small generator/subobject classifier). So it is only a pretopos — this example illustrates exactly the usefulness of the notion "pretopos": it captures the layer where "one can form quotients and equivalence relations".

Hypotheses used in each direction (the pretopos conclusion is guaranteed jointly by compactness and Hausdorffness):

  • The listed finite limits, finite disjoint universal coproducts and effective equivalence relations   ⟹  \implies⟹ pretopos: unfold the respective definitions; arbitrary small limits and colimits are extra properties.
  • Compact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ arbitrary small products are compact Hausdorff: Tychonoff's theorem; existence of colimits uses the reflection into the category of compact Hausdorff spaces and cannot be inferred merely from the right adjoint preserving limits.
  • Epimorphism   ⟹  \implies⟹ surjection: if not surjective the image is closed, and continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space separate points from closed sets; surjection   ⟹  \implies⟹ regular epimorphism uses that a continuous surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a closed quotient map.
  • Internal equivalence relation   ⟹  \implies⟹ effective and universal: the relation is a closed subspace of the product, and the quotient of a closed equivalence relation is again compact Hausdorff; general topological equivalence relations have no such guarantee.
  • Finite disjoint unions are again compact, giving the finite-coproduct property; disjoint unions of infinitely many non-empty clopen branches are usually not compact. The original note's claim that this category lacks arbitrary colimits is not correct; the lack of topos structure cannot be attributed to the non-existence of these colimits.

Items 2 and 3 can also be inherited from Set\mathbf{Set}Set: disjointness, universality, and effectiveness are all "pointwise" properties, and constructions in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus inherit sets pointwise.
