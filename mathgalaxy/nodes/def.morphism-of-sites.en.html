Statement

A morphism of sites is a continuous functor f−1:=g:C→C′f^{-1} := g : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'f−1:=g:C→C′ such that f−1f^{-1}f−1 is left exact (preserves finite limits). If f−1f^{-1}f−1 has a further right adjoint f∗f_{*}f∗​, write f−1⊣f∗f^{-1} \dashv f_{*}f−1⊣f∗​.

Notes

⭐ A pair of adjoints: f−1⊣f∗f^{-1} \dashv f_{*}f−1⊣f∗​ — f−1f^{-1}f−1 is the left adjoint (so preserves colimits), f∗f_{*}f∗​ the right adjoint (so preserves limits). Together with "continuous" (f−1f^{-1}f−1 pulls sheaves back to sheaves), this pair constitutes a morphism between topoi (see "morphism of topoi").

Why left exactness is required: finite limits in a category of sheaves are computed pointwise in presheaves; for f−1f^{-1}f−1 to send sheaves to sheaves and still preserve finite limits, it must preserve finite limits already at the level of sites.

Geometric intuition: continuous maps between spaces give morphisms of sites; a morphism of sites is the "algebraic substitute" for that — morphisms of topoi are then defined on the same pattern, with "site" replaced by "category of sheaves".
