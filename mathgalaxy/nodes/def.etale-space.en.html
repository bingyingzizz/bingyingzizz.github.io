Three things to remember at once:

⭐ Equivalence with sheaves: sheaves on XXX and étale spaces over XXX form an equivalence of categories, the functor being given by

F⟼(∐x∈XFx⟶X)\mathcal{F} \longmapsto \Bigl(\coprod_{x \in X} \mathcal{F}_{x} \longrightarrow X\Bigr)F⟼(x∈X∐​Fx​⟶X)

the right-hand side carries the "topology of stalks" — each pair (U,s)(U, s)(U,s) (UUU open, s∈F(U)s \in \mathcal{F}(U)s∈F(U)) contributes the open set { sx:x∈U }\{\, s_{x} : x \in U \,\}{sx​:x∈U}.

This is the precise meaning of "a sheaf = a space spread locally isomorphically over the base", and it also explains why operations such as "pushforward sheaf / pushforward open set" become "glue the fibres together" on étale spaces.

⚠️ A terminology trap: here we mean a local homeomorphism of topological spaces (French espace étalé).

In algebraic geometry, an étale map is something else (locally of finite presentation + flat + unramified), see "étale sheaf".

The two share a name — Grothendieck did this on purpose, since étale maps of schemes really are the algebraic substitute for "local isomorphism + finite covering" — but they cannot be interchanged: fibres of a local homeomorphism are arbitrary discrete sets, while fibres of an étale morphism of schemes are generally discrete finite schemes.
