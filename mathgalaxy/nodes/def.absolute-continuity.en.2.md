## Notes · **draft**
In one sentence: **wherever $\mu$ sees nothing, $\nu$ must also see nothing**.

⚠ Note that $\mu (E) = 0$ uses $\mu$ (a positive measure), whereas $\nu (E) = 0$ uses the signed measure; an equivalent formulation is $|\nu|(E) = 0$.

Clearly $\mu \ll  |\mu|$ and $|\mu| \ll  \mu$ (for the measure $\mu$ itself).

📌 Example: $\nu(E) := \int_{E} f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ (with $f \ge 0$ integrable) satisfies $\nu \ll \mu$ and $\mathrm{d}\nu/\mathrm{d}\mu = f$; the opposite case is $\delta_{0} \perp \mu$ (all mass concentrated on a null set, see `ex.density-abs-continuous`).
