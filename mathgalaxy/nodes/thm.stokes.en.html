Statement

What it does: raise the fundamental theorem of calculus to arbitrary dimension — the integral over the boundary equals the integral of the exterior derivative inside; a single formula absorbs Green, Gauss–Ostrogradsky and Kelvin–Stokes.

Let MMM be a compact, oriented smooth nnn-manifold with boundary, and ω\omegaω an (n−1)(n-1)(n−1)-form on MMM (continuous up to the boundary suffices). Then

∫Mdω=∫∂Mω,\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega ,∫M​dω=∫∂M​ω,

where ∂M\partial M∂M carries the induced orientation (outward normal first, then the orientation of MMM); ∂M\partial M∂M is allowed to be empty (then the right-hand side is zero).

Notes

Proof route: use a partition of unity to reduce to a cube in the half-space Hn={xn≤0}\mathbb{H}^{n} = \{x^{n}\le 0\}Hn={xn≤0}; write ω\omegaω as ∑i(−1)i−1fi dx1∧⋯dxi^∧⋯∧dxn\sum_{i}(-1)^{i-1}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\widehat{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}∑i​(−1)i−1fi​dx1∧⋯dxi∧⋯∧dxn (a hat means that factor is omitted), so that dω=∑i∂ifi dx1∧⋯∧dxn\mathrm{d}\omega = \sum_{i}\partial_{i}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}dω=∑i​∂i​fi​dx1∧⋯∧dxn.

For i<ni<ni<n the components have compact support in the corresponding coordinate direction, so their integrals vanish;

only the i=ni = ni=n component remains, and one-variable FTC integrates it to fn(x1,…,xn−1,0)f_{n}(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},0)fn​(x1,…,xn−1,0) — which is precisely the integral of ω\omegaω over the boundary.

List of special cases: n=1n = 1n=1 is the fundamental theorem of calculus; n=2n = 2n=2 is Green's formula; the two faces for n=3n = 3n=3 are Gauss–Ostrogradsky (flux) and Kelvin–Stokes (circulation). They are not four theorems but one.

Relation to Gauss–Bonnet: on a surface K dA=dω 21K\,\mathrm{d}A = \mathrm{d}\omega^{1}_{\ 2}KdA=dω 21​ (Gauss's equation, Gauss 方程), so the global Gauss–Bonnet formula can be seen as Stokes plus the boundary term ∫kg ds\int k_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s∫kg​ds — the two theorems share the same root at this level.

⚠️ Boundary orientation is the easiest place to err: the convention is "outward normal first", i.e. the orientation of ∂M\partial M∂M is given by "outward normal ∧\wedge∧ orientation of MMM". A sign error changes the whole formula by a minus sign, while every special case still "looks right".

Why orientability is needed: both sides depend on the orientation and change sign together, so the identity is independent of the choice of orientation; but without an orientation one cannot even write down "the integral" (形式的积分).
