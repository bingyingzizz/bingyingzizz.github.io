Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be a non-empty family of sets with finite character. Then (A,⊆)(\mathcal{A}, \subseteq )(A,⊆) has a maximal element.

That is, there is A∈AA \in \mathcal{A}A∈A such that no B∈AB \in \mathcal{A}B∈A satisfies A⊂BA \subset BA⊂B.

Notes

The advantage of Tukey's lemma is that it saves verification: as soon as the family in question has finite character, the tedious check that "every chain has an upper bound" can be skipped.

Example: the family of linearly independent sets has finite character (XXX is linearly independent   ⟺  \iff⟺ every finite subset of XXX is linearly independent), so "every linearly independent set extends to a maximal one" holds automatically.

History: Teichmüller (1939) and Tukey (1940) stated it independently.
