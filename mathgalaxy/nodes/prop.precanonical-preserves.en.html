Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a pretopos equipped with the precanonical topology. Then

  1. when III is finite, ∐i∈IXi\coprod_{i \in I} X_{i}∐i∈I​Xi​ is still that coproduct in the sheaf category;
  2. if RRR is an equivalence relation on XXX, then the quotient X/RX/RX/R is still that quotient in the sheaf category.

In other words: between C\mathcal{C}C and C\mathcal{C}C — that is, for X↦hXX \mapsto h_{X}X↦hX​ — coproducts and quotients come out the same on both sides.

Notes

Hypotheses used in each direction (preservation of coproducts and quotients comes from the universal property of the sheaf condition):

  • The sheaf condition for finite coproducts F(∐iXi)≅∏iF(Xi)F(\coprod_iX_i)\cong\prod_iF(X_i)F(∐i​Xi​)≅∏i​F(Xi​)   ⟹  \implies⟹ the Yoneda embedding preserves that coproduct: apply Yoneda's lemma to an arbitrary sheaf, and this is the universal property of the coproduct.
  • Conversely, if the coproduct of representables really is h∐iXih_{\coprod_iX_i}h∐i​Xi​​, taking Hom into an arbitrary sheaf recovers the product formula; this step is a free formal conversion in category theory.
  • The sheaf condition for quotients F(X/R)≅Eq⁡(F(X)⇉F(R))F(X/R)\cong\operatorname{Eq}(F(X)\rightrightarrows F(R))F(X/R)≅Eq(F(X)⇉F(R))   ⟺  \iff⟺ hX/Rh_{X/R}hX/R​ is the corresponding coequalizer, again converted by Yoneda and universal properties; effective descent comes from the pretopos and the precanonical topology.
  • The topology hypothesis cannot be dropped: on the category of finite sets with the trivial topology all presheaves are sheaves, yet the constant two-element presheaf satisfies F({1,2})F(\{1,2\})F({1,2}) with only two elements, and cannot equal the four elements of F({1})×F({2})F(\{1\})\times F(\{2\})F({1})×F({2}). The conclusion for finite coproducts also does not automatically cover infinite coproducts.
