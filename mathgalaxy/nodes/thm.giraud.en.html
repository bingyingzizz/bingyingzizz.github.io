thm.giraud · Giraud

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

For a category T\mathcal{T}T, the following four conditions are equivalent:

  1. T\mathcal{T}T is a topos;
  2. every sheaf on T\mathcal{T}T for the standard topology is representable, and a small set of generators exists;
  3. there is a site C\mathcal{C}C such that T≃C^\mathcal{T} \simeq \widehat{\mathcal{C}}T≃C;
  4. there is a category such that T\mathcal{T}T is a reflective subcategory of it, and the reflection is exact (preserves finite limits).

Notes

Translations among the four: (2)  ⟺  \iff⟺(3) take C=T\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{T}C=T with the standard topology; (3)  ⟺  \iff⟺(4) is trivial (sheafification from presheaves to sheaves is an exact reflection); (4)  ⟹  \implies⟹(1) uses "the presheaf category is a topos" together with the reflection to transport the structure down;

(1)  ⟹  \implies⟹(2) also equips T\mathcal{T}T with the standard topology.

Item 4 is the most useful: to build a topos, it suffices to build a presheaf category together with an exact reflection. Condensed sets arise exactly this way — as sheaves on compact Hausdorff spaces.

The four are four different levels of description (the value of this theorem is that it welds together the "axiomatic", "sheaf-theoretic", and "reflective" characterizations):

  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): from axioms to sheaf theory. Use exactness and a small generator in the topos to verify "every sheaf for the standard topology is representable" — this step uses ① (small generator) and ③ (arbitrary colimits), and is the most technical link of the four.
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): from sheaf theory to representability. Take C\mathcal{C}C to be all objects of a small generator and JJJ the standard topology ("RRR covers   ⟺  \iff⟺ ∐Y∈C/RY‾→X‾\coprod_{Y\in\mathcal{C}/R}\underline Y \to \underline X∐Y∈C/R​Y​→X​ is an epimorphism"), then T≃C^\mathcal{T} \simeq \widehat{\mathcal{C}}T≃C. This step is exactly the definitional unfolding of "topos = category of sheaves", and needs the small generator to ensure that C\mathcal{C}C is a small category.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (4): from sheaf categories to reflections. C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}C is a reflective subcategory of SetCop\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}SetCop (sheafification a⊣ι\mathbf{a} \dashv \iotaa⊣ι), and sheafification preserves finite limits (the core property of Grothendieck topologies). Note that "exact reflection" cannot be weakened to "has a reflection" — an arbitrary reflective subcategory is not exact, and exactness is precisely the hallmark of "sheaves".
  • (4)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): from reflections to axioms. Exactness of the reflection gives finite limits (preserved by the reflection), and colimits and effective equivalence relations of SetCop\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}SetCop descend along the reflection, giving ②③④.

⚠️ The four words "exact reflection" are the whole content: with only a reflection (left adjoint) and no exactness, what one gets is a pretopos (some books define a pretopos exactly this way: a pretopos === a reflective subcategory with exact reflection, but without requiring a small generator); adding smallness and colimits upgrades it to a topos.

⚠️ The comparison to keep in view: (1)  ⟺  \iff⟺(3) is the backbone of Giraud's theorem (topos === category of sheaves), and is the most commonly used criterion in practice: to prove that a category is a topos, one usually realizes it directly as a category of sheaves, rather than verifying the four axioms one by one.
