Statement

In Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond, every equivalence relation R⇉XR \rightrightarrows XR⇉X (that is, R⊆X×XR \subseteq X \times XR⊆X×X is a subobject that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive) is effective:

R≅X×YX,Y:=coeq⁡(R⇉X).R \cong X \times_{Y} X,\qquad Y := \operatorname{coeq}\bigl(R \rightrightarrows X\bigr).R≅X×Y​X,Y:=coeq(R⇉X).

That is: the quotient by the equivalence relation exists, and RRR is exactly the kernel pair of that quotient.

Notes

What "effective" means. Given an equivalence relation, first take the quotient Y=X/RY = X/RY=X/R (always possible), then pull back YYY along the quotient map to X×XX \times XX×X. In a general category the pullback can be larger than RRR (it glues extra things). Effective equivalence relations are exactly those that pull back to RRR itself.

Why it matters: only effective equivalence relations allow "quotient and pull back to recover", that is, the operation of "taking quotients" loses no information. Specialized to Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond, it guarantees: gluing two compact Hausdorff pieces along an equivalence relation, the quotient object "is" that equivalence relation in the sheaf-theoretic sense.

⭐ Together these three are exactly the input to Giraud's theorem: a category with all colimits, universal colimits, and effective equivalence relations is a topos. Conversely, that Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is a topos can be checked item by item from these three, without returning to the definition "it is a category of sheaves on a site".

📌 Compare with "Cond is a topos": that one gives the conclusion, these three give the reason; with the reason one sees concretely what is good about the topos structure of Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond (it can take quotients, form internal Hom, and do sheaf cohomology).
