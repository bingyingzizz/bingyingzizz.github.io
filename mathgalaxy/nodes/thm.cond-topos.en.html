Statement

The category of condensed sets Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is a topos.

Notes

What this sentence costs: once Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is a topos, three things come for free — internal Hom (Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is cartesian closed), sheaf cohomology (one can always take cohomology of abelian groups on a topos), and taking quotients (equivalence relations can always be quotiented out). Everything later in condensed mathematics is built on these three tools.

⭐ The reason is not in this item but in the three beside it: Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is a topos because it satisfies Giraud's criterion item by item —

  • limits and colimits are computed pointwise ("limits and colimits in Cond"): this guarantees colimits exist and are universal;
  • every epimorphism is regular ("epimorphisms in Cond are regular"): this guarantees quotient maps are well behaved;
  • every equivalence relation is effective ("equivalence relations in Cond are effective"): this guarantees that quotienting and pulling back recovers the original.

The three together   ⟹  \implies⟹ a topos. So this item is the conclusion and those three are the reasons; reading this cluster, first look at the conclusion for direction, then at the three for why it holds.
