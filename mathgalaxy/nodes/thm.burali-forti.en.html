Statement

The class On\mathrm{On}On of all ordinals is not a set: there is no set whose elements are exactly all the ordinals.

Notes

The proof is one line: if On\mathrm{On}On were a set, it would be transitive (elements of ordinals are ordinals) and ∈\in∈ would well-order it (trichotomy gives comparability), so On\mathrm{On}On would itself be an ordinal, hence On∈On\mathrm{On} \in \mathrm{On}On∈On — contradicting the fact that no ordinal is an element of itself (∈\in∈ is a well-order on ordinals, which forbids ∈\in∈-cycles).

This is exactly the other face of Hartogs' theorem: there are too many ordinals to fit into any set. "There is an ordinal that does not inject into XXX" and "the ordinals do not form a set" say the same thing.

Relation to Russell's paradox: both contradictions come from treating a certain "totality" as a set. In ZFC they are resolved the same way — that totality is not a set, so the Axiom Scheme of Separation cannot even be applied to it.
