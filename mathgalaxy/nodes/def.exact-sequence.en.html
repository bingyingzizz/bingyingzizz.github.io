Statement

Let

⋯⟶Mn−1→ dn−1 Mn→ dn Mn+1⟶⋯\cdots \longrightarrow M^{n-1} \xrightarrow{\ d^{n-1}\ } M^{n} \xrightarrow{\ d^{n}\ } M^{n+1} \longrightarrow \cdots⋯⟶Mn−1 dn−1 ​Mn dn ​Mn+1⟶⋯

be a complex in an abelian category (dn∘dn−1=0d^{n} \circ d^{n-1} = 0dn∘dn−1=0). It is called exact at MnM^{n}Mn if

im⁡(dn−1)=ker⁡(dn).\operatorname{im}(d^{n-1}) = \ker(d^{n}).im(dn−1)=ker(dn).

A complex exact everywhere is an exact sequence. In particular, a short exact sequence

0⟶M′→ f M→ g M′′⟶00 \longrightarrow M' \xrightarrow{\ f\ } M \xrightarrow{\ g\ } M'' \longrightarrow 00⟶M′ f ​M g ​M′′⟶0

means: fff is a monomorphism, ggg is an epimorphism, and im⁡(f)=ker⁡(g)\operatorname{im}(f) = \ker(g)im(f)=ker(g).

Notes

In one sentence: exact = "what comes in is exactly the kernel of what goes out" — no more, no less. im⁡⊆ker⁡\operatorname{im} \subseteq \kerim⊆ker is automatic from the complex condition (d∘d=0d \circ d = 0d∘d=0); exactness requires only the reverse inclusion.

What the 000 at the two ends do: 0→M′0 \to M'0→M′ at the left end says ker⁡(f)=0\ker(f) = 0ker(f)=0, i.e. fff is monic; M′′→0M'' \to 0M′′→0 at the right end says im⁡(g)=M′′\operatorname{im}(g) = M''im(g)=M′′, i.e. ggg is epic. Thus "short exact sequence" packages monic, epic, and exact into one statement.

Another way to write the isomorphism: a short exact sequence is equivalent to "M′M'M′ is a subobject of MMM, and M′′≅M/M′M'' \cong M/M'M′′≅M/M′". This is the shortest way to make "extension" precise.

📌 Compare with "split short exact sequence": a general exact sequence is not guaranteed to be a direct sum; those that can be written as M≅M′⊕M′′M \cong M' \oplus M''M≅M′⊕M′′ are special cases.
