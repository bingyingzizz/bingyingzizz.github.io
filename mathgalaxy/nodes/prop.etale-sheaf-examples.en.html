prop.etale-sheaf-examples

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a scheme.

  1. Open immersions: an open immersion j:U→Xj : U \to Xj:U→X is étale, hence UUU is an object of XetX_{\text{et}}Xet​; every open cover is an étale cover, so every étale sheaf is a Zariski sheaf. The converse fails.
  1. Finite étale coverings: if Y→XY \to XY→X is finite, flat, and unramified (i.e. "finite étale"), then it is a cover for the étale topology, and
GY(U):=Hom⁡X(U, Y)\mathcal{G}_{Y}(U) := \operatorname{Hom}_{X}\bigl(U,\ Y\bigr)GY​(U):=HomX​(U, Y)

is a sheaf on XetX_{\text{et}}Xet​ — this is the correct generalization of "constant sheaf".

  1. Constant sheaves: A‾\underline{A}A​ (regarding AAA as a discrete group and taking locally constant functions) is an étale sheaf, and the most commonly used coefficient in étale cohomology. ⚠️ The constant presheaf (for every UUU the set AAA, with the identity on every map) is in general not a sheaf and must be sheafified.
  1. Concrete examples: Spec⁡C→Spec⁡R\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C} \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{R}SpecC→SpecR is finite étale (of degree 222);

t↦tn:Gm→Gmt \mapsto t^{n} : \mathbb{G}_{m} \to \mathbb{G}_{m}t↦tn:Gm​→Gm​ is finite étale when nnn is invertible on the base (a Kummer covering).

Notes

How to read it: an étale sheaf = a presheaf "on which descent along étale covers can be performed". The common feature of the three examples above is — they are all objects that look locally constant, or are determined by a covering, so descent applies.

The meaning of item 1 (Zariski is coarser than étale): the finer the topology, the fewer sheaves and the stricter the condition. Hence étale sheaves are automatically Zariski sheaves; and étale cohomology computes more than Zariski cohomology precisely because it also recognizes the information in "finite coverings".

Why item 2 is called a "generalization of constant": when Y→XY \to XY→X is finite étale, YYY looks locally (for the étale topology) like nnn copies of XXX, so GY\mathcal{G}_{Y}GY​ is locally isomorphic to a constant sheaf. The analogy is the topological fact "an nnn-sheeted covering E→XE \to XE→X gives a local system".

Intuition for the two examples in item 4: Spec⁡C→Spec⁡R\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C} \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{R}SpecC→SpecR is "222 points covering 111 points", the prototype of a Galois covering;

t↦tnt \mapsto t^{n}t↦tn is an "nnn-fold Kummer covering", turning the operation of taking the nnn-th root into a linear structure on a sheaf — Kummer theory says "μn\mu_{n}μn​-torsors are classified by H1(Xet,μn)H^{1}(X_{\text{et}}, \mu_{n})H1(Xet​,μn​)".

⚠️ Do not read item 3 as a tautology: the fact that the constant presheaf is not a sheaf already happens for the Zariski topology (one must glue over intersections of two open sets), and the gluing condition becomes stronger for the étale topology. Only the sheafified A‾\underline{A}A​ is the sheaf of "locally constant functions with values in AAA".
