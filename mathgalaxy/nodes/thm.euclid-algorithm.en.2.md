# Euclidean algorithm　`thm.euclid-algorithm`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.euclid-algorithm)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.euclid-algorithm.md)

The remainders decrease strictly, so there is an $n$ with $r_{n+1} = 0$; then $r_{n} = \gcd(a, b)$, and this $r_{n}$ can be written as $u a + v b$ with $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}$: back-substitute the remainders step by step.

## Notes · **draft**
This is the only tool in elementary number theory that does not depend on unique factorization: the existence of $\gcd$ and the Bézout coefficients both come from it, while the fundamental theorem of arithmetic in turn uses $\gcd$.
