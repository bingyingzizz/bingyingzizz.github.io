Statement

The exactness levels of a category C\mathcal{C}C (Grothendieck's AB axioms) strengthen one another as follows:

  1. AB1: pre-abelian category;
  2. AB2: abelian category;
  3. AB3: AB2 and all colimits exist (AB3* is the dual);
  4. AB4: AB3 and coproducts are exact (AB4* is the dual);
  5. AB5: AB4 and filtered colimits are exact (AB5* is the dual);
  6. AB6: AB5 and filtered colimits commute with products (AB5* is the dual).

Notes

Reading in one word: the first two supply "do morphisms have kernels and cokernels, are mono and epi regular"; the rest keep adding "more and more colimit operations are exact, and commute with other operations".

⭐ There are also implications between these axioms (see "the relation of AB5 to AB3*/AB4*"): a Grothendieck category automatically satisfies AB3*; AB5 and AB5* are mutually exclusive (apart from the zero category they cannot both hold);

on a topos Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) stops at AB5 + AB3*, while CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb reaches AB6 + AB4*.

⭐ Which categories sit at which level is a separate matter, see "the table of examples for the AB axioms" — that table ranges over Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab, AbTop\mathbf{AbTop}AbTop, AbCHaus\mathbf{AbCHaus}AbCHaus, presheaf categories, abelian groups on a topos, and CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb.

⚠️ This node gives only definitions and names: the AB axioms are a ruler for measuring "how much homological algebra a category can do". What is really useful is the conclusions obtained by applying it to concrete categories (namely the two nodes above).
