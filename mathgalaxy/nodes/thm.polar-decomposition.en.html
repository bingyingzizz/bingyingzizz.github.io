Statement

What it does: a complex number can be written as z=eiθ∣z∣z = e^{i\theta}\lvert z\rvertz=eiθ∣z∣ (direction ×\times× length). Matrices have the same kind of decomposition: unitary (direction) ×\times× positive semidefinite (length).

Let A∈Mn(C)A \in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})A∈Mn​(C). Then there exist a unitary matrix UUU and a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix PPP such that

A=UP,A = UP ,A=UP,

and PPP is uniquely determined by AAA (P=A∗AP = \sqrt{A^{*}A}P=A∗A​); if AAA is invertible, then UUU is also unique, and PPP is positive definite.

The other order: likewise A=P′U′A = P'U'A=P′U′, where P′=AA∗P' = \sqrt{AA^{*}}P′=AA∗​ is positive semidefinite and U′U'U′ is unitary.

Relation to the SVD: diagonalizing PPP (P=VΣV∗P = V\Sigma V^{*}P=VΣV∗) gives A=(UV)ΣV∗A = (UV)\Sigma V^{*}A=(UV)ΣV∗ — this is exactly the singular value decomposition; the diagonal entries of Σ\SigmaΣ are the singular values.

Notes

Why P=A∗AP = \sqrt{A^{*}A}P=A∗A​: just as ∣z∣=zˉz\lvert z\rvert = \sqrt{\bar z z}∣z∣=zˉz​.

A∗AA^{*}AA∗A is always positive semidefinite Hermitian (see the notes to def.positive-definite-matrix), so it has a unique positive semidefinite square root.

Define U:=AP−1U := AP^{-1}U:=AP−1 (when AAA is invertible); it remains only to check that UUU preserves the inner product — a one-line computation: U∗U=P−1A∗AP−1=P−1P2P−1=IU^{*}U = P^{-1}A^{*}AP^{-1} = P^{-1}P^{2}P^{-1} = IU∗U=P−1A∗AP−1=P−1P2P−1=I.

⭐ Geometric reading: every linear map is "first stretch along a set of orthogonal directions (PPP), then rotate as a whole (UUU)". The stretch factors are the singular values. This also holds for non-square matrices (with an isometry in place of a unitary).

Division of labour with the SVD: the polar decomposition emphasizes the structure "rotation ×\times× stretch", while the SVD emphasizes the coordinate expression "A=UΣV∗A = U\Sigma V^{*}A=UΣV∗". Both carry the same information; the SVD gives two extra bases.

⚠️ Uniqueness must be stated carefully: PPP is always unique, but when AAA is singular, UUU may be chosen arbitrarily on the kernel of AAA — so the sentence "the polar decomposition is unique" holds only in the invertible case.

The proof is on the outgoing edge: the full proof is in the corollary edge "spectral theorem ⟹\Longrightarrow⟹ polar decomposition" of this cluster — from the spectral decomposition of A∗AA^{*}AA∗A construct A∗A\sqrt{A^{*}A}A∗A​ and prove uniqueness; when U=AP−1U=AP^{-1}U=AP−1 is invertible it is unitary, and in the singular case one first defines an isometry on im⁡P\operatorname{im}PimP and extends it to a unitary.
