# structure theorem for locally compact abelian groups　`thm.lc-structure`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.lc-structure)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.lc-structure.md)

**Why $V$ must be finite-dimensional**: The additive group of a real vector space is locally compact $\iff$ it is finite-dimensional. Infinite-dimensional real Banach spaces (such as $\ell^{2}$) **fail to be locally compact** and fall outside the scope of the structure theorem.

**What an open subgroup means**: $V \oplus K \subseteq M$ is an open subgroup, so the cokernel $D = M/(V\oplus K)$ carries the quotient topology. Because $V \oplus K$ is open, the quotient is **discrete**. Openness is what makes the quotient discrete.
