## Notes · **draft**
PID $\implies$ Bézout (every ideal is principal, so certainly every finitely generated one is), but not conversely: a Bézout domain need not be Noetherian, and may have ideals that are not finitely generated.

$\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is **not** Bézout: $\gcd(2, x) = 1$, but $2u + xv = 1$ has no solution (set $x = 0$ to get $2u(0) = 1$) — so the "Bézout property" is stronger than being a gcd domain.

In a Bézout domain "irreducible = prime" (because $\gcd$ is given by a linear combination, so the proof of Euclid's lemma carries over directly).
