Statement

Let E⊆P(X)\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)E⊆P(X), ∅∈E\emptyset \in \mathcal{E}∅∈E, X∈EX \in \mathcal{E}X∈E, ρ:E→[0,+∞]\rho : \mathcal{E} \to [0, +\infty ]ρ:E→[0,+∞] satisfy ρ(∅)=0\rho (\emptyset ) = 0ρ(∅)=0.

For A⊆XA \subseteq XA⊆X define

μ∗(A):=inf⁡{∑j=1∞ρ(Ej):Ej∈E,A⊆⋃j=1∞Ej}\mu^*(A) := \inf \{ \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \rho(E_j) : E_j \in \mathcal{E}, A \subseteq \bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} E_j \}μ∗(A):=inf{j=1∑∞​ρ(Ej​):Ej​∈E,A⊆j=1⋃∞​Ej​}

(covering AAA by countably many sets from E\mathcal{E}E and taking the infimum.) Then μ∗\mu^{*}μ∗ is an outer measure on XXX.

Notes

This formula is just "cover AAA with bricks of known area and take the cheapest covering" — the Lebesgue outer measure is exactly the special case (E\mathcal{E}E open intervals, ρ\rhoρ length).

Why require X∈EX \in \mathcal{E}X∈E: to guarantee that A⊆XA \subseteq XA⊆X always has a cover, so that the infimum is not taken over the empty set.

⚠ This step uses only ρ(∅)=0\rho (\emptyset ) = 0ρ(∅)=0, not additivity — so in general μ∗\mu^{*}μ∗ does not recover ρ\rhoρ. Recovering it is precisely Carathéodory's job.
