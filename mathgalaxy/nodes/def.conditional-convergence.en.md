## Statement · **draft**
A real (or complex) series $\sum a_n$ is called **conditionally convergent** if and only if it converges but $\sum |a_n|$ **diverges**.

The typical example is the alternating harmonic series:

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n} = \ln 2$$

It converges, while after taking absolute values it becomes the divergent harmonic series.
