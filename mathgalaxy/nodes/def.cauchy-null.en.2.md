## Abstract · **reviewed**
Over $\mathbb{Q}$ alone — $\varepsilon$ ranges over positive rationals, so no reals are presupposed — a rational sequence $(x_n)$ is Cauchy when $\forall \varepsilon > 0\, \exists N\, \forall m,n \ge N : |x_m - x_n| < \varepsilon$, is null when $\forall \varepsilon > 0\, \exists N\, \forall n \ge N : |x_n| < \varepsilon$, and two Cauchy sequences are equivalent when their difference is null. The quotient by this relation is how $\mathbb{R}$ is built: it glues together the many ways of approximating one number.
