## English abstract · reviewed by bingyingzizz (2026-10-16) · Chinese is authoritative（续）
For $F$ of normalised bounded variation, $F' \in L^1(m)$ and $\mu_F \perp m \iff F' = 0$ a.e., while $\mu_F \ll m \iff F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} F'(t)\,dt$. Both follow by writing the Lebesgue decomposition $d\mu_F = d\lambda + f\,dm$ and using $f = F'$ a.e.: vanishing of the absolutely continuous part is the first case, $\lambda = 0$ is the second. This is the measure-theoretic form of the fundamental theorem of calculus — $F'$ integrates back to $F$ exactly when $\mu_F$ is absolutely continuous.
