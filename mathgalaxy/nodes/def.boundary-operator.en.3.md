# Boundary Operator　`def.boundary-operator`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.boundary-operator)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.boundary-operator.md)

By convention, when $k = 0$ set $\partial c := 0$ (a point is zero-dimensional and has no faces). Thus $\partial : C_{k}(M)\to C_{k-1}(M)$ is a group homomorphism.

## Notes · **draft**
**Where the sign $(-1)^{i+\alpha}$ comes from**: it is not forced.

Take the end whose outward normal is $+\partial_{i}$ (that is, $\alpha = 1$) and the sign $(-1)^{i+1}$; the other end has normal $-\partial_{i}$ and sign $(-1)^{i}$; the difference between the two ends is exactly the sign of the permutation "moving the outward normal to the front crosses $i-1$ coordinates".
