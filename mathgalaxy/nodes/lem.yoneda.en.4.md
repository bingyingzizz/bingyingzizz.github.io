# Yoneda lemma　`lem.yoneda`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=lem.yoneda)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./lem.yoneda.md)

This bijection is natural in **both** variables: natural in $F$ (natural transformations) and in $X$ (morphisms). Naturality on both sides is what gives the lemma its force.

It compresses the set "all natural transformations from $h^{X}$ to $F$", which is in principle intractable, into **a single element** of $F(X)$. Whenever "natural transformations are hard to count", this is the step that saves the day.

Dual form: if $T$ is a presheaf ($T : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}$) and $h_{X} = \operatorname{Hom}(-, X)$ is a representable presheaf, then likewise $\operatorname{Hom}(h_{X}, T) \cong T(X)$.
