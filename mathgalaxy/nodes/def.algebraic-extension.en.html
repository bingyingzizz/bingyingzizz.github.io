Statement

A field extension K/FK/FK/F is algebraic if every element of KKK is algebraic over FFF; otherwise it is transcendental.

A finite extension is always algebraic: if [K:F]=n<∞[K:F] = n < \infty[K:F]=n<∞, take 0≠α∈K0 \ne \alpha \in K0=α∈K; then 1,α,…,αn1, \alpha, \ldots, \alpha^{n}1,α,…,αn — that is, n+1n+1n+1 vectors — are linearly dependent in the nnn-dimensional space, which gives a nonzero polynomial with root α\alphaα.

Notes

⭐ The converse fails: Q‾/Q\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}Q​/Q (the algebraic closure of Q\mathbb{Q}Q) is algebraic but of infinite degree. So “algebraic” and “finite” are two different things — a distinction that matters in Galois theory (the fundamental theorem holds only for finite Galois extensions).

⭐ Algebraic extensions are closed under towers: F⊆K⊆LF \subseteq K \subseteq LF⊆K⊆L, if K/FK/FK/F and L/KL/KL/K are both algebraic, then L/FL/FL/F is algebraic. (The proof passes through “finitely generated”; it is not expanded here.)

⚠ The standard transcendental example: Q(x)/Q\mathbb{Q}(x)/\mathbb{Q}Q(x)/Q (the field of rational functions) — one cannot even speak of its degree.
