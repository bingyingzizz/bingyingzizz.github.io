## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **What it means: normality = "there are enough continuous functions".** Saying only that "closed sets can be separated by open sets" is still purely topological; Urysohn's lemma turns it into **a single function** — from then on one can measure topology with real numbers, and analysis can connect.

**How the proof goes (the "bisection" construction of $f$).** First build a family of open sets between the two closed sets: for every dyadic rational $q \in [0,1]$ assign an open set $U_{q}$ such that

$$A \subseteq U_{q} \subseteq \overline{U_{q}} \subseteq U_{q'} \quad (q < q'),$$
