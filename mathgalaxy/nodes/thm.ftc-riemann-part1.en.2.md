# Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part I　`thm.ftc-riemann-part1`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.ftc-riemann-part1)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.ftc-riemann-part1.md)

**(b)** if $f$ is continuous at $x_0 \in (a, b)$, then $F$ is differentiable at $x_0$, and

$$F'(x_0) = f(x_0)$$

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This theorem says: **integrate, then differentiate, and you return to the original function** (at the points where $f$ is continuous).

Thus “finding an antiderivative” acquires a **concrete construction**, rather than relying on reciting the derivative table backwards.

⚠ (b) only requires $f$ to be continuous at **that point**: where $f$ has a jump, $F$ has a corner ($F$ is not differentiable there), but $F$ is still continuous everywhere.
