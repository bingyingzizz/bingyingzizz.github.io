Statement

Let T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W be linear. The rank of TTT is the dimension of its image:

rank⁡T:=dim⁡im⁡T\operatorname{rank} T := \dim \operatorname{im} TrankT:=dimimT

The rank of a matrix A∈Mm×n(K)A \in M_{m \times n}(K)A∈Mm×n​(K) is the rank of the linear map Kn→KmK^{n} \to K^{m}Kn→Km (x↦Axx \mapsto Axx↦Ax) it defines.

Notes

⭐ Rank measures how much information, in dimensions, TTT retains.

It is the only numerical invariant of linear algebra: two matrices are equivalent under a change of bases (not necessarily the same basis) just when their ranks agree.

⚠ Does rank depend on the choice of basis? No: no basis appears in the definition (dim⁡im⁡T\dim \operatorname{im} TdimimT is basis-independent, because dimension is well defined).

That is exactly why rank is useful.
