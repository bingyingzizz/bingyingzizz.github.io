# natural transformation　`def.natural-transformation`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.natural-transformation)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.natural-transformation.md)

When every $\alpha_X$ is an isomorphism, $\alpha$ is called a **natural isomorphism**, written $F \cong G$.

## Notes · **draft**
In one sentence: the two components of $\alpha$ control the two ends, and commutativity says that "changing functor first and then applying $f$" agrees with "applying $f$ first and then changing functor".

With natural transformations, **functors themselves become objects**: taking functors $\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'$ as objects and natural transformations as morphisms gives a category denoted $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}')$;
