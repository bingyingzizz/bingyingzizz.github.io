Statement

Let KKK be compact Hausdorff and A⊆C(K,K)A \subseteq C(K, \mathbb{K})A⊆C(K,K).

  • AAA is a subalgebra: it is a vector space and is closed under multiplication;
  • AAA separates points: x≠y  ⟹  ∃f∈Ax \ne y \implies \exists f \in Ax=y⟹∃f∈A, f(x)≠f(y)f(x) \ne f(y)f(x)=f(y);
  • AAA is a lattice: closed under min⁡,max⁡\min, \maxmin,max.

Notes

"Separates points" is a necessary condition for Stone–Weierstrass: if two points are not separated, glue them into one, and AAA cannot generate the functions on the glued space, CCC.

⚠️ The example of self-adjoint matrices (from the lectures): self-adjoint matrices ordered by A≥B  ⟺  A−BA \ge B \iff A - BA≥B⟺A−B being positive semidefinite do not in general form a lattice (the pointwise min of two self-adjoint matrices need not be self-adjoint). So "lattice" is not automatic; it must be bought by "closed + contains the unit".
