# intersection of subobjects　`def.intersection-subobject`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.intersection-subobject)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.intersection-subobject.md)

**Why the fibred product**: the intersection must express "lies in $Y$ and $Z$ simultaneously". In terms of elements this is $t \in Y \cap Z$; without elements it is $T \to X$ factoring simultaneously through $Y \rightarrowtail X$ and $Z \rightarrowtail X$. The universal property of the fibred product is exactly the universal solution of this "simultaneous factorization"; hence any object with that property factors uniquely through $Y \times_{X} Z$, i.e. it is the greatest lower bound.
