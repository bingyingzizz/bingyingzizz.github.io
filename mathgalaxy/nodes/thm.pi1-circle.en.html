Notes

Geometric translation: the fundamental group of S1S^{1}S1 carries only one piece of information, "how many times it winds", and that is a complete invariant — two closed paths are homotopic iff they have the same winding number. Nothing else can hide in S1S^{1}S1.

⭐ Interface with complex analysis: the winding number is written in complex analysis as n(γ,0)=12πi∮γdzzn(\gamma, 0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}n(γ,0)=2πi1​∮γ​zdz​ (绕数的积分表示). The two are the same integer: lifting a closed curve to polar coordinates gives the lift. This also explains the topological source of the theorem that the winding number is invariant under homotopies of the curve (绕数的同伦不变性).

⚠️ Structure of the proof (important): 覆叠的指数 gives only that π1(S1)\pi_{1}(S^{1})π1​(S1) and Z\mathbb{Z}Z are in bijection, because π1(R)=1\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}) = 1π1​(R)=1 and the fibre is Z\mathbb{Z}Z. To obtain an isomorphism one must additionally prove that deg⁡\degdeg is a homomorphism — this uses "the lift of a concatenation is the translate of the lift" (uniqueness of lifts). Both halves are needed; see the proof on the edge.

💡 This is the first non-trivial computation in all of algebraic topology. Its value: π1(S1)≠1\pi_{1}(S^{1}) \ne 1π1​(S1)=1 immediately gives "S1S^{1}S1 is not contractible", "the disc cannot retract onto its boundary", and the Brouwer fixed point theorem — one computation, a chain of geometric theorems.
