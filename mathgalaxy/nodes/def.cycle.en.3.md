## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **The word "closed" comes from topology**: in a simplicial complex a chain with $\partial z = 0$ is a "chain without boundary" — intuitively a bunch of simplices closed up (a circle, a sphere). In the de Rham complex a form with $d\omega = 0$ is called **closed**, with the very same definition. So "cycle / closed form / cocycle" are three ways of saying one thing.

⚠️ **Origin of the letter $Z$**: German *Zyklus* (cycle). $B$ comes from *boundary* (edge) — see @@def.boundary@@. Older literature also writes $C^{n}$ (cocycle) and $B^{n}$; check the notation when reading.
