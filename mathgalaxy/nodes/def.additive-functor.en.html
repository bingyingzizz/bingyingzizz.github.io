Statement

Let C,D\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}C,D be preadditive categories. A functor F:C→DF : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}F:C→D is called an additive functor if for every pair M,N∈CM, N \in \mathcal{C}M,N∈C, the map

Hom⁡C(M,N)⟶Hom⁡D(F(M),F(N)),f⟼F(f)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(M, N) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(M), F(N)), \qquad f \longmapsto F(f)HomC​(M,N)⟶HomD​(F(M),F(N)),f⟼F(f)

is an abelian group homomorphism (that is, preserves addition and 000).

Notes

Equivalent description: an additive functor automatically preserves finite direct sums (F(M⊕N)≅F(M)⊕F(N)F(M \oplus N) \cong F(M) \oplus F(N)F(M⊕N)≅F(M)⊕F(N)), and F(0)=0F(0) = 0F(0)=0. Conversely, a functor preserving finite direct sums (between preadditive categories) is additive.

⭐ Why homological algebra speaks only of additive functors: derived functors, Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext, HnH^{n}Hn are all additive. Every operation on complexes (d2=0d^{2}=0d2=0, taking homology, mapping cones) is addition and subtraction at each step; a non-additive functor cannot even send complexes to complexes.

Examples: Hom⁡(X,−)\operatorname{Hom}(X, -)Hom(X,−) is additive (Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom is itself an abelian group); M⊗A−M \otimes_{A} -M⊗A​− is additive; the forgetful functor A-Mod→AbA\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \to \mathbf{Ab}A-Mod→Ab is additive.

⚠️ "Additive" and "left exact/right exact" are two different things: additive only preserves the group structure, exactness preserves kernels and cokernels. Additivity is a prerequisite; exactness is a further condition.
