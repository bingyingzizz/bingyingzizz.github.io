## Notes · **draft**
**Cauchy in measure $Cauchy \iff$ convergence in measure**: both directions can be proved (convergence $\implies Cauchy$ by the triangle inequality; $Cauchy \implies$ convergence is the next theorem).

This is one virtue of the “in measure” topology: it is **complete**. By contrast, a.e. convergence is neither strong nor complete, while $L^{1}$ convergence is complete (Riesz–Fischer).
