Statement

In plain words: put a vector field into the first slot of a form and the degree drops by one — that is the interior product. It is the half of Cartan's formula that pairs with the exterior derivative.

It is called the interior product, but it is not the same thing as ⟨⋅,⋅⟩\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle⟨⋅,⋅⟩ in an inner product space: the former eats "one vector +++ and one form" and returns a form of one lower degree, the latter eats "two vectors" and returns a number.

Notes

Why it is "pointwise" while the Lie derivative is "global": the interior product is computed from XpX_{p}Xp​ alone, yielding (ιXω)p(\iota_{X}\omega)_{p}(ιX​ω)p​, so it is a tensor operation (linear in XXX over C∞C^{\infty}C∞) and requires solving no equation;

the Lie derivative differentiates along the flow, is defined only on vector fields, and is linear in XXX only over R\mathbb{R}R.

The two are exactly complementary in Cartan's formula: dιX\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}dιX​ is "contract first, then differentiate", ιXd\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}ιX​d is "differentiate first, then contract".

Why ιX2=0\iota_{X}^{2} = 0ιX2​=0: contracting twice puts two vectors into the same slot, and forms are alternating, so exchanging the two contractions produces a sign −-−; hence (ιX2ω)(v3,…)=ω(X,X,v3,…)=0(\iota_{X}^{2}\omega)(v_{3},\ldots) = \omega(X,X,v_{3},\ldots) = 0(ιX2​ω)(v3​,…)=ω(X,X,v3​,…)=0 (alternation plus the fact that a vector paired with itself gives zero).

In physics notation it is "contracting one index": the electromagnetic field F=12Fij dxi∧dxjF = \frac{1}{2}F_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}F=21​Fij​dxi∧dxj and ιEF\iota_{E}FιE​F give quantities such as "the magnetic flux in the direction of the electric field";

Relation to contraction: ιXω\iota_{X}\omegaιX​ω is exactly putting XXX into the first slot and contracting — a special case of tensor contraction on alternating tensors, except that the inserted vector need not be a basis vector.

Interaction with the wedge product: the combination dιX+ιXd\mathrm{d}\iota_{X} + \iota_{X}\mathrm{d}dιX​+ιX​d is natural because d\mathrm{d}d is an antiderivation of degree +1+1+1 and ιX\iota_{X}ιX​ is an antiderivation of degree −1-1−1, and the composite of two antiderivations is a "derivation of degree 000" — the same type as the Lie derivative.
