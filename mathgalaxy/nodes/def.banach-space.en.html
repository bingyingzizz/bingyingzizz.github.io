Statement

A normed space VVV is complete iff it is complete with respect to the metric d(v,w)=∥v−w∥d(v, w) = \|v - w\|d(v,w)=∥v−w∥, i.e. every Cauchy sequence converges. A complete normed space is a Banach space.

Notes

⭐ Three standard examples: Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn (completeness is the finite-dimensional version of ℝ 是完备度量空间), LpL^{p}Lp (L^p 是 Banach 空间, Riesz–Fischer), and C(S)C(S)C(S) on a compact Hausdorff space with the sup norm.

⚠ Completeness is not automatic: C[0,1]C[0, 1]C[0,1] with ∥f∥1=∫01∣f∣\|f\|_{1} = \int_0^1 |f|∥f∥1​=∫01​∣f∣ is not complete (one can approximate a discontinuous function).

The same set of functions is complete under one norm and incomplete under another — "complete" is a property of the norm, not of the set.

⭐ Completeness is the engine of the contraction mapping principle below (压缩映射原理): without it one only gets a Cauchy sequence, not a fixed point.

The whole constructive proof of the inverse function theorem rests on this.
