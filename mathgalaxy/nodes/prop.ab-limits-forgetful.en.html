Statement

If C\mathcal{C}C has all limits, then Ab(C)\mathrm{Ab}(\mathcal{C})Ab(C) also has all limits, and the forgetful functor Ab(C)→C\mathrm{Ab}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathcal{C}Ab(C)→C preserves them.

Notes

⭐ Practical value: this makes "computing limits in C\mathcal{C}C" and "computing limits in Ab(C)\mathrm{Ab}(\mathcal{C})Ab(C)" completely consistent — the condensed side Ab(Cond)=CondAb\mathrm{Ab}(\mathrm{Cond}) = \mathrm{CondAb}Ab(Cond)=CondAb having all limits is one application of this with C=Cond\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Cond}C=Cond.
