Statement

Let fff be bounded on [a,b][a, b][a,b]. We say fff is Riemann integrable if and only if there exists a real number III such that

∀ε>0, ∃δ>0: ∥P∥<δ  ⟹  ∣S(f,P,ξ)−I∣<ε\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ \exists \delta > 0 :\ \|P\| < \delta \implies \bigl| S(f, P, \xi) - I \bigr| < \varepsilon∀ε>0, ∃δ>0: ∥P∥<δ⟹​S(f,P,ξ)−I​<ε

holds for every partition PPP and every choice of tags ξ\xiξ. Then III is unique and is written

I=∫abf=∫abf(x) dxI = \int_a^b f = \int_a^b f(x)\,dxI=∫ab​f=∫ab​f(x)dx

with the conventions ∫baf:=−∫abf\int_b^a f := -\int_a^b f∫ba​f:=−∫ab​f and ∫aaf:=0\int_a^a f := 0∫aa​f:=0.

Notes

⭐ The two words most easily missed in the definition: every and norm.

It is not “there is a sequence of partitions whose sums converge”, but “all partitions of sufficiently small norm, with all tags, land near III”.

⭐ Equivalent formulation (Darboux form, see “Darboux criterion”): sup⁡PL(f,P)=inf⁡PU(f,P)\sup_P L(f, P) = \inf_P U(f, P)supP​L(f,P)=infP​U(f,P).

The equivalence of the two definitions is not free — it needs a proof, and that proof is 可积的振幅判据.

⚠ For functions taking values in C\mathbb{C}C, integrability is defined by integrability of the real and imaginary parts;

the vector-valued case is analogous.

⚠ Boundedness is a prerequisite for integrability: an unbounded function is not Riemann integrable (it may be Lebesgue integrable, provided ∫∣f∣<∞\int |f| < \infty∫∣f∣<∞).
