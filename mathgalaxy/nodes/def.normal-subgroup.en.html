Statement

Let H≤GH \le GH≤G and g∈Gg \in Gg∈G. The left coset and right coset are

gH:={ gh:h∈H },Hg:={ hg:h∈H }.gH := \{\, gh : h \in H \,\}, \qquad Hg := \{\, hg : h \in H \,\}.gH:={gh:h∈H},Hg:={hg:h∈H}.

We call HHH a normal subgroup of GGG (written H⊴GH \trianglelefteq GH⊴G) if gH=HggH = HggH=Hg for all g∈Gg \in Gg∈G, equivalently gHg−1=HgHg^{-1} = HgHg−1=H.

Notes

⭐ Why normality. A coset gHgHgH is a set, and to turn the quotient set into a group via “gH⋅g′H:=gg′HgH \cdot g'H := gg'HgH⋅g′H:=gg′H” the definition must be independent of the choice of representatives. Compute where the difference lies:

(gh)(g′h′)=gg′⋅((g′)−1hg′)h′,(g h)(g' h') = g g' \cdot \bigl((g')^{-1} h g'\bigr) h',(gh)(g′h′)=gg′⋅((g′)−1hg′)h′,

The element (g′)−1hg′(g')^{-1} h g'(g′)−1hg′ in the rightmost bracket must still lie in HHH — “HHH is closed under conjugation” is exactly normality. Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal (conjugation does nothing), so this step never causes trouble in an abelian group.

Cosets are an equivalence relation. Define a∼b  ⟺  a−1b∈Ha \sim b \iff a^{-1} b \in Ha∼b⟺a−1b∈H; then the equivalence classes are precisely the left cosets, and all left cosets partition GGG — this is the quotient set used for the quotient group below.

Notation: H≤GH \le GH≤G denotes a subgroup, H⊴GH \trianglelefteq GH⊴G a normal subgroup (⊴\trianglelefteq⊴ is read “is normal in”).
