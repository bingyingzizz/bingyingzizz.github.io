def.eigenspace

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

What it does: collects all eigenvectors belonging to the same eigenvalue into a subspace. With it, "diagonalizable" acquires a clean criterion (the sum of the dimensions of the eigenspaces equals the whole space).

Let T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V be linear and let λ\lambdaλ be an eigenvalue of TTT (def.eigenvalue). The eigenspace of λ\lambdaλ is

Vλ:=ker⁡(T−λI)={ v∈V:T(v)=λv }.V_{\lambda} := \ker(T - \lambda I) = \{\, v \in V : T(v) = \lambda v \,\} .Vλ​:=ker(T−λI)={v∈V:T(v)=λv}.

It is a TTT-invariant subspace; its dimension dim⁡Vλ\dim V_{\lambda}dimVλ​ is called the geometric multiplicity of λ\lambdaλ, while the multiplicity of λ\lambdaλ in the characteristic polynomial is called the algebraic multiplicity.

One always has: 1≤1 \le1≤ geometric multiplicity ≤\le≤ algebraic multiplicity.

Diagonalizability criterion: TTT is diagonalizable   ⟺  \iff⟺ ∑λdim⁡Vλ=dim⁡V\sum_{\lambda} \dim V_{\lambda} = \dim V∑λ​dimVλ​=dimV   ⟺  \iff⟺ the two multiplicities of every eigenvalue are equal.

Notes

Why it is a "subspace": ker⁡(T−λI)\ker(T - \lambda I)ker(T−λI) is a kernel, hence certainly a subspace. The point is that it is not an arbitrary subspace — it is determined by a single eigenvalue, and the sum of the VλV_{\lambda}Vλ​ for distinct eigenvalues is direct (eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent).

Why the two multiplicities differ: (1101)\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}(10​11​) has the single eigenvalue 111, with algebraic multiplicity 222, but ker⁡(T−I)\ker(T - I)ker(T−I) is only one-dimensional (geometric multiplicity 111) — the missing dimension is the source of Jordan blocks (thm.jordan-canonical-form).

⭐ The generalized eigenspace is its generalization: iterate ker⁡(T−λI)k\ker(T - \lambda I)^{k}ker(T−λI)k until it stabilizes, and the dimension is exactly the algebraic multiplicity. The blocks of the Jordan form are organized accordingly (see thm.primary-decomposition).

⚠️ Notational conventions differ: some books write EλE_{\lambda}Eλ​ (eigenspace), others V(λ)V(\lambda)V(λ). This graph uses VλV_{\lambda}Vλ​, aligning with the notation of def.eigenvalue.
