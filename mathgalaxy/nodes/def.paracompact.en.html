Statement

A topological space XXX is paracompact if every open cover has a locally finite open refinement: for every open cover U\mathcal{U}U there exists an open cover V\mathcal{V}V such that

V refines UandV is locally finite.\mathcal{V}\ \text{refines}\ \mathcal{U}\quad\text{and}\quad \mathcal{V}\ \text{is locally finite}.V refines UandV is locally finite.

Notes

⚠ Convention: Hausdorffness is not built into the definition of "paracompact" here (some books include it). Whenever separation is needed, "paracompact Hausdorff" is written out explicitly.

Where it sits in the hierarchy: compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ paracompact (a finite subcover is certainly locally finite); paracompact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ regular   ⟹  \implies⟹ Hausdorff (theorems of this pack); metric spaces   ⟹  \implies⟹ paracompact (Stone's theorem).

⭐ What it buys: partitions of unity (hence smoothing, integration and Stokes on manifolds, and extension theorems), and the local finiteness that sheaf theory needs so that "Čech cohomology equals sheaf cohomology" holds in detail. Manifolds are second countable by definition, hence automatically paracompact — which is why analysis never worries about it.

Heredity: open and closed subspaces both inherit paracompactness (open subspace YYY: one cannot simply add X∖YX\setminus YX∖Y to the cover — it need not be open; the correct route is first to take YYY open, extend each member openly inside XXX, lift the cover to an open cover of XXX, then take a locally finite open refinement and intersect it with YYY; closed subspace: intersect the members of the open cover with the closed subspace). A general subspace need not be paracompact.

⚠ The converse fails completely: a normal space need not be paracompact — [0,ω1)[0, \omega_{1})[0,ω1​) (the set of all ordinals below the first uncountable ordinal) is normal and countably compact but not compact, and "countably compact +++ paracompact   ⟹  \implies⟹ compact", so it is not paracompact.
