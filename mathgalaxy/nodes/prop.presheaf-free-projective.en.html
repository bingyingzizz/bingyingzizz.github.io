Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a category and X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C. Then Z⋅hX\mathbb{Z}\cdot h_{X}Z⋅hX​ is a projective object in the presheaf category C^(Ab)\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab})C(Ab); more generally, Z⋅T\mathbb{Z}\cdot TZ⋅T (with TTT any presheaf) is also projective.

Notes

Proof. Projectivity is equivalent to Hom⁡Z(Z⋅T,−)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}\cdot T, -)HomZ​(Z⋅T,−) being exact ✓.

By the universal property of free objects, Hom⁡Z(Z⋅T,M)≅M(T)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}\cdot T, M) \cong M(T)HomZ​(Z⋅T,M)≅M(T) — here M(T)M(T)M(T) means the family of pointwise evaluations "evaluate a presheaf at TTT" ✓;

and exactness in a presheaf category is judged pointwise, and pointwise it is just Hom between abelian groups, hence exact ✓.

∎

⭐ This is what "free implies projective" looks like in sheaf theory, and it is also the basis for Z⋅X∙\mathbb{Z}\cdot X_{\bullet}Z⋅X∙​ serving as a projective resolution.
