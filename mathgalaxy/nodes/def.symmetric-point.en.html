Notes

Why define it this way: inside the circle ∣z−a∣<R|z - a| < R∣z−a∣<R, the symmetric point lands outside — this is an inversion. It makes the "centre" and the "point at infinity" correspond (z=az = az=a gives z∗=∞z^{*} = \inftyz∗=∞), which is exactly why 分式线性变换保对称点 can preserve symmetry under fractional linear transformations.

⚠ Symmetric points are not "points at equal distance from the centre": those are antipodal points. Symmetry is inversion, with a fixed product of distances.
