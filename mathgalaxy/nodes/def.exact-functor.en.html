def.exact-functor

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A functor FFF is left exact if it preserves all finite limits; right exact if it preserves all finite colimits; and exact if both.

Notes

Why "finite": in general categories the usage of "left exact" comes from abelian categories — there it suffices to preserve 000, finite products, and kernels, which is equivalent to sending the short exact sequence 0→M′→M→M′′→00 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 00→M′→M→M′′→0 to 0→FM′→FM→FM′′0 \to FM' \to FM \to FM''0→FM′→FM→FM′′ (exact, exact on the left). So "exact" in the name refers to exact sequences.

⭐ Right adjoints are left exact and left adjoints are right exact — a direct consequence of "adjoint   ⟹  \implies⟹ preserves limits / colimits", and the most commonly used criterion.

Better in abelian categories: FFF is left exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ it preserves kernels (F(ker⁡)=ker⁡F(\ker) = \kerF(ker)=ker); FFF is exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ it sends short exact sequences to short exact sequences.

⚠️ The name is ambiguous: some books use "exact" for preserving finite limits and others for all limits. This graph follows the definition above (finite limits/colimits).
