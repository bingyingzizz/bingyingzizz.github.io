Statement

Let RRR be a ring and FFF a right RRR-module. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. FFF is flat;
  2. For every left RRR-module NNN, Tor⁡1R(F,N)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(F, N) = 0Tor1R​(F,N)=0;
  3. For every left RRR-module NNN and every n≥1n \ge 1n≥1, Tor⁡nR(F,N)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R}(F, N) = 0TornR​(F,N)=0.

Thus the flat modules are precisely those for which all higher Tor groups vanish. This is exactly parallel to the characterization of projective modules by the vanishing of all higher Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext groups.

Notes

Proof. (1)  ⟹  (3)(1) \implies (3)(1)⟹(3): For FFF take a projective resolution P∙→FP_{\bullet} \to FP∙​→F.

When FFF is flat, Tor⁡n\operatorname{Tor}_{n}Torn​ can also be computed using a resolution of NNN. Take a projective resolution of NNN, denoted by Q∙Q_{\bullet}Q∙​. The resolution on the NNN side gives Tor⁡n(F,N)=Hn(F⊗Q∙)\operatorname{Tor}_{n}(F, N) = H_{n}(F\otimes Q_{\bullet})Torn​(F,N)=Hn​(F⊗Q∙​).

Since F⊗−F\otimes -F⊗− is exact and Q∙→NQ_{\bullet} \to NQ∙​→N is exact, F⊗Q∙F\otimes Q_{\bullet}F⊗Q∙​ has homology only in degree 000, and its higher homology vanishes ✓. (3)  ⟹  (2)(3) \implies (2)(3)⟹(2): Take n=1n = 1n=1 ✓. (2)  ⟹  (1)(2) \implies (1)(2)⟹(1): Take any injection f:M1↣Mf : M_{1} \rightarrowtail Mf:M1​↣M. We must show that F⊗M1→F⊗MF\otimes M_{1} \to F\otimes MF⊗M1​→F⊗M is injective. Include it in the short exact sequence 0→M1→M→M/M1→00 \to M_{1} \to M \to M/M_{1} \to 00→M1​→M→M/M1​→0, tensor with FFF, and take the long exact sequence

Tor⁡1R(F, M/M1)→F⊗M1→F⊗M→F⊗M/M1→0,\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(F,\ M/M_{1}) \to F\otimes M_{1} \to F\otimes M \to F\otimes M/M_{1} \to 0 ,Tor1R​(F, M/M1​)→F⊗M1​→F⊗M→F⊗M/M1​→0,

The leftmost term is zero by condition 2, so F⊗M1→F⊗MF\otimes M_{1} \to F\otimes MF⊗M1​→F⊗M is injective ✓, which gives exactness of F⊗−F\otimes -F⊗− ✓. ∎

Of the three conditions, (2)  ⟹  \implies⟹(3) is the only step that requires induction. The equivalence (1)  ⟺  \iff⟺(2) uses a long exact sequence:

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (2): A long exact sequence is needed (flatness means that (−)⊗F(-)\otimes F(−)⊗F is exact. Its equivalence to the universal vanishing of Tor⁡1\operatorname{Tor}_{1}Tor1​ follows from the long exact sequence for Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor).
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): This step uses dimension shifting. If Tor⁡1(F,N)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(F, N) = 0Tor1​(F,N)=0 for all NNN, take the short exact sequence for NNN and use the long exact sequence to obtain Tor⁡n=0\operatorname{Tor}_{n} = 0Torn​=0 by induction. ⚠️ This requires the long exact sequence (the Tor version of long exact sequence).
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): A special case (n=1n = 1n=1).

⚠️ Comparison with projectivity: Projective   ⟺  \iff⟺ Ext⁡n(M,−)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M,-) = 0Extn(M,−)=0 for all n≥1n \ge 1n≥1. Flat   ⟺  \iff⟺ Tor⁡n(−,N)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{n}(-,N) = 0Torn​(−,N)=0 for all n≥1n \ge 1n≥1. The criteria are parallel in form, but flatness is weaker than projectivity (projective   ⟹  \implies⟹ flat, and the converse fails. For example, Q\mathbb{Q}Q is flat over Z\mathbb{Z}Z but is not projective). ⚠️ Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor can be computed in either variable. Here FFF is on the left and NNN is on the right. Reversing the order gives isomorphic results (Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor is balanced), but over a noncommutative ring one must also pass to the opposite ring: Tor⁡nR(M,N)≅Tor⁡nRop(N,M)\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R}(M,N) \cong \operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(N,M)TornR​(M,N)≅TornRop​(N,M) (for example, when R=M2(k)R = M_{2}(k)R=M2​(k), the original structures put NNN and MMM on different sides).

Assumptions used in each direction (flatness is defined by exactness of tensoring, and the Tor criterion uses derived functors):

  • Flatness   ⟺  \iff⟺ exactness of F⊗R−F\otimes_R-F⊗R​− is the definition. The implication (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3) also uses a projective resolution of the left module NNN to compute Tor and uses the fact that exact functors preserve exactness of resolutions.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): Take n=1n=1n=1, a direct specialization. (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): The Tor long exact sequence of a short exact sequence shows that tensoring preserves injections. Combine this with the right exactness of tensoring.
  • These implications hold over any unital ring, with no commutativity, Noetherian, or PID hypothesis. The pairing of FFF as a right module with NNN as a left module is essential. Over a noncommutative ring the two inputs cannot be interchanged freely.
  • Vanishing is required for every NNN. Testing only N=RN=RN=R gives vanishing for any FFF, including F=Z/2F=\mathbb Z/2F=Z/2, which is not flat. Flatness cannot be replaced by projectivity either. For example, Q\mathbb QQ is a flat, nonprojective Z\mathbb ZZ-module.

⭐ This gives a condition for flatness that can be checked one case at a time. Instead of examining all injections, compute, for each NNN, the group Tor⁡1\operatorname{Tor}_{1}Tor1​. In practice this is often used with Lambek's criterion, which requires testing only finitely generated ideals.

📌 Corollaries: Direct summands of flat modules are flat (Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor commutes with direct sums), and direct sums of flat modules are flat. Projective modules are flat (vanishing in higher degrees on the Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext side is the dual manifestation of the same phenomenon).

⚠️ Here Tor⁡nR(F,−)\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R}(F, -)TornR​(F,−) treats FFF as a right module. Over a noncommutative ring, right flatness of FFF and left flatness of FFF (that is, Tor⁡nR(−,F)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R}(-, F) = 0TornR​(−,F)=0) are two distinct conditions. This result concerns right flatness. Over a commutative ring the two conditions coincide ✓.
