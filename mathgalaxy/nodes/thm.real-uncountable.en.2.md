## Abstract · **reviewed**
$\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable — there is no surjection $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ — and in fact $|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0$. Uncountability is proved by nested closed intervals and completeness alone, with no cardinal theory; the cardinality statement pairs binary expansions with $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ and quotes Cantor. The counterintuitive point: $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}$ are each dense in the other, yet $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable — density and cardinality are different things, which is why analysis needs $\mathbb{R}$.
