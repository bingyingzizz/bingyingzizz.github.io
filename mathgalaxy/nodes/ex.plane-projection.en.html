Statement

In R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 (with the standard inner product) take the subspace W={ (x,y,0) }W = \{\, (x, y, 0) \,\}W={(x,y,0)}. The orthogonal projection onto WWW is

P=(100010000).P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.P=​100​010​000​​.

Notes

Checking the three properties one by one: P2=PP^{2} = PP2=P (idempotent); P∗=PP^{\ast} = PP∗=P (symmetric); ker⁡P=span⁡(e3)\ker P = \operatorname{span}(e_{3})kerP=span(e3​), im⁡P=W\operatorname{im} P = WimP=W, and R3=ker⁡P⊕im⁡P\mathbb{R}^{3} = \ker P \oplus \operatorname{im} PR3=kerP⊕imP is an orthogonal direct sum. Conversely: idempotent + symmetric   ⟹  \implies⟹ orthogonal projection (idempotent alone gives an oblique projection, whose image and kernel are not perpendicular).

⭐ It is the geometric core of least squares: Pb=(b1,b2,0)P b = (b_{1}, b_{2}, 0)Pb=(b1​,b2​,0) is exactly the point nearest bbb among the points of WWW, and the residual b−Pb=(0,0,b3)b - Pb = (0, 0, b_{3})b−Pb=(0,0,b3​) is orthogonal to WWW — precisely the geometry behind the normal equations. In general, when W=col⁡(A)W = \operatorname{col}(A)W=col(A) the projection matrix is A(ATA)−1ATA(A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}A(ATA)−1AT.

📌 Relation to the pseudoinverse: A=PA = PA=P is itself idempotent and symmetric, so A+=AA^{+} = AA+=A (the four Penrose conditions hold directly). This says that a projection is, in the language of pseudoinverses, the self-adjoint idempotent kind.

⚠ Do not read the projection matrix as “deleting a coordinate”: in a different non-orthonormal basis the matrix is no longer diagonal — projection is a geometric notion; diag⁡(1,1,0)\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)diag(1,1,0) is only its appearance in the standard basis.
