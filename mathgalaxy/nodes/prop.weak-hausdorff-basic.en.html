Statement

Let XXX be weak Hausdorff. Then

  • every compact Hausdorff subspace of XXX is closed;
  • XXX is T1T_{1}T1​ (a point is a compact Hausdorff subspace);
  • for every continuous f:S→Xf : S \to Xf:S→X (SSS compact Hausdorff), f(S)f(S)f(S) is compact Hausdorff;
  • the diagonal ΔX⊆X×X\Delta_{X} \subseteq X \times XΔX​⊆X×X is kkk-closed; conversely, if XXX is compactly generated, then diagonal kkk-closed   ⟹  \implies⟹ XXX weak Hausdorff;
  • if moreover YYY is weak Hausdorff and f,g:X⇉Yf, g : X \rightrightarrows Yf,g:X⇉Y continuous, then ker⁡(f,g)={ x:f(x)=g(x) }⊆X\ker(f, g) = \{\, x : f(x) = g(x) \,\} \subseteq Xker(f,g)={x:f(x)=g(x)}⊆X is kkk-closed.

Notes

Why "weak" Hausdorff. Hausdorff asks that open neighbourhoods separate any two points; weak Hausdorff only asks that "images of compact pieces are closed" — a much weaker condition, yet exactly enough: the constructions appearing in CGWH\mathrm{CGWH}CGWH (quotients, function spaces, fibred products) do not need full Hausdorffness.

⚠️ Weak Hausdorff does not imply Hausdorff (there are compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces that are not Hausdorff); but it implies T1T_{1}T1​, which is the second item above.

The role of the diagonal item: ΔX\Delta_{X}ΔX​ kkk-closed   ⟺  \iff⟺ "the set of points where two maps agree" is kkk-closed — parallel to the characterization of T1T_{1}T1​, T2T_{2}T2​ in general topology (closed diagonal), only the closedness criterion is now kkk-closed. Compact generation is the extra condition that makes the equivalence reversible.
