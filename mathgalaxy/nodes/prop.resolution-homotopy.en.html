Statement

Let P∙↠MP_{\bullet} \twoheadrightarrow MP∙​↠M and Q∙↠MQ_{\bullet} \twoheadrightarrow MQ∙​↠M be two projective resolutions of the same RRR-module MMM. Then there are chain maps

P∙→ φ Q∙,Q∙→ ψ P∙P_{\bullet} \xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ } Q_{\bullet}, \qquad Q_{\bullet} \xrightarrow{\ \psi\ } P_{\bullet}P∙​ φ ​Q∙​,Q∙​ ψ ​P∙​

such that ψφ∼1P∙\psi\varphi \sim 1_{P_{\bullet}}ψφ∼1P∙​​, φψ∼1Q∙\varphi\psi \sim 1_{Q_{\bullet}}φψ∼1Q∙​​. In other words: any two projective resolutions are isomorphic in the homotopy category.

In particular, for every RRR-module NNN and every n≥0n \ge 0n≥0, the groups obtained by applying Hom⁡(−,N)\operatorname{Hom}(- , N)Hom(−,N) and taking homology are all isomorphic — hence Ext⁡Rn(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(M, N)ExtRn​(M,N) is independent of the choice of resolution.

∎

⭐ This is the legitimate basis for "resolutions may be chosen freely". In computations one usually picks the shortest and most explicit one (free modules, two terms, Koszul complex); changing it does not affect the result — but only isomorphism of the result is guaranteed, not that the families appearing in the middle agree.

Example (homotopy equivalent but not isomorphic): take the direct sum of P∙P_{\bullet}P∙​ with a contractible complex of the form

⋯→0→P0→ 1 P0→0→⋯\cdots \to 0 \to P_{0} \xrightarrow{\ 1\ } P_{0} \to 0 \to \cdots⋯→0→P0​ 1 ​P0​→0→⋯

This gives another projective resolution of MMM, visibly not isomorphic to P∙P_{\bullet}P∙​ (every term has doubled), yet homotopy equivalent ✓.

⚠️ Dual case: injective resolutions satisfy the same conclusion (apply the comparison theorem on the other side). Together with "lifts between injective resolutions", this is the entire basis for "compute derived functors from resolutions on either side".
