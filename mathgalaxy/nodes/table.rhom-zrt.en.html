Statement

The table gives RHom⁡Z(M,N)\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N)RHomZ​(M,N) (rows correspond to MMM and columns to NNN):

ZRTZZRTR0RRTZ[−1]0Z\begin{array}{c|ccc} & \mathbb{Z} & \mathbb{R} & T \\ \hline \mathbb{Z} & \mathbb{Z} & \mathbb{R} & T \\ \mathbb{R} & 0 & \mathbb{R} & \mathbb{R} \\ T & \mathbb{Z}[-1] & 0 & \mathbb{Z} \end{array}ZRT​ZZ0Z[−1]​RRR0​TTRZ​​

Computing the entries: Two facts suffice for the calculation

  1. Z\mathbb{Z}Z is free (projective): A resolution of Z\mathbb{Z}Z can be taken to be the one-dimensional [Z][\mathbb{Z}][Z]. Thus RHom⁡Z(Z,N)=Hom⁡Z(Z,N)=N\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = NRHomZ​(Z,N)=HomZ​(Z,N)=N, with no higher terms.

This gives the first row directly.

  1. R\mathbb{R}R is divisible and hence Z\mathbb{Z}Z-injective: Ext⁡Zn(M,R)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{R}) = 0ExtZn​(M,R)=0 for all MMM and n≥1n \ge 1n≥1.

Thus the entries with target R\mathbb{R}R have only degree 000: RHom⁡(M,R)=Hom⁡Z(M,R)\operatorname{RHom}(M, \mathbb{R}) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{R})RHom(M,R)=HomZ​(M,R).

Replace the TTT column by Z\mathbb{Z}Z and shift up one degree: Take the short exact sequence 0→Z→R→T→00 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to T \to 00→Z→R→T→0 (T=R/ZT = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}T=R/Z).

For fixed MMM, apply RHom⁡(M,−)\operatorname{RHom}(M, -)RHom(M,−) to obtain the long exact sequence

⋯→Ext⁡n(M,R)→Ext⁡n(M,T)→Ext⁡n+1(M,Z)→Ext⁡n+1(M,R)→⋯\cdots \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, \mathbb{R}) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, T) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, \mathbb{Z}) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, \mathbb{R}) \to \cdots⋯→Extn(M,R)→Extn(M,T)→Extn+1(M,Z)→Extn+1(M,R)→⋯

Since the surrounding R\mathbb{R}R terms vanish for n≥1n \ge 1n≥1, exactness determines the middle term:

Ext⁡n(M,T)  ≅  Ext⁡n+1(M,Z)(n≥1),\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, T) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, \mathbb{Z}) \qquad (n \ge 1),Extn(M,T)≅Extn+1(M,Z)(n≥1),

and 0→Hom⁡(M,Z)→Hom⁡(M,R)→Hom⁡(M,T)→Ext⁡1(M,Z)→00 \to \operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z}) \to \operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{R}) \to \operatorname{Hom}(M,T) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}) \to 00→Hom(M,Z)→Hom(M,R)→Hom(M,T)→Ext1(M,Z)→0.

This explains why the TTT and Z\mathbb{Z}Z columns differ by exactly one degree (for example, the third row, first column is Z[−1]\mathbb{Z}[-1]Z[−1]: when M=TM = TM=T, we have Ext⁡0(T,Z)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{0}(T,\mathbb{Z}) = 0Ext0(T,Z)=0 and Ext⁡1(T,Z)≅Ext⁡0(T,T)=Z\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(T,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{0}(T,T) = \mathbb{Z}Ext1(T,Z)≅Ext0(T,T)=Z).

Why ‘real vector space →\to→ discrete’ vanishes in every degree: Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn is divisible, so maps from a divisible group into any group have images in which division by nnn is possible. Thus Hom⁡Z(Rn,Z)=0\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0HomZ​(Rn,Z)=0 (elements of the image of 1/n1/n1/n must be divisible by nnn, leaving only 000).

Together with divisibility Ext⁡1(Rn,Z)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0Ext1(Rn,Z)=0, this gives zero for the entire RHom⁡\operatorname{RHom}RHom.

Notes

How to read the table (T=R/ZT = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}T=R/Z):

  • First row (source Z\mathbb{Z}Z): RHom⁡(Z,N)=N\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = NRHom(Z,N)=N. A free source has no higher terms, leaving only degree zero.
  • Second row, first column 000: Real vector space →\to→ discrete vanishes in every degree by divisibility.
  • Remaining entries of the second row: RHom between finite-dimensional real Banach spaces is concentrated in degree zero, with value Hom⁡R(M,N)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M, N)HomR​(M,N). Here the spaces are Hom⁡R(R,R)=R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{R}HomR​(R,R)=R and Hom⁡R(R,T)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}, T)HomR​(R,T).
  • Third row, second column 000: Compact Hausdorff →\to→ real Banach vanishes in every degree.
  • Third row, first column Z[−1]\mathbb{Z}[-1]Z[−1]: By 0→Z→R→T→00 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to T \to 00→Z→R→T→0, the middle term is 000, leaving only Z\mathbb{Z}Z shifted down by one degree.
  • Bottom right Z\mathbb{Z}Z: This follows similarly from 0→Z→R→T→00 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to T \to 00→Z→R→T→0 and RHom⁡(T,R)=0\operatorname{RHom}(T, \mathbb{R}) = 0RHom(T,R)=0.
