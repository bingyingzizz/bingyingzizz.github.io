prop.ind-representable-iff-left-exact

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a small category with finite colimits. The following three statements about the presheaf T∈C^T \in \widehat{\mathcal{C}}T∈C are equivalent.

  1. TTT is ind-representable (it can be expressed as a filtered colimit of representable presheaves).
  2. The slice category C/T\mathcal{C}/TC/T is filtered.
  3. TTT is left exact (it preserves finite limits).

Notes

Assumptions used in each direction (the variance of the presheaf and the existence of finite colimits must be specified)

  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1) follows from Yoneda density, which gives T=lim→⁡hX→ThXT=\varinjlim_{h_X\to T}h_XT=lim​hX​→T​hX​, together with filteredness, which makes this an ind-representation. The converse requires the fact that in a filtered colimit, finitely many data can be represented at a common stage and finitely many equalities can be witnessed at a common stage.
  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3) holds because the representable terms in the presentation of T:Cop→SetT:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}T:Cop→Set preserve limits in the source category, and filtered colimits commute with finite limits in sets. Here the finite limits are those of Cop\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}Cop, equivalently the finite colimits of C\mathcal CC.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2) generally requires C\mathcal CC to have finite colimits. An initial object ensures that the category of elements is nonempty, binary coproducts give common upper bounds, and coequalizers equalize parallel arrows. Smallness or suitable universe conventions are also needed to ensure a presentation as a small filtered colimit.
  • Preserving existing finite limits alone does not allow the existence assumption to be dropped. Take C\mathcal CC to be the discrete category on two objects and let the presheaf take a singleton value at both objects. It preserves the finite limits that exist in Cop\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}Cop, but its category of elements consists of two disconnected objects and fails to be filtered.
