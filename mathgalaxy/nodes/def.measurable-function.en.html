Statement

Let (X,M)(X, \mathcal{M})(X,M) and (Y,N)(Y, \mathcal{N})(Y,N) be measurable spaces. A function f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is **(M,N)(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{N})(M,N)-measurable** if and only if the preimage of every measurable set is measurable:

f−1(E)∈M∀E∈Nf^{-1}(E) \in \mathcal{M}\quad \forall E \in \mathcal{N}f−1(E)∈M∀E∈N

Notes

Why **preimages** rather than images: preimages preserve intersections, unions and complements (f−1(⋃Ei)=⋃f−1(Ei)f^{-1}(\bigcup E_i) = \bigcup f^{-1}(E_i)f−1(⋃Ei​)=⋃f−1(Ei​), etc.), whereas images lack these good properties. Measurability is therefore a condition pulled back.

Equivalent formulation (when YYY is a topological space and N=BY\mathcal{N} = \mathfrak{B}_YN=BY​): fff is measurable   ⟺  \iff⟺ the preimage of every open set is measurable.

⚠ "Measurable on EEE" is a relative notion: fff is measurable on EEE   ⟺  \iff⟺ f∣Ef|_Ef∣E​ is measurable with respect to the subspace σ\sigmaσ-algebra ME\mathcal{M}_EME​.

Four characterizations, the cost being "which family of sets is tested":

  • preimages of open sets** are measurable   ⟺  \iff⟺ preimages of Borel sets** are measurable: (⇐)(\Leftarrow)(⇐) is clear; (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) uses the fact that preimages preserve σ\sigmaσ-algebra operations (Borel sets are generated by open sets).
  •   ⟺  \iff⟺ all level sets {x:f(x)>a}\{x : f(x) > a\}{x:f(x)>a} are measurable** (a∈Ra \in \mathbb{R}a∈R): this is the most-used criterion**, since one only has to test a generating family instead of all open sets. ⚠️ Using {f>a}\{f > a\}{f>a} or {f≥a}\{f \ge a\}{f≥a} both work, **and mixing them is harmless** ({f>a}=⋃q∈Q,q>aHq\{f > a\} = \bigcup_{q \in \mathbb{Q}, q > a} H_q{f>a}=⋃q∈Q,q>a​Hq​, and each HqH_qHq​ may be taken in either form).
  •   ⟺  \iff⟺ **both f+,f−f^{+}, f^{-}f+,f− are measurable**: split a real-valued function into positive and negative parts, then handle the non-negative case with {f>a}\{f > a\}{f>a}.

⚠️ The necessary hypothesis is "the target carries the Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra": measurability on R\mathbb{R}R is always relative to some σ\sigmaσ-algebra; for R‾\overline{\mathbb{R}}R (including ±∞\pm\infty±∞) no extra convention is needed**: {f=+∞}=⋂n{f>n}\{f = +\infty\} = \bigcap_n \{f > n\}{f=+∞}=⋂n​{f>n} and {f=−∞}=⋂n{f≤−n}\{f = -\infty\} = \bigcap_n \{f \le -n\}{f=−∞}=⋂n​{f≤−n} are automatically measurable from the level sets. ⚠️ A composition of measurable functions need not be measurable**: fff measurable and ggg Borel measurable   ⟹  \implies⟹ g∘fg \circ fg∘f measurable; but if ggg is merely Lebesgue measurable this can fail — this is why "Borel measurable" and "Lebesgue measurable" must be distinguished.
