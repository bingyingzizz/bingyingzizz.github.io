Statement

Fix a site C\mathcal{C}C. Write

C^(Ab):=Hom⁡(Cop,Ab)\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab}) := \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Ab})C(Ab):=Hom(Cop,Ab)

for the category of presheaves of abelian groups, and C~(Ab)\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab})C(Ab) for the full subcategory of sheaves in it. The latter is called the abelian sheaves on C\mathcal{C}C, also the abelian groups on C\mathcal{C}C.

Notation: Hom⁡Z(M,N):=Hom⁡C^(Ab)(M,N)=Hom⁡C~(Ab)(M,N)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N) := \operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab})}(M, N) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab})}(M, N)HomZ​(M,N):=HomC(Ab)​(M,N)=HomC(Ab)​(M,N).

Notes

⭐ A presheaf of abelian groups is exactly a sheaf for the coarsest topology — the two descriptions are one and the same thing.

The forgetful functor Ab→Set\mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Set}Ab→Set induces, by composition, a forgetful functor C^(Ab)→C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab}) \to \widehat{\mathcal{C}}C(Ab)→C.

Two basic facts:

  • Abelian sheaves are detected by their underlying sheaves of sets: MMM is an abelian sheaf   ⟺  \iff⟺ its underlying presheaf of sets is a sheaf.

Because the forgetful functor preserves all limits and the sheaf condition is a limit diagram — using Yoneda's lemma to replace Hom⁡Z(N,M(−))\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, M(-))HomZ​(N,M(−)) by M(−)M(-)M(−), the condition becomes the same one.

  • Abelian sheaves = abelian group objects in the sheaf category: C~(Ab)≃Ab(C~)\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{Ab}) \simeq \mathbf{Ab}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\bigr)C(Ab)≃Ab(C).

This is the general version of the definition "condensed abelian groups = abelian groups in the category of condensed sets".
