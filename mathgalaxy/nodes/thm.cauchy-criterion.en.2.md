## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The two directions have completely different content: "convergent $\implies$ Cauchy" uses only the triangle inequality and is **trivial**; "Cauchy $\implies$ convergent" is **completeness itself** — it says that a sequence whose own terms are eventually close to each other must have a limit waiting for it.

⭐ Practical value: to prove convergence one need not guess the limit first.

The convergence radius of power series, the completeness of function spaces, and the definition of a Banach space (every Cauchy sequence converges) are all direct descendants of this result.
