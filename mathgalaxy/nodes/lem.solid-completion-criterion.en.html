Statement

In words: this lemma is a universal wrench — to show that some subcategory works as well as modules (compact objects, a left adjoint, a fully faithful derived category), one need not check each item; a single RHom⁡\operatorname{RHom}RHom condition suffices.

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category with arbitrary colimits and A0⊂A\mathcal{A}_0 \subset \mathcal{A}A0​⊂A a family of compact projective generators (= a generating family in which every object is both compact and projective; this is the abstract version of "finite free modules" in module categories).

for every X∈A0X \in \mathcal{A}_0X∈A0​, every map built from direct sums of images of FFF, say f:Y→Zf : Y \to Zf:Y→Z, with kernel KKK,

(i) AF⊂A\mathcal{A}_F \subset \mathcal{A}AF​⊂A is an abelian subcategory stable under all limits, colimits and extensions; the F(X)F(X)F(X) (X∈A0X \in \mathcal{A}_0X∈A0​) form a family of compact projective generators of AF\mathcal{A}_FAF​; the inclusion has a left adjoint LLL, which is the unique colimit-preserving extension of FFF.

Notes

⭐ This is a purely category-theoretic lemma: it compresses "prove that the completed subcategory behaves well" into one Ext-vanishing condition.

⚠ The condition requires an isomorphism only for kernels, not for cokernels — but dually, writing it for cokernels works the same (see Remark 5.11 of the source).
