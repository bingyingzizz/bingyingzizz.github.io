Statement

In plain terms: the solid subcategory Solid\mathbf{Solid}Solid is a place "as convenient as a category of modules" — it has enough compact projective objects (∏IZ\prod_I \mathbb{Z}∏I​Z), so one can form tensor products, Ext and derived categories without pathologies. This is the engine of the whole theory.

Let Solid⊂Cond⁡(Ab)\mathbf{Solid} \subset \operatorname{Cond}(\mathbf{Ab})Solid⊂Cond(Ab) be the full subcategory of solid abelian groups. Then:

(i) Solid\mathbf{Solid}Solid is an abelian subcategory, stable under all limits, colimits and extensions; the objects ∏IZ\prod_I \mathbb{Z}∏I​Z (III arbitrary) form a family of compact projective generators; the inclusion has a left adjoint

M⟼M■:Cond⁡(Ab)⟶Solid,M \longmapsto M^{\blacksquare} : \operatorname{Cond}(\mathbf{Ab}) \longrightarrow \mathbf{Solid},M⟼M■:Cond(Ab)⟶Solid,

which is the unique colimit-preserving extension of Z[S]↦Z[S]■\mathbb{Z}[S] \mapsto Z[S]^{\blacksquare}Z[S]↦Z[S]■ (solidification).
