Statement

An abelian group DDD is divisible if for every d∈Dd \in Dd∈D and every 0≠n∈Z0 \ne n \in \mathbb{Z}0=n∈Z the equation

nx=dn x = dnx=d

has a solution in DDD; equivalently, the multiplication map n⋅:D→Dn \cdot : D \to Dn⋅:D→D is surjective for every n≠0n \ne 0n=0.

A Z\mathbb{Z}Z-module is just an abelian group (Z-模就是阿贝尔群), so "divisible group" = "divisible Z\mathbb{Z}Z-module"; the definition carries over verbatim to modules over any ring (replace Z\mathbb{Z}Z by RRR and nnn by a non-zero-divisor).

Notes

How to read it: divisible means "division by any positive integer is always possible". Q\mathbb{Q}Q has this property; Z\mathbb{Z}Z does not (2x=12x = 12x=1 has no solution); Z/2\mathbb{Z}/2Z/2 does not either — 2x=02x = 02x=0 always has a solution, but 2x=12x = 12x=1 does not. The point is that the nnn-fold map is surjective (onto the whole group), not merely that the equation is solvable for 000.

Structure theorem: every divisible group is isomorphic to Q(I)⊕⨁p(Z[1/p]/Z)(Jp)\mathbb{Q}^{(I)} \oplus \bigoplus_{p}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}\bigr)^{(J_{p})}Q(I)⊕⨁p​(Z[1/p]/Z)(Jp​), so a divisible group is determined entirely by these "ranks".

⭐ Over Z\mathbb{Z}Z: "divisible" and "injective" are the same thing (内射等价于可除). Intuitively "divide by nnn" is exactly "extend along nZ↣Zn\mathbb{Z} \rightarrowtail \mathbb{Z}nZ↣Z".

📌 Quotients: a quotient of a divisible group is divisible (可除群的商); subgroups need not be (Z⊆Q\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}Z⊆Q).
