Statement

Let MMM be a GGG-module. For g∈Gg \in Gg∈G, write g−1:M→Mg - 1 : M \to Mg−1:M→M for the RRR-linear map m↦g⋅m−mm \mapsto g \cdot m - mm↦g⋅m−m. The invariant submodule of MMM is

M↦MGM \mapsto M^{G}M↦MG, f↦f∣MGf \mapsto f|_{M^{G}}f↦f∣MG​ constitute the invariant functor

(−)G:R[G]-Mod⟶R-Mod.(-)^{G} : R[G]\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \longrightarrow R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} .(−)G:R[G]-Mod⟶R-Mod.

It is naturally isomorphic to Hom⁡R[G](R,−)\operatorname{Hom}_{R[G]}(R, -)HomR[G]​(R,−) (RRR with the trivial GGG-action), hence is left exact, but in general not right exact.

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: (−)G(-)^{G}(−)G is "taking fixed points". It compresses the information of the group action into RRR-modules, and is the starting point of group cohomology (H0=(−)GH^{0} = (-)^{G}H0=(−)G).

Why it is Hom⁡R[G](R,−)\operatorname{Hom}_{R[G]}(R, -)HomR[G]​(R,−): an R[G]R[G]R[G]-linear map f:R→Mf : R \to Mf:R→M is determined by f(1)f(1)f(1), and f(1)=f(g⋅1)=g⋅f(1)f(1) = f(g \cdot 1) = g \cdot f(1)f(1)=f(g⋅1)=g⋅f(1), so f(1)∈MGf(1) \in M^{G}f(1)∈MG;

conversely, given any m∈MGm \in M^{G}m∈MG one can define fff in this way.

Hence (−)G=Hom⁡R[G](R,−)(-)^{G} = \operatorname{Hom}_{R[G]}(R, -)(−)G=HomR[G]​(R,−) — it is left exact in its second variable.

Left exact but not right exact: taking invariants of a short exact sequence 0→L→M→N→00 \to L \to M \to N \to 00→L→M→N→0 gives only

0⟶LG⟶MG⟶NG,0 \longrightarrow L^{G} \longrightarrow M^{G} \longrightarrow N^{G},0⟶LG⟶MG⟶NG,

and the right-hand side does not connect.
