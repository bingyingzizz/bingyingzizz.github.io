# open mapping theorem　`thm.open-mapping`

[in graph](../#n=thm.open-mapping)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.open-mapping.md)

Now take any $|w| < \delta$ and compare $f$ with the constant $-w$. On the circle, $|f(z)| \ge \delta > |w| = |{-w} - f(z) + f(z)|$… More directly, on the circle, $|f(z) - (-w)| \ge |f(z)| - |w| > 0$. **Rouché's theorem** compares the number of zeros of $f$ and $-w$ inside the circle. When $|f| > |{-w} - f|$ holds on the circle, the two have the same number of zeros.

Inside the circle, $f$ has $m \ge 1$ zeros counted with multiplicity, so $f + w$ also has a zero inside the circle, meaning that $w = -f(z)$ is attained.

Thus $B(0, \delta) \subseteq f(\Omega)$, and $w_{0}$ is an interior point.
