Statement

What this does: Applies Lagrangian Subspace pointwise on a manifold. It describes a maximal submanifold on which the symplectic form vanishes, and gives a mathematical model for configuration space in physics.

Let (M2n,ω)(M^{2n}, \omega)(M2n,ω) be a symplectic manifold. An immersed submanifold L⊆ML \subseteq ML⊆M is called a Lagrangian submanifold if

  1. dim⁡L=n\dim L = ndimL=n (half the dimension of dim⁡M\dim MdimM).
  2. ω∣L=0\omega|_{L} = 0ω∣L​=0 (on LLL the form ω\omegaω vanishes identically, so LLL is isotropic).

Equivalently, for every p∈Lp \in Lp∈L, TpLT_{p}LTp​L is a Lagrangian subspace of TpMT_{p}MTp​M.

Standard examples:

  • In R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n, the zero section {p=0}\{p = 0\}{p=0} (the position space, with all momenta zero).
  • More generally, the graph associated with any function, {(q,p):p=dF(q)}\{(q, p) : p = dF(q)\}{(q,p):p=dF(q)}, is a Lagrangian submanifold because ω∣L=dq∧d(dF)=0\omega|_{L} = dq \wedge d(dF) = 0ω∣L​=dq∧d(dF)=0.

Notes

⭐ ‘ω∣L=0\omega|_L = 0ω∣L​=0’ is equivalent to ‘p dqp\,dqpdq is closed on LLL’: Take ω=d(p dq)\omega = d(p\,dq)ω=d(pdq). On LLL, p dqp\,dqpdq is closed   ⟺  \iff⟺ it is locally of the form dFdFdF   ⟺  \iff⟺ LLL is locally the graph associated with a function.

Thus a Lagrangian submanifold is locally such a graph, and FFF is called a generating function.

This connects geometry with analysis and underlies Hamilton–Jacobi theory.

⚠️ dim⁡L=n\dim L = ndimL=n does not follow from isotropy: An isotropic submanifold can have dimension far below nnn (even a line in a symplectic vector space is isotropic). One must also require half dimension or, equivalently, maximality (TpL=(TpL)⊥T_pL = (T_pL)^{\perp}Tp​L=(Tp​L)⊥). This is exactly as in the linear case (see the notes to Lagrangian Subspace).

⚠️ Lagrangian submanifolds need not be closed or embedded: Physics often uses immersed submanifolds and even multiple covers (such as periodic orbits in Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization). The definition here allows immersions.

⚠️ Terminology: Lagrangian subspaces and Lagrangian submanifolds are named for the same person and express the same concept at two scales. They are unrelated to Lagrange’s Identity in inner product geometry.

Of the two defining conditions, ‘dim⁡=n\dim = ndim=n’ does not follow from isotropy (this is the step most often omitted):

  • ω∣L≡0\omega|_{L} \equiv 0ω∣L​≡0 gives only isotropy. An isotropic submanifold can have dimension far below nnn (even a line in a symplectic vector space is isotropic), so isotropy alone does not give maximality.
  • dim⁡L=n\dim L = ndimL=n (must be imposed separately): Equivalently, require TpL=(TpL)⊥T_{p}L = (T_{p}L)^{\perp}Tp​L=(Tp​L)⊥ (pointwise maximality). For a nondegenerate form, dim⁡W+dim⁡W⊥=dim⁡V\dim W + \dim W^{\perp} = \dim VdimW+dimW⊥=dimV, so ‘W=W⊥W = W^{\perp}W=W⊥’ forces the dimension to be half. This is what it means to be Lagrangian.
  • Directions of the equivalence: Pointwise, TpLT_{p}LTp​L is a Lagrangian subspace   ⟺  \iff⟺ LLL is a Lagrangian submanifold (LLL is immersed, and ω∣L=0\omega|_L = 0ω∣L​=0 and dim⁡=n\dim = ndim=n hold). The direction (⇐)(\Leftarrow)(⇐) is immediate pointwise. The direction (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) requires that LLL have constant dimension, which the immersion guarantees.

⚠️ Closedness is not part of the definition: LLL need not be closed, compact, or embedded. It can be immersed or even multiply covered. The Bohr–Sommerfeld condition in physics uses Lagrangian submanifolds that may only be immersed. ⚠️ The equivalence with being locally the graph associated with a function holds only when the projection π:L→Q\pi:L\to Qπ:L→Q (with QQQ the zero section containing a Lagrangian subspace) is a local diffeomorphism (exactness of p dqp\,\mathrm{d}qpdq). This is a local property, so generating functions exist only locally.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the pointwise definition requires no extra hypotheses, while a generating function requires transversality):

  • dim⁡L=n\dim L=ndimL=n and ω∣L=0\omega|_L=0ω∣L​=0   ⟺  \iff⟺ each tangent space is a Lagrangian subspace. This is the pointwise definition applied to the tangent map of the immersion. It does not require LLL to be closed, compact, or embedded.
  • Isotropic and half-dimensional   ⟺  \iff⟺ TpL=(TpL)ωT_pL=(T_pL)^\omegaTp​L=(Tp​L)ω uses nondegeneracy of the symplectic form and dim⁡W+dim⁡Wω=2n\dim W+\dim W^\omega=2ndimW+dimWω=2n. Isotropy alone is insufficient, as a one-dimensional submanifold when n>1n>1n>1 shows.
  • In cotangent coordinates, the graph of a closed 1-form   ⟺  \iff⟺ a Lagrangian graph, by computing the pullback of the canonical symplectic form. A closed 1-form is locally of the form dFdFdF by the Poincaré lemma. Global existence also requires exactness.
  • To express a general Lagrangian submanifold as a graph in fixed position coordinates, the projection must also be a local diffeomorphism. A cotangent fiber is itself Lagrangian but cannot be written as p=dF(q)p=dF(q)p=dF(q) under that position projection.
