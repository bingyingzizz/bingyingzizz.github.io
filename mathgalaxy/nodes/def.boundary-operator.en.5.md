# Boundary Operator　`def.boundary-operator`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.boundary-operator)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.boundary-operator.md)

**Why linear extension rather than term-by-term definition**: a chain is already a formal sum; if $\partial$ were not a homomorphism one could not compute $\partial(c_{1}+c_{2})$, and Stokes' theorem for chains requires exactly this.

**⚠️ Notation trap**: $\partial$ is used simultaneously for the boundary of a half-space ($\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}$), the boundary of a manifold ($\partial M$), and the boundary of a chain ($\partial c$). The three uses are unified in `prop.boundary-sign-convention`: for orientation-preserving cubes, the chain boundary and the manifold boundary agree face by face.
