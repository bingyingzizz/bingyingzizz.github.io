# integral of a complex-valued function　`def.integral-complex`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.integral-complex)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.integral-complex.md)

For complex-valued functions, split into real and imaginary parts: $\int f := \int \operatorname{Re} f + i \int \operatorname{Im} f$ (both integrals being required to make sense).

## Notes · **draft**
⚠ “At least one finite” avoids $\infty - \infty$: that is an indeterminate form and must be excluded.

If both are finite, $f$ is called **integrable** (see the next item).

For complex-valued functions, $\operatorname{Re} f$ and $\operatorname{Im} f$ integrable $\iff f$ integrable, because $|\operatorname{Re} f|, |\operatorname{Im} f| \le |f| \le |\operatorname{Re} f| + |\operatorname{Im} f|$.
