Statement

Let 1<p<∞1 < p < \infty1<p<∞, and let qqq be the **conjugate exponent** of ppp: 1p+1q=1\dfrac{1}{p} + \dfrac{1}{q} = 1p1​+q1​=1. Then for measurable f,gf, gf,g:

∥fg∥1≤∥f∥p ∥g∥q\|fg\|_1 \le \|f\|_p \, \|g\|_q∥fg∥1​≤∥f∥p​∥g∥q​

Notes

**Equality condition**: equality holds   ⟺  \iff⟺ ∣f∣p|f|^p∣f∣p and ∣g∣q|g|^q∣g∣q are proportional a.e. (that is, α∣f∣p=β∣g∣q\alpha |f|^p = \beta |g|^qα∣f∣p=β∣g∣q a.e.).

Proof: first assume ∥f∥p=∥g∥q=1\|f\|_p = \|g\|_q = 1∥f∥p​=∥g∥q​=1. By Young's inequality, substituting λ=1/p\lambda = 1/pλ=1/p, a=∣f∣pa = |f|^pa=∣f∣p, b=∣g∣qb = |g|^qb=∣g∣q gives

∣fg∣≤∣f∣pp+∣g∣qq|fg| \le \frac{|f|^p}{p} + \frac{|g|^q}{q}∣fg∣≤p∣f∣p​+q∣g∣q​

Integrating both sides gives ∥fg∥1≤1p+1q=1\|fg\|_1 \le \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1∥fg∥1​≤p1​+q1​=1. In the general case, normalize f,gf, gf,g each.
