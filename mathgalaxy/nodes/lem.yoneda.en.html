Statement

Let F:C→SetF : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}F:C→Set be a functor, X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C, and write

hX=Hom⁡C(X,−):C→Seth^{X} = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, -) : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}hX=HomC​(X,−):C→Set

Hom⁡(hX,F)  ≅  F(X),α↦αX(1X)\operatorname{Hom}(h^{X}, F) \;\cong\; F(X), \qquad \alpha \mapsto \alpha_{X}(1_{X})Hom(hX,F)≅F(X),α↦αX​(1X​)

This bijection is natural in **both** variables: natural in FFF (natural transformations) and in XXX (morphisms). Naturality on both sides is what gives the lemma its force.

It compresses the set "all natural transformations from hXh^{X}hX to FFF", which is in principle intractable, into **a single element** of F(X)F(X)F(X). Whenever "natural transformations are hard to count", this is the step that saves the day.

Dual form: if TTT is a presheaf (T:Cop→SetT : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}T:Cop→Set) and hX=Hom⁡(−,X)h_{X} = \operatorname{Hom}(-, X)hX​=Hom(−,X) is a representable presheaf, then likewise Hom⁡(hX,T)≅T(X)\operatorname{Hom}(h_{X}, T) \cong T(X)Hom(hX​,T)≅T(X).

The two formulas differ only by replacing C\mathcal{C}C with Cop\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}Cop, so both versions are used in what follows.
