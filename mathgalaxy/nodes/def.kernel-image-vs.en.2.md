## Notes · **draft**
⭐ "Injective iff the kernel is zero" is the most useful fact about linear maps: $\iff$, because $T(u) = T(v) \iff T(u - v) = 0$ — **linearity turns "two points coincide" into "their difference lies in the kernel"**.

General maps have no such convenience.

⭐ The kernel and the image are "the information lost" and "the information retained": the rank–nullity theorem says precisely that together they account for $V$.

⚠ $\ker T$ is a subspace of $V$, while $\operatorname{im} T$ is a subspace of $W$ — do not swap them.
