Statement

The product of any family of compact spaces (with the product topology) is again compact — for any number of factors, including uncountably many.

Notes

Proof (Alexander's subbase lemma + the axiom of choice). Write X=∏i∈IXiX = \prod_{i\in I} X_{i}X=∏i∈I​Xi​, with projections pi:X→Xip_{i} : X\to X_{i}pi​:X→Xi​. Take the subbase

S:={ pi−1(Ui)  :  i∈I, Ui⊆Xi open }.\mathcal{S} := \{\, p_{i}^{-1}(U_{i}) \;:\; i\in I,\ U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}\ \text{open} \,\}.S:={pi−1​(Ui​):i∈I, Ui​⊆Xi​ open}.

Alexander's subbase lemma: a topological space is compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ every cover by members of a subbase has a finite subcover. So it suffices to verify the latter. Let S0⊆S\mathcal{S}_{0} \subseteq \mathcal{S}S0​⊆S cover XXX, and suppose it has no finite subcover. For each iii put

Ui:={ Ui:pi−1(Ui)∈S0 },Fi:=Xi∖⋃Ui.\mathcal{U}_{i} := \{\, U_{i} : p_{i}^{-1}(U_{i}) \in \mathcal{S}_{0} \,\},\qquad F_{i} := X_{i}\setminus \bigcup \mathcal{U}_{i}.Ui​:={Ui​:pi−1​(Ui​)∈S0​},Fi​:=Xi​∖⋃Ui​.

  • By the axiom of choice choose xi∈Fix_{i} \in F_{i}xi​∈Fi​, and set x:=(xi)i∈I∈Xx := (x_{i})_{i\in I} \in Xx:=(xi​)i∈I​∈X.
  • Then for every pi−1(Ui)∈S0p_{i}^{-1}(U_{i})\in \mathcal{S}_{0}pi−1​(Ui​)∈S0​ we have xi∉Uix_{i} \notin U_{i}xi​∈/Ui​ (because xi∈Fix_{i}\in F_{i}xi​∈Fi​), so x∉pi−1(Ui)x \notin p_{i}^{-1}(U_{i})x∈/pi−1​(Ui​) holds for all members — contradicting that S0\mathcal{S}_{0}S0​ covers XXX ✓.

Thus every subbase cover has a finite subcover, and by Alexander's lemma XXX is compact. ∎ (Alexander's lemma itself: if a family of covers has no finite subcover, use Zorn's lemma to enlarge it to a maximal such family M\mathcal{M}M; then choosing the "non-M\mathcal{M}M members" produces data attached to the subbase, and one deduces step by step that M\mathcal{M}M is actually a cover — a contradiction.)

⭐ Tychonoff's theorem is equivalent to the axiom of choice (they imply each other in ZF) — a standard gauge of how strong the axiom of choice is.
