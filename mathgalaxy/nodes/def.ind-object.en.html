Statement

For a category C\mathcal{C}C, an Ind-object "lim→⁡i∈IXi\varinjlim_{i \in I} X_{i}lim​i∈I​Xi​" is a filtered diagram (Xi)i∈I(X_{i})_{i \in I}(Xi​)i∈I​. These form a category Ind⁡(C)\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})Ind(C), whose Hom is

Hom⁡Ind⁡(C)(“lim→⁡i∈IXi”, “lim→⁡j∈JYj”)  =  lim←⁡i∈I lim→⁡j∈J Hom⁡C(Xi,Yj).\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})}\Bigl(\text{“}\varinjlim_{i \in I} X_{i}\text{”},\ \text{“}\varinjlim_{j \in J} Y_{j}\text{”}\Bigr) \;=\; \varprojlim_{i \in I}\ \varinjlim_{j \in J}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i}, Y_{j}).HomInd(C)​(“i∈Ilim​​Xi​”, “j∈Jlim​​Yj​”)=i∈Ilim​​ j∈Jlim​​ HomC​(Xi​,Yj​).

Notes

Reading: the lim→⁡Xi\varinjlim X_{i}lim​Xi​ in quotes is a formal notation — the object is the "filtered diagram" itself, not its colimit (the colimit may not exist at all).

Why the formula looks like this: to give a map between two formal colimits, first fix iii and give, compatibly with the variation of iii, a family Xi→YjX_{i} \to Y_{j}Xi​→Yj​ (take the filtered colimit over jjj, which is lim→⁡j\varinjlim_{j}lim​j​), then take the limit over iii (as XiX_{i}Xi​ grows, maps given later can "fill in" earlier ones).

Examples: Ind⁡(FinSet)\operatorname{Ind}(\mathbf{FinSet})Ind(FinSet) is "all sets" (every set is the filtered colimit of its finite subsets); the completion of vector bundles under direct sums and colimits in KKK-theory also uses it.

📌 Dually there are Pro-objects: replacing filtered diagrams by cofiltered diagrams gives Pro⁡(C)\operatorname{Pro}(\mathcal{C})Pro(C) (profinite spaces are exactly Pro⁡(FinSet)\operatorname{Pro}(\mathbf{FinSet})Pro(FinSet)).
