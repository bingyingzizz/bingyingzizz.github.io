def.global-dimension

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

The (left) global dimension of a ring RRR is the supremum of the projective dimensions of all left modules:

gl.dim⁡R:=sup⁡Mpd⁡RM,\operatorname{gl.dim} R := \sup_{M} \operatorname{pd}_{R} M ,gl.dimR:=Msup​pdR​M,

The supremum ranges over all left RRR-modules MMM. Equivalently,

gl.dim⁡R=sup⁡Mid⁡RM,\operatorname{gl.dim} R = \sup_{M} \operatorname{id}_{R} M ,gl.dimR=Msup​idR​M,

It is also the least bound for which “Ext⁡Rn(−,−)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(-,-)ExtRn​(−,−) vanishes for all n>dn > dn>d” holds, with the bound denoted by ddd.

There is a corresponding right global dimension gl.dim⁡Rop\operatorname{gl.dim} R^{\mathrm{op}}gl.dimRop. The two can differ.

Notes

Why the three descriptions agree: By the vanishing characterizations of dimension, “pd⁡RM≤d\operatorname{pd}_{R} M \le dpdR​M≤d for all MMM,” “id⁡RN≤d\operatorname{id}_{R} N \le didR​N≤d for all NNN,” and “Ext⁡Rn(−,−)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(-,-)ExtRn​(−,−) vanishes for all n>dn > dn>d” are equivalent. Each says that this entire family of groups vanishes in degrees n>dn > dn>d ✓.

Four basic examples:

  • gl.dim⁡R=0  ⟺  R\operatorname{gl.dim} R = 0 \iff Rgl.dimR=0⟺R is semisimple (Wedderburn–Artin: every module is projective). Over a field the global dimension is 000.
  • gl.dim⁡Z=1\operatorname{gl.dim} \mathbb{Z} = 1gl.dimZ=1 (a principal ideal domain, see “Global Dimension of a Principal Ideal Domain”).
  • gl.dim⁡k[x1,…,xn]=n\operatorname{gl.dim} k[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}] = ngl.dimk[x1​,…,xn​]=n (Hilbert’s syzygy theorem and the Koszul complex).
  • gl.dim⁡R=∞\operatorname{gl.dim} R = \inftygl.dimR=∞ can occur (for example, for k[x,y]/(xy)k[x,y]/(xy)k[x,y]/(xy)). In this case there is no uniform upper bound.

⭐ It measures how many steps are needed to compute Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext: The smaller the global dimension, the shorter the resolutions needed to compute derived functors. Dimension 000 means there is nothing to compute, and dimension 111 means it suffices to relate two exact sequences. This is also why the homological algebra of abelian groups is so simple.

⚠️ The distinction between left and right matters: Over a noncommutative ring, the left and right global dimensions can differ. Over a commutative ring they agree, so a single notation suffices.
