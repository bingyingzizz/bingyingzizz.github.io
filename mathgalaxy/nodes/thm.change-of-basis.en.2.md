In this case the two matrices are called **similar** (written $A \sim B$). Similarity is an equivalence relation.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ In one sentence: **changing basis is conjugation**.

The $P^{-1}AP$ in $P$ governs "how coordinates change", and one factor entering and one leaving on the two sides exactly cancels the choice of basis — so "similar" is an **intrinsic** property of the matrix (belonging to the linear map), not a property of the array of numbers itself.

⭐ A division of labour worth remembering: $A$ and $B$ are similar $\iff$ they are the matrices of **one and the same** linear map in two bases;
