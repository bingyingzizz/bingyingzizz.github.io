## Notes · **draft**
⭐ "Being bounded" and "attaining an extremum" are two different things, which is why they are separate nodes here: boundedness says only that $\sup f(X)$ is **finite**, while attaining an extremum says that this supremum is **actually reached at some point**.

Compactness is responsible for the latter.

⚠ Replacing the space by an open interval makes it fail at once: $f(x) = x$ is bounded on $(0, 1)$, yet attains neither a maximum nor a minimum.

⭐ The commonly used form over $\mathbb{R}$: **a continuous function on a closed interval $[a, b]$ attains its extrema** — the first step of the proof is that a closed interval is compact (Heine–Borel).
