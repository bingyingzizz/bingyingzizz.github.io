# Runge approximation theorem　`thm.runge`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.runge)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.runge.md)

**General case**: In $\mathbb{C}\setminus K$, choose in advance, for each connected component $\Omega$, one point $b_{\Omega}$. Step 1 gives poles in $\Gamma$ for a rational function $R$. Since $\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus K$, each pole lies in some component $\Omega$, and Step 2 moves it to $b_{\Omega}$. There are finitely many poles and the error in each step can be made arbitrarily small, so $R$ is uniformly approximated by a rational function with poles at $\{b_{\Omega}\}$ to within $\varepsilon/2$. Combining this with the error $\varepsilon/2$ from Step 1 proves the result. ∎
