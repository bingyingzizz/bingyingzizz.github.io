## English abstract · **draft** · machine-drafted, pending author review; Chinese is authoritative（续）
Let $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ be non-empty. The $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal{E}$ coincides with the monotone class $\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E})$ exactly when the complement of every $A \in \mathcal{E}$ and the union of any two members of $\mathcal{E}$ lie in $\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E})$. Taking $\mathcal{E}$ to be an algebra recovers the standard monotone class theorem; the proof first closes $\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{E})$ under complements and then under unions by three monotone-class arguments, so the resulting class is a $\sigma$-algebra.
