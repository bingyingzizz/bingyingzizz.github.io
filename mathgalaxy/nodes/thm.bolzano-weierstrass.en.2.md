## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This is the analytic core of "compact $\iff$ bounded and closed in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$" (Heine–Borel): the reason a continuous function on a bounded closed set attains its extreme values ultimately lies here.

⚠ Completeness is **indispensable** here: in $\mathbb{Q}$, the sequence $1, 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, \ldots$ is bounded but has no subsequence converging to a rational number.

⭐ The standard proof is **bisection**: halve the interval each time, keep the half containing infinitely many terms, and apply the nested-interval theorem.
