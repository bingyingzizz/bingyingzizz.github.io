Statement

Let MMM be an RRR-module and choose a surjection π0:P0↠M\pi_{0} : P_{0} \twoheadrightarrow Mπ0​:P0​↠M (usually with P0P_{0}P0​ free). Its kernel

ΩM:=ker⁡(π0:P0→M)\Omega M := \ker\bigl(\pi_{0} : P_{0} \to M\bigr)ΩM:=ker(π0​:P0​→M)

is called the (first) syzygy of MMM and is also denoted Ω1M\Omega^{1}MΩ1M. Recursively, given πn:Pn↠ΩnM\pi_{n} : P_{n} \twoheadrightarrow \Omega^{n}Mπn​:Pn​↠ΩnM, set

Ωn+1M:=ker⁡(πn),Ω0M:=M.\Omega^{n+1}M := \ker\bigl(\pi_{n}\bigr), \qquad \Omega^{0}M := M .Ωn+1M:=ker(πn​),Ω0M:=M.

The terms PnP_{n}Pn​ and maps πn\pi_{n}πn​ then form a projective (free) resolution of MMM, and ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM is the image or kernel in this resolution at stage nnn.

Notes

Origin of the name When Hilbert solved systems of linear equations, ΩM\Omega MΩM described the linear relations among the remaining unknowns (the Greek word behind “syzygy” originally meant “pairing”).

In the case M=R/(a1,…,am)M = R/(a_{1}, \ldots, a_{m})M=R/(a1​,…,am​), ΩM\Omega MΩM is the submodule generated by the relations ∑aiei=0\sum a_{i}e_{i} = 0∑ai​ei​=0, the first syzygy of the system of equations.

Syzygies express a resolution as a sequence of modules. A resolution

⋯→P2→P1→P0→M→0\cdots \to P_{2} \to P_{1} \to P_{0} \to M \to 0⋯→P2​→P1​→P0​→M→0

and a sequence of syzygies ΩM↪P0\Omega M \hookrightarrow P_{0}ΩM↪P0​, Ω2M↪P1\Omega^{2}M \hookrightarrow P_{1}Ω2M↪P1​, …\ldots… encode the same data. Thus asking whether MMM has a projective resolution of length nnn is equivalent to asking whether ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM is projective (see “Projective Dimension and Vanishing of Ext”).

⚠️ Syzygies depend on the resolution Changing the resolution can change ΩM\Omega MΩM. It is unique only up to adding projective direct summands, as stated in “Stability of Syzygies,” which follows from the syzygy comparison lemma.

📌 Hilbert's syzygy theorem is the best-known result along these lines. Over R=k[x1,…,xn]R = k[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}]R=k[x1​,…,xn​], the syzygy of order nnn of every finitely generated module is free, so gl.dim⁡R=n\operatorname{gl.dim} R = ngl.dimR=n (for a polynomial ring, the Koszul complex gives an explicit resolution of length nnn).
