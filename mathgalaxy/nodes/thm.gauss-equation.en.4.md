[in graph](../#n=thm.gauss-equation)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.gauss-equation.md)

if one takes $R_{ijkl} = h_{ik}h_{jl}-h_{il}h_{jk}$, which corresponds to placing $W$ in the second pair, then $R_{1212} = LN-M^{2} = K(EG-F^{2})$ holds.

Align the convention on $R$ before using index formulas, or the signs are meaningless.

**Only one independent component in dimension two**: the components $R_{ijkl}$ are pinned by symmetries, and in dimension two there is only $R_{1212}$, so the Gauss equation in dimension two is **one** equation — which is why surface theory is computable.
