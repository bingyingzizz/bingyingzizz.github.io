## Notes · **draft**
**Where the comparison map comes from**: $\lim D$ is the universal cone of $D$; applying $F$ gives a cone over $F \circ D$, so the universal property of $\lim(F\circ D)$ yields a unique map $F(\lim D) \to \lim(F\circ D)$. Thus "preserves limits" always means that this map, **which exists automatically by the universal property**, happens to be invertible.

⭐ **The most used case**: **right adjoints preserve all limits** (dually left adjoints preserve all colimits). The proof uses only the adjunction isomorphism and the universal property of limits, with no concrete construction.
