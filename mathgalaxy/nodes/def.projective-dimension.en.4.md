The reason is general. For a Noetherian local ring, $\operatorname{gl.dim} R < \infty$ is equivalent to $R$ being regular, and $k[x,y]/(xy)$ localized at $(x,y)$ is singular (its dimension is $1$ and its embedding dimension is $2$) ✓.

⭐ **Equivalent characterization** (see “Projective Dimension and Vanishing of Ext”)
$\operatorname{pd}_{R} M \le n$ if and only if $\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{R}(M, -) = 0$ holds for all $k > n$. Thus the dimension is one less than the first degree at which vanishing fails.
