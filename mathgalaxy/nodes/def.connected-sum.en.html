Statement

Let MMM and NNN be two nnn-dimensional connected topological manifolds (n≥1n \ge 1n≥1). Choose an open ball in MMM, denoted BMB_{M}BM​, and an open ball in NNN, denoted BNB_{N}BN​. Remove their interiors to obtain two manifolds with spherical boundary:

M∘:=M∖int⁡BM,N∘:=N∖int⁡BN,M^{\circ} := M \setminus \operatorname{int} B_{M}, \qquad N^{\circ} := N \setminus \operatorname{int} B_{N},M∘:=M∖intBM​,N∘:=N∖intBN​,

Both boundaries are Sn−1S^{n-1}Sn−1. Choose any homeomorphism h:∂M∘→∂N∘h : \partial M^{\circ} \to \partial N^{\circ}h:∂M∘→∂N∘ and glue the manifolds along their boundaries:

M#N:=M∘⊔N∘/(x∼h(x)).M \# N := M^{\circ} \sqcup N^{\circ} \big/ \bigl( x \sim h(x) \bigr).M#N:=M∘⊔N∘/(x∼h(x)).

The resulting topological space is the connected sum of MMM and NNN.

Notes

⭐ Gluing is taking a quotient. After ⊔\sqcup⊔, take the quotient topology according to x∼h(x)x \sim h(x)x∼h(x) (as in quotient set and equivalence class, where a set is open if its preimage is open). The connected sum thus combines disjoint union and quotient.

⭐ The result is a manifold and is independent of the choices. At the seam, the ring of points in M∘M^{\circ}M∘, together with Sn−1S^{n-1}Sn−1 and the corresponding ring in N∘N^{\circ}N∘, has half-neighborhoods on the two sides. Gluing them gives an open set in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, so every point has a chart. Choosing different balls or a different homeomorphism gives a homeomorphic result (this uses the fact that any two embedded balls inside a ball are isotopic, which is not developed here).

⭐ The sphere is an identity element: Sn#M≅MS^{n} \# M \cong MSn#M≅M (remove a ball from MMM and a ball from SnS^{n}Sn, so that gluing fills the hole and restores the original manifold). Thus the nnn-dimensional closed manifolds form a commutative monoid under connected sum. The operation is commutative and has an identity, but inverses need not exist, which is part of what makes classification interesting.

⚠ Connected sums must be taken in the same dimension, and the manifolds must be connected. Otherwise the balls could be removed from different components, making the result depend on the choice.

📌 Genus (genus) counts how many tori occur in the connected sum describing a surface. The classification theorem establishes this equivalence. This node gives the definition of the operation.
