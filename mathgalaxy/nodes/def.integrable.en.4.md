- $f$ is integrable $\iff$ $\int f^{+} < \infty$ and $\int f^{-} < \infty$: **this is the definition** (split $f$ into positive and negative parts, each a **non-negative** measurable function, and use the non-negative case).
- $\iff$ $\int \lvert f\rvert < \infty$: **one step** ($\lvert f\rvert = f^{+} + f^{-}$), and it **uses additivity of the non-negative integral**. ⚠️ "$\int f$ is finite" and integrability are **not** the same thing: if $\int f$ is a **finite real number**, then $f^{\pm}$ are finite and $f$ is necessarily integrable; the danger is $\int f^{+} = \int f^{-} = +\infty$, where $\int f$ is **undefined** (not $0$) — which
