**IX. Notation.** Here we use the language of families of $k$-dimensional subspaces. Textbooks often use integral manifolds of a differential ideal $I\subseteq\Omega^{\bullet}(M)$. The two formulations are equivalent (take $I$ to be the ideal generated by forms annihilating $\mathcal{D}$). We use only the distribution formulation here to avoid introducing a second notation. The cases $k=n$ and $k=0$ are trivial. $k=1$ is always involutive, and the nontrivial examples begin at $2\le k\le n-1$.
