# uniform convergence　`def.uniform-convergence`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.uniform-convergence)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.uniform-convergence.md)

$$\sup_{x \in X} |f_n(x) - f(x)| \xrightarrow{\;n \to \infty\;} 0$$

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The only difference from pointwise convergence is the **order of quantifiers**: here $\exists N$ comes **before** $\forall x$ — a single $N$ works for the whole domain.

⭐ The supremum form is the most-used criterion: uniform convergence says "the supremum of the error function tends to zero", that is, **convergence in the sup norm**.

This also explains why it is "convergence in a function space" rather than "convergence on a set of points".
