Statement

In plain words: a complex integral along a closed curve can be converted into a double integral over the area inside the curve, whose integrand is exactly ∂f/∂zˉ\partial f/\partial \bar z∂f/∂zˉ — so "holomorphic   ⟹  \implies⟹ integral zero" becomes "the integrand is 000".

Let Ω\OmegaΩ be a bounded region whose boundary ∂Ω\partial\Omega∂Ω is a piecewise smooth simple closed curve, positively oriented (counterclockwise). If f∈C1(Ω‾)f \in C^1(\overline{\Omega})f∈C1(Ω), then

∫∂Ωf(z) dz=2i∬Ω∂f∂zˉ(z) dx dy.\int_{\partial\Omega} f(z)\,\mathrm{d}z = 2i \iint_{\Omega} \frac{\partial f}{\partial \bar z}(z)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.∫∂Ω​f(z)dz=2i∬Ω​∂zˉ∂f​(z)dxdy.

In particular, if fff is holomorphic (one need no longer say "f′f'f′ is continuous": the derivative of a holomorphic function is again holomorphic and hence continuous), then the right-hand side is zero, i.e. ∮∂Ωf dz=0\displaystyle\oint_{\partial\Omega} f\,\mathrm{d}z = 0∮∂Ω​fdz=0.
