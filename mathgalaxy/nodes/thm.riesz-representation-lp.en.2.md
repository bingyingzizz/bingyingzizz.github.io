# Riesz representation theorem for Lp　`thm.riesz-representation-lp`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.riesz-representation-lp)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.riesz-representation-lp.md)

In particular, for $p = 2$ the space $L^2$ is self-dual.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This is the climax of $L^p$ theory: **every continuous linear functional on $L^p$ is just "multiply by an $L^q$ function and integrate"**.

The same holds for $p = 1$ when $\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite ($(L^1)^* \cong L^\infty$);

⚠ but it **fails** for $p = \infty$: $(L^\infty)^*$ is strictly larger than $L^1$ (there are bounded linear functionals not given by an $L^1$ function, for instance those obtained by applying Hahn–Banach on $C[0,1]$).
