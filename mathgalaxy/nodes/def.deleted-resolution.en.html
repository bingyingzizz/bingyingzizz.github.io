Statement

Let ε:P0→M\varepsilon : P_{0} \to Mε:P0​→M be the augmentation of a projective resolution of the RRR-module MMM. Thus

⋯→P2→ d2 P1→ d1 P0→ ε M→0\cdots \to P_{2} \xrightarrow{\ d_{2}\ } P_{1} \xrightarrow{\ d_{1}\ } P_{0} \xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ } M \to 0⋯→P2​ d2​ ​P1​ d1​ ​P0​ ε ​M→0

is exact everywhere, and each PnP_{n}Pn​ is projective. The deleted resolution is the chain complex obtained by removing the augmentation:

P∙:⋯→P2→ d2 P1→ d1 P0→0.P_{\bullet} : \quad \cdots \to P_{2} \xrightarrow{\ d_{2}\ } P_{1} \xrightarrow{\ d_{1}\ } P_{0} \to 0 .P∙​:⋯→P2​ d2​ ​P1​ d1​ ​P0​→0.

In other words, in the exact complex with MMM in degree −1-1−1, delete the term in degree −1-1−1 and keep the rest as it is.

Notes

Why it deserves a name of its own. To compute derived functors, apply the functor to the deleted resolution. The augmentation is excluded from the computation:

Ext⁡Rn(M,N)=Hn(Hom⁡R(P∙,N)),Tor⁡nR(M,N)=Hn(P∙⊗RN).\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(M, N) = H^{n}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(P_{\bullet}, N)\bigr), \qquad \operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R}(M, N) = H_{n}\bigl(P_{\bullet} \otimes_{R} N\bigr).ExtRn​(M,N)=Hn(HomR​(P∙​,N)),TornR​(M,N)=Hn​(P∙​⊗R​N).

Both occurrences of HHH on the right-hand sides use the deleted resolution, with the term MMM omitted. See “Computing Ext in Either Variable” and “Tor.”

Two details that are easy to get wrong. ① The deleted resolution is no longer exact: H0(P∙)=P0/im⁡d1≅MH_{0}(P_{\bullet}) = P_{0}/\operatorname{im} d_{1} \cong MH0​(P∙​)=P0​/imd1​≅M, and all higher homology vanishes. Its exactness thus differs from that of the original complex only in degree 000.

② Some books retain MMM in degree −1-1−1 and call the result the augmented complex. The two complexes differ by a single term, so specify which one is used in a formula.

⚠️ Notation trap: Hom⁡(P∙,N)\operatorname{Hom}(P_{\bullet}, N)Hom(P∙​,N) is the Hom complex obtained by viewing NNN as a complex concentrated in degree 000. Its degrees run in the opposite direction from the subscripts of P∙P_{\bullet}P∙​ (without dualizing, one obtains a chain complex). Thus use HnH^{n}Hn for Ext and HnH_{n}Hn​ for Tor.
