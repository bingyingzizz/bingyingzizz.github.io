Statement

A topological space XXX is locally compact if every point has a compact neighbourhood: for every x∈Xx \in Xx∈X there is a compact set KKK with

x∈K˚x \in \mathring{K}x∈K˚

Here XXX is not required to be Hausdorff.

Notes

Difference from the Hausdorff version: in a Hausdorff space, "every point has a compact neighbourhood" is equivalent to "every point has a base of compact neighbourhoods" (inside every neighbourhood one can find a compact neighbourhood); without Hausdorffness this equivalence fails (compact sets need not be closed, and the relation between neighbourhoods and compact sets degenerates).

Examples: every compact space (take K=XK = XK=X), Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, and every discrete space is locally compact; Q\mathbb{Q}Q is not. A compact space is always locally compact, but not necessarily Hausdorff.

Use: when Hausdorffness is needed, combine this with Hausdorff 空间; without it the definition still stands, but the stronger property of having a base of compact neighbourhoods may fail.
