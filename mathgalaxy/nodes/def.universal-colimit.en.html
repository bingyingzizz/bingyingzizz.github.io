An effective equivalence relation is called universal if its quotient map is a universal regular epimorphism.

Notes

"Universal" = still there after pullback: no matter along which map the whole diagram is pulled back, the property is preserved. These three are the template for translating "the obviously good properties in sets" into category language — they cause no trouble in Set\mathbf{Set}Set, but need not hold in another category, which is why they must be written down.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the three kinds of terms are different pullback-stability requirements):

  • Universal colimits   ⟺  \iff⟺ remain the colimit of that diagram after an arbitrary base change: unfolding the definition is free, provided the pullbacks and colimits written exist.
  • Universal regular epimorphisms   ⟺  \iff⟺ have all pullbacks still regular epi: again unfolding the definition; taking only the identity base change gives only regular epi, which does not conversely imply universal.
  • Disjoint coproducts   ⟺  \iff⟺ have injections that are monomorphisms and fibred products of distinct terms initial: unfolding the definition; this does not automatically imply that the coproduct is universal.
  • Stability in sets needs the concrete constructions of set colimits and pullbacks; a general category cannot copy this, for example the coproduct in groups is the free product, but colimits in the category of groups are not universally so.
