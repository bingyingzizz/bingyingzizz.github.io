## Notes · **draft**
The proof is one line: if $\mathrm{On}$ were a set, it would be **transitive** (elements of ordinals are ordinals) and $\in$ would **well-order** it (trichotomy gives comparability), so $\mathrm{On}$ would itself be an ordinal, hence $\mathrm{On} \in \mathrm{On}$ — contradicting the fact that no ordinal is an element of itself ($\in$ is a well-order on ordinals, which forbids $\in$-cycles).

This is exactly the other face of **Hartogs' theorem**: there are too many ordinals to fit into any set. "There is an ordinal that does not inject into $X$" and "the ordinals do not form a set" say the same thing.
