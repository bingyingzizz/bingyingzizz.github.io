## Statement · **draft**
Let $(V,\rho)$ be a representation of a group $G$. The symmetric square representation is the quotient representation $\operatorname{Sym}^2V=(V\otimes V)/\langle v\otimes w-w\otimes v\rangle$, with $G$ acting by $g(vw)=\rho(g)v\,\rho(g)w$ (where $vw$ denotes the class of $v\otimes w$). If $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is a basis of $V$, then $\{e_ie_j:1\le i\le j\le n\}$ is a basis of $\operatorname{Sym}^2V$.
