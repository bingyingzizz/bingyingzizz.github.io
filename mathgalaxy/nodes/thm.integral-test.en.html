Statement

Let f:[1,+∞)→[0,+∞)f : [1, +\infty) \to [0, +\infty)f:[1,+∞)→[0,+∞) be non-negative and decreasing. Then the series ∑f(n)\sum f(n)∑f(n) and the improper integral ∫1+∞f\int_1^{+\infty} f∫1+∞​f converge or diverge together, that is,

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: the series and the integral are squeezed by the same ruler.

The upper bound f(n)≤∫n−1nff(n) \le \int_{n-1}^{n} ff(n)≤∫n−1n​f and the lower bound ∫nn+1f≤f(n)\int_{n}^{n+1} f \le f(n)∫nn+1​f≤f(n) each come from monotonicity; adding them over the intervals gives the squeezing inequality.

⭐ The classic application: ∑1/ns\sum 1/n^{s}∑1/ns converges for s>1s > 1s>1 and diverges for s≤1s \le 1s≤1 — because the same threshold for ∫1∞x−s dx\int_1^{\infty} x^{-s}\,dx∫1∞​x−sdx lies at s=1s = 1s=1.

⚠ The function must be non-negative and decreasing: if fff is defined only at the integers, or oscillates, neither inequality holds.

⚠ It is not a corollary of Kummer: the route here — translating a sum into an improper integral — is completely different.
