Statement

Regard the integers as the poset category (Z,≤)(\mathbb{Z}, \le)(Z,≤). The inclusion {0}↪(Z,≤)\{0\} \hookrightarrow (\mathbb{Z},\le){0}↪(Z,≤) gives a functor

C⟶C(C),M⟼[M],[M]n:={M,n=00,n≠0\mathcal{C} \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}), \qquad M \longmapsto [M], \qquad [M]^{n} := \begin{cases} M, & n = 0 \\ 0, & n \ne 0\end{cases}C⟶C(C),M⟼[M],[M]n:={M,0,​n=0n=0​

(all differentials zero). Then:

  1. this functor is fully faithful;
  2. it preserves all limits and all colimits.

Notes

⭐ Use: an "object" may be regarded as a complex concentrated in one term. So constructions in C\mathcal{C}C and constructions in C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C) may be moved back and forth freely, and a short exact sequence 0→M→N→P→00 \to M \to N \to P \to 00→M→N→P→0 no longer needs to be distinguished from an exact sequence of two-term complexes.
