Statement

For A,B∈Mn(K)A, B \in M_n(K)A,B∈Mn​(K):

det⁡(AB)=det⁡A⋅det⁡B,det⁡AT=det⁡A,det⁡(cA)=cndet⁡A\det(AB) = \det A \cdot \det B, \qquad \det A^{\mathsf{T}} = \det A, \qquad \det(cA) = c^{n} \det Adet(AB)=detA⋅detB,detAT=detA,det(cA)=cndetA

Hence if AAA is invertible then det⁡(A−1)=(det⁡A)−1\det(A^{-1}) = (\det A)^{-1}det(A−1)=(detA)−1; conversely AAA is invertible   ⟺  det⁡A≠0\iff \det A \ne 0⟺detA=0.

(This is the contrapositive of "det⁡\detdet is a multiplicative homomorphism": det⁡A=0\det A = 0detA=0   ⟺  \iff⟺ AAA is not invertible.)

Notes

⭐ Multiplicativity is the most important property of the determinant: it translates "matrix multiplication (composition)" into "multiplication of scalars", so det⁡\detdet is a multiplicative homomorphism from the ring Mn(K)M_n(K)Mn​(K) to the field KKK (sending GL⁡n(K)\operatorname{GL}_n(K)GLn​(K) to K×K^{\times}K×).

⭐ The trick of the proof: fix BBB and look at A↦det⁡(AB)A \mapsto \det(AB)A↦det(AB) — it is alternating multilinear in the columns of AAA (because the columns of ABABAB are linear combinations of the columns of AAA), so by existence and uniqueness it equals a constant multiple of det⁡A\det AdetA;

taking A=IA = IA=I determines the constant =det⁡B= \det B=detB.

⚠ Note det⁡(cA)=cndet⁡A\det(cA) = c^{n}\det Adet(cA)=cndetA (not cdet⁡Ac\det AcdetA) — every column is multiplied by ccc, nnn times in total.
