## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The **analytic definition** of the trigonometric functions (rather than the geometric one): starting from the series, every identity and derivative formula can be computed, and they hold automatically for complex numbers.

⭐ The relation to $\exp$ is revealed in the entry on Euler's formula: splitting the series of $\exp$ according to the parity of $n$ gives $\cos$ and $\sin$.

⚠ The geometric meaning (unit circle, angle, radian) is attached only **later**: the series first tell us that $\cos$ has zeros and $\sin$ has a period, and the geometric picture follows.
