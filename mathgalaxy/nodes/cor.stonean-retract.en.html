Statement

Stonean spaces are exactly the retracts of free compact Hausdorff spaces: there is a continuous map r:X→Ar : X \to Ar:X→A with r∣A=1Ar|_{A} = 1_{A}r∣A​=1A​ (such an AAA is called a retract of XXX).

Notes

This translates Gleason's theorem into one sentence of "concrete topology": projectivity = retract. It is completely parallel to "projective module = direct summand of a free module" — both say "projective = cut a piece off a free object".

Proof (combining Gleason with "every object of CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is a quotient of a free one").

  • (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹): let AAA be Stonean.

Take a free compact Hausdorff space FFF and a surjection p:F↠Ap : F\twoheadrightarrow Ap:F↠A ("every object of CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is a quotient of a free one").

By Gleason's theorem, Stonean   ⟹  \implies⟹ AAA is a projective object of CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus, so the surjection p:F→Ap : F\to Ap:F→A has a section s:A→Fs : A\to Fs:A→F, i.e. p∘s=idAp\circ s = \mathrm{id}_{A}p∘s=idA​ — which is exactly "AAA is a retract of FFF" (take r:=pr := pr:=p) ✓.

  • (⟸)(\Longleftarrow)(⟸): if AAA is a retract of a free object FFF (r:F→Ar: F\to Ar:F→A, r∣A=idr|_A = \mathrm{id}r∣A​=id), then, since a retract of a projective object is projective (standard fact: a direct summand/retract of a projective object is projective), AAA is projective in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus;

and by Gleason's theorem again, AAA is Stonean ✓.

∎ ⭐ This is the complete argument for "Stonean = retract of free compact Hausdorff", and it is also the source of "Z[S]\mathbb{Z}[S]Z[S] is projective" later (a retract of SSS gives a section, and a section gives the projectivity of Z[S]\mathbb{Z}[S]Z[S]).
