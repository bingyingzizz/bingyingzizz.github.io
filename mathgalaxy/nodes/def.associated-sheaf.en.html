Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a site and X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C. The associated sheaf is "first Yoneda-embed, then sheafify":

X♯  :=  hX#,hX=Hom⁡C(−,X).X^{\sharp} \;:=\; h_{X}^{\#}, \qquad h_{X} = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-, X).X♯:=hX#​,hX​=HomC​(−,X).

It is characterized by a universal property: for every sheaf FFF,

Hom⁡(X♯, F)  ≅  F(X).\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(X^{\sharp},\ F\bigr) \;\cong\; F(X).Hom(X♯, F)≅F(X).

Notes

Where this isomorphism comes from. Sheafification (−)#(-)^{\#}(−)# and inclusion C^#↪C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\#} \hookrightarrow \widehat{\mathcal{C}}C#↪C are adjoint, so

Hom⁡(hX#, F)≅Hom⁡(hX,F)≅F(X),\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(h_{X}^{\#},\ F\bigr) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(h_{X}, F) \cong F(X),Hom(hX#​, F)≅Hom(hX​,F)≅F(X),

the last step being Yoneda's lemma ✓.

⭐ Use: it gives the canonical way to "regard an object of C\mathcal{C}C as a sheaf", namely X↦X♯X \mapsto X^{\sharp}X↦X♯. When the topology is subcanonical, hXh_{X}hX​ is already a sheaf, so X♯=hXX^{\sharp} = h_{X}X♯=hX​, and the Yoneda embedding factors directly through the sheaf category ✓ (this is also the most common equivalent form of "subcanonical").

⚠️ This functor is left exact (the Yoneda embedding preserves limits and sheafification is left exact), but in general not faithful, and it does not preserve colimits or infinite limits: sheafification glues together "different presheaf descriptions of the same object".
