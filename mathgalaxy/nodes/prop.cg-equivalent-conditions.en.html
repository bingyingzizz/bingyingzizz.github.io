Statement

The following six conditions are equivalent:

  1. XXX is compactly generated;
  2. XXX is a quotient space of a "disjoint union of compact Hausdorff spaces";
  3. XXX is a quotient space of a "locally compact Hausdorff space";
  4. Y⊆XY \subseteq XY⊆X is open   ⟺  \iff⟺ for every continuous f:S→Xf : S \to Xf:S→X from a compact Hausdorff space, f−1(Y)f^{-1}(Y)f−1(Y) is open;
  5. X→YX \to YX→Y is continuous   ⟺  \iff⟺ for every continuous f:S→Xf : S \to Xf:S→X, the composite S→X→YS \to X \to YS→X→Y is continuous;
  6. XXX is the colimit of all continuous maps S→XS \to XS→X (SSS compact Hausdorff).

Notes

Hypotheses used in each direction (this list is the actual content of "six equivalent conditions" — some directions are almost unpacking the definition, others really cost something):

  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): only the definition. A compactly generated space is by definition one whose open sets are determined by probes from compact Hausdorff spaces; passing to a quotient presentation is a standard rearrangement.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): no extra hypothesis needed (a locally compact Hausdorff space is a special case of a compact Hausdorff space — take each piece as itself).
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): this step uses the universal property of "quotients of compact Hausdorff spaces" — the universal property of the quotient map pulls "testing open sets" back to the covering space, so it suffices to test on compact Hausdorff spaces.
  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (4): tautological ((4) is just "compactly generated" written as "test open sets using SSS").
  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (5): again only a restatement (testing open sets   ⟺  \iff⟺ testing continuity, since continuity of fff requires that the preimage of every open set be open).
  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (6): this one uses the colimit characterization (existence and universal property of the colimit), the most "categorical" step of the six; the other five can be stated in point-set topology.

Where to be careful: SSS uses only compact Hausdorff spaces (not "arbitrary compact spaces"). For compact Hausdorff spaces are closed under quotients and products (@@thm.tykhonov@@, @@prop.quotient-of-compact@@), so they suffice as probes; replacing them by "all compact spaces" would make the equivalence of (2) and (3) fail.

⭐ The test spaces are uniformly taken compact Hausdorff. The defining item and items (2)(3)(4)(6) here use the same family of test spaces — that is: XXX compactly generated   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is a disjoint union of compact Hausdorff spaces ∐S↠X\coprod S \twoheadrightarrow X∐S↠X that is a quotient map   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is a locally compact Hausdorff space Z↠XZ \twoheadrightarrow XZ↠X that is a quotient map.

The three descriptions give the same category; use whichever is easier to compute with.
