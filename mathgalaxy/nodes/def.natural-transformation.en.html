Statement

Let F,G:C→DF, G : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}F,G:C→D be two functors. A natural transformation α:F  ⟹  G\alpha : F \implies Gα:F⟹G from FFF to GGG is a family of morphisms

αX:F(X)→G(X)(X∈C)\alpha_X : F(X) \to G(X) \qquad (X \in \mathcal{C})αX​:F(X)→G(X)(X∈C)

such that for every f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y the square commutes:

G(f)∘αX=αY∘F(f)G(f) \circ \alpha_X = \alpha_Y \circ F(f)G(f)∘αX​=αY​∘F(f)

When every αX\alpha_XαX​ is an isomorphism, α\alphaα is called a natural isomorphism, written F≅GF \cong GF≅G.

Notes

In one sentence: the two components of α\alphaα control the two ends, and commutativity says that "changing functor first and then applying fff" agrees with "applying fff first and then changing functor".

With natural transformations, functors themselves become objects: taking functors C→C′\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'C→C′ as objects and natural transformations as morphisms gives a category denoted Hom⁡(C,C′)\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}')Hom(C,C′);

the set of all natural transformations F  ⟹  GF \implies GF⟹G is denoted Nat⁡(F,G)\operatorname{Nat}(F, G)Nat(F,G).

Example: there are two functors CRng→Grp\mathbf{CRng} \to \mathbf{Grp}CRng→Grp — A↦GLn(A)A \mapsto GL_n(A)A↦GLn​(A) and A↦A∗A \mapsto A^{*}A↦A∗. The determinant

det⁡A:GLn(A)→A∗\det_A : GL_n(A) \to A^{*}Adet​:GLn​(A)→A∗

assembles into a natural transformation: det⁡\detdet "commutes with" ring homomorphisms. This is exactly what "natural" means — not a coincidence verified case by case, but forced by structure.
