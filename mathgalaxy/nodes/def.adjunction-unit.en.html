Statement

Let F⊣GF \dashv GF⊣G, and write the natural isomorphism as

ΦX,Y:Hom⁡D(F(X),Y)⟶Hom⁡C(X,G(Y))\Phi_{X, Y} : \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl(F(X), Y\bigr) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}\bigl(X, G(Y)\bigr)ΦX,Y​:HomD​(F(X),Y)⟶HomC​(X,G(Y))

Evaluating it on the two "identity morphisms" gives two natural transformations:

ηX:=ΦX,F(X)(1F(X)):X→GF(X)\eta_{X} := \Phi_{X, F(X)}(1_{F(X)}) : X \to G F(X)ηX​:=ΦX,F(X)​(1F(X)​):X→GF(X)εY:=ΦG(Y),Y−1(1G(Y)):FG(Y)→Y\varepsilon_{Y} := \Phi_{G(Y), Y}^{-1}(1_{G(Y)}) : F G(Y) \to YεY​:=ΦG(Y),Y−1​(1G(Y)​):FG(Y)→Y

They satisfy the triangle identities

εF(X)∘F(ηX)=1F(X),G(εY)∘ηG(Y)=1G(Y)\varepsilon_{F(X)} \circ F(\eta_{X}) = 1_{F(X)}, \qquad G(\varepsilon_{Y}) \circ \eta_{G(Y)} = 1_{G(Y)}εF(X)​∘F(ηX​)=1F(X)​,G(εY​)∘ηG(Y)​=1G(Y)​

Conversely, given η:1C  ⟹  G∘F\eta : 1_{\mathcal{C}} \implies G \circ Fη:1C​⟹G∘F and ε:F∘G  ⟹  1D\varepsilon : F \circ G \implies 1_{\mathcal{D}}ε:F∘G⟹1D​ satisfying these two identities, they determine a pair of adjoint functors uniquely.

Notes

In one sentence: the unit "sends oneself in", the counit "brings others back", and the triangle identities ensure the two directions do not conflict.

⚠️ The unit and counit are in general not isomorphisms; this is the biggest difference between an adjunction and a categorical equivalence. They are isomorphisms simultaneously exactly when it is an equivalence.
