Statement

Let U⊆VU \subseteq VU⊆V be a subspace. Define an equivalence relation on VVV:

v∼v′  ⟺  v−v′∈Uv \sim v' \iff v - v' \in Uv∼v′⟺v−v′∈U

The equivalence class v+U:={ v+u:u∈U }v + U := \{\, v + u : u \in U \,\}v+U:={v+u:u∈U} is called a coset of UUU. The set of all cosets is denoted V/UV / UV/U; define on it

(v+U)+(v′+U):=(v+v′)+U,c (v+U):=(cv)+U(v + U) + (v' + U) := (v + v') + U, \qquad c\,(v + U) := (cv) + U(v+U)+(v′+U):=(v+v′)+U,c(v+U):=(cv)+U

These two operations do not depend on the choice of representatives (because UUU is a subspace), so V/UV / UV/U becomes a KKK-vector space, called the quotient space.

The quotient map π:V→V/U\pi : V \to V/Uπ:V→V/U, v↦v+Uv \mapsto v + Uv↦v+U is a surjective linear map, and ker⁡π=U\ker \pi = Ukerπ=U.

Notes

⭐ This is the quotient set 商集与等价类 plus structure: first compress VVV by UUU into a set, then check that the operations are well defined.

The well-definedness step is the whole content — because the operations are written on representatives, and there are infinitely many ways to choose them.

⭐ "Every subspace is the kernel of some linear map" follows: take π\piπ.

This is completely parallel to the group case (every normal subgroup is the kernel of some homomorphism).

⚠ What is quotiented is a subspace (not an arbitrary subset): otherwise the sum of cosets is not closed.

On the group side the counterpart is "normal subgroup"; here every subspace is automatically normal (because KKK-vector spaces are abelian groups).
