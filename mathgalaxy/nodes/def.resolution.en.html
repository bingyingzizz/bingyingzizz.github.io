Statement

Let f:K∙→L∙f : K^{\bullet} \to L^{\bullet}f:K∙→L∙ be a quasi-isomorphism between complexes; then L∙L^{\bullet}L∙ is called a right resolution (also replacement) of K∙K^{\bullet}K∙. If every LnL^{n}Ln is an injective object, it is called an injective resolution.

Reversing the arrows (that is, taking the same definition with values in Aop\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}Aop) gives left resolutions; if every LnL^{n}Ln is a projective object it is called a projective resolution.

Notes

Why "resolve". Derived functors must be computed with computable complexes: on injective objects every additive functor has vanishing higher derived functors, so to compute RnFR^{n}FRnF from an injective resolution one only needs to apply FFF and then take homology — that is the whole point of a "resolution" ✓.

⚠️ The name refers to which side the complex extends toward (a right resolution extends toward higher degrees), not the direction of the arrow: the arrow is always K∙→L∙K^{\bullet} \to L^{\bullet}K∙→L∙. The reversed arrow gives a left resolution.

Existence: with enough injectives every bounded-below complex has an injective resolution (embed the kernels into injective objects step by step) ✓; dually, with enough projectives every bounded-above complex has a projective resolution ✓.

Example: the Z⋅X∙→Z⋅X\mathbb{Z}\cdot X_{\bullet} \to \mathbb{Z}\cdot XZ⋅X∙​→Z⋅X assembled from free abelian sheaves is a projective resolution (see "the free complex given by a cover is acyclic").
