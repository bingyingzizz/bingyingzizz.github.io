Notes

⭐ The rank theorem says "a constant-rank map has only one local shape": every map of constant rank rrr looks the same locally, and the only invariant is the rank rrr.

This is a classification theorem in calculus, and the culmination of the "local →\to→ normal form" method.

⭐ Three corollaries at once:

  • r=n=mr = n = mr=n=m: ψ∘f∘φ−1=id\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1} = \mathrm{id}ψ∘f∘φ−1=id   ⟹  \implies⟹ a local isomorphism, i.e. the inverse mapping theorem;
  • r=n<mr = n < mr=n<m: x↦(x,0)x \mapsto (x, 0)x↦(x,0), the normal form of an immersion;
  • r=m<nr = m < nr=m<n: (x1,…,xn)↦(x1,…,xm)(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \mapsto (x_1, \ldots, x_m)(x1​,…,xn​)↦(x1​,…,xm​), the normal form of a submersion (local projection).

⚠ The hypothesis of constant rank cannot be dropped: f(x)=x2f(x) = x^{2}f(x)=x2 (R→R\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}R→R) has rank 111 at points x≠0x \ne 0x=0 and rank 000 at 000, so its local shape is not a standard form (it is a "fold").
