# a degenerate row of a spectral sequence　`prop.spectral-degenerate-row`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.spectral-degenerate-row)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.spectral-degenerate-row.md)

Dually, if for some $r \ge 2$ we have $q \ne 0 \implies E^{p,q}_{r} = 0$ ($\forall p$), then for every $p$ we have $E^{p,0}_{r} \cong H^{p}$.

## Notes · **draft**
**Proof.** Fix $q$. In the first quadrant the terms with $p < 0$ or $q < 0$ are always zero ✓, so every differential into or out of $E^{0,q}$ has its other endpoint off the line $p = 0$ or outside the first quadrant. Hence

$$E^{0,q}_{r} = E^{0,q}_{r+1} = \cdots = E^{0,q}_{\infty} ✓$$
