Statement

The calculation on a standard cube: Stokes' theorem is first verified on a single standard cube. The left-hand side is a multiple integral, and the right-hand side integrates over 2k2k2k faces. The two sides agree term by term, checking all the sign conventions.

Why this case comes first: The case of a general cube ccc follows by pullback (c∗d=dc∗c^{\ast}\mathrm{d} = \mathrm{d}c^{\ast}c∗d=dc∗), which reduces it to this lemma. This lemma therefore supplies the only substantive calculation in Stokes' theorem for chains. The rest is the passage to general cubes and chains.

⚠️ The most common sign error: When η\etaη is written as ∑j(−1)j−1fjdx1∧⋯dxj^⋯∧dxk\sum_{j}(-1)^{j-1}f_{j}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\widehat{\mathrm{d}x^{j}}\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{k}∑j​(−1)j−1fj​dx1∧⋯dxj⋯∧dxk (a hat indicates that the factor is omitted), there is no additional sign in dη=∑j∂jfjdx1∧⋯∧dxk\mathrm{d}\eta = \sum_{j}\partial_{j}f_{j}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{k}dη=∑j​∂j​fj​dx1∧⋯∧dxk. Inserting an extra (−1)j−1(-1)^{j-1}(−1)j−1 here throws off the matching of the face terms on the right-hand side.

⚠️ Measure-zero sets on the boundary: The form η\etaη need only be defined on an open set and continuous up to the boundary. The facewise pullbacks use the values of η\etaη on each face, and ∂Ik\partial I^{k}∂Ik has measure zero, so the integrals are independent of the choice of extension.

Relation to the classical formulas: When k=2k = 2k=2, this is Green's theorem. When k=3k = 3k=3, it is the Kelvin–Stokes theorem in circulation form. Both are special cases of this lemma.
