Statement

What this does: The system Ax=bAx = bAx=b may have no solution (bbb lies outside the column space). Least squares instead finds an xxx for which AxAxAx is closest to bbb.

Suppose A∈Mm×n(R)A \in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{R})A∈Mm×n​(R) has full column rank and let b∈Rmb \in \mathbb{R}^{m}b∈Rm. The least-squares solution is the minimizer of

∥Ax−b∥2\lVert Ax - b\rVert_{2}∥Ax−b∥2​

with respect to xxx. It is uniquely determined by the normal equations:

ATA x=ATb.A^{T}A\, x = A^{T} b .ATAx=ATb.

With full column rank, ATAA^{T}AATA is positive definite and hence invertible, so the solution is x=(ATA)−1ATbx = (A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}bx=(ATA)−1ATb.

Geometric meaning: Ax∗Ax^{*}Ax∗ is the orthogonal projection of bbb onto the column space of AAA, and the residual b−Ax∗b - Ax^{*}b−Ax∗ is orthogonal to that space.

Notes

The normal equations express orthogonality of the residual to the column space: (b−Ax)⊥col⁡(A)(b - Ax) \perp \operatorname{col}(A)(b−Ax)⊥col(A)   ⟺  \iff⟺ AT(b−Ax)=0A^{T}(b - Ax) = 0AT(b−Ax)=0   ⟺  \iff⟺ ATAx=ATbA^{T}Ax = A^{T}bATAx=ATb. This is the defining property of the projection (orthogonal projection).

⭐ A concrete formula for orthogonal projection: The matrix form of projection onto the subspace is P=A(ATA)−1ATP = A(A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}P=A(ATA)−1AT. This PPP is idempotent and symmetric (the ‘orthogonal idempotent’ in the notes to Idempotent Operator), so it is precisely the projection operator.

⚠️ Avoid explicitly inverting ATAA^{T}AATA (the formula is mathematically valid, but the condition number is squared). In practice, use QR or SVD: x=R−1QTbx = R^{-1}Q^{T}bx=R−1QTb (see the notes to QR Decomposition). Only the mathematical result is given here.

⚠️ Without full column rank, the least-squares solution is not unique (infinitely many solutions give the same residual). The pseudoinverse selects the one of minimum norm (Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse).
