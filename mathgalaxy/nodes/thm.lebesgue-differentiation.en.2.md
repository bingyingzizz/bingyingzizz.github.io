This is also the most intuitive respect in which the Lebesgue integral beats the Riemann integral: a Riemann integrable function is continuous almost everywhere, while here not even continuity is needed.

The three steps of the proof (expanded in the next arrow): first approximate $\to$ by continuous functions, then use the maximal theorem to squeeze the "bad set" down to measure zero.
