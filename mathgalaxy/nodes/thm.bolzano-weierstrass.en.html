Statement

Every bounded sequence of real numbers has a convergent subsequence. Equivalently: every bounded infinite subset of R\mathbb{R}R has a limit point.

Applying this coordinatewise in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn gives: every bounded sequence in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn has a convergent subsequence.

Notes

⭐ This is the analytic core of "compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ bounded and closed in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn" (Heine–Borel): the reason a continuous function on a bounded closed set attains its extreme values ultimately lies here.

⚠ Completeness is indispensable here: in Q\mathbb{Q}Q, the sequence 1,1.4,1.41,1.414,…1, 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, \ldots1,1.4,1.41,1.414,… is bounded but has no subsequence converging to a rational number.

⭐ The standard proof is bisection: halve the interval each time, keep the half containing infinitely many terms, and apply the nested-interval theorem.
