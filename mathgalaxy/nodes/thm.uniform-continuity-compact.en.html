Statement

Let XXX be a compact metric space and f:X→Rf : X \to \mathbb{R}f:X→R continuous. Then fff is uniformly continuous on XXX.

In particular: a continuous function on a closed interval [a,b][a, b][a,b] is uniformly continuous.

Notes

⭐ The only price for upgrading "continuous" to "uniformly continuous" is compactness — it lets δ\deltaδ be chosen "all at once" instead of "point by point", and this is the template for every elementary proof that "takes a finite subcover and then the minimum δ\deltaδ".

⚠ Replacing the closed interval by an open one fails immediately: f(x)=1/xf(x) = 1/xf(x)=1/x is continuous on (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1) but not uniformly continuous — the closer to 000 the smaller the δ\deltaδ needed, and 000 is not in the domain, so there is nowhere to put that lower bound.

⭐ Direct uses: Riemann integrability (oscillation sums over partitions can be made uniformly small), uniform convergence of function sequences, and integrals depending on a parameter.
