When it is an isomorphism, $f$ is called **strict**.

## Notes · **draft**
In $\mathbf{Set}$, $\mathbf{Ab}$, $\mathbf{Top}$ and similar categories $\operatorname{coim} f \to \operatorname{im} f$ is always an isomorphism, so one need not distinguish the two; in a general category this fails.

Compared with $\mathbf{Set}$, where "the image is $\{f(x)\}$", what is added here is **universality**: the image is no longer a set of points but "the smallest intermediate object through which everything factors". Looking at subobjects, monomorphisms, and images together is a sample of "replacing the language of elements by the language of arrows".
