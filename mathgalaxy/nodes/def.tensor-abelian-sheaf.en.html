Statement

Let T\mathcal{T}T be a topos and M,N∈T(Ab)M, N \in \mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Ab})M,N∈T(Ab). The functor

P⟼Hom⁡Z(M, Hom⁡Z(N,P))P \longmapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(M,\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)\bigr)P⟼HomZ​(M, HomZ​(N,P))

is representable; its representing object is written M⊗ZNM \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} NM⊗Z​N. Thus

Hom⁡Z(M⊗ZN, P)  ≅  Hom⁡Z(M, Hom⁡Z(N,P))\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} N,\ P\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(M,\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)\bigr)HomZ​(M⊗Z​N, P)≅HomZ​(M, HomZ​(N,P))

in other words T(Ab)\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{Ab})T(Ab) is a closed symmetric monoidal category: for fixed NNN, M↦M⊗ZNM \mapsto M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} NM↦M⊗Z​N is left adjoint to P↦Hom⁡Z(N,P)P \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(N, P)P↦HomZ​(N,P).

Notes

How to compute. M⊗ZNM \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} NM⊗Z​N is the sheafification of the presheaf X↦M(X)⊗ZN(X)X \mapsto M(X) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} N(X)X↦M(X)⊗Z​N(X): first tensor pointwise, then sheafify.

(For the proof it suffices to consider the case T=C^\mathcal{T} = \widehat{\mathcal{C}}T=C and then sheafify;

finally reduce to the same-named statement for ordinary abelian groups.)

Basic properties. Commutative (M⊗ZN≅N⊗ZMM \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} N \cong N \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} MM⊗Z​N≅N⊗Z​M), associative, unit M⊗ZZ⋅1≅MM \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}\cdot\mathbf{1} \cong MM⊗Z​Z⋅1≅M; and

Z⋅X  ⊗Z  Z⋅Y  ≅  Z⋅(X×Y)\mathbb{Z}\cdot X \;\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\; \mathbb{Z}\cdot Y \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}\cdot (X \times Y)Z⋅X⊗Z​Z⋅Y≅Z⋅(X×Y)

Writing M⋅X:=M⊗ZZ⋅XM \cdot X := M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}\cdot XM⋅X:=M⊗Z​Z⋅X, one has Hom⁡Z(M,N)(X)≅Hom⁡Z(M⋅X, N)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N)(X) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M \cdot X,\ N)HomZ​(M,N)(X)≅HomZ​(M⋅X, N) — "evaluating at a point" = "tensoring at the point, then taking Hom".

⭐ Condensed abelian groups CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb are exactly the special case here (T=Cond\mathcal{T} = \mathrm{Cond}T=Cond): every item above carries over verbatim, with no need to write it out separately. In particular CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb is a closed symmetric monoidal category, hence enriched over itself, and one can speak of tensor products, duality, and commutative algebras.

⚠️ But note that Hom⁡Z(M,N)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N)HomZ​(M,N) is naturally a compactly generated topological space, yet not a topological abelian group — because compactly generated spaces are not closed under products (of topological spaces). Treating CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb as a "topological abelian group" goes wrong at this step.
