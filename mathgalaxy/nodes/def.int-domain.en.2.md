## Notes · **draft**
An integral domain is the smallest setting in which divisibility and cancellation make sense: $\mathbb{Z}$, $K[x]$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$ are all integral domains; $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ ($n$ composite) and $M_{n}(K)$ ($n \ge 2$) are not.

It is also where divisibility lands: $a \mid b$ has decent cancellation only in the absence of zero divisors — otherwise $2 \cdot 3 \equiv 0 \pmod 6$ wrecks divisibility and order completely.
