Statement

The limit of the parallel pair f,g:X⇉Yf, g : X \rightrightarrows Yf,g:X⇉Y is called the equaliser**, written eq⁡(f,g)=ker⁡(f,g)\operatorname{eq}(f,g) = \ker(f,g)eq(f,g)=ker(f,g); the colimit is the coequaliser**, written coker⁡(f,g)\operatorname{coker}(f,g)coker(f,g). They fit into

ker⁡(f,g)⟶X⇉gfY⟶coker⁡(f,g)\ker(f,g) \longrightarrow X \overset{f}{\underset{g}{\rightrightarrows}} Y \longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}(f,g)ker(f,g)⟶Xg⇉​f​Y⟶coker(f,g)

and the middle map ker⁡(f,g)→X\ker(f,g) \to Xker(f,g)→X is a monomorphism, while the map on the right Y→coker⁡(f,g)Y \to \operatorname{coker}(f,g)Y→coker(f,g) is an epimorphism. This segment is called **left exact**.

Notes

Intuition: the equaliser is the part of XXX “sent to the same place by fff and ggg”; the coequaliser is the quotient of YYY that “glues f(x)f(x)f(x) and g(x)g(x)g(x) together”.

Example: in Set\mathbf{Set}Set, ker⁡(f,g)={x∈X:f(x)=g(x)}\ker(f,g) = \{x \in X : f(x) = g(x)\}ker(f,g)={x∈X:f(x)=g(x)}, and coker⁡(f,g)=Y/ ⁣∼\operatorname{coker}(f,g) = Y/\!\simcoker(f,g)=Y/∼ (∼\sim∼ generated by f(x)∼g(x)f(x) \sim g(x)f(x)∼g(x)).

In Top\mathbf{Top}Top one adds the subspace topology / the quotient topology.

In Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab, ker⁡(f,g)=ker⁡(f−g)\ker(f,g) = \ker(f - g)ker(f,g)=ker(f−g) — the equaliser of two maps degenerates to the kernel of **one** map, which is exactly where the name “kernel” comes from.
