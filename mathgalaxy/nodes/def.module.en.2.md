An abelian-group homomorphism preserving this action is called a **$A$-module homomorphism**: $f(am) = a f(m)$. All $A$-modules with module homomorphisms form the category $A\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **A module = an abelian group + a ring action**, so it is a child of both "group" and "ring": a $\mathbb{Z}$-module **is** an abelian group ($n \cdot m$ admits only one definition); a module over a field **is** a vector space. These two statements place "module" midway between known examples.
