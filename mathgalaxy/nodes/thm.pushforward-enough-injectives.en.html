Statement

Let XXX be a topological space. Then the abelian sheaf category Ab(X)\mathbf{Ab}(X)Ab(X) has enough injectives: for each F∈Ab(X)\mathcal{F} \in \mathbf{Ab}(X)F∈Ab(X), embed every stalk into its injective hull in Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab, namely Fx↣Ix\mathcal{F}_{x} \rightarrowtail I_{x}Fx​↣Ix​; then the natural map

F⟶∏x∈Xix,∗ Ix\mathcal{F} \longrightarrow \prod_{x \in X} i_{x,*}\, I_{x}F⟶x∈X∏​ix,∗​Ix​

is injective, and its target is an injective sheaf. Consequently:

  1. Every sheaf has an injective resolution, and one can take it bounded below;
  2. The right derived functors of the section functor Γ(X,−)\Gamma(X, -)Γ(X,−), and of the pushforward along any continuous map fff — that is, f∗f_{*}f∗​ — namely RqΓR^{q}\GammaRqΓ and Rqf∗R^{q}f_{*}Rqf∗​, are defined on bounded-below complexes and are independent of the choice of injective resolution;
  3. f∗f_{*}f∗​ sends injective sheaves to injective sheaves (f−1f^{-1}f−1 is exact and f−1⊣f∗f^{-1} \dashv f_{*}f−1⊣f∗​), so the injectives "on the f∗f_{*}f∗​ side" also suffice to compute Rf∗Rf_{*}Rf∗​.

Proof: injectivity. A map of sheaves is injective   ⟺  \iff⟺ it is injective on sections over every open set.

Take UUU open and s∈F(U)s \in \mathcal{F}(U)s∈F(U); its image in the xxx-th component (when x∈Ux \in Ux∈U) is the stalk of sss, namely sx∈Fx↣Ixs_{x} \in \mathcal{F}_{x} \rightarrowtail I_{x}sx​∈Fx​↣Ix​ (when x∉Ux \notin Ux∈/U the component lands in 000).

Hence F→∏xix,∗Ix\mathcal{F} \to \prod_{x} i_{x,*}I_{x}F→∏x​ix,∗​Ix​ is injective.

∎

Proof: the target is injective. We need Hom⁡Ab(X)(−,∏xix,∗Ix)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}(X)}(-, \prod_{x} i_{x,*}I_{x})HomAb(X)​(−,∏x​ix,∗​Ix​) to be exact.

Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom and the direct product both preserve limits in the second variable, so it suffices, for each xxx, to check ix,∗Ixi_{x,*}I_{x}ix,∗​Ix​: by adjunction and by "ix−1=i_{x}^{-1} = ix−1​= takes stalks",

Hom⁡Ab(X)(F, ix,∗Ix)  ≅  Hom⁡Ab(Fx, Ix),\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}(X)}\bigl(\mathcal{F},\ i_{x,*}I_{x}\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{x},\ I_{x}\bigr),HomAb(X)​(F, ix,∗​Ix​)≅HomAb​(Fx​, Ix​),

and F↦Fx\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}_{x}F↦Fx​ is exact while IxI_{x}Ix​ is injective, so the right-hand side is exact in F\mathcal{F}F — each ix,∗Ixi_{x,*}I_{x}ix,∗​Ix​ is injective, and a direct product of injectives is injective (the pointwise argument carries over to the product verbatim). ∎

⭐ Why this makes the definition of sheaf cohomology legitimate: defining Hn(X,F):=RnΓ(X,F)H^{n}(X, \mathcal{F}) := R^{n}\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})Hn(X,F):=RnΓ(X,F) presupposes an injective resolution.

Without enough injectives, resolutions do not exist at all, and RnΓR^{n}\GammaRnΓ cannot be defined.

With the two facts above, every F\mathcal{F}F has an injective resolution;

then by the comparison theorem (maps between two injective resolutions are unique up to homotopy), HnH^{n}Hn is independent of the choice of resolution — sheaf cohomology is only now a well-defined functor.

The same statement holds for Rnf∗R^{n}f_{*}Rnf∗​.

⭐ The shape of the argument is worth remembering: it uses only two things, "stalks" and "direct products", so it holds for any site with "enough points": in the abelian sheaf categories of topological spaces and of topoi, injectivity can be checked pointwise. This is the most naive source of "sheaf cohomology can be read off stalk by stalk".

⚠️ Choosing IxI_{x}Ix​ uses that Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab has enough injectives (products of Q/Z\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}Q/Z give injective hulls) — that is the Baer criterion for Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab, not redone here.
