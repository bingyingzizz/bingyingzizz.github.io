prop.site-properties

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

If C\mathcal{C}C is a site, then all limits and colimits exist in the sheaf category C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}C, and:

  1. colimits are universal;
  2. filtered colimits are exact;
  3. epimorphisms are regular and universal;
  4. coproducts are disjoint;
  5. equivalence relations are effective and universal.

Notes

One-line reason: these properties hold in Set\mathbf{Set}Set, and constructions in a sheaf category inherit the properties of Set\mathbf{Set}Set pointwise, together with sheafification preserving finite limits and colimits. Item 3 is especially worth remembering: here "epi = regular epi" is automatic, with no extra hypothesis — this is the source of that axiom in the definition of a pretopos below.

The five items are not parallel axioms but properties a sheaf category has automatically (only item 2 needs an extra condition):

  • ① Universal colimits: automatic in a sheaf category (sheaves are limits along covers, and limits commute with pullback). No condition on the site is needed, only that C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}C exists.
  • ② Filtered colimits are exact: this needs "sheafification commutes with filtered colimits" — true for a general Grothendieck topology; for finite filtered colimits (i.e. over Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab) the site must satisfy an extra condition (for instance "quasi-compact + quasi-separated").
  • ③ Epimorphisms are regular and universal: a sheaf category is a regular category, so epi = regular epi; universality comes from pullback stability as in ①.
  • ④ Coproducts are disjoint: from "a sheaf decomposes along a disjoint union" (F(∐Xi)≃∏F(Xi)F(\coprod X_{i}) \simeq \prod F(X_{i})F(∐Xi​)≃∏F(Xi​)), as long as the site has disjoint unions.
  • ⑤ Equivalence relations are effective and universal: a sheaf category is an exact category — by ③ plus effective equivalence relations.

⚠️ This is the "reverse direction" of Giraud's theorem: Giraud says "a topos   ⟺  \iff⟺ is a category satisfying these five items plus a small generator and arbitrary colimits"; this section is exactly the half "a sheaf category   ⟹  \implies⟹ satisfies the five". ⚠️ Do not treat ② as unconditional: finite filtered colimits and arbitrary filtered colimits behave differently on a general site.

Hypotheses used in each direction (these are theorems for set-valued sheaf categories, not five equivalent definitions):

  • The five properties of the sheaf category   ⟹  \implies⟹: one must use a small site, or a site small enough in a fixed universe, to guarantee the relevant sheafifications and small colimits exist.
  • Presheaf limits are computed pointwise for free; sheaf colimits must first take the presheaf colimit and then sheafify, at the cost that sheafification is a left exact left adjoint, so one cannot say sheaf colimits are all computed pointwise.
  • Filtered colimits are exact: use that filtered colimits in sets commute with finite limits and that sheafification is left exact; no extra quasi-compact or quasi-separated hypothesis is needed, and the remark above about an extra condition there should be corrected.
  • Disjoint coproducts, regular epimorphisms, and effective equivalence relations need the topos theorems; one does not require the underlying site itself to have disjoint coproducts.
  • That the five properties   ⟹  \implies⟹ come from some site is not free; the Giraud characterization also needs a small generating family and so on; this node does not give the complete reverse hypotheses, so one cannot infer backwards from the five alone.
