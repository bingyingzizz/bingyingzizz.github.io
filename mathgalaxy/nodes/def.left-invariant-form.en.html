Statement

In words: a differential form on a Lie group that is "untouched" by left translations is completely determined by a multilinear form at the identity — hence on GGG the left-invariant kkk-forms coincide with ⋀kg∗\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}⋀kg∗.

Let GGG be an nnn-dimensional Lie group, g=TeG\mathfrak{g} = T_{e}Gg=Te​G, and LgL_{g}Lg​ the left translation. A kkk-form ω∈Ωk(G)\omega \in \Omega^{k}(G)ω∈Ωk(G) is left-invariant if

Lg∗ω=ω(g∈G),L_{g}^{\ast}\omega = \omega\qquad (g \in G),Lg∗​ω=ω(g∈G),

equivalently ωg(v1,…,vk)=ωe(d(Lg−1)gv1,…,d(Lg−1)gvk)\omega_{g}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}) = \omega_{e}\bigl(\mathrm{d}(L_{g^{-1}})_{g}v_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{d}(L_{g^{-1}})_{g}v_{k}\bigr)ωg​(v1​,…,vk​)=ωe​(d(Lg−1​)g​v1​,…,d(Lg−1​)g​vk​) — that is, the values of ω\omegaω on GGG are uniquely determined by ωe\omega_{e}ωe​.

Thus the evaluation

ω⟼ωe\omega \longmapsto \omega_{e}ω⟼ωe​

is a linear isomorphism from the space of left-invariant kkk-forms onto ⋀kg∗\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}⋀kg∗, of dimension (nk)\binom{n}{k}(kn​).

Left-invariant coframe: take a basis of g\mathfrak{g}g, say e1,…,ene_{1},\ldots,e_{n}e1​,…,en​, and the dual basis ωe1,…,ωen\omega^{1}_{e},\ldots,\omega^{n}_{e}ωe1​,…,ωen​ (ωei(ej)=δ ji\omega^{i}_{e}(e_{j}) = \delta^{i}_{\ j}ωei​(ej​)=δ ji​), and translate them to the left:

ωgi:=d(Lg−1)e∗ ωei,ωgi((e~j)g)=δ ji,\omega^{i}_{g} := \mathrm{d}(L_{g^{-1}})^{\ast}_{e}\,\omega^{i}_{e},\qquad \omega^{i}_{g}\bigl((\tilde e_{j})_{g}\bigr) = \delta^{i}_{\ j},ωgi​:=d(Lg−1​)e∗​ωei​,ωgi​((e~j​)g​)=δ ji​,

where e~j\tilde e_{j}e~j​ is the left-invariant vector field corresponding to eje_{j}ej​. This family ω1,…,ωn\omega^{1},\ldots,\omega^{n}ω1,…,ωn is called a left-invariant coframe; the structure constants are defined by [ej,ek]=∑ic jkiei[e_{j},e_{k}] = \sum_{i}c^{i}_{\ jk}e_{i}[ej​,ek​]=∑i​c jki​ei​.

Notes

Why the evaluation is an isomorphism: setting h=eh = eh=e in left-invariance gives ωg(d(Lg)ew)=ωe(w)\omega_{g}(\mathrm{d}(L_{g})_{e}w) = \omega_{e}(w)ωg​(d(Lg​)e​w)=ωe​(w), hence ωg=d(Lg−1)∗ωe\omega_{g} = \mathrm{d}(L_{g^{-1}})^{\ast}\omega_{e}ωg​=d(Lg−1​)∗ωe​, i.e. ω\omegaω is determined by ωe\omega_{e}ωe​ (injectivity).

Conversely: given α∈⋀kg∗\alpha \in \bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}α∈⋀kg∗, put ωg:=d(Lg−1)∗α\omega_{g} := \mathrm{d}(L_{g^{-1}})^{\ast}\alphaωg​:=d(Lg−1​)∗α; then Lh∗ω=ωL_{h}^{\ast}\omega = \omegaLh∗​ω=ω, because

L(hg)−1=Lg−1∘Lh−1,Lh−1∘Lh=id⁡,L_{(hg)^{-1}} = L_{g^{-1}}\circ L_{h^{-1}},\qquad L_{h^{-1}}\circ L_{h} = \operatorname{id},L(hg)−1​=Lg−1​∘Lh−1​,Lh−1​∘Lh​=id,

the two left translations cancel d(Lh)\mathrm{d}(L_{h})d(Lh​) and restore it. Hence ω↦ωe\omega \mapsto \omega_{e}ω↦ωe​ is a bijection, and linearity is clear.

Dual to left-invariant vector fields: 左不变向量场 says left-invariant vector fields are isomorphic to g\mathfrak{g}g; here left-invariant kkk-forms are isomorphic to ⋀kg∗\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}⋀kg∗.

For k=1k = 1k=1 the two are dual bases — this is the version on a group of the method of moving frames (活动标架): the frame itself does not move, only the point does.

Orientability: for k=nk = nk=n, ⋀ng∗\bigwedge^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}⋀ng∗ is one-dimensional; translating a nonzero element to the left gives an everywhere nonzero nnn-form, so Lie 群可定向 (a left-invariant top form is everywhere nonzero) shows every Lie group is orientable (S1S^{1}S1, SO⁡(3)\operatorname{SO}(3)SO(3) and GL⁡(n,R)\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})GL(n,R) are, while a general manifold need not be).

The Haar-measure half: ω1∧⋯∧ωn\omega^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega^{n}ω1∧⋯∧ωn is a left-invariant volume form, and its absolute value gives a left-invariant measure (Haar measure). On a compact group left- and right-invariant measures differ by a constant (the modular function); in the noncompact case they need not — here only the form side is used.

⚠ Left-invariant forms and left-invariant vector fields are different families of objects, both indexed by ggg; do not confuse them: the component matrix of the former is the inverse transpose of the Jacobian, that of the latter is the Jacobian itself.
