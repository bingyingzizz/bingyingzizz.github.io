## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Both items are **consequences of completeness (the supremum principle)**; no further assumption is needed.

Their uses are extensive:

- Proving the **uniqueness of the complete ordered field** (well-definedness of $\mathbb{R}$) relies on (ii) — the rationals are dense on both sides, which is what allows approximation of arbitrary elements by "images of rationals";

- Every "take $n$ large enough" and "take $\varepsilon = 1/n$" in analysis is really an application of (i);
