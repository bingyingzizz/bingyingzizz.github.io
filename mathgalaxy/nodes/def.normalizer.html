Statement draft

Let GGG be a group and S⊆GS \subseteq GS⊆G. The set

NG(S):={ g∈G:gSg−1=S }≤GN_G(S) := \{\, g \in G : g S g^{-1} = S \,\} \le GNG​(S):={g∈G:gSg−1=S}≤G

is the normalizer of SSS in GGG. For a subgroup H≤GH \le GH≤G, NG(H)N_G(H)NG​(H) is the largest subgroup of GGG containing HHH in which HHH is normal.

If H≤GH \le GH≤G, then H⊴G  ⟺  NG(H)=GH \trianglelefteq G \iff N_G(H) = GH⊴G⟺NG​(H)=G; also H⊆NG(H)H \subseteq N_G(H)H⊆NG​(H) and H⊴NG(H)H \trianglelefteq N_G(H)H⊴NG​(H). Sylow counting: np=[G:NG(P)]n_p = [G : N_G(P)]np​=[G:NG​(P)].

Notes draft

⭐ Normalizer vs centralizer: CG(H)⊆NG(H)C_G(H) \subseteq N_G(H)CG​(H)⊆NG​(H), and in general they are not equal. NG(H)/CG(H)N_G(H)/C_G(H)NG​(H)/CG​(H) embeds into Aut⁡(H)\operatorname{Aut}(H)Aut(H) (by conjugation); this is the standard "normalizer modulo centralizer" statement.

⭐ Examples: in S3S_3S3​, NS3(⟨(12)⟩)=⟨(12)⟩N_{S_3}(\langle(12)\rangle) = \langle(12)\rangleNS3​​(⟨(12)⟩)=⟨(12)⟩ (the three transpositions are conjugate to one another, so the normalizer does not grow); NS3(A3)=S3N_{S_3}(A_3) = S_3NS3​​(A3​)=S3​.
