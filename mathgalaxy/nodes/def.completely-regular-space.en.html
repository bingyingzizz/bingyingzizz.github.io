Statement

What this condition is: T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ together with T1T_{1}T1​. This class is the standard setting for "there are enough continuous functions", and the default hypothesis in compactification and metrization theory.

A topological space XXX is a completely regular space if

  1. XXX is T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​: a point and a closed set not containing it can be separated by a continuous function (T3.5 空间);
  2. XXX is T1T_{1}T1​.

A space satisfying both is also called a Tychonoff space.

Equivalent form: XXX embeds in some cube [0,1]I[0,1]^{I}[0,1]I (that is, "there are enough continuous functions to separate points from closed sets").

Position in the chain: normal space   ⟹  \implies⟹ completely regular space   ⟹  \implies⟹ regular space   ⟹  T2\implies T_{2}⟹T2​. The only non-trivial step is "normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ completely regular" (Urysohn's lemma, Урысон 引理).

Notes

⚠️ Naming convention: the T-number denotes the bare property, and the named property denotes the bare property plus T1T_{1}T1​.

Thus T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ means that a point and a closed set not containing it can be separated by a continuous function (T3.5 空间, without T1T_{1}T1​), while complete regularity is T3.5+T1T_{3.5} + T_{1}T3.5​+T1​.

This is the same convention as "T3T_{3}T3​ versus regular space".

⭐ Why adding T1T_{1}T1​ is not redundant: without T1T_{1}T1​, T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ does not even guarantee that points are closed, so T2T_{2}T2​ and uniqueness of limits all fail. Once T1T_{1}T1​ is added, {y}\{y\}{y} is closed, and a continuous function separating xxx from {y}\{y\}{y} immediately gives disjoint open sets — hence a completely regular space is automatically T2T_{2}T2​.

⭐ Why "embeds in a cube" is the same statement: if XXX is completely regular, assemble all continuous f:X→[0,1]f : X \to [0,1]f:X→[0,1] into a map X→[0,1]IX \to [0,1]^{I}X→[0,1]I; T1T_{1}T1​ (points closed) together with T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ (points and closed sets separable by functions) is exactly what makes it an embedding. Conversely, an embedding into a cube obviously supplies enough functions. This is the other half of Tychonoff's theorem.

⚠️ Name clash: Tychonoff 定理 is the product compactness theorem (an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact) — same person, different contribution. Do not confuse the two.

The hypotheses used in each direction (the named properties include T1T_1T1​, and the cube characterisation is the embedding theorem):

  • completely regular   ⟺  \iff⟺ T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ and T1T_1T1​: this is a definitional unfolding of the notion used here, free of charge; it does not derive T1T_1T1​ from bare T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​.
  • completely regular   ⟹  \implies⟹ embedding in [0,1]I[0,1]^I[0,1]I: take the evaluation map of all continuous interval-valued functions; T1T_1T1​ (points closed) together with point–closed separation gives injectivity, and point–closed separation recovers the induced topology. The product topology is used, not the product compactness theorem.
  • embedding in a cube   ⟹  \implies⟹ completely regular: from coordinate separation, basic neighbourhoods in finitely many coordinates, and the hereditary nature of complete regularity for subspaces; a continuous injection alone is not enough.
  • T1T_1T1​ cannot be dropped: the indiscrete topology on at least two points satisfies bare T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​, but does not embed in a Hausdorff cube; and normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ completely regular needs Urysohn's lemma.
