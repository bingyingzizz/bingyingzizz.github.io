Statement

A graph is a quadruple

(V,E,s,t)( V, E, s, t )(V,E,s,t)

where VVV and EEE are sets and s,t:E→Vs, t : E \to Vs,t:E→V are functions — called the source map and the target map. Elements of VVV are vertices and elements of EEE are edges.

Notes

"Graph" here means a directed multigraph: s(e)≠t(e)s(e) \ne t(e)s(e)=t(e) is not required (loops are allowed), and two edges are not required to have different endpoints (parallel edges are allowed). So it is broader than the everyday picture of a graph — it is just the data of which edge goes from where to where.

A quadruple is a nested ordered pair, so the encoding is not immaterial. (x,y):={{x},{x,y}}(x, y) := \{ \{x\}, \{x,y\} \}(x,y):={{x},{x,y}} satisfies the ordered-pair axiom

(a,b)=(c,d)  ⟺  a=c∧b=d(a, b) = (c, d) \iff a = c \wedge b = d(a,b)=(c,d)⟺a=c∧b=d

whereas with {x,{x,y}}\{ x, \{x,y\} \}{x,{x,y}} one must verify that axiom case by case, not take it for granted.
