Statement

In plain terms: integral curves of a vector field always exist and are unique locally, and the family of solutions depends smoothly on the starting point and on time — that is, a local flow always exists.

Let X∈X(M)X \in \mathfrak{X}(M)X∈X(M) and p∈Mp \in Mp∈M. Then there exist an open neighbourhood UUU of ppp, some ε>0\varepsilon > 0ε>0, and a smooth map φ:(−ε,ε)×U→M\varphi : (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times U \to Mφ:(−ε,ε)×U→M such that

  1. φ0=id⁡U\varphi_{0} = \operatorname{id}_{U}φ0​=idU​;
  2. ∂∂tφt(q)=X(φt(q))\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_{t}(q) = X\bigl(\varphi_{t}(q)\bigr)∂t∂​φt​(q)=X(φt​(q)) for all t∈(−ε,ε)t \in (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)t∈(−ε,ε) and q∈Uq \in Uq∈U;
  3. Uniqueness: two integral curves through the same point agree on their common interval of definition;
  4. Local one-parameter group: whenever ∣s∣,∣t∣,∣s+t∣<ε|s|, |t|, |s+t| < \varepsilon∣s∣,∣t∣,∣s+t∣<ε and all intermediate points lie in UUU, φs+t=φs∘φt\varphi_{s+t} = \varphi_{s}\circ\varphi_{t}φs+t​=φs​∘φt​.

Moreover, gluing the local flows of the various points along curves yields a unique maximal flow (its domain W⊆R×M\mathcal{W} \subseteq \mathbb{R}\times MW⊆R×M is open; see 流).

Notes

This is a pure ODE theorem: in a chart (V,x)(V,x)(V,x), X=∑iXi∂iX = \sum_{i}X^{i}\partial_{i}X=∑i​Xi∂i​, the equation becomes the autonomous system

y˙i(t)=Xi(y(t))(i=1,…,n),\dot y^{i}(t) = X^{i}\bigl(y(t)\bigr)\qquad (i = 1,\ldots,n),y˙​i(t)=Xi(y(t))(i=1,…,n),

whose right-hand side is smooth (XiX^{i}Xi is smooth), so Picard–Lindelöf gives local existence and uniqueness, and smooth dependence of solutions on initial data (C∞C^{\infty}C∞) gives the smoothness of φ\varphiφ. The manifold enters in exactly one place: do it in a chart.

"Autonomous" is the source of the one-parameter group property: solutions of a non-autonomous equation y˙=f(t,y)\dot y = f(t,y)y˙​=f(t,y) do not form a group (going first for sss and then for ttt differs from going directly for s+ts+ts+t, because the "environment" changes). The flow of a vector field is autonomous, hence invariant under time translation — this is φs∘φt=φs+t\varphi_{s}\circ\varphi_{t} = \varphi_{s+t}φs​∘φt​=φs+t​.

Why the maximal flow is unique: two maximal flows have the same initial-value problem at an intersection point, so by uniqueness they agree locally; gluing all local solutions along curves gives the one with maximal domain. The domain W\mathcal{W}W is open: near each point there is a time interval supplied by existence, and this time can be chosen lower semicontinuous in ppp.

The cost: this map does not prove Picard–Lindelöf (a theorem from ODE courses); it only performs the reduction "turn a flow on a manifold into an autonomous system on Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn". A similar trade-off appears in 测地线的局部存在唯一性 (existence and uniqueness of geodesics are treated the same way).

⚠ Uniqueness means "uniqueness as the same curve": reparametrizing gives a reparametrization of the same curve, not a second solution. φt(q)\varphi_{t}(q)φt​(q) already fixes the parameter as "the time of the flow", so there is no ambiguity here.
