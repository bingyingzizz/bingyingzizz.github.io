Statement

  1. A Grothendieck category automatically satisfies AB3* (has all limits) — that is, AB5 together with a generator gives AB3* for free;
  2. apart from the zero category {0}\{0\}{0}, no category satisfies both AB5 and AB5*;
  3. Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) on a topos satisfies AB5 and AB3*, but in general does not satisfy AB6 or AB4*.

Notes

Why item 1 deserves separate mention: AB3 and AB3* are dual (one has all colimits, the other all limits), and in general neither implies the other. Together, "has a generator + filtered colimits are exact" brings along the dual statement — this is the most nontrivial "free lunch" in the theory of Grothendieck categories.

The meaning of item 2: AB5 (filtered colimits are exact) and AB5* (filtered limits are exact) are mutually exclusive; only one side can hold. The zero category is the only exception (it has nothing, so both hold).

Item 3 is the answer to "why condensed mathematics uses CondAb rather than an arbitrary topos": Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) has only AB5 and AB3*, which suffices for general homological algebra;

but CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb additionally satisfies AB6 and AB4* — that is, "filtered colimits commute with products" and "products are exact".

Only with these two can one freely interchange limits and colimits as in Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab.

The reason for each of the three.

  • Item 1: use Freyd's special adjoint functor theorem.

A cocomplete category with a small cogenerating set and co-well-powered (each object has only a small set of quotients) is complete.

For a Grothendieck category A\mathcal{A}A: cocomplete ✓ (AB5);

co-well-poweredness and a small cogenerating set both come from a generator UUU — a quotient is determined by its "points over UUU", so the quotients of UUU form a set, and their product is the cogenerator.

SAFT then gives that all limits exist ✓.

  • Item 2: this is a classical result of Roos.

Idea: AB5 makes colimits of shape N\mathbb{N}N well behaved, AB5* makes inverse limits of shape N\mathbb{N}N well behaved;

if both held, one would be forced into a Milnor-type exact sequence (comparing ∏\prod∏ and ⨁\bigoplus⨁), which in an abelian category can hold only for the zero object ✓.

  • Item 3: first half — Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) is a sheaf category on a topos, hence a Grothendieck category, so AB5 + AB3* (item 1) ✓;

second half — "AB6 and AB4* fail in general" has counterexamples on a general topos (products of abelian sheaves need not be exact, filtered colimits need not commute with products), so these two cannot be taken for free ✓.

∎

⭐ So the AB6 / AB4* of CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb is an extra gain, coming from the concrete shape of the site CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus (termwise reduction to Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab), not from any topos.
