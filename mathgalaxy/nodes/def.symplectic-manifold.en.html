Statement

What this does: Extends the notion of a symplectic vector space to a manifold. Each tangent space carries a symplectic form, and these forms together must be closed (dω=0d\omega = 0dω=0). This differential condition is the source of rigidity in symplectic geometry.

Let MMM be a smooth manifold (there is no need to assume even dimension in advance, as shown below). A symplectic form on MMM is a 2-form ω∈Ω2(M)\omega \in \Omega^{2}(M)ω∈Ω2(M) satisfying

  1. Closedness: dω=0d\omega = 0dω=0.
  2. Nondegeneracy: At each point ppp, ωp:TpM×TpM→R\omega_{p} : T_{p}M \times T_{p}M \to \mathbb{R}ωp​:Tp​M×Tp​M→R is nondegenerate (the manifold version of Symplectic Form).

We call (M,ω)(M, \omega)(M,ω) a symplectic manifold. Nondegeneracy implies dim⁡M=2n\dim M = 2ndimM=2n (apply Symplectic Normal Form Theorem pointwise).

Standard example: On R2n=T∗Rn\mathbb{R}^{2n} = T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}R2n=T∗Rn (the cotangent bundle), take

ω0=∑i=1ndqi∧dpi,\omega_{0} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dq_{i} \wedge dp_{i} ,ω0​=i=1∑n​dqi​∧dpi​,

This is the phase space of classical mechanics.

Notes

⚠️ Closedness is essential: Nondegenerate 2-forms exist pointwise (the cotangent bundle provides an example), whereas closedness is a condition on the form as a whole. Dropping it gives an almost symplectic manifold, which carries a nondegenerate 2-form with no closedness requirement (a different theory). ⚠ Integrability is a property of an almost complex structure. It requires an additional almost complex structure and plays no role in the definition of an almost symplectic manifold. S6S^{6}S6 is a standard example of an almost symplectic manifold that is not symplectic.

⭐ Why physics needs it: ω\omegaω gives rise to Hamiltonian flows (Hamiltonian Vector Field), and dω=0d\omega = 0dω=0 ensures that these flows preserve ω\omegaω (the Lie derivative LXHω=0\mathcal{L}_{X_{H}}\omega = 0LXH​​ω=0). This is the basis of Liouville's theorem on conservation of phase volume.

⚠️ A symplectic manifold is necessarily orientable (ωn\omega^{n}ωn is a nowhere-vanishing volume form), but need not be Kähler. A Kähler structure also requires a complex structure and a compatible metric. Symplectic geometry is more general than Kähler geometry.

⚠️ Compact symplectic manifolds are subject to a strong restriction: [ω]∈H2(M)[\omega] \in H^{2}(M)[ω]∈H2(M) is nonzero (otherwise ω=dθ\omega=d\thetaω=dθ would make ωn=d(θ∧ωn−1)\omega^{n}=d(\theta\wedge\omega^{n-1})ωn=d(θ∧ωn−1) exact, giving integral zero and contradicting the fact that ωn\omega^{n}ωn is a nowhere-vanishing volume form). This is why a symplectic manifold cannot be S2nS^{2n}S2n, n≥2n \ge 2n≥2. The cohomological argument is omitted here.
