# absolute continuity of functions and of measures　`prop.ac-iff-measure-ac`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.ac-iff-measure-ac)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.ac-iff-measure-ac.md)

Each $U_k$ is a countable disjoint union of intervals; absolute continuity of the function and $m(U_k) < \delta$ give $|\mu_F(U_j)| < \varepsilon$;

then upper continuity of the measure and $\bigcap U_k = E$ give $|\mu_F(E)| \le \varepsilon$.

Since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, $\mu_F(E) = 0$.

∎

⭐ This connects the two notions of "absolute continuity": for functions and for measures. From now on, the calculus notion of an absolutely continuous function has a measure-theoretic interpretation.
