Statement

Let fff be bounded on [a,b][a, b][a,b]. Then fff is integrable if and only if for every ε>0\varepsilon > 0ε>0 there exists a partition PPP such that

U(f,P)−L(f,P)=∑iωi Δxi<εU(f, P) - L(f, P) = \sum_{i} \omega_i\,\Delta x_i < \varepsilonU(f,P)−L(f,P)=i∑​ωi​Δxi​<ε

(ωi\omega_iωi​ is the oscillation of fff on the iii-th subinterval.) Moreover the two definitions (limit of tagged sums / Darboux suprema) give the same value of the integral.

Notes

⭐ Oscillation sums can be made arbitrarily small is the most useful integrability criterion in the subject: every proof that "some function is integrable" reduces to an estimate of oscillation sums.

⭐ Example of use: for continuous functions use uniform continuity to control ωi\omega_iωi​;

for monotonically increasing functions use equal partitions to make ∑ωiΔxi≤(f(b)−f(a))∥P∥\sum \omega_i \Delta x_i \le (f(b) - f(a))\|P\|∑ωi​Δxi​≤(f(b)−f(a))∥P∥ (for decreasing functions take absolute values on the right).

⚠ The criterion requires only that some partition makes the oscillation sum small, not all partitions — because the oscillation sum is monotone nonincreasing under refinement.
