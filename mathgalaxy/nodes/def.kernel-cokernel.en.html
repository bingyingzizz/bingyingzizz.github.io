Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a (pre)additive category and f:M→Nf : M \to Nf:M→N.

  • The kernel of fff, written ker⁡f:=ker⁡(f,0)\ker f := \ker(f, 0)kerf:=ker(f,0), is the equalizer of fff and the zero morphism 0M,N0_{M,N}0M,N​ (if it exists);
  • the cokernel of fff, written coker⁡f\operatorname{coker} fcokerf, is the kernel of fff in Cop\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}Cop (if it exists), i.e. the coequalizer of fff and the zero morphism;
  • the sequence 0→M′→fM→gM′′0 \to M' \xrightarrow{f} M \xrightarrow{g} M''0→M′f​Mg​M′′ is called left exact if fff realizes ker⁡g\ker gkerg (that is, M′≅ker⁡gM' \cong \ker gM′≅kerg);

dually M→gM′′→0M \xrightarrow{g} M'' \to 0Mg​M′′→0 is called right exact if ggg realizes coker⁡g\operatorname{coker} gcokerg;

  • a sequence 0→M′→φM→ψM′′→00 \to M' \xrightarrow{\varphi} M \xrightarrow{\psi} M'' \to 00→M′φ​Mψ​M′′→0 that is both left exact and right exact is called a short exact sequence; then MMM is called an extension of M′M'M′ by M′′M''M′′.

Notes

Why the kernel is an "equalizer" and not a "preimage": the equalizer of fff and 0M,N0_{M,N}0M,N​ is the universal object among "all morphisms killed by fff" — it is far more basic than "f−1(0)f^{-1}(0)f−1(0)", because in a general category there are no elements to take preimages of. (In Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab the two agree: ker⁡f=f−1(0)\ker f = f^{-1}(0)kerf=f−1(0).)

In an additive category kernels and cokernels behave regularly: ker⁡f→M\ker f \to Mkerf→M is always a monomorphism and M→coker⁡fM \to \operatorname{coker} fM→cokerf always an epimorphism (equalizers are monic, coequalizers epic; no abelian-category hypothesis is needed for this).

⚠️ "Every morphism has a kernel and a cokernel" is not enough: in a preabelian category coim⁡f→im⁡f\operatorname{coim} f \to \operatorname{im} fcoimf→imf is in general not an isomorphism, and abelian categories are exactly the condition that fills this gap.
