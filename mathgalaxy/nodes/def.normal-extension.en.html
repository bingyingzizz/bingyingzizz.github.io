def.normal-extension

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

An algebraic extension K/FK/FK/F is normal if every irreducible polynomial in F[x]F[x]F[x] that has one root in KKK splits in KKK (all its roots lie in KKK).

Equivalent form: KKK is the splitting field of some family of polynomials in F[x]F[x]F[x].

Notes

⭐ It measures exactly “is adjoining one root enough”: Q(23)/Q\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}Q(32​)/Q is not normal — x3−2x^{3} - 2x3−2 has a root in it, but ω23\omega\sqrt[3]{2}ω32​ does not. While Q(2)/Q\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}Q(2​)/Q is normal.

⭐ Unrelated to “normal subgroup”: here normal means a normal extension; the group-theoretic normal means a normal subgroup. They do meet in the Galois correspondence (an intermediate field normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ a normal subgroup), but the definitions are completely different, so do not conflate the words.

⚠ Normality is relative to the base field: K/FK/FK/F normal does not imply that K/EK/EK/E is normal over the intermediate field EEE (one needs Galois for both ends to behave).
