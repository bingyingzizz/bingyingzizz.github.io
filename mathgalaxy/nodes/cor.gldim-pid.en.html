Statement

Let RRR be a principal ideal domain. Then every RRR-module MMM has a free resolution of length at most 111, so

pd⁡RM≤1,gl.dim⁡R≤1.\operatorname{pd}_{R} M \le 1 , \qquad \operatorname{gl.dim} R \le 1 .pdR​M≤1,gl.dimR≤1.

(The preceding inequalities hold for every RRR-module MMM.) Furthermore, if RRR is a field, then gl.dim⁡R=0\operatorname{gl.dim} R = 0gl.dimR=0. Otherwise, gl.dim⁡R=1\operatorname{gl.dim} R = 1gl.dimR=1. In particular,

gl.dim⁡Z=1.\operatorname{gl.dim} \mathbb{Z} = 1 .gl.dimZ=1.

Notes

Proof. ① A free resolution of length 222. Choose a generating set III and a surjection π:R(I)↠M\pi : R^{(I)} \twoheadrightarrow Mπ:R(I)↠M with free domain. Its kernel KKK is a submodule of the free module R(I)R^{(I)}R(I), and every submodule of a free module over a principal ideal domain is free ✓. Take a surjection R(J)↠KR^{(J)} \twoheadrightarrow KR(J)↠K and splice it into the sequence to obtain

0→R(J)→R(I)→M→0,0 \to R^{(J)} \to R^{(I)} \to M \to 0 ,0→R(J)→R(I)→M→0,

which is a free, hence projective, resolution of length 111 ✓. Thus pd⁡RM≤1\operatorname{pd}_{R} M \le 1pdR​M≤1 for every MMM ✓. ② The case gl.dim⁡=0\operatorname{gl.dim} = 0gl.dim=0. If RRR is a field, every module is free because every vector space has a basis. Every module is therefore projective, so pd⁡=0\operatorname{pd} = 0pd=0 and gl.dim⁡R=0\operatorname{gl.dim} R = 0gl.dimR=0 ✓. ③ If the ring is not a field, then gl.dim⁡=1\operatorname{gl.dim} = 1gl.dim=1. Choose a nonzero nonunit a∈Ra \in Ra∈R and consider M:=R/(a)M := R/(a)M:=R/(a).

If this module were projective, the short exact sequence 0→(a)→R→R/(a)→00 \to (a) \to R \to R/(a) \to 00→(a)→R→R/(a)→0 would split by projectivity of R/(a)R/(a)R/(a). Thus (a)(a)(a) would be a direct summand of RRR cut out by an idempotent.

The only idempotents in an integral domain are 0,10, 10,1. Since (a)≠0(a) \ne 0(a)=0 and (a)≠R(a) \ne R(a)=R, this is a contradiction ✓.

Hence pd⁡RM=1\operatorname{pd}_{R} M = 1pdR​M=1, with the upper bound supplied by ①, and therefore gl.dim⁡R=1\operatorname{gl.dim} R = 1gl.dimR=1 ✓.

∎

⭐ This is equivalent to the result on Ext for abelian groups. Expressing gl.dim⁡Z=1\operatorname{gl.dim}\mathbb{Z} = 1gl.dimZ=1 in terms of Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext gives “Ext⁡Zn(M,N)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N) = 0ExtZn​(M,N)=0 for every n≥2n \ge 2n≥2,” precisely the proposition on Ext for abelian groups.

The two propositions express the same fact in different ways.

⚠️ Why a PID? The proof uses the ring hypothesis only to ensure that submodules of free modules are free. This fails over k[x,y]k[x,y]k[x,y], and over k[x,y]k[x,y]k[x,y] there is indeed a module with pd⁡=2\operatorname{pd} = 2pd=2 (namely kkk itself). The global dimension rises to 222.
