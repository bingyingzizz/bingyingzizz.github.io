Statement

Let M,NM, NM,N be discrete abelian groups and T=R/ZT = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}T=R/Z. Then

RHom⁡Z(RHom⁡Z(M,T), N)≅M⊗ZLN[−1].\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, T),\ N\bigr) \cong M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Z}} N[-1].RHomZ​(RHomZ​(M,T), N)≅M⊗ZL​N[−1].

Notes

What it says. The round trip "dualize, then compute RHom into a target" is turned into a derived tensor product, with the degree lowered by one ([−1][-1][−1]). Thus "computing Ext of a dual" becomes "computing a tensor product" — and the latter can be done on discrete groups by a free resolution.

Proof.

Use the short exact sequence 0→Z→R→T→00 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to T \to 00→Z→R→T→0.

By the two vanishing theorems above, RHom⁡Z(R,N)=0\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R}, N) = 0RHomZ​(R,N)=0, hence RHom⁡Z(T,N)≅N[−1]\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T, N) \cong N[-1]RHomZ​(T,N)≅N[−1].

Substitute into RHom⁡(M,T)\operatorname{RHom}(M, T)RHom(M,T), unfold layer by layer, and use the derived tensor computation in the free case to obtain the claim.

⭐ This explains the role of the "circle" TTT: in the derived world TTT is equivalent to a degree shift (RHom⁡(−,T)\operatorname{RHom}(-, T)RHom(−,T) sends a discrete group to its own [−1][-1][−1]). This is why Pontryagin duality can be written with RHom in the next section.

Proof (build the map, then check it is an isomorphism in the free case).

  • Build the map: concatenate three isomorphisms
M⊗ZLN[−1]  ≅  M⊗ZLRHom⁡Z(Z[−1],N)  ≅  M⊗ZLRHom⁡Z(T,N)  ≅  RHom⁡Z(RHom⁡Z(M,T), N),M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Z}} N[-1] \;\cong\; M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Z}} \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}[-1], N) \;\cong\; M \otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathbb{Z}} \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T, N) \;\cong\; \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, T),\ N\bigr),M⊗ZL​N[−1]≅M⊗ZL​RHomZ​(Z[−1],N)≅M⊗ZL​RHomZ​(T,N)≅RHomZ​(RHomZ​(M,T), N),

where the first comes from "RHom⁡(Z,N)=N\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{Z}, N) = NRHom(Z,N)=N for discrete groups", the second from 0→Z→R→T→00\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\to T\to 00→Z→R→T→0 (with RHom⁡(R,N)=0\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{R}, N) = 0RHom(R,N)=0, hence RHom⁡(T,N)≅RHom⁡(Z,N)[−1]=N[−1]\operatorname{RHom}(T,N)\cong\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{Z},N)[-1] = N[-1]RHom(T,N)≅RHom(Z,N)[−1]=N[−1]), and the third is the adjunction between tensor and internal Hom.

  • Check the isomorphism: it suffices to check for M=Z⋅EM = \mathbb{Z}\cdot EM=Z⋅E (free).

Then "RHom⁡(Z⋅E,N)=NE\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{Z}\cdot E, N) = N^{E}RHom(Z⋅E,N)=NE in the discrete case" gives RHom⁡(M,T)=TE\operatorname{RHom}(M, T) = T^{E}RHom(M,T)=TE, while M⊗LN=N⋅EM\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}N = N\cdot EM⊗LN=N⋅E;

the problem reduces to proving RHom⁡Z(TE,N)≅N⋅E[−1]\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T^{E}, N) \cong N\cdot E[-1]RHomZ​(TE,N)≅N⋅E[−1].

  • When EEE is finite: the triangle RHom⁡(Z,N)→RHom⁡(R,N)→RHom⁡(T,N)→\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{Z},N)\to\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{R},N)\to\operatorname{RHom}(T,N)\toRHom(Z,N)→RHom(R,N)→RHom(T,N)→ together with RHom⁡(R,N)=0\operatorname{RHom}(\mathbb{R},N)=0RHom(R,N)=0 gives RHom⁡(T,N)≅N[−1]\operatorname{RHom}(T,N)\cong N[-1]RHom(T,N)≅N[−1]; take the EEE-th power.
  • When EEE is arbitrary: write it as the filtered colimit E=lim→⁡E′E = \varinjlim E'E=lim​E′ of finite subsets and propagate the conclusion with the Breen–Deligne spectral sequence plus exactness of filtered colimits, where the needed step is only Hq((Ts)E×S,N)=lim→⁡E′Hq((Ts)E′×S,N)H^{q}\bigl((T^{s})^{E}\times S, N\bigr) = \varinjlim_{E'} H^{q}\bigl((T^{s})^{E'}\times S, N\bigr)Hq((Ts)E×S,N)=lim​E′​Hq((Ts)E′×S,N) — supplied by Dyckhoff and by "cohomology with constant coefficients commutes with filtered limits of compact Hausdorff spaces".
