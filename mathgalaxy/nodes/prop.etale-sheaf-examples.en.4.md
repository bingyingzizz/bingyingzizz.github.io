## Notes · **draft**
**How to read it**: an étale sheaf = a presheaf "on which descent along étale covers can be performed". The common feature of the three examples above is — they are all objects that **look locally constant, or are determined by a covering**, so descent applies.

**The meaning of item 1 (Zariski is coarser than étale)**: the finer the topology, the fewer sheaves and the stricter the condition. Hence étale sheaves are automatically Zariski sheaves; and étale cohomology computes more than Zariski cohomology precisely because it also recognizes the information in "finite coverings".
