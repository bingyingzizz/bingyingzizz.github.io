Statement

A morphism from a graph (V,E,s,t)(V, E, s, t)(V,E,s,t) to a graph (V′,E′,s′,t′)(V', E', s', t')(V′,E′,s′,t′) is a pair of functions

φ1:V→V′,φ2:E→E′\varphi_1 : V \to V', \qquad \varphi_2 : E \to E'φ1​:V→V′,φ2​:E→E′

such that both squares commute:

s′∘φ2=φ1∘s,t′∘φ2=φ1∘ts' \circ \varphi_2 = \varphi_1 \circ s, \qquad t' \circ \varphi_2 = \varphi_1 \circ ts′∘φ2​=φ1​∘s,t′∘φ2​=φ1​∘t

That is, φ1\varphi_1φ1​ sends the source of an edge to the source of the image of that edge, and the target to the target.

Notes

The two equations govern the two ends separately. Drawn as squares, they are the entire content of the name "morphism of graphs": the vertex layer and the edge layer are each continuous, and the directions match.
