Statement

A morphism i:Y→Xi : Y \to Xi:Y→X is a monomorphism (mono) if for every object ZZZ and all f,g:Z→Yf, g : Z \to Yf,g:Z→Y,

i∘f=i∘g  ⟹  f=gi \circ f = i \circ g \;\Longrightarrow\; f = gi∘f=i∘g⟹f=g

(left cancellable). Equivalently: the square Y→Y×XYY \to Y \times_X YY→Y×X​Y is cartesian, i.e. Y≅Y×XYY \cong Y \times_X YY≅Y×X​Y, where Y×XYY \times_X YY×X​Y is the two pullbacks along iii. Reversing all arrows gives epimorphism (epi, right cancellable).

Isomorphism is another notion: f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is an isomorphism if there is g:Y→Xg : Y \to Xg:Y→X with

g∘f=1X,f∘g=1Yg \circ f = 1_{X}, \qquad f \circ g = 1_{Y}g∘f=1X​,f∘g=1Y​

then ggg is the inverse of fff, written f−1f^{-1}f−1, and is uniquely determined by fff. A category in which every morphism that is both mono and epi is an isomorphism is called balanced; Set\mathbf{Set}Set and Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab are balanced, Ring\mathbf{Ring}Ring is not.

Notes

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set monomorphisms are exactly injections and epimorphisms exactly surjections — the latter does not need the axiom of choice: if fff is not surjective, choose b∉im⁡fb \notin \operatorname{im} fb∈/imf and on the two-point set {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1} set g(b)=0g(b)=0g(b)=0, h(b)=1h(b)=1h(b)=1, while both agree on im⁡f\operatorname{im} fimf; then g∘f=h∘fg \circ f = h \circ fg∘f=h∘f but g≠hg \ne hg=h. In a general category, however, "cancellable" is the definition, and "one-to-one / onto" is only its manifestation in concrete categories: in the category of rings Z→Q\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}Z→Q is an epimorphism, though its underlying map is not surjective — the forgetful functor sends it to something no longer epi.

The inverse is unique: if g,g′g, g'g,g′ are both inverses of fff, then g=g∘1Y=g∘(f∘g′)=(g∘f)∘g′=1X∘g′=g′g = g \circ 1_{Y} = g \circ (f \circ g') = (g \circ f) \circ g' = 1_{X} \circ g' = g'g=g∘1Y​=g∘(f∘g′)=(g∘f)∘g′=1X​∘g′=g′ ✓.

Isomorphism vs monomorphism: fff is an isomorphism   ⟺  \iff⟺ fff is a monomorphism with a section.

(  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) follows directly from f∘g=1Yf \circ g = 1_{Y}f∘g=1Y​, g∘f=1Xg \circ f = 1_{X}g∘f=1X​ (the latter says fff is a split mono, hence mono).

(⟸)(\Longleftarrow)(⟸) Suppose f∘g=1Yf \circ g = 1_{Y}f∘g=1Y​.

Then f∘(g∘f)=(f∘g)∘f=1Y∘f=f=f∘1Xf \circ (g \circ f) = (f \circ g) \circ f = 1_{Y} \circ f = f = f \circ 1_{X}f∘(g∘f)=(f∘g)∘f=1Y​∘f=f=f∘1X​, ; since fff is mono, g∘f=1Xg \circ f = 1_{X}g∘f=1X​, and fff is an isomorphism ✓.

Reversing arrows gives the dual statement: fff is an isomorphism   ⟺  \iff⟺ fff is an epimorphism with a retraction.

⚠️ "Mono +++ epi" is not enough: in the category of rings Z→Q\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}Z→Q is both mono and epi, yet not an isomorphism — a section (or retraction) is still missing.
