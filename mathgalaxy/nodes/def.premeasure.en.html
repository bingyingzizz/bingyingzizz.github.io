Statement

If M\mathcal{M}M is only a ring (not necessarily a σ\sigmaσ-algebra), while μ:M→[0,+∞]\mu : \mathcal{M} \to [0, +\infty]μ:M→[0,+∞] still satisfies the two axioms of a measure:

then μ\muμ is called a premeasure on M\mathcal{M}M.

Notes

Why this intermediate notion: on R\mathbb{R}R the half-open intervals (a, b] form only a basic class; their finite disjoint unions form a ring, not yet a σ\sigmaσ-algebra — but length can already be defined there, and that is the premeasure.

Carathéodory's extension theorem lifts the premeasure from the ring all the way to the generated σ\sigmaσ-algebra. So "build the premeasure first, then extend" is the standard two-step construction of a measure.
