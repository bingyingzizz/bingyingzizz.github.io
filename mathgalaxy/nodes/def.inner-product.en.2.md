# inner product　`def.inner-product`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.inner-product)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.inner-product.md)

- **linear in the first argument**: $\langle u + c\,w, v \rangle = \langle u, v \rangle + c\langle w, v \rangle$;
- **conjugate symmetric**: $\langle v, u \rangle = \overline{\langle u, v \rangle}$ (for $K = \mathbb{R}$ this is just symmetry);
- **positive definite**: $\langle v, v \rangle \ge 0$, and $\langle v, v \rangle = 0 \iff v = 0$.

A $V$ equipped with an inner product is an **inner product space**. In the real case an inner product is exactly a positive definite symmetric bilinear form.

Standard example: on $K^{n}$ one sets $\langle x, y \rangle := \sum_i x_i\, \overline{y_i}$.
