Statement

Let γ\gammaγ be an arc-length parametrized C3C^{3}C3 regular curve with κ>0\kappa>0κ>0. Then

T′=κN,N′=−κT+τB,B′=−τN.T' = \kappa N,\qquad N' = -\kappa T+\tau B,\qquad B' = -\tau N .T′=κN,N′=−κT+τB,B′=−τN.

In matrix form,

(T′N′B′)=(0κ0−κ0τ0−τ0)(TNB).\begin{pmatrix} T' \\ N' \\ B' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \kappa & 0 \\ -\kappa & 0 & \tau \\ 0 & -\tau & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} T \\ N \\ B \end{pmatrix}.​T′N′B′​​=​0−κ0​κ0−τ​0τ0​​​TNB​​.

The coefficient matrix is antisymmetric — this is precisely the infinitesimal expression of "the frame is always orthonormal".

Notes

⭐ Why only two functions: a 3×33\times33×3 antisymmetric matrix has 333 degrees of freedom, but the direction of T′T'T′ is forced by the definition of NNN (⟨T′,T⟩=⟨T′,B⟩=0\langle T',T\rangle = \langle T',B\rangle = 0⟨T′,T⟩=⟨T′,B⟩=0 are identities), leaving only κ\kappaκ and τ\tauτ.

It is the same thing as the structure equations: expanding dT,dN,dB\mathrm{d}T,\mathrm{d}N,\mathrm{d}BdT,dN,dB in the frame, the coefficients are κ ds\kappa\,\mathrm{d}sκds and τ ds\tau\,\mathrm{d}sτds.

The surface quantities ω ji\omega^{i}_{\ j}ω ji​ (联络形式) degenerate in the curve case to these three numbers times ds\mathrm{d}sds.

Where antisymmetry comes from: ⟨T,T⟩=⟨N,N⟩=⟨B,B⟩=1\langle T,T\rangle = \langle N,N\rangle = \langle B,B\rangle = 1⟨T,T⟩=⟨N,N⟩=⟨B,B⟩=1 and ⟨T,N⟩=⟨T,B⟩=⟨N,B⟩=0\langle T,N\rangle = \langle T,B\rangle = \langle N,B\rangle = 0⟨T,N⟩=⟨T,B⟩=⟨N,B⟩=0 are all constants; differentiating gives the antisymmetry of the matrix — literally the same proof as 联络形式反对称.

⚠ It holds only where κ>0\kappa>0κ>0. At an isolated point with κ=0\kappa = 0κ=0, NNN does not exist and the formula is meaningless (although κ\kappaκ itself is still defined).

What it is good for: it turns a geometric problem about curves into a linear system — so "existence + uniqueness" can be handed over wholesale to ODEs (曲线基本定理).
