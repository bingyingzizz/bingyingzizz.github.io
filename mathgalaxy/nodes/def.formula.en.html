Statement

(In a given first-order language L\mathcal{L}L) a formula is an expression generated by syntax rule 3, that is,

φ::=R(t1,…,tn)  ∣  ¬φ  ∣  (φ∧ψ)  ∣  (φ∨ψ)  ∣  (φ→ψ)  ∣  ∀viφ  ∣  ∃viφ\varphi ::= R(t_{1}, \ldots, t_{n}) \;\mid\; \neg \varphi \;\mid\; (\varphi \wedge \psi) \;\mid\; (\varphi \vee \psi) \;\mid\; (\varphi \to \psi) \;\mid\; \forall v_{i} \varphi \;\mid\; \exists v_{i} \varphiφ::=R(t1​,…,tn​)∣¬φ∣(φ∧ψ)∣(φ∨ψ)∣(φ→ψ)∣∀vi​φ∣∃vi​φ

where RRR ranges over relation symbols of L\mathcal{L}L and tjt_{j}tj​ ranges over terms (strictly speaking this is an inductive definition: the set of formulas is the smallest set containing all atomic formulas and closed under the operations above).

An occurrence of a variable inside the scope of ∀vi\forall v_{i}∀vi​ or ∃vi\exists v_{i}∃vi​ is called bound, otherwise free; a formula with no free variables is a sentence (closed formula).

The complexity of a formula (the number of connectives and quantifiers) supports induction — this is the basis for "induction on formulas".

Notes

⭐ What the notation "φ(x,p)\varphi (x, p)φ(x,p)" says: it says that φ\varphiφ is a formula and that its free variables all lie in {x,p}\{ x, p \}{x,p} (not necessarily all used).

The phrase "for every formula φ\varphiφ (BBB does not occur)" in the separation scheme is exactly this convention — formulas are metamathematical objects, not objects of ZFC, so "does not occur" is a statement about strings of symbols.

⭐ Inductive definition = smallest set: behind this is induction on N\mathbb{N}N (see "the principle of induction and recursive definitions"). It is also the reason "induction on the complexity of a formula" works — the two are two sides of the same thing.

⚠ Only some primitive symbols are kept: ∃\exists∃ is an abbreviation for ¬∀¬\neg \forall \neg¬∀¬, and ∨\vee∨, →\to→ can also be defined from ¬,∧\neg, \wedge¬,∧. The fewer primitive symbols, the shorter the syntax rules and subsequent proofs, at the cost of more parentheses when writing formulas.

⚠ The operation "replace a variable by a term" is substitution, and it has a trap that requires care (variable capture); it has its own node.
