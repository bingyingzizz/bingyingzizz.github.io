Statement

Let Γ\GammaΓ be a set of formulas (a theory) and φ\varphiφ a formula. Say that φ\varphiφ is provable from Γ\GammaΓ, written

Γ⊢φ,\Gamma \vdash \varphi,Γ⊢φ,

if there is a finite sequence of formulas φ1,…,φn=φ\varphi_{1}, \ldots, \varphi_{n} = \varphiφ1​,…,φn​=φ in which each step φk\varphi_{k}φk​ satisfies one of the following:

  • φk\varphi_{k}φk​ is a logical axiom;
  • φk∈Γ\varphi_{k} \in \Gammaφk​∈Γ;
  • φk\varphi_{k}φk​ is obtained from the two preceding formulas φi\varphi_{i}φi​, φj\varphi_{j}φj​ (where φj=φi→φk\varphi_{j} = \varphi_{i} \to \varphi_{k}φj​=φi​→φk​) by modus ponens;
  • φk=∀x ψ\varphi_{k} = \forall x\,\psiφk​=∀xψ, and ψ\psiψ occurs earlier (generalization rule).

Such a sequence is a proof of φ\varphiφ. Γ\GammaΓ is inconsistent if there is φ\varphiφ with Γ⊢φ\Gamma \vdash \varphiΓ⊢φ and Γ⊢¬φ\Gamma \vdash \neg\varphiΓ⊢¬φ.

Notes

⭐ This is purely syntactic: the definition mentions neither "truth", "model", nor "satisfaction" — only a list of formulas, a few axioms, and two inference rules, each step machine-checkable. This is also the whole reason "formal proof" can be verified by computer.

⭐ The division of labour between ⊢ and ⊨ (the axis of comparison in this map): ⊨ (the satisfaction relation) says "true in all models", ⊢ says "there is a step-by-step proof". That the two coincide is the completeness theorem (@@thm.completeness-first-order@@) — the only bridge between these two concepts, and the most basic theorem of mathematical logic.

⚠ The choice of logical axioms is not unique: Hilbert-style (the system above), natural deduction, and sequent calculus give the same provability relation (under the same completeness theorem). So "the definition of ⊢\vdash⊢ depends on a particular axiom system" is not a problem: it is extensionally invariant.

⚠ The word "finite" is essential: a proof is a finite sequence. The compactness theorem is exactly what upgrades "every finite subset has a model" to "the whole theory has a model", and it relies on this.
