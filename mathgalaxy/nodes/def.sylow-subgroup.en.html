Statement

Let GGG be a finite group with ∣G∣=pam|G| = p^{a} m∣G∣=pam, where ppp is a prime and p∤mp \nmid mp∤m. A subgroup of GGG of order pap^{a}pa, call it PPP, is called a Sylow ppp-subgroup.

Equivalently, PPP is, in GGG, a maximal ppp-subgroup (under inclusion). The set of all Sylow ppp-subgroups is denoted Syl⁡p(G)\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)Sylp​(G); if P∈Syl⁡p(G)P \in \operatorname{Syl}_p(G)P∈Sylp​(G), then [G:P]=m[G : P] = m[G:P]=m.

Notes

⭐ Existence, conjugacy and counting are in Sylow theorems; this node only defines the object.

⭐ Example: in S3S_3S3​ (∣S3∣=6=2⋅3|S_3| = 6 = 2 \cdot 3∣S3​∣=6=2⋅3) the Sylow 333-subgroups are A3A_3A3​ (unique, hence normal), and the Sylow 222-subgroups are the three subgroups of order 222 generated by the three transpositions.

⚠ A ppp-subgroup is a subgroup whose order is a prime power; a Sylow ppp-subgroup is the largest such subgroup, and a group may have several Sylow ppp-subgroups.
