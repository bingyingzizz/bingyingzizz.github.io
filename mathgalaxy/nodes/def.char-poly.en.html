Statement

Let T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V be linear, dim⁡V=n<∞\dim V = n < \inftydimV=n<∞. Choose any basis and write TTT as a matrix AAA, and define

χT(x):=det⁡(x In−A)∈K[x]\chi_T(x) := \det(x\,I_n - A) \in K[x]χT​(x):=det(xIn​−A)∈K[x]

called the characteristic polynomial of TTT. It is a monic polynomial of degree nnn, independent of the choice of basis (a change of basis gives a similar AAA, and det⁡(P−1(xI−A)P)=det⁡(xI−A)\det(P^{-1}(xI - A)P) = \det(xI - A)det(P−1(xI−A)P)=det(xI−A)).

Its roots (in KKK, or in an extension field) are exactly the eigenvalues of TTT, and their multiplicities are called algebraic multiplicities. Expanded,

χT(x)=xn−(tr⁡T) xn−1+⋯+(−1)ndet⁡T\chi_T(x) = x^{n} - (\operatorname{tr} T)\,x^{n-1} + \cdots + (-1)^{n} \det TχT​(x)=xn−(trT)xn−1+⋯+(−1)ndetT

Notes

⭐ The characteristic polynomial turns the geometric problem "eigenvalues" into the algebraic problem "roots of a polynomial" — so the fundamental theorem of algebra guarantees nnn eigenvalues over C\mathbb{C}C (counted with multiplicity).

⭐ Relation of coefficients to invariants: the coefficient of xn−1x^{n-1}xn−1 is −tr⁡T-\operatorname{tr} T−trT, and the constant term is (−1)ndet⁡T(-1)^{n}\det T(−1)ndetT.

Thus "trace = sum of eigenvalues" and "determinant = product of eigenvalues" become readings of Vieta's formulas, without actually solving for the eigenvalues.

⚠ Geometric multiplicity ≤\le≤ algebraic multiplicity;

the standard example where they differ is (1101)\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}(10​11​) (λ=1\lambda = 1λ=1: algebraic multiplicity 2, geometric multiplicity 1) — which is exactly why it is not diagonalizable.
