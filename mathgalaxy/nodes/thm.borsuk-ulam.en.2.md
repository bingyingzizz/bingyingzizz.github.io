# Borsuk–Ulam theorem in dimension two　`thm.borsuk-ulam`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.borsuk-ulam)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.borsuk-ulam.md)

$$g(x) := \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{|f(x) - f(-x)|} \in S^{1},$$

then $g$ is continuous and $g(-x) = -g(x)$.

Restricting $g$ to the equator $S^{1} \subset S^{2}$ gives a continuous odd map $S^{1} \to S^{1}$, whose **degree** must be odd (the degree of an odd map is $\equiv 1 \bmod 2$);

but an odd map $S^{1} \to S^{1}$ also extends to the upper hemisphere, and an extendable map has degree $0$ — contradiction.

⭐ **Intuition**: at any moment on Earth there is a pair of antipodal points with the same temperature and the same pressure. This is the case $n = 2$ of Borsuk–Ulam, not a metaphor but a theorem.
