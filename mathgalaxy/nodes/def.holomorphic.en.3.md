# Holomorphic Function　`def.holomorphic`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.holomorphic)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.holomorphic.md)

In the real case it suffices that the left and right directions agree; in the complex case **infinitely many directions** ($h = t e^{i\theta}$, $\theta$ arbitrary) must all agree. The requirement is very strong and the reward is very large: one derivative gives infinitely many.

⚠ This requirement is **not automatic**.

$f(z) = \bar z$ is nowhere complex differentiable: along the real axis, $h = t$ gives difference quotient $1$; along the imaginary axis, $h = it$ gives quotient $-1$.

The two one-sided limits differ.

So "real differentiable" is far from implying "complex differentiable".
