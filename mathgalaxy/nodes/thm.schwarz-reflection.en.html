Statement

Let Ω\OmegaΩ be symmetric about the real axis and Ω+=Ω∩{ Im⁡z>0 }\Omega^{+} = \Omega \cap \{\, \operatorname{Im} z > 0 \,\}Ω+=Ω∩{Imz>0}. If fff is holomorphic on Ω+\Omega^{+}Ω+, continuous on Ω∩R\Omega \cap \mathbb{R}Ω∩R and real-valued there, then fff extends to a holomorphic function on Ω\OmegaΩ, namely FFF, and

F(zˉ)=F(z)‾.F(\bar z) = \overline{F(z)}.F(zˉ)=F(z)​.

(Replace the "real axis" by any arc or line and the conclusion changes accordingly: the two symmetric pieces reflect into each other by the same formula.)

Notes

Proof: define

F(z)={f(z),Im⁡z>0,f(zˉ)‾,Im⁡z<0.F(z) = \begin{cases} f(z), & \operatorname{Im} z > 0,\\ \overline{f(\bar z)}, & \operatorname{Im} z < 0.\end{cases}F(z)={f(z),f(zˉ)​,​Imz>0,Imz<0.​

The lower half-plane FFF is holomorphic (conjugate, then conjugate again — the two anti-holomorphic steps cancel). It remains to show that FFF is holomorphic across the real axis: use Morera (Morera 定理) — take a closed triangle crossing the axis, use fff on the upper part and the definition on the lower part; the integral along the real-axis piece tends to zero by continuity (fff is real-valued on the axis, so the two sides cancel), hence the integral over the whole boundary is zero. ∎
