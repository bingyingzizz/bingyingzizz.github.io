Statement

For every set AAA:

#A<#P(A)\#A < \#\mathcal{P}(A)#A<#P(A)

That is, there is no surjection from AAA onto P(A)\mathcal{P}(A)P(A) (and hence no bijection either), while a↦{a}a \mapsto \{a\}a↦{a} is an injection.

Notes

Corollary: there is no largest cardinal — from any set, taking the power set gives a larger one.

Hence

ℵ0<2ℵ0<22ℵ0<⋯\aleph_0 < 2^{\aleph_0} < 2^{2^{\aleph_0}} < \cdotsℵ0​<2ℵ0​<22ℵ0​<⋯

Taking A=NA = \mathbb{N}A=N gives "R\mathbb{R}R is uncountable" (#R=2ℵ0\#\mathbb{R} = 2^{\aleph_0}#R=2ℵ0​).

The proof uses the diagonal method (see the proof on the edge): the "diagonal set" D={a∈A:a∉f(a)}D = \{ a \in A : a \notin f(a) \}D={a∈A:a∈/f(a)} is not in the image of fff.

This is the most theorem-producing construction in the history of mathematics; Cantor–Bernstein, Gödel's incompleteness theorem and the halting problem all use it.
