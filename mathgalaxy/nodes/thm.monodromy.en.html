Statement

Let Ω⊂C\Omega \subset \mathbb{C}Ω⊂C be a simply connected domain, (f,D)(f, D)(f,D) a function element at some point of Ω\OmegaΩ, and suppose fff can be analytically continued along every curve in Ω\OmegaΩ. Then there is on Ω\OmegaΩ a single-valued holomorphic function FFF which, on DDD, equals fff.

Equivalent statement: if γ0,γ1\gamma_{0}, \gamma_{1}γ0​,γ1​ are two curves in Ω\OmegaΩ starting at the same point and ending at the same point, then when they are homotopic in Ω\OmegaΩ they give the same terminal value of the continuation.

Notes

Idea of the proof: regard the homotopy as a continuous family γs\gamma_{s}γs​ (s∈[0,1]s \in [0,1]s∈[0,1]). For each sss, continuing along γs\gamma_{s}γs​ gives a function element φ(s)\varphi(s)φ(s) at the endpoint.

By continuity of continuation, φ(s)\varphi(s)φ(s) is continuous; and “two elements are equal at the endpoint” is a closed condition (equality is preserved by limits), so { s:φ(s)=φ(0) }\{\, s : \varphi(s) = \varphi(0) \,\}{s:φ(s)=φ(0)} is both open and closed and nonempty, hence all of it — that is, φ(1)=φ(0)\varphi(1) = \varphi(0)φ(1)=φ(0).

∎

Why simple connectivity is necessary: for Ω=C∖{0}\Omega = \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}Ω=C∖{0}, going once around the origin and not going around are not homotopic, and the continuations of log⁡\loglog differ exactly by 2πi2\pi i2πi — single-valuedness breaks precisely here.

⭐ Its use: it upgrades “continuation along paths” to “a single-valued function on the domain”, so the statement in criterion for a single-valued branch that “on a simply connected   ⟹  \implies⟹ the logarithm has a single-valued branch” becomes a special case. Conversely, it explains why the “branches” of a multivalued function can go wrong only on domains that are not simply connected.

⚠️ The hypothesis “can be continued along every curve” cannot be dropped: z\sqrt{z}z​ can of course be continued on a domain with a slit removed, but once a curve hits the singularity the chain breaks.
