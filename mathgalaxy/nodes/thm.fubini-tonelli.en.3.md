## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **Tonelli needs no extra hypothesis** (nonnegativity suffices), so in practice one first uses it to "test": if one of the two iterated integrals comes out finite, then $f$ is integrable and the order may safely be swapped.

**Fubini, by contrast, must assume integrability** — otherwise one side is $\infty$ and the other $-\infty$, and swapping goes wrong.

⚠ Counterexample (not integrable, iterated integrals unequal): on $[0,1]^{2}$ take $f(x, y) = (x^2 - y^2) / (x^2 + y^2)^2$; the two iterated integrals are $\pi/4$ and $-\pi/4$ (for exponent $3$ both sides diverge and give no such value).
