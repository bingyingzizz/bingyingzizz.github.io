def.simple-algebra

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A nonzero algebra AAA is called simple if its only two-sided ideals are 000 and AAA.

Notes

The matrix algebra Mn(D)M_n(D)Mn​(D) over a division algebra DDD is a simple algebra and serves as the model case. A finite-dimensional simple algebra is isomorphic to a matrix algebra over a division algebra (Wedderburn). A group algebra F[G]F[G]F[G] is simple only in very small cases. Simplicity is a two-sided condition and is not the same as having no nontrivial left ideal.

Terminology: this map calls an algebra with no nontrivial two-sided ideals simple; the lecture calls this property quasi-simple and reserves simple for semisimple algebras with a unique isomorphism class of simple left ideals. In finite dimension the two notions agree (see thm.wedderburn-simple).
