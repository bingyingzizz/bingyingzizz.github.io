Statement

Let In=[an,bn]I_n = [a_n, b_n]In​=[an​,bn​] be a sequence of **closed** intervals satisfying

  • I1⊇I2⊇⋯I_1 \supseteq I_2 \supseteq \cdotsI1​⊇I2​⊇⋯ (nested),
  • bn−an→0b_n - a_n \to 0bn​−an​→0 (the lengths tend to zero).

Then ⋂n=1∞In\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} I_n⋂n=1∞​In​ contains exactly **one** point.

Notes

⭐ This is the formal form of "bisection": applied to a bounded sequence it gives the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem; every step of a proof that keeps halving an interval is this lemma.

⚠ Neither condition can be relaxed: closed** — ⋂n(0,1/n]=∅\bigcap_{n} (0, 1/n] = \emptyset⋂n​(0,1/n]=∅; lengths tending to zero** — ⋂n[n,+∞)=∅\bigcap_{n} [n, +\infty) = \emptyset⋂n​[n,+∞)=∅ while the lengths do not tend to zero.

If only nesting is required and the lengths need not tend to zero, the conclusion weakens to "the intersection is nonempty" (the intersection is then a point or a closed interval).
