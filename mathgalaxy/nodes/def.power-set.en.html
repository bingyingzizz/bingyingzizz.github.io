Statement

Let XXX be a set. The power set of XXX is the set whose elements are all subsets of XXX:

P(X):={A:A⊆X}\mathcal{P}(X) := \{ A : A \subseteq X \}P(X):={A:A⊆X}

Thus A∈P(X)  ⟺  A⊆XA \in \mathcal{P}(X) \iff A \subseteq XA∈P(X)⟺A⊆X, and every element of P(X)\mathcal{P}(X)P(X) is itself a set.

By the Axiom of Power Set, P(X)\mathcal{P}(X)P(X) is indeed a set; by Extensionality it is unique.

In the finite case the number of elements is given by #X=n  ⟹  #P(X)=2n\#X = n \implies \#\mathcal{P}(X) = 2^{n}#X=n⟹#P(X)=2n (each element is either taken or not).

Notes

The power set is the step that turns a property into an object: to speak about "all subsets of XXX" as a thing in itself (rather than one subset at a time) one needs the power set.

P(X)\mathcal{P}(X)P(X) is also the stage for several important constructions:

  • Outer measures are defined on all subsets: μ∗:P(X)→[0,+∞]\mu^* : \mathcal{P}(X) \to [0, +\infty]μ∗:P(X)→[0,+∞];
  • Rings, σ\sigmaσ-algebras and monotone classes are each a family of subsets of XXX, i.e. a subfamily of P(X)\mathcal{P}(X)P(X);
  • The generated σ\sigmaσ-algebra is the intersection of all σ\sigmaσ-algebras containing E\mathcal{E}E, and P(X)\mathcal{P}(X)P(X) guarantees that this intersection is non-empty.

∅∈P(X)\emptyset \in \mathcal{P}(X)∅∈P(X) and X∈P(X)X \in \mathcal{P}(X)X∈P(X) for every XXX — a power set is never empty.
