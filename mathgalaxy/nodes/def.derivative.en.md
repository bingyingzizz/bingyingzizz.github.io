## Statement · **draft**
$F : (a, b) \to \mathbb{C}$ is **differentiable** at $x_0 \in (a, b)$ if and only if the limit

$$F'(x_0) := \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{F(x_0 + h) - F(x_0)}{h}$$

exists (and is finite). This number is the **derivative** of $F$ at $x_0$.

If it is differentiable everywhere on $(a, b)$, we obtain a function $F'$; $F$ is then called **differentiable**.
