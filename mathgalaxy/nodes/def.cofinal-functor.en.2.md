## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **Intuition**: the image of $F$ in $I$ is "everywhere present and not disconnected". Non-empty = every $i$ has a path into the image of $F$; connected = different paths can be converted into one another. **With these two, $J$ "fills $I$ densely enough"**, and taking a colimit along it is the same as along all of $I$ (see the next item).

**Dual**: replacing $i \downarrow F$ by the **lower** comma category $F \downarrow i$ (objects are $\{\, f : F(j) \to i \,\}$) gives the condition called **coinitial**, which preserves **limits**.
