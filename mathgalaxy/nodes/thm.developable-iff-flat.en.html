Statement

What this does: it gives a workable criterion for whether a ruled surface is developable — a determinant identity, with no PDE to solve.

Let X(u,v)=a(u)+v b(u)X(u,v) = a(u) + v\,b(u)X(u,v)=a(u)+vb(u) be a ruled surface (直纹面). Then the following are equivalent:

  1. the tangent plane is constant along each ruling (the normal vector is independent of vvv);
  2. det⁡(a′(u), b(u), b′(u))=0\det\bigl(a'(u),\ b(u),\ b'(u)\bigr) = 0det(a′(u), b(u), b′(u))=0 for all uuu;
  3. Gauss curvature vanishes identically: K≡0K \equiv 0K≡0;
  4. developable (locally isometric to the plane, 可展曲面).

How to use it: substitute a,ba, ba,b into that third-order determinant. For a cylinder b′=0b' = 0b′=0, hence developable; for the hyperbolic paraboloid X=(u,v,uv)X = (u,v,uv)X=(u,v,uv) the determinant is 1≠01 \ne 01=0, so it is not developable.

  • (2) and (3): compute directly from K=det⁡(hij)/det⁡(gij)K = \det(h_{ij})/\det(g_{ij})K=det(hij​)/det(gij​); on a ruled surface det⁡(hij)\det(h_{ij})det(hij​) and det⁡(a′,b,b′)2\det(a',b,b')^{2}det(a′,b,b′)2 differ by a sign (not necessarily a positive factor: X=(u,v,uv)X = (u,v,uv)X=(u,v,uv) gives det⁡h=−1\det h = -1deth=−1, while det⁡(a′,b,b′)2>0\det(a',b,b')^{2} > 0det(a′,b,b′)2>0) — but "being zero" is unaffected by the sign, so K≡0K \equiv 0K≡0 is still equivalent to the determinant vanishing identically.   • (3) and (4): if K≡0K \equiv 0K≡0, the local classification of constant-curvature surfaces (常 Gauss 曲率曲面, case K=0K = 0K=0) gives local isometry to the plane; conversely Gauss's theorema egregium (KKK is an isometry invariant, and the plane has KKK zero).

⚠ With flat points, 1 and 4 decouple: there are surfaces with K≡0K \equiv 0K≡0 containing flat patches that cannot be written with "tangent plane constant along the rulings". So 4 should be read as "locally isometric to the plane", which always holds.
