Statement

Given a sequence {an}⊆R\{a_n\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}{an​}⊆R (or C\mathbb{C}C) and a centre x0x_0x0​, an expression of the form

∑n=0∞an(x−x0)n\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - x_0)^{n}n=0∑∞​an​(x−x0​)n

is called a power series. The set of xxx at which it converges is its domain of convergence; the interior of that domain is always an interval (or disk) centred at x0x_0x0​, and each xxx of it is thus an interior point of convergence.

Notes

⭐ A power series is where "series" and "function" meet: it is both a series (every term computable) and a function (on its domain of convergence).

So the tools of the two previous packs converge here.

⚠ The domain of convergence need not be open: the endpoints require separate discussion (they may converge, diverge, or converge conditionally) — that is exactly what Abel 第二定理 handles.

⭐ A striking phenomenon: inside its domain of convergence a power series has no "wild" behaviour at all (it is infinitely differentiable, see "term-by-term differentiation"), in sharp contrast with general series of functions.
