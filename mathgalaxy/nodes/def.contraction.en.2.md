## Notes · **draft**
⚠ The point is that $q < 1$ must hold **uniformly**, not merely "less than $1$ near each point".

Counterexample: $f(x) = x + 1/x$ on $[1, \infty)$ satisfies $|f'(x)| = 1 - 1/x^{2} < 1$ everywhere, but it is **not** a contraction and has **no** fixed point ($f(x) = x$ has no solution).

The difference between "locally contracting at every point" and "uniformly contracting overall" is the life-or-death line for the theorem.

⭐ A contraction is uniformly continuous, much stronger than continuity; and its Lipschitz constant is exactly $q$.
