Statement

Every set fits into some universe. This is an independent axiom:

∀x ∃U[U∈Univ∧x∈U]\forall x \, \exists U \left[ U \in \mathrm{Univ} \wedge x \in U \right]∀x∃U[U∈Univ∧x∈U]

where "universe" is as in the definition of a Grothendieck universe: transitive, and closed under pairing, power sets, and unions indexed by internal sets.

⭐ A common equivalent form: strongly inaccessible cardinals are unbounded — for every ordinal α\alphaα there is a strongly inaccessible cardinal κ>α\kappa > \alphaκ>α, and then VκV_{\kappa}Vκ​ itself is a universe.

Notes

It is not in ZFC. Taking a strongly inaccessible cardinal κ\kappaκ, Vκ⊨ZFCV_{\kappa} \models \mathrm{ZFC}Vκ​⊨ZFC, so this axiom implies Con(ZFC)\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})Con(ZFC); by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, ZFC cannot prove it if consistent. So any theorem using it must list it as a hypothesis, not assume it silently.

It is stronger than "there exists a universe": it says universes are unbounded. That is exactly what category theory needs — Set\mathbf{Set}Set is a small category relative to UUU, while UUU itself lies in a larger universe U+U^{+}U+, so "the category of UUU-small categories" is again small in U+U^{+}U+. One always has room to go up a level.

Weak form: when only finitely many fixed categories are involved, assuming "there exists a universe" (equivalently, "there exists a strongly inaccessible cardinal") suffices; unboundedness is not needed.

⚠️ A practical substitute: type theory automates this with universe levels (Lean's Type u, Coq's Type_i); this graph follows the SGA convention and settles it once and for all with one axiom.
