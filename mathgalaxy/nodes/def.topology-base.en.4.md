# base and subbase　`def.topology-base`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.topology-base)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.topology-base.md)

- In metric spaces: all open balls form a base;
- On $\mathbb{R}$: open intervals form a base; the half-open intervals $\{ (a, b] \}$ generate a base for **another topology** (the upper limit topology) — they are not the usual open sets, hence **not** a base for the usual topology (although they are handier when constructing the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure);

- The cylinder sets of the product topology and of the $\sigma$-algebra follow the same pattern: first fix a convenient family of sets, then take what it generates.

⚠ A base is not unique: the same topology can have many bases.
