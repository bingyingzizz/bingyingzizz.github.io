# the integrally closed subring is determined by the spectrum　`prop.huber-subring-recovery`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.huber-subring-recovery)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.huber-subring-recovery.md)

In other words, $(A,A^+)$ and "$A$ together with a clopen subset of its spectrum" are the same thing — exactly where ring theory becomes geometry.

⚠ In general $A^+$ cannot be an arbitrary subring: it must be integrally closed.

Counterexample: $A = \mathbb{Z}[T]$, $A^+ = \mathbb{Z}[T^2]$ is not integrally closed ($T$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}[T^2]$), and the corresponding subset is not the $\operatorname{Spa}$ of any **integrally closed** subring (the inverse map returns the integral closure $\widetilde{\mathbb{Z}[T^{2}]} \ne \mathbb{Z}[T^{2}]$).
