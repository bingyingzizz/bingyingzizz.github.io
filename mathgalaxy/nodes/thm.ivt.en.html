Notes

⭐ Geometric content: in passing from f(a)f(a)f(a) to f(b)f(b)f(b), the graph of a continuous function **cannot skip any intermediate value** — a continuous function maps an interval to an interval.

⭐ This is the most frequently used existence tool in elementary analysis: existence of roots, of inverse functions, of fixed points, and comparison of intermediate values all follow from it.

It also has **constructive** content: the bisection method used in the proof is itself a root-finding algorithm, and every step is computable.

⚠ It guarantees only **existence**, not uniqueness (x3−xx^{3} - xx3−x has three zeros on [−2,2][-2, 2][−2,2]);

uniqueness requires an additional assumption of **strict monotonicity**.

⚠ It uses completeness of R\mathbb{R}R (the least-upper-bound principle), not compactness: with [a,b][a, b][a,b] replaced by a general connected space it still holds, and that is the fixed-point / connectedness branch.
