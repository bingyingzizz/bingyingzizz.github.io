# when a ring is a sigma-ring　`thm.ring-monotone-sigma`

[in graph](../#n=thm.ring-monotone-sigma)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.ring-monotone-sigma.md)

- (1) $\implies$ (3): a countable disjoint union is a special case of a countable union — free.
- (1) $\implies$ (2): increasing unions are immediate from the definition; for decreasing intersections take the first term minus the successive differences as a countable union (the whole set need not belong to the family).
- (2) $\implies$ (1): the ring gives finite unions, and the monotone class keeps their increasing limit inside the family.
- (3) $\implies$ (1): set $B_n = A_n \setminus \bigcup_{j<n} A_j$; the ring guarantees every $B_n$ lies in the family, and closure under disjoint unions finishes.
