Statement

Let AAA be an integral domain. On the set A×(A∖{0})A \times (A \setminus \{0\})A×(A∖{0}) define

(a,s)∼(b,t)  ⟺  at=bs.(a, s) \sim (b, t) \iff a t = b s .(a,s)∼(b,t)⟺at=bs.

This is an equivalence relation; the quotient set is denoted Frac⁡(A)\operatorname{Frac}(A)Frac(A), its elements are written as\frac{a}{s}sa​, and with the operations

as+bt:=at+bsst,as⋅bt:=abst\frac{a}{s} + \frac{b}{t} := \frac{a t + b s}{s t}, \qquad \frac{a}{s} \cdot \frac{b}{t} := \frac{a b}{s t}sa​+tb​:=stat+bs​,sa​⋅tb​:=stab​

it is a field, the field of fractions of AAA. The map a↦a1a \mapsto \frac{a}{1}a↦1a​ is an injective ring homomorphism.

Universal property: if KKK is a field and φ:A→K\varphi : A \to Kφ:A→K is an injective ring homomorphism, then there is a unique field homomorphism φˉ:Frac⁡(A)→K\bar\varphi : \operatorname{Frac}(A) \to Kφˉ​:Frac(A)→K with φˉ(a/1)=φ(a)\bar\varphi(a/1) = \varphi(a)φˉ​(a/1)=φ(a).

Notes

⭐ Examples: Frac⁡(Z)=Q\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Q}Frac(Z)=Q; Frac⁡(K[x])=K(x)\operatorname{Frac}(K[x]) = K(x)Frac(K[x])=K(x) (the field of rational functions).

⚠ The ring must be an integral domain: with zero divisors the equivalence class of as\frac{a}{s}sa​ degenerates (sss may become a zero divisor) and the construction fails.

⭐ The field of fractions is "the smallest field containing AAA"; the universal property says exactly this.
