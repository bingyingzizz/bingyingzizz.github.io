def.intersection-subobject

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let Y↣XY \rightarrowtail XY↣X, Z↣XZ \rightarrowtail XZ↣X be two subobjects of XXX (isomorphism classes of monomorphisms, see @@def.subobject@@). Their intersection is the fibred product

Y∩Z:=Y×XZ  ⟶  X,Y \cap Z := Y \times_{X} Z \;\longrightarrow\; X ,Y∩Z:=Y×X​Z⟶X,

together with the projection to XXX (if the fibred product exists). It is universal among subobjects "contained in both YYY and ZZZ": every T→XT \to XT→X that factors through both monomorphisms factors uniquely through Y∩Z→XY \cap Z \to XY∩Z→X.

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set, the intersection is the set-theoretic intersection Y∩ZY \cap ZY∩Z; for preimages along a morphism see @@def.preimage-subobject@@.

Notes

Disambiguation: in a general category there is no "intersection" of objects; what one can speak of is the intersection of subobjects (fibred intersection), and the relevant fibred product must exist.

Why the fibred product: the intersection must express "lies in YYY and ZZZ simultaneously". In terms of elements this is t∈Y∩Zt \in Y \cap Zt∈Y∩Z; without elements it is T→XT \to XT→X factoring simultaneously through Y↣XY \rightarrowtail XY↣X and Z↣XZ \rightarrowtail XZ↣X. The universal property of the fibred product is exactly the universal solution of this "simultaneous factorization"; hence any object with that property factors uniquely through Y×XZY \times_{X} ZY×X​Z, i.e. it is the greatest lower bound.

Existence: the intersection is a fibred product, so "the intersection exists" iff the fibred product along XXX exists; in a category without fibred products one cannot speak of intersections. The pullback of a monomorphism along any morphism is still a monomorphism (see @@def.mono@@), so Y×XZ→XY \times_{X} Z \to XY×X​Z→X really is a subobject.

Relation to set-theoretic intersection: in Set\mathbf{Set}Set, Y×XZ={(y,z):y=z}Y \times_{X} Z = \{(y,z) : y = z\}Y×X​Z={(y,z):y=z}, the map t↦(t,t)t \mapsto (t,t)t↦(t,t) gives an isomorphism Y∩Z→Y×XZY \cap Z \to Y \times_{X} ZY∩Z→Y×X​Z; so the intersection is the set-theoretic one. In concrete categories such as Grp\mathbf{Grp}Grp, Top\mathbf{Top}Top, subobjects are determined by injections, and the underlying set of the intersection is likewise the set-theoretic intersection.

Relation to union: the union is the image of the coproduct Y∪Z:=im⁡(Y∐Z→X)Y \cup Z := \operatorname{im}(Y \coprod Z \to X)Y∪Z:=im(Y∐Z→X), not a fibred coproduct. In a pretopos the subobjects form a bounded lattice: meet = fibred product (greatest lower bound), join = image (least upper bound), see @@prop.subobject-lattice@@.

Warning: in a general category Y∩ZY \cap ZY∩Z need not exist; even when it does, do not confuse it with the product Y×ZY \times ZY×Z or the coproduct Y∐ZY \coprod ZY∐Z. The intersection is "the fibred product along XXX", not "the product of two objects".
