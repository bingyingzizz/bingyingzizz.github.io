# simple group　`def.simple-group`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.simple-group)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.simple-group.md)

⭐ Basic examples and counterexamples:
- $\mathbb{Z}/p$ ($p$ prime) is simple: every subgroup is normal (abelian) and there are only the two trivial ones;
- $A_n$ ($n \ge 5$) is simple and **non-abelian**;
- $S_n$ ($n \ge 3$) is **not** simple: $A_n \trianglelefteq S_n$ is a proper normal subgroup;
- $A_4$ is **not** simple: $V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$ is normal.

⭐ The relation between “simple” and “non-solvable” is a clean chain: simple and solvable $\iff$ simple abelian $\iff$ cyclic of prime order.

So **non-abelian simple = the unsolvable brick**, and $A_5$ is the smallest one.
