The two coefficients are **uniquely** determined by the increment: if $A\Delta z + B\overline{\Delta z} = a\Delta z + b\overline{\Delta z} + o(\Delta z)$, then taking $\Delta z = t$ (with $t$ real) and $\Delta z = it$, and letting $t \to 0$, gives $A = a$, $B = b$.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ $\partial/\partial z$ and $\partial/\partial \bar z$ are called **Wirtinger derivatives**.

They are **not** difference-quotient limits: $\bar z$ behaves differently in different directions, so the difference quotient has no limit.

They are merely a pair of linear combinations of real partial derivatives, a bookkeeping device.
