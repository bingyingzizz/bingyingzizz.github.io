Statement

In plain words: a fractional linear transformation is the most "rigid" class of holomorphic bijections of the Riemann sphere — it sends circles (including lines) to circles and preserves the cross-ratio.

A map of the form

f(z)=az+bcz+d,a,b,c,d∈C, ad−bc≠0f(z) = \frac{az + b}{cz + d}, \qquad a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{C},\ ad - bc \ne 0f(z)=cz+daz+b​,a,b,c,d∈C, ad−bc=0

is called a fractional linear transformation (Möbius transformation).

It extends to a bijection of the Riemann sphere C^=C∪{∞}\hat{\mathbb{C}} = \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}C^=C∪{∞} onto itself: when c≠0c \ne 0c=0, f(−d/c)=∞f(-d/c) = \inftyf(−d/c)=∞ and f(∞)=a/cf(\infty) = a/cf(∞)=a/c;

when c=0c = 0c=0, f(∞)=∞f(\infty) = \inftyf(∞)=∞.

The inverse is again a fractional linear transformation:

f−1(w)=dw−b−cw+a.f^{-1}(w) = \frac{dw - b}{-cw + a}.f−1(w)=−cw+adw−b​.

Notes

⭐ ad−bc≠0ad - bc \ne 0ad−bc=0 is the nondegeneracy condition: when it equals 000, fff degenerates to a constant.

It also guarantees f′(z)=(ad−bc)/(cz+d)2≠0f'(z) = (ad - bc)/(cz + d)^2 \ne 0f′(z)=(ad−bc)/(cz+d)2=0, so fff is conformal everywhere (angle-preserving; see the entry on conformality).

⭐ Three basic building blocks: translation z↦z+bz \mapsto z + bz↦z+b, dilation-rotation z↦azz \mapsto azz↦az (a≠0a \ne 0a=0), inversion z↦1/zz \mapsto 1/zz↦1/z.

Every Möbius transformation is a composite of these, and ad−bc≠0ad - bc \ne 0ad−bc=0 guarantees that the composite does not collapse to a constant.

⭐ Geometric properties (Chapter 13): it sends circles or lines to circles or lines (collectively "generalized circles", which on the sphere are simply "circles");

and it preserves the cross-ratio:

(z1,z2;z3,z4)=(z1−z3)(z2−z4)(z1−z4)(z2−z3).(z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4) = \frac{(z_1 - z_3)(z_2 - z_4)}{(z_1 - z_4)(z_2 - z_3)}.(z1​,z2​;z3​,z4​)=(z1​−z4​)(z2​−z3​)(z1​−z3​)(z2​−z4​)​.

Thus fff is uniquely determined by the images of three points — the cross-ratio is a complete invariant of Möbius transformations.

⚠ The point ∞\infty∞ on the sphere is not "a very large number": it is a point adjoined by stereographic projection.

Only on the sphere is the statement "fff is a bijection" clean — on C\mathbb{C}C it always misses one or two points.
