## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The proof is two lines: divide $f$ by $(x - a)$ ($x - a$ is monic, so division with remainder applies); the remainder $r$ has degree $< 1$, so $r$ is a constant; substituting $x = a$ gives $f(a) = r$.

So "the remainder" is exactly "the value of the function".

⭐ This immediately gives **that the number of roots does not exceed the degree** (next entry), and "a linear polynomial always has a root".

⚠ $a$ must be an element of $K$: $x^{2} + 1$ has no root in $\mathbb{R}$, but of course it is not divisible by $(x - a)$ ($a \in \mathbb{R}$).
