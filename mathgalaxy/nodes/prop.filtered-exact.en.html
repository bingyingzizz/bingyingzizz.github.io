Statement

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set, filtered colimits are exact: when III is a filtered category, the colimit functor

lim→⁡:SetI⟶Set\varinjlim : \mathbf{Set}^{I} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Set}lim​:SetI⟶Set

preserves all finite limits.

Notes

"Exact" here amounts to one sentence: filtered colimits commute with finite limits. The most basic example is that it preserves finite products:

lim→⁡i(Xi×Yi)  ≅  (lim→⁡iXi)×(lim→⁡iYi)\varinjlim_{i} (X_{i} \times Y_{i}) \;\cong\; \bigl(\varinjlim_{i} X_{i}\bigr) \times \bigl(\varinjlim_{i} Y_{i}\bigr)ilim​​(Xi​×Yi​)≅(ilim​​Xi​)×(ilim​​Yi​)

The method is to take representatives on both sides and compare: an element of the filtered colimit Set\mathbf{Set}Set is exactly an element at some index, so "one representative on each side" can be lifted to "a pair at the same index".

Why this matters: when we discuss sheaves later, sheafification is built out of filtered colimits, and only filtered colimits fail to destroy finite limits, so this construction can coexist peacefully with finite limits (that is, the part encoding topological information).
