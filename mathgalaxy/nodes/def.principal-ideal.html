Statement draft

Let AAA be a commutative ring with identity and a∈Aa \in Aa∈A. The set

(a):=aA:={ ax:x∈A }(a) := aA := \{\, a x : x \in A \,\}(a):=aA:={ax:x∈A}

is an ideal of AAA, the principal ideal generated by aaa. If an ideal III, for some aaa, satisfies I=(a)I = (a)I=(a), then III is principal.

If every ideal of AAA is principal, then AAA is a principal ideal domain (PID; see also 主理想整环).

Notes draft

⭐ Examples: every ideal of Z\mathbb{Z}Z is (n)(n)(n), so Z\mathbb{Z}Z is a PID; over a field KKK, K[x]K[x]K[x] is also a PID.

⚠ In a general ring, "the multiples of aaa" need not be a two-sided ideal: in M2(R)M_2(\mathbb{R})M2​(R), E11M2E_{11} M_2E11​M2​ is not a two-sided ideal; for noncommutative rings one takes "the smallest two-sided ideal containing aaa".
