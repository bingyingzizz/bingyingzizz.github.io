thm.ring-monotone-sigma · 定理 · 环 · 单调类 · σ-环

Reviewed by the author. The Chinese text remains authoritative.

Abstract

For a ring A\mathfrak{A}A of sets, the following are equivalent: A\mathfrak{A}A is a σ\sigmaσ-ring, A\mathfrak{A}A is a monotone class, and A\mathfrak{A}A is closed under countable disjoint unions. The hypothesis that A\mathfrak{A}A is a ring cannot be dropped: a monotone class need not be closed under finite unions. This equivalence is the technical core of the monotone class theorem, since it reduces "true for the σ\sigmaσ-algebra" to "check a ring, then take monotone limits".

Statement

Let A\mathfrak{A}A be a ring. Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

  1. A\mathfrak{A}A is a σ\sigmaσ-ring;
  2. A\mathfrak{A}A is a monotone class;
  3. A\mathfrak{A}A is closed under countable disjoint unions.

Notes

Note that the hypothesis "A\mathfrak{A}A is a ring" cannot be dropped — a monotone class is in general not closed under finite unions, so being a monotone class by itself does not imply being a σ\sigmaσ-ring.

This theorem is the technical core of the monotone class theorem: it is precisely what lets one reduce "true for all σ\sigmaσ-algebras" to "first verify it on a ring, then pass to monotone limits".Which hypothesis each direction uses (all three conditions are purely about set operations, but the ring hypothesis cannot be dropped):

  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): a countable disjoint union is a special case of a countable union — free.
  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): increasing unions are immediate from the definition; for decreasing intersections take the first term minus the successive differences as a countable union (the whole set need not belong to the family).
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): the ring gives finite unions, and the monotone class keeps their increasing limit inside the family.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): set Bn=An∖⋃j<nAjB_n = A_n \setminus \bigcup_{j<n} A_jBn​=An​∖⋃j<n​Aj​; the ring guarantees every BnB_nBn​ lies in the family, and closure under disjoint unions finishes.
  • The ring hypothesis cannot be dropped: on a two-point set, the family of the two singletons is a monotone class, yet it does not contain their union.
