## Notes · **draft**
**Reading**: $F \downarrow G$ organizes all ways of "stretching an arrow from the world of $F$ to the world of $G$" into a category. **The objects are the arrows themselves** (together with their sources at both ends), and the morphisms are the pair of morphisms that let the arrows be compared.

**Notation**: the symbol in $F \downarrow G$ is a "comma", hence the name; some texts write $(F \downarrow G)$ or $(F/G)$.

⭐ **Why it appears everywhere**: universal properties are often "the **terminal/initial object** in some comma category".
