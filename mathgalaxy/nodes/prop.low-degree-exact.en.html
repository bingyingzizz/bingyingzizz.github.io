Statement

Let MMM be a GGG-module. The low-degree terms of the standard cochain complex give the exact sequence

0⟶MG⟶M→ δ0 Z1(G,M)⟶H1(G,M)⟶0,0 \longrightarrow M^{G} \longrightarrow M \xrightarrow{\ \delta^{0}\ } Z^{1}(G, M) \longrightarrow H^{1}(G, M) \longrightarrow 0 ,0⟶MG⟶M δ0 ​Z1(G,M)⟶H1(G,M)⟶0,

where (δ0m)(g)=gm−m(\delta^{0} m)(g) = g m - m(δ0m)(g)=gm−m and Z1(G,M)Z^{1}(G, M)Z1(G,M) is the group of crossed homomorphisms. In particular:

  • If GGG acts trivially on MMM, then δ0=0\delta^{0} = 0δ0=0, so MG=MM^{G} = MMG=M and H1(G,M)=Hom⁡(G,M)H^{1}(G, M) = \operatorname{Hom}(G, M)H1(G,M)=Hom(G,M);
  • For a short exact sequence 0→A→B→C→00 \to A \to B \to C \to 00→A→B→C→0 of GGG-modules, there is an exact sequence
0→AG→BG→CG→ ∂ H1(G,A)→H1(G,B)→H1(G,C).0 \to A^{G} \to B^{G} \to C^{G} \xrightarrow{\ \partial\ } H^{1}(G, A) \to H^{1}(G, B) \to H^{1}(G, C).0→AG→BG→CG ∂ ​H1(G,A)→H1(G,B)→H1(G,C).

The "trivial action" clause: δ0m\delta^{0} mδ0m is the function g↦gm−mg \mapsto g m - mg↦gm−m (not a constant function: for G=C2G = C_{2}G=C2​ acting by signs and m=1m = 1m=1, it takes 111 at 000 and −2-2−2 at the generator); it vanishes identically (that is, equals 000) only when the action is trivial, so then B1=0B^{1} = 0B1=0 and Z1=Hom⁡(G,M)Z^{1} = \operatorname{Hom}(G, M)Z1=Hom(G,M).

The second line is the first few terms of the long exact sequence: applying the right derived functor to 0→A→B→C→00 \to A \to B \to C \to 00→A→B→C→0 (namely R0=(−)GR^{0} = (-)^{G}R0=(−)G, R1=H1R^{1} = H^{1}R1=H1) gives the long exact sequence, and this segment is exactly that by definition; the connecting map ∂\partial∂ sends "fixed points of CCC" to "one-dimensional cohomology of AAA".

⭐ These two facts are the technical source of the concrete meaning of H1H^{1}H1 and H2H^{2}H2: the first says that H1H^{1}H1 is the space of "sections / crossed homomorphisms", and the second says that the obstruction to splitting a short exact sequence lands exactly in H1H^{1}H1 — the next two classification theorems add semantics to Z1Z^{1}Z1 and Z2Z^{2}Z2.
