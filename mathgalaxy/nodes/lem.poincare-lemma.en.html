Statement

What it does: it gives a clean answer to "when is a closed form exact" — as long as the region can be contracted to a point (star-shaped), closed implies exact for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1. So cohomology is not an analytic phenomenon but a topological one.

Let U⊆RnU \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}U⊆Rn be a star-shaped open set: there is p0∈Up_{0} \in Up0​∈U such that for every x∈Ux \in Ux∈U, the segment {p0+t(x−p0):t∈[0,1]}\{p_{0} + t(x - p_{0}) : t \in [0,1]\}{p0​+t(x−p0​):t∈[0,1]} lies entirely in UUU. Then for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1,

dω=0  ⟹  ∃ η∈Ωk−1(U) such that ω=dη.\mathrm{d}\omega = 0 \implies \exists\ \eta \in \Omega^{k-1}(U)\ \text{such that}\ \omega = \mathrm{d}\eta .dω=0⟹∃ η∈Ωk−1(U) such that ω=dη.

Equivalently: HdRk(U)=0H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) = 0HdRk​(U)=0 for all k≥1k \ge 1k≥1, while HdR0(U)=RH^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(U) = \mathbb{R}HdR0​(U)=R (a star-shaped region is connected).

The conclusion also holds for contractible open sets: star-shaped implies contractible, and the argument uses only the contraction homotopy, not the specific shape of a star-shaped region.
