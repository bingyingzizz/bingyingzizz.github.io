Statement

If 0→K∙→  f  L∙→  g  M∙→00 \to K^{\bullet} \xrightarrow{\;f\;} L^{\bullet} \xrightarrow{\;g\;} M^{\bullet} \to 00→K∙f​L∙g​M∙→0 is a short exact sequence of complexes, then in D(A)D(\mathcal{A})D(A) there is a derived triangle

K∙→  f  L∙→  g  M∙⟶K∙[1].K^{\bullet} \xrightarrow{\;f\;} L^{\bullet} \xrightarrow{\;g\;} M^{\bullet} \longrightarrow K^{\bullet}[1].K∙f​L∙g​M∙⟶K∙[1].

Notes

Essentially it says that this short exact sequence "is" a certain mapping-cone triangle (isomorphic in D(A)D(\mathcal{A})D(A) to K∙→fL∙→M(f)→K∙[1]K^{\bullet} \xrightarrow{f} L^{\bullet} \to M(f) \to K^{\bullet}[1]K∙f​L∙→M(f)→K∙[1]).

⭐ This is where long exact sequences come from: given the triangle, applying the homology functor (a triangle   ⟹  \implies⟹ long exact homology sequence) immediately yields the long exact sequence — see "long exact sequence of derived functors".
