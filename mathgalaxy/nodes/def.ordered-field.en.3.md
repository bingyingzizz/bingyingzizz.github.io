# ordered field　`def.ordered-field`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.ordered-field)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.ordered-field.md)

All the usual rules for inequalities over the rationals and the reals ultimately come down to this one axiom.

From the two axioms one immediately gets: **squares are nonnegative** ($a \ge 0 \implies a^2 \ge 0$;

$a \le 0 \implies -a \ge 0$ and $a^2 = (-a)^2 \ge 0$), in particular $1 = 1^2 > 0$, hence $-1 < 0$.

📌 **Completeness is not part of this definition**; it is one more axiom imposed on an ordered field: **every nonempty subset bounded above has a supremum**.

$\mathbb{Q}$ is the example of "ordered but not completable" — $\{q \in \mathbb{Q} : q^2 < 2\}$ is bounded above but has no supremum.
