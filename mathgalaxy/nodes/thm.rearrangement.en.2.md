## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The substance of the theorem is: **an absolutely convergent series is a "commutative sum"** — as with finite sums, the order of addition does not affect the result.

⭐ Proof idea: first prove it for nonnegative series (the partial sums of the rearrangement and of the original series bound each other), then split into positive and negative parts, or real and imaginary parts.

⚠ For conditionally convergent series the conclusion **fails completely** (see "Riemann's rearrangement theorem").

So "can it be rearranged" is the most intuitive test of absolute convergence.
