## Notes · **reviewed**
⭐ The proof goes by **nested closed intervals + diagonalisation** (see the edge): assume $\mathbb{R}$ can be arranged as $x_1, x_2, \ldots$, then bisect to build a nested sequence of closed intervals, ruling out the $x_n$ one by one, and use **completeness** to extract their unique common point — which differs from every $x_n$.
