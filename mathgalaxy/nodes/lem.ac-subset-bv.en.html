Statement

AC([a,b])⊆BV([a,b])AC([a, b]) \subseteq BV([a, b])AC([a,b])⊆BV([a,b])

Notes

Proof: by absolute continuity choose δ\deltaδ such that “total length <δ  ⟹  < \delta \implies<δ⟹ total variation <1< 1<1”. Split [a,b] into finitely many pieces each of length <δ< \delta<δ; then the variation of each piece is ≤1\le 1≤1, and summing gives ≤\le≤ the number of pieces — finite. ∎

⚠ The converse fails: the Cantor function is of bounded variation on [0,1] but not absolutely continuous. So the inclusion is strict.

⭐ This is the preparation for the “fundamental theorem of calculus TFAE” below: it guarantees (a)  ⟹  BV(a) \implies BV(a)⟹BV, hence one may speak of F′F'F′.
