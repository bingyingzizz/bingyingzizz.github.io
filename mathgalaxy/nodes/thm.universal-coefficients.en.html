thm.universal-coefficients

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let RRR be a principal ideal domain, C∙C_{\bullet}C∙​ a chain complex free in every degree, and AAA an RRR-module. Then for every nnn there is a natural short exact sequence

0→Hn(C)⊗RA→Hn(C⊗RA)→Tor⁡1R(Hn−1(C), A)→0.0 \to H_{n}(C)\otimes_{R} A \to H_{n}\bigl(C\otimes_{R} A\bigr) \to \operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}\bigl(H_{n-1}(C),\ A\bigr) \to 0 .0→Hn​(C)⊗R​A→Hn​(C⊗R​A)→Tor1R​(Hn−1​(C), A)→0.

which splits (though the splitting is not usually natural: it depends on choosing a splitting).

Thus Hn(C⊗RA)H_{n}(C\otimes_{R} A)Hn​(C⊗R​A) is determined by Hn(C)⊗AH_{n}(C)\otimes AHn​(C)⊗A and a torsion term, the latter depending only on the next lower homology.

Notes

⭐ The most common use: take AAA to be a field (with Z\mathbb{Z}Z as the base and Q\mathbb{Q}Q as coefficients, or with a field kkk and kkk as coefficients); the Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor terms automatically vanish, so

Hn(C⊗Rk)≅Hn(C)⊗Rk,H_{n}(C\otimes_{R} k) \cong H_{n}(C)\otimes_{R} k ,Hn​(C⊗R​k)≅Hn​(C)⊗R​k,

"Extending coefficients to a field" merely tensors homology and produces no new classes. Conversely, when AAA has torsion, the extra piece comes from the next lower homology — this is the real content of the universal coefficient theorem.

⚠️ Two hypotheses: RRR a principal ideal domain (ensuring submodules are free) and C∙C_{\bullet}C∙​ free in every degree. Over a general ring one must replace this by "the coboundaries BnB_{n}Bn​ are all projective" (i.e. the complex splits in every degree), otherwise the first step of ① breaks.

📌 There is also a cohomology version (0→Ext⁡1(Hn−1(C),A)→Hn(Hom⁡(C,A))→Hom⁡(Hn(C),A)→00 \to \operatorname{Ext}^{1}(H_{n-1}(C), A) \to H^{n}(\operatorname{Hom}(C, A)) \to \operatorname{Hom}(H_{n}(C), A) \to 00→Ext1(Hn−1​(C),A)→Hn(Hom(C,A))→Hom(Hn​(C),A)→0), in which the roles of the two terms are exchanged from Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor to Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext.
