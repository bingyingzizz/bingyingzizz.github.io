(3) if there is $z_0 \ne 0$ with $|f(z_0)| = |z_0|$, or if $|f'(0)| = 1$, then $f$ is a rotation $f(z) = e^{i\theta} z$.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The proof route: $f(0) = 0$ makes $f(z)/z$ have a removable singularity at $0$; filling in $f'(0)$ gives a holomorphic function $g$ on the disk. On $|z| = r$ one has $|g| \le 1/r$, and the maximum modulus principle followed by $r \to 1$ gives the result.

⭐ The three conclusions are really one chain of inequalities: $|g(z)| \le 1$ immediately yields (1) and (2); (3) is "when equality holds in the maximum modulus principle".
