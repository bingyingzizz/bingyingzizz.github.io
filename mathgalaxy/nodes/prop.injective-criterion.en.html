Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category and I∈AI \in \mathcal{A}I∈A. The following are equivalent:

  1. III is an injective object;
  2. every monomorphism I↣MI \rightarrowtail MI↣M admits a retraction M→IM \to IM→I;
  3. the functor Hom⁡A(−,I)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-, I)HomA​(−,I) is exact;
  4. every short exact sequence 0→I→M→M′′→00 \to I \to M \to M'' \to 00→I→M→M′′→0 splits.

Moreover: if 0→M′→M→M′′→00 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 00→M′→M→M′′→0 is exact and M′M'M′ is injective, then

MMM injective   ⟺  M′′\iff M\prime\prime⟺M′′ injective

Notes

The proof uses a 3×33 \times 33×3 Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom diagram: stretch the short exact sequence 0→A→B→C→00 \to A \to B \to C \to 00→A→B→C→0 into three rows by the family Hom⁡(−,M)\operatorname{Hom}(-, M)Hom(−,M), read the middle row 0→ker⁡f→ker⁡g→ker⁡h→coker⁡f→coker⁡g→00 \to \ker f \to \ker g \to \ker h \to \operatorname{coker} f \to \operatorname{coker} g \to 00→kerf→kerg→kerh→cokerf→cokerg→0 off two applications of the snake lemma, and observe that the first and third rows are exact, so ker⁡g=coker⁡g=0\ker g = \operatorname{coker} g = 0kerg=cokerg=0.

The four conditions form a cycle, but each step costs differently (which is why injectivity has so many "equivalent formulations" yet is always verified through the same one):

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (3): almost a rewriting of the definition. Hom⁡(−,I)\operatorname{Hom}(-,I)Hom(−,I) is exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ it sends monomorphisms to epimorphisms (the other two exactness conditions are automatic for Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom, which is left exact). This step does not need enough injectives in the category.
  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): one line: given a monomorphism ι:I↣M\iota : I \rightarrowtail Mι:I↣M, injectivity gives r:M→Ir : M \to Ir:M→I with rι=idIr\iota = \mathrm{id}_{I}rι=idI​ — that is the retraction.
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): this step uses the "lifting" argument of Baer's criterion: given a monomorphism M′↣MM' \rightarrowtail MM′↣M and f:M′→If : M' \to If:M′→I, form the pushout, turn fff into I↣PI \rightarrowtail PI↣P and take a retraction, obtaining M→IM \to IM→I making the diagram commute. This is the only step of the four with real work.
  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (4): only for short exact sequences with III at the left end. A general short exact sequence splits   ⟺  \iff⟺ its left end is injective (or its right end projective); so the correct reading of (4) is "every sequence of the form 0→I→M→M′′→00 \to I \to M \to M'' \to 00→I→M→M′′→0 splits".

One supplement uses Zorn (crucial): in the category of RRR-modules, the Bolzano–Weierstrass-style argument — "III injective   ⟺  \iff⟺ for every ideal J⊆RJ \subseteq RJ⊆R, every J→IJ \to IJ→I extends to R→IR \to IR→I" (Baer's criterion) — uses the Zorn lemma (take a maximal extendable submodule). So the convenience "injectivity can be tested on ideals" carries the cost of the axiom of choice; in a general Grothendieck category the analogue is "test on subobjects", which needs no AC.

⚠️ Hypothesis of the second assertion: when M′M'M′ is injective, MMM injective   ⟺  M′′\iff M''⟺M′′ injective — this is "closure of injective objects under extensions", using only the short exact sequence and (4), and does not need A\mathcal{A}A to have enough injectives. Do not confuse it with the category-level assumption "A\mathcal{A}A has enough injectives".
