prop.injective-criterion

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category and I∈AI \in \mathcal{A}I∈A. The following are equivalent:

  1. III is an injective object;
  2. every monomorphism I↣MI \rightarrowtail MI↣M admits a retraction M→IM \to IM→I;
  3. the functor Hom⁡A(−,I)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-, I)HomA​(−,I) is exact;
  4. every short exact sequence 0→I→M→M′′→00 \to I \to M \to M'' \to 00→I→M→M′′→0 splits.

Moreover: if 0→M′→M→M′′→00 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 00→M′→M→M′′→0 is exact and M′M'M′ is injective, then

MMM injective   ⟺  M′′\iff M\prime\prime⟺M′′ injective

Notes

A Zorn-dependent supplement (crucial): in the category of RRR-modules, the Bolzano–Weierstrass-style argument — "III is injective   ⟺  \iff⟺ for every ideal J⊆RJ \subseteq RJ⊆R, every J→IJ \to IJ→I extends to R→IR \to IR→I" (Baer's criterion) — uses Zorn's lemma (take a maximal extendable submodule). So the convenience "injectivity can be tested on ideals" costs the axiom of choice; the corresponding statement in a general Grothendieck category tests on subobjects and needs no AC.

⚠️ The hypothesis of the second assertion: when M′M'M′ is injective, MMM injective   ⟺  M′′\iff M''⟺M′′ injective — this is "extensions of injectives are injective", which uses only short exact sequences and (4), and does not need A\mathcal{A}A to have enough injectives. Do not confuse it with the category-level hypothesis "A\mathcal{A}A has enough injectives".
