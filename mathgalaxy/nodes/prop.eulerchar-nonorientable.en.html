Statement

Let SSS be a closed nonorientable surface already in standard form, the connected sum of kkk copies of RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 (k≥1k\ge1k≥1, see classification of closed surfaces; kkk is called the nonorientable genus). Then

χ(S)=2−k.\chi(S) = 2 - k .χ(S)=2−k.

Notes

This proposition assumes standard form. Here SSS is the connected sum of kkk copies of RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 (k≥1k\ge1k≥1), so the computation below needs no classification theorem. Reduction of a general closed nonorientable surface to standard form belongs to classification of closed surfaces (still a skeleton) and is outside the scope of the proposition.

1. Triangulating RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2. Identify antipodal points of the regular icosahedron (121212 vertices, 303030 edges, and 202020 faces) to obtain a triangulation of RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 with 666 vertices, 151515 edges, and 101010 faces, so χ(RP2)=6−15+10=1=2−1\chi(\mathbb{RP}^{2})=6-15+10=1=2-1χ(RP2)=6−15+10=1=2−1. The antipodal action is free and simplicial (each simplex is distinct from its antipodal image), so the quotient complex is still a simplicial complex.

2. The connected sum formula. Let M,NM,NM,N be triangulated closed surfaces. Choose a 222-simplex in each and remove its interior, leaving a surface with triangular boundary. Glue the two triangular boundaries by matching vertices to obtain a triangulation of M#NM\#NM#N. The counts are V=VM+VN−3V=V_{M}+V_{N}-3V=VM​+VN​−3, E=EM+EN−3E=E_{M}+E_{N}-3E=EM​+EN​−3, and F=(FM−1)+(FN−1)=FM+FN−2F=(F_{M}-1)+(F_{N}-1)=F_{M}+F_{N}-2F=(FM​−1)+(FN​−1)=FM​+FN​−2, so χ(M#N)=χ(M)+χ(N)−2\chi(M\#N)=\chi(M)+\chi(N)-2χ(M#N)=χ(M)+χ(N)−2.
