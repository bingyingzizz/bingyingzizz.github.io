Statement

In plain words: a continuous function whose integral along every closed triangle (equivalently, every small rectangle) is zero is holomorphic — this is the converse of Cauchy's theorem.

Let Ω⊂C\Omega \subset \mathbb{C}Ω⊂C be an open set (no connectedness needed — holomorphy is checked pointwise, applying Morera on a small disk at each point), and f:Ω→Cf: \Omega \to \mathbb{C}f:Ω→C continuous. If for every closed triangle T⊂ΩT \subset \OmegaT⊂Ω we have

∮∂Tf(ζ) dζ=0,\oint_{\partial T} f(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta = 0,∮∂T​f(ζ)dζ=0,

then fff is holomorphic on Ω\OmegaΩ; moreover f′f'f′ is also holomorphic, and fff has a Taylor expansion at every point.

Replacing "closed triangle" by "closed rectangle with sides parallel to the coordinate axes" leaves the conclusion unchanged (a rectangle is split into two triangles along a diagonal).

Notes

⭐ Use: this is the most convenient tool for testing holomorphy — no a priori differentiability is needed, only "continuous" plus "some integral vanishes identically". Holomorphy is traded for an integral equation.

⭐ The most-used corollary: the limit of a locally uniformly convergent sequence of holomorphic functions is holomorphic. Because ∂T\partial T∂T is compact, ∮∂Tfk=0\oint_{\partial T} f_k = 0∮∂T​fk​=0 can be taken to the limit to get ∮∂Tf=0\oint_{\partial T} f = 0∮∂T​f=0, which is then fed to Morera. Hurwitz's theorem is the "zero version" of this.

⚠ Continuity cannot be dropped. Without it, the same integral condition does not imply differentiability.

⚠ The hypothesis "all triangles" can be replaced by "all circles": under continuity, it already suffices that the integral over every circle inside a closed disk vanishes to guarantee holomorphy — convolving with a mollifier keeps the circle condition, the expansion in small circles gives ∂zˉfε=0\partial_{\bar z} f_\varepsilon = 0∂zˉ​fε​=0, and then one takes a locally uniform limit.

📌 Together with Cauchy–Goursat: when fff is continuous, "fff holomorphic" and "fff has zero integral along every closed triangle" are equivalent statements.
