Statement

Let n≥1n \ge 1n≥1. One says aaa and bbb are congruent modulo nnn, written a≡b(modn)a \equiv b \pmod na≡b(modn), if n∣(a−b)n \mid (a - b)n∣(a−b).

Congruence is an equivalence relation on Z\mathbb{Z}Z, compatible with addition and multiplication: from a≡ba \equiv ba≡b and c≡dc \equiv dc≡d one obtains a+c≡b+da + c \equiv b + da+c≡b+d and ac≡bdac \equiv bdac≡bd.

Notes

"Equivalence relation + compatible with operations" is precisely the general definition of a congruence relation; quotienting by it gives the quotient ring Z/nZ\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}Z/nZ.

For n=0n = 0n=0 one sets a≡b(mod0)  ⟺  a=ba \equiv b \pmod 0 \iff a = ba≡b(mod0)⟺a=b: congruence modulo 000 is equality.

Congruence turns "divisibility" into "equality in a finite ring", the most central step of elementary number theory: applying Lagrange's theorem to Z/nZ\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}Z/nZ later yields Euler's theorem and Fermat's little theorem.
