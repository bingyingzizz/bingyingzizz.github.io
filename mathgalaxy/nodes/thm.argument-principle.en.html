Statement

In plain words: the number of turns made by a holomorphic function along a closed curve equals the number of zeros minus the number of poles inside the curve (both counted with multiplicity) — counting the change of argument is the same as counting zeros and poles.

Let γ\gammaγ be a positively oriented, simple piecewise smooth closed curve (counterclockwise, no self-intersection), let Ω\OmegaΩ contain γ\gammaγ and its interior, let fff be holomorphic on Ω\OmegaΩ except at finitely many poles, and suppose γ\gammaγ contains neither zeros nor poles. Then

12πi∮γf′(z)f(z) dz=Z−P=n(f∘γ, 0),\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma} \frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,\mathrm{d}z = Z - P = n(f \circ \gamma,\, 0),2πi1​∮γ​f(z)f′(z)​dz=Z−P=n(f∘γ,0),

where ZZZ is the number of zeros of fff inside γ\gammaγ (counted with multiplicity), PPP is the number of poles (counted with order), and nnn is the winding number.

Notes

⭐ The most common special case: when fff is holomorphic inside, P=0P = 0P=0 and hence ∮γf′/f=2πiZ\oint_{\gamma} f'/f = 2\pi i Z∮γ​f′/f=2πiZ — "the number of zeros" becomes an integral.

⭐ Geometric reading: f∘γf \circ \gammaf∘γ is a closed curve around 000, and Z−PZ - PZ−P is the number of times it winds around 000. Zeros twist the image curve one turn outward, poles one turn inward.

⚠ There must be no zeros or poles on the boundary — otherwise f′/ff'/ff′/f is undefined on γ\gammaγ and the integral does not exist at all.

⭐ A by-product: Z−PZ - PZ−P is invariant under small deformations of the curve (as long as no zero or pole is swept), because it is an integer and varies continuously.

📌 Every technique for "counting zeros" (Rouché, Hurwitz) starts from this identity; it also explains why zeros and poles can cancel each other at the level of integrals.

⚠ "Simple" cannot be dropped: the assertion is the unweighted Z−PZ - PZ−P, valid only for positively oriented simple closed curves.
