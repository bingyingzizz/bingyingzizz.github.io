## Notes · **draft**
⚠ **The hypothesis that $X$ is complete cannot be dropped.** The reason is the middle step: $A$ is complete as a subspace $\iff A$ closed in $X$ — this step uses completeness of $X$. Without it, $A$ closed and $A$ totally bounded does not imply $A$ compact.

This is also a generalization of **Heine–Borel**: $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is complete, and in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, $A$ bounded $\iff A$ totally bounded, so "bounded closed sets are compact" is exactly the special case.

**Proof.**
- **$\implies$**: $A$ compact $\implies$ $A$ closed in $X$ (the previous item).
