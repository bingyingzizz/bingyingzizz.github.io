Statement

For a normed space EEE, the closed unit ball BE={x∈E:∥x∥≤1}B_E = \{x \in E : \|x\| \le 1\}BE​={x∈E:∥x∥≤1} is compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ dim⁡E<∞\dim E < \inftydimE<∞.

Notes

"⟸\Longleftarrow⟸": in finite dimension all norms are equivalent (有限维范数等价), so compactness can be tested using the coordinate ball of ∥⋅∥∞\|\cdot\|_\infty∥⋅∥∞​;

the coordinate ball B‾∞(0,1)\overline{B}^{\infty}(0,1)B∞(0,1) is bounded and closed (in the real case it is [−1,1]n[-1,1]^{n}[−1,1]n; in the complex case it is the unit polydisc, regarded as a closed bounded subset of R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n), and is compact by Heine–Borel (有界闭 ⟺ 紧（ℝⁿ）).

∎

"  ⟹  \implies⟹": if dim⁡E=∞\dim E = \inftydimE=∞, use Riesz's lemma (几乎正交引理) to construct recursively unit vectors with ∥en−em∥≥1/2\|e_n - e_m\| \ge 1/2∥en​−em​∥≥1/2 (n≠mn \ne mn=m); such a sequence has no convergent subsequence, so the closed unit ball is not sequentially compact and hence not compact.

⭐ This is one half of "finite-dimensional if and only if locally compact": finite-dimensional normed spaces are locally compact, while infinite-dimensional ones are never locally compact. It is also the precise version of "bounded closed sets are compact" holding only in finite dimension — Heine–Borel fails in infinite dimension.
