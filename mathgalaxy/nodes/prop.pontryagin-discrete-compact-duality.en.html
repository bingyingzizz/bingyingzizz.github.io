prop.pontryagin-discrete-compact-duality

Statement

Let MMM be a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group and let M^=CZ(M,T)\widehat{M} = C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{T})M=CZ​(M,T) be its Pontryagin dual. Then

  • MMM is discrete   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat{M}M is compact Hausdorff;
  • MMM is compact Hausdorff   ⟺  \iff⟺ M^\widehat{M}M is discrete.

Thus the duality functor exchanges the categories of discrete abelian groups and compact Hausdorff abelian groups.

Notes

Why this holds. T=R/Z\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}T=R/Z is compact: the continuous homomorphisms from the discrete group MMM to the compact group T\mathbb{T}T form a compact space in the compact-open topology (the product TM\mathbb{T}^{M}TM is compact, and closed subsets of compact spaces are compact);

conversely, when M^\widehat{M}M is compact, M=M^^M = \widehat{\widehat{M}}M=M is discrete.

Two examples: Z^≅T\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \mathbb{T}Z≅T (discrete →\to→ compact), T^≅Z\widehat{\mathbb{T}} \cong \mathbb{Z}T≅Z (compact →\to→ discrete).

⭐ This illustrates the origin of the idea of using compact Hausdorff spaces to probe other objects: A discrete group can be turned into a compact Hausdorff space by duality. Taking quotients or subgroups of discrete groups then becomes taking closed subspaces or quotients on the compact side, turning these into topological problems.

📌 This is one half of the Pontryagin duality dictionary (the other half concerns torsion and connectedness).

Proof.

  • MMM discrete   ⟹  M^\implies \widehat M⟹M compact: Take a surjection Z(I)↠M\mathbb{Z}^{(I)} \twoheadrightarrow MZ(I)↠M. Duality turns the surjection into an injection (by precomposition), and its image is closed (M^↪TI\widehat M \hookrightarrow \mathbb{T}^{I}M↪TI is a closed embedding).

T\mathbb{T}T is compact Hausdorff, and TI\mathbb{T}^{I}TI is compact. Closed subsets of compact spaces are compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ M^\widehat MM is compact ✓.

  • M^\widehat MM compact   ⟹  M\implies M⟹M discrete: By the duality theorem (a self-equivalence), M≅M^^M \cong \widehat{\widehat M}M≅M. Since M^\widehat MM is compact, the preceding direction (replacing MMM with M^\widehat MM) gives the dual of the dual of MMM ... A more direct attempt would be to start from the compact group M^\widehat MM and infer compactness of its dual M^^=M\widehat{\widehat M} = MM=M from the preceding direction. The required claim, however, is that the dual of a compact group is discrete. Take the upper half-plane U⊆TU\subseteq\mathbb{T}U⊆T (Im⁡z>0\operatorname{Im} z > 0Imz>0), whose closure omits the identity. Then the image of any continuous homomorphism M^→T\widehat M \to \mathbb{T}M→T lies in a connected component of some T\mathbb{T}T. Since M^\widehat MM is compact and characters separate points, M^^\widehat{\widehat M}M has a discrete neighborhood at the origin, so M=M^^M = \widehat{\widehat M}M=M is discrete ✓.
  • Compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ discrete dual is the same as the second direction above (duality is a self-equivalence, and its exchanges of discrete and compact groups are inverse to each other) ✓. ∎

Hypotheses used in each direction (the forward compactness proof and the biduality theorem play different roles):

  • MMM discrete   ⟹  \implies⟹ M^\widehat MM compact Hausdorff: The characters form a closed subgroup of TM\mathbb T^MTM, and the compact-open topology equals the topology of pointwise convergence. This uses Tychonoff's theorem, usually in the framework of the axiom of choice, and goes beyond unpacking the definition.
  • MMM compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ M^\widehat MM discrete: Take a small arc about the identity containing no nontrivial subgroup. Requiring a character to take values in that arc on the entire compact group leaves only the zero character. This uses the definition of the compact-open topology and the no-small-subgroups property of the circle group.
  • M^\widehat MM compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ MMM discrete and M^\widehat MM discrete   ⟹  \implies⟹ MMM compact: Apply the first two statements, respectively, and use the Pontryagin biduality isomorphism. Local compactness, the Hausdorff property, and commutativity are hypotheses of the duality theorem used here.
  • The upper semicircle used in the original note omits the identity and cannot give a neighborhood of the zero character. Use a small arc containing the identity. Both directions of the exchange between discreteness and compactness require proof. Circle-valued characters of a nonabelian group see only its abelianization and cannot recover the original group.
