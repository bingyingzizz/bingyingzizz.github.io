**Hypotheses used in each direction** (invertibility in the homotopy category is entirely an unfolding of the quotient-category definition):
- $[f]$ invertible $\implies$ there is a homotopy inverse $g$: take a representative of the inverse morphism; morphisms in the quotient category are homotopy classes, so the composite class equals the identity class precisely in the two homotopies listed. No injectivity or boundedness is required.
