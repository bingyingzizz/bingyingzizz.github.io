# Quasi-Compact and Quasi-Separated Condensed Sets　`thm.qcqs-chaus-equiv`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.qcqs-chaus-equiv)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.qcqs-chaus-equiv.md)

**Why item 1 uses a "free" $F$.** The general definition of "quasi-compact" is "every cover has a finite subcover", a purely logical condition. This item replaces it by a **geometric** condition: one can map **onto** the object from a (possibly very large) free compact Hausdorff piece. Hence quasi-compact becomes "can be covered by a compact Hausdorff piece".

**What "free" means**: things like $\mathbb{Z}[S]$, generated by a set of points with no extra relations — being able to fit means "there are enough points to cover".
