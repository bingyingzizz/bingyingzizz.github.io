Notes

Only open sets are used: the definition mentions no metric and no Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn — compactness is a purely topological property, so it is preserved by continuous maps and invariant under homeomorphisms (连续映射 / 同胚).

Equivalent form (closed sets): every family of closed sets with the finite intersection property has non-empty intersection — that is, "{Fi}\{F_{i}\}{Fi​} closed and every finite subfamily has non-empty intersection   ⟹  \implies⟹ the whole family has non-empty intersection". This form is the one most often used in proofs by contradiction.

In metric spaces: on a metric space, "compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ sequentially compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ complete + totally bounded" (紧的三个等价刻画) — that is a theorem (provable only with the metric), not part of the definition. In a general topological space compactness and sequential compactness imply neither of the other: [0,ω1)[0, \omega_{1})[0,ω1​) with the order topology is sequentially compact but not compact (the cover {[0,α):α<ω1}\{[0, \alpha) : \alpha < \omega_{1}\}{[0,α):α<ω1​} has no finite subcover); {0,1}P(N)\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})}{0,1}P(N) is compact by Tychonoff but not sequentially compact (take xn(A)=1  ⟺  n∈Ax_{n}(A) = 1 \iff n \in Axn​(A)=1⟺n∈A; every subsequence fails to converge at the coordinate A={n2k}A = \{n_{2k}\}A={n2k​}).
