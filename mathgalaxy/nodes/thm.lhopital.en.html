Notes

⭐ It replaces "the ratio of two things that both tend to zero" by "the ratio of the derivatives" — the only justification is Cauchy's mean value theorem, used in a converse-style habit: first compute the derivative ratio, which is easier, then declare the original ratio equal to it.

⚠ Do not forget the three hypotheses: an indeterminate form (0/00/00/0 or ∞/∞\infty/\infty∞/∞; something like 0∞\dfrac{0}{\infty}∞0​ is directly 000 and does not need it), g′≠0g' \ne 0g′=0, and the existence of the limit on the right.

The last one is the most dangerous: when lim⁡f′/g′\lim f'/g'limf′/g′ does not exist, the right side need not equal the left — apart from saying nothing, one can say nothing (a typical example: f=x+sin⁡xf = x + \sin xf=x+sinx, g=xg = xg=x).

⚠ The proof for the "∞/∞\infty/\infty∞/∞ type" is a little more involved than for the "0/00/00/0 type" (one first truncates using the monotonicity of ggg), but the conclusion is the same.
