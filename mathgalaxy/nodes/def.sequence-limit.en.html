That is: no matter how small ε\varepsilonε is given, from some index on all terms lie in the xxx-neighbourhood of ε\varepsilonε.

A sequence that does not converge is called divergent.

Notes

⭐ This is the "ε\varepsilonε-NNN language": it turns "getting closer and closer" into a condition that can be checked one step at a time.

Every proof in analysis rests on this format.

Basic properties (all verified directly from the definition):

  • the limit is unique;
  • a convergent sequence is bounded;
  • limits commute with the four arithmetic operations (when the limit of the denominator is nonzero).

⚠ "Bounded" does not guarantee convergence ((−1)n(-1)^n(−1)n);

the order "ε\varepsilonε fixed, NNN pushed later" cannot be reversed.
