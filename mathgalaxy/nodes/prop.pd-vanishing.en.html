Statement

Let MMM be an RRR-module and let n≥0n \ge 0n≥0. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. pd⁡RM≤n\operatorname{pd}_{R} M \le npdR​M≤n.
  2. The nnnth syzygy ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM is projective (equivalently, MMM has a projective resolution of length nnn).
  3. For every RRR-module NNN and every k>nk > nk>n, we have Ext⁡Rk(M,N)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{R}(M, N) = 0ExtRk​(M,N)=0.
  4. For every RRR-module NNN, we have Ext⁡Rn+1(M,N)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}_{R}(M, N) = 0ExtRn+1​(M,N)=0.

Dually, id⁡RM≤n\operatorname{id}_{R} M \le nidR​M≤n if and only if Ext⁡Rk(N,M)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{k}_{R}(N, M) = 0ExtRk​(N,M)=0 holds for every NNN and every k>nk > nk>n.

Notes

Proof. (1)  ⟹  (3)(1) \implies (3)(1)⟹(3): Take a length-nnn projective resolution P∙P_{\bullet}P∙​. The truncated resolution is zero at k>nk > nk>n, so Hom⁡(P∙,N)\operatorname{Hom}(P_{\bullet}, N)Hom(P∙​,N) is also zero in degree k>nk > nk>n, and hence its cohomology vanishes ✓ (this uses the fact that projective and injective resolutions compute the same group, as in the two ways of computing Ext). (3)  ⟹  (4)(3) \implies (4)(3)⟹(4): Take k=n+1k = n+1k=n+1 ✓. (4)  ⟹  (1)(4) \implies (1)(4)⟹(1): Take any projective resolution and compute K:=ΩnMK := \Omega^{n}MK:=ΩnM. Applying dimension shifting nnn times gives Ext⁡R1(K,N)≅Ext⁡Rn+1(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(K, N) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}_{R}(M, N)ExtR1​(K,N)≅ExtRn+1​(M,N), which vanishes for every NNN ✓. The vanishing of Ext⁡R1(K,−)\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(K, -)ExtR1​(K,−) in every case forces KKK to be projective. If KKK were not projective, a surjection F↠KF \twoheadrightarrow KF↠K with FFF free would fail to split. The classification of first extensions then gives Ext⁡R1(K,ker⁡)≠0\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(K, \ker) \ne 0ExtR1​(K,ker)=0, a contradiction ✓.

Thus MMM has a projective resolution of length nnn ✓, giving (1)(1)(1). (1)  ⟺  (2)(1) \iff (2)(1)⟺(2): For a fixed resolution, this restates the definition. Passing to a different resolution requires Schanuel's lemma (stability of syzygies) to show that projectivity of the nnnth syzygy is independent of the resolution ✓. ∎

Of the four conditions, 2   ⟹  \implies⟹ 3 is the only substantive step, and it requires a long exact sequence and induction:

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (2): This unpacks the definition for a fixed resolution (pd≤n\mathrm{pd} \le npd≤n means there is a projective resolution of length nnn). Projectivity of the nnnth syzygy is a statement independent of the resolution by Schanuel's lemma / stability of syzygies (Stability of Syzygies).
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): This is the substantive step. Use the projective resolution ⋯→P1→P0→M→0\cdots \to P_{1} \to P_{0} \to M \to 0⋯→P1​→P0​→M→0 to compute Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext. The group Ext⁡k(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(M,N)Extk(M,N) is the cohomology computed from the resolution, and a resolution of length nnn has no term at k>nk > nk>n, so the group vanishes. ⚠️ This uses the fact that projective resolutions compute Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext (dimension shifting / balancedness), which itself requires proof.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (4): Special case (k=n+1k = n+1k=n+1).
  • (4)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): This step uses dimension shifting in reverse. If Ext⁡n+1(M,−)\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, -)Extn+1(M,−) always vanishes, take a partial resolution of length nnn whose nnnth syzygy KKK satisfies Ext⁡1(K,−)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(K, -) = 0Ext1(K,−)=0. Then KKK is projective (the converse in Ext Vanishes on Projectives). ⚠️ This step uses the projectivity criterion for Ext⁡1=0  ⟹  \operatorname{Ext}^{1} = 0 \impliesExt1=0⟹ in the module category (which has enough projectives, so no further hypothesis is needed). Care is needed in more general abelian categories.

⚠️ The case n=0n = 0n=0: pdM≤0\mathrm{pd} M \le 0pdM≤0   ⟺  \iff⟺ MMM is projective   ⟺  \iff⟺ Ext⁡k(M,−)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(M,-) = 0Extk(M,−)=0 for all k≥1k \ge 1k≥1. The assertion that Ext⁡1=0\operatorname{Ext}^{1} = 0Ext1=0 implies projectivity holds for arbitrary nnn: only the case n=0n = 0n=0 (that is, Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1) behaves this way.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the dimension-shifting criterion in module categories):

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ existence of a projective resolution of length nnn is the definition. If (2) refers to the nnnth syzygy in an arbitrarily chosen resolution, independence of the resolution also requires Schanuel's lemma or dimension shifting.
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): Truncating the projective resolution and using it to compute Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext requires the theorem on computation by resolutions. For (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (4), simply take k=n+1k=n+1k=n+1.
  • (4)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): Dimension shifting reduces the condition to Ext⁡1(ΩnM,N)=0\operatorname{Ext}^1(\Omega^nM,N)=0Ext1(ΩnM,N)=0 for all NNN, and splitting short exact sequences then gives projectivity. Module categories have enough projectives, with no requirement that the ring be a PID.
  • The quantifier over all NNN cannot be replaced by a single NNN. Taking N=0N=0N=0 gives vanishing for arbitrary MMM. For example, Z/2\mathbb Z/2Z/2 is not a projective Z\mathbb ZZ-module.

⭐ Practical form: To test the dimension, one generally computes just one group for each coefficient module. It suffices that Ext⁡Rn+1(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}_{R}(M, N)ExtRn+1​(M,N) vanish for every NNN, without checking every degree. Conversely, to determine how large pd⁡\operatorname{pd}pd is, find a witness for which Ext⁡n\operatorname{Ext}^{n}Extn is nonzero, namely some NNN.

⚠️ Keep the two directions straight: pd⁡RM\operatorname{pd}_{R} MpdR​M concerns Ext⁡(M,−)\operatorname{Ext}(M, -)Ext(M,−) (the first variable), and id⁡RN\operatorname{id}_{R} NidR​N concerns Ext⁡(−,N)\operatorname{Ext}(-, N)Ext(−,N) (the second variable).

📌 An immediate corollary: RRR is a semisimple ring   ⟺  \iff⟺ every module is projective   ⟺  gl.dim⁡R=0\iff \operatorname{gl.dim} R = 0⟺gl.dimR=0.
