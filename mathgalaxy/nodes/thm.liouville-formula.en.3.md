In particular, $\gamma$ is a geodesic if and only if $\theta$ satisfies this first-order equation. The change in direction of a geodesic is completely determined by the derivatives of the metric coefficients.

## Notes · **draft**
**Structure**: $k_{g} = \frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}s}-\omega^{1}_{2}(\dot\gamma)$, where the first term is the turning rate of the curve relative to the frame and the second is the turning rate of the frame itself.

Substituting $\omega^{1}_{2} = \frac{(\ln E)_{v}}{2\sqrt G}\omega^{1}-\frac{(\ln G)_{u}}{2\sqrt E}\omega^{2}$ gives the formula above.
