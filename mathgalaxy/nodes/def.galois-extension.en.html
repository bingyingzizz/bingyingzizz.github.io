Statement

A finite extension K/FK/FK/F is Galois if it is both normal and separable.

Equivalent conditions (any one of them):

  • ∣Gal⁡(K/F)∣=[K:F]|\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)| = [K:F]∣Gal(K/F)∣=[K:F];
  • F=KGal⁡(K/F)F = K^{\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)}F=KGal(K/F) (the elements fixed by the group are exactly FFF itself);
  • KKK is the splitting field over FFF of some separable polynomial.

Notes

⭐ Why the three conditions are equivalent: normal + separable ensures that roots go to roots and that roots are distinct, so automorphisms can spread over all [K:F][K:F][K:F] conjugates; conversely, enough automorphisms force normality and separability. The full proof of the equivalence is the backbone of the course; it is not expanded here.

⭐ The invariant is the fixed field: KG:={ x∈K:σ(x)=x  ∀σ∈G }K^{G} := \{\, x \in K : \sigma(x) = x\ \ \forall \sigma \in G \,\}KG:={x∈K:σ(x)=x  ∀σ∈G} is a subfield of KKK and automatically contains FFF; the Galois condition says precisely that it equals FFF, no more.

⚠ The most common misunderstanding: taking “splitting field = Galois extension”. What is missing is separability: in characteristic ppp the splitting field of xp−tx^{p} - txp−t over Fp(t)\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)Fp​(t) is inseparable, hence not Galois. In characteristic 0 the distinction disappears — which is why it is invisible at first.
