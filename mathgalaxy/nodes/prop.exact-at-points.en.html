prop.exact-at-points

Statement

Let 0→M′→ i M→ p M′′→00 \to M' \xrightarrow{\ i\ } M \xrightarrow{\ p\ } M'' \to 00→M′ i ​M p ​M′′→0 be a sequence in an abelian category. Then

  • short exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ is exact at M′M'M′, MMM, and M′′M''M′′ — all three places;
  • left exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ is exact at M′M'M′ and MMM;
  • right exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ is exact at MMM and M′′M''M′′.

Notes

⭐ Use: split the global matter "the whole sequence is exact" into pointwise checks; this is what is used in practice.

Hypotheses used in each direction (exactness at each place is a combination of definitions; elementwise judgement has further restrictions):

  • Short exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ exact at all three places, left exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ exact at the first two, right exact   ⟺  \iff⟺ exact at the last two: both directions are unfoldings of the definition of an exact sequence, and need no splitting, injective objects, or the axiom of choice.
  • Exactness at the left end   ⟺  ker⁡i=0  ⟺  i\iff\ker i=0\iff i⟺keri=0⟺i is a monomorphism, exactness at the right end   ⟺  coker⁡p=0  ⟺  p\iff\operatorname{coker}p=0\iff p⟺cokerp=0⟺p is an epimorphism: the first step uses the zero object, the second the kernel and cokernel criteria of an abelian category.
  • Exactness in the middle   ⟺  im⁡i=ker⁡p\iff\operatorname{im}i=\ker p⟺imi=kerp is by definition; "pointwise" means the positions of the objects in the sequence, and an abstract abelian category cannot use elements or set-theoretic surjections without interpretation.
  • Short exact does not imply split: 0→Z→2Z→Z/2→00\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2\to00→Z2​Z→Z/2→0 is a counterexample; checking only the middle is also not enough, since 0→Z→0→0→00\to\mathbb Z\to0\to0\to00→Z→0→0→0 is exact in the middle but not at the left end.
