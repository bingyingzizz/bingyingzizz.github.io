# K[x] is a unique factorisation domain　`thm.poly-ufd`

[in graph](../#n=thm.poly-ufd)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.poly-ufd.md)

and the decomposition is **unique** up to order and constant factor: if $f = c' q_1 \cdots q_s$ is another such decomposition, then $r = s$ and there is a permutation $\sigma$ such that $p_i = q_{\sigma(i)}$ (and of course $c = c'$).

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This is the **end point** of the division algorithm: Euclidean domain $\implies$ principal ideal domain $\implies$ unique factorization domain.

All three "domain" definitions along the chain are worth remembering, because each has counterexamples in abstract algebra (a UFD need not be a PID: $\mathbb{Z}[x]$;

a PID need not be a Euclidean domain: certain rings).
