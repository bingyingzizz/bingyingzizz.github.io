Notes

⭐ "Being bounded" and "attaining an extremum" are two different things, which is why they are separate nodes here: boundedness says only that sup⁡f(X)\sup f(X)supf(X) is finite, while attaining an extremum says that this supremum is actually reached at some point.

Compactness is responsible for the latter.

⚠ Replacing the space by an open interval makes it fail at once: f(x)=xf(x) = xf(x)=x is bounded on (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1), yet attains neither a maximum nor a minimum.

⭐ The commonly used form over R\mathbb{R}R: a continuous function on a closed interval [a,b][a, b][a,b] attains its extrema — the first step of the proof is that a closed interval is compact (Heine–Borel).

The whole procedure for finding extrema in elementary calculus (locate critical points and compare endpoints) rests on this.
