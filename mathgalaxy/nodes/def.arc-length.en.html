Statement

What it does: it gives a curve a ruler — accumulate the speed along the curve to get its length, then use length as the parameter to rewrite the curve at constant speed.

Let γ:[a,b]→R3\gamma : [a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}γ:[a,b]→R3 be a C1C^{1}C1 curve. Its arc length is

L(γ):=∫ab∥γ′(t)∥ dt,∥γ′(t)∥=x′(t)2+y′(t)2+z′(t)2.L(\gamma) := \int_{a}^{b}\lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert\,\mathrm{d}t ,\qquad \lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert = \sqrt{x'(t)^{2}+y'(t)^{2}+z'(t)^{2}} .L(γ):=∫ab​∥γ′(t)∥dt,∥γ′(t)∥=x′(t)2+y′(t)2+z′(t)2​.

The arc-length function measured from t0∈[a,b]t_{0}\in[a,b]t0​∈[a,b] is

s(t):=∫t0t∥γ′(u)∥ du,s′(t)=∥γ′(t)∥.s(t) := \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\lVert\gamma'(u)\rVert\,\mathrm{d}u ,\qquad s'(t) = \lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert .s(t):=∫t0​t​∥γ′(u)∥du,s′(t)=∥γ′(t)∥.

If ∥γ′(t)∥≡1\lVert\gamma'(t)\rVert\equiv 1∥γ′(t)∥≡1, one calls γ\gammaγ arc-length parametrized (unit-speed parametrized); then arc length is just the difference of parameters. A regular curve can always be reparametrized by arc length (the construction is on the edge); from then on one writes sss for the parameter and T:=γ′(s)T := \gamma'(s)T:=γ′(s) for the unit tangent vector.

Notes

Arc length is independent of the parametrization: L(γ∘h)=L(γ)L(\gamma\circ h) = L(\gamma)L(γ∘h)=L(γ) (with hhh monotone C1C^{1}C1), a one-line change of variables. This is the formal statement of "length belongs to the curve, not to the parametrization".

The arc-length parameter is unique up to a constant and an orientation: two arc-length parameters differ by a constant; traversing the curve backwards gives s↦−ss\mapsto -ss↦−s. Hence in formulas written in the arc-length parameter, every constant that appears has no geometric meaning, and every derivative that appears does.

Notation discipline: a dot means differentiation with respect to arc length (γ˙=T\dot\gamma = Tγ˙​=T, γ¨=κN\ddot\gamma = \kappa Nγ¨​=κN), while a prime means differentiation with respect to an arbitrary parameter. Mixing them produces a missing factor of ∥γ′∥\lVert\gamma'\rVert∥γ′∥ — the most common copying error in curve theory.

⚠️ Arc length uses only the inner product, no other structure of the ambient space, so it carries over verbatim to Riemannian manifolds: replace ∥γ′∥\lVert\gamma'\rVert∥γ′∥ by g(γ˙,γ˙)\sqrt{g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)}g(γ˙​,γ˙​)​ to get the length in Riemann 度量.

⚠️ Arc length is a one-dimensional measure; its two-dimensional counterpart is 面积元. Both are "writing the metric as an integrable quantity".
