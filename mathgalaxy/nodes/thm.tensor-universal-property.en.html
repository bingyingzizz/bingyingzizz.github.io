Statement

For every bilinear map B:V×W→UB : V \times W \to UB:V×W→U (a linear map on the product space need not be bilinear, e.g. B(v,w)=vB(v,w) = vB(v,w)=v), there is a unique linear map

B~:V⊗W→U,B~(v⊗w)=B(v,w)\widetilde{B} : V \otimes W \to U, \qquad \widetilde{B}(v \otimes w) = B(v, w)B:V⊗W→U,B(v⊗w)=B(v,w)

Conversely, any linear map B~\widetilde BB composed with ⊗\otimes⊗ gives a bilinear map. Hence there is a natural isomorphism

Hom⁡K(V⊗W, U)  ≅  Bil⁡(V,W;U)\operatorname{Hom}_K(V \otimes W,\ U) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Bil}(V, W; U)HomK​(V⊗W, U)≅Bil(V,W;U)

(natural in UUU; this is "the tensor product is the representation of the bilinear functor").

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: the tensor product is the linearization device for "multivariable linearity" — everything bilinear, once written as a map on V⊗WV \otimes WV⊗W, automatically becomes linear, and the whole machinery of linear algebra applies.

⭐ "Uniqueness" is the core of a universal property: it ensures V⊗WV \otimes WV⊗W is determined up to unique isomorphism (existence gives the construction, uniqueness gives "independence of the construction").

This is the third appearance of "the same trick" on the graph (tensor product, quotient space, exterior power).

⭐ It also holds over an arbitrary commutative ring (张量积对双线性映射万有); a coefficient field is just the simplest case.
