Abstract

Assuming the axiom of choice, any two sets AAA, BBB satisfy #A≤#B\#A \le \#B#A≤#B or #B≤#A\#B \le \#A#B≤#A, that is, any two cardinals are comparable. This is the licence for ordering cardinals at all: without it even "AAA is bigger than BBB" can be meaningless. The proof runs through the well-ordering theorem: well-order both sets, compare the corresponding ordinals, and read off an injection in one direction. Without AC there really are incomparable cardinals.

Statement

Under the axiom of choice: for any two sets AAA and BBB we have #A≤#B\#A \le \#B#A≤#B or #B≤#A\#B \le \#A#B≤#A.

That is, any two cardinals are comparable.

Notes

⭐ This is the licence to order cardinals: without it, even the sentence "AAA is bigger than BBB" can be meaningless.

Route of the proof (see the edge): the well-ordering theorem well-orders both AAA and BBB, the corresponding ordinals are comparable, and hence one direction admits an injection. So this theorem, AC and the well-ordering theorem are three ways of saying the same thing.

⚠ Without AC there really are incomparable cardinals (Jech, The Axiom of Choice).
