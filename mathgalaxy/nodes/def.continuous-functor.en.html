Statement

Let C,C′\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{C}'C,C′ be two sites. A functor

f−1:=g:C⟶C′f^{-1} := g : \mathcal{C} \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}'f−1:=g:C⟶C′

is called continuous if precomposition along ggg pulls sheaves on C′\mathcal{C}'C′ back to sheaves on C\mathcal{C}C:

g∗:C′~⟶C~,F⟼F∘gg^{*} : \widetilde{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}} \longrightarrow \widetilde{\mathcal{C}}, \qquad F \longmapsto F \circ gg∗:C′⟶C,F⟼F∘g

(it sends sheaves to sheaves, so it restricts to the sheaf categories.)

Notes

Reading: the direction of ggg is C→C′\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'C→C′, but sheaves are contravariant, so ggg pulls sheaves on C′\mathcal{C}'C′ back to C\mathcal{C}C. This "twist of direction" is the source of all awkwardness with morphisms of sites.

⭐ It is equivalent to: ggg pulls covering families of C′\mathcal{C}'C′ back to covering families of C\mathcal{C}C (the preimage of a covering sieve is again a covering sieve). So "continuous" = "covering relations are preserved".

Notation: the name f−1f^{-1}f−1 comes from topology: a continuous map f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y between spaces gives a functor f−1:Open⁡(Y)→Open⁡(X)f^{-1} : \operatorname{Open}(Y) \to \operatorname{Open}(X)f−1:Open(Y)→Open(X) between the categories of open sets (taking preimages), and sheaves are pulled back along it.

📌 Next step: add one more condition, "f−1f^{-1}f−1 is left exact", and one obtains a morphism of sites.
