Statement

Let (X,T)(X, \mathcal{T})(X,T) be a topological space and π:X↠Y\pi : X \twoheadrightarrow Yπ:X↠Y a surjection. The quotient topology on YYY is

TY:={ V⊆Y:π−1(V)∈T }\mathcal{T}_{Y} := \{\, V \subseteq Y : \pi^{-1}(V) \in \mathcal{T} \,\}TY​:={V⊆Y:π−1(V)∈T}

This is the finest topology making π\piπ continuous; YYY with this topology is the quotient space of XXX.

Notes

Intuition: the open sets of the quotient are those whose preimage is open. So properties of the quotient must be read "from above": whether two points in YYY can be separated by open sets asks whether their preimages can be separated by open sets of XXX.

The quotient and subspace topologies are opposite directions: one takes "the coarsest obtained by cutting", the other "the finest obtained by gluing back".

📌 The "quotient space" of topology is this one; the quotient of a vector space is 商空间.
