Counterexample: on $X = \mathbb{R}$, the family of all finite and cofinite sets is an algebra, while $\mathbb{N} = \{1\} \cup \{2\} \cup \{3\} \cup \cdots$ is a union of countably many of its elements and is itself neither finite nor cofinite, so that algebra is not closed under countable unions.

The "$\sigma$" in the notation means **countable** (from the initial letter of German Summe / French somme); it has nothing to do with the $\sigma$ of standard deviation.
