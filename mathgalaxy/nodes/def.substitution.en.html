def.substitution

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let xxx be a variable, ttt a term, φ\varphiφ a formula. Replacing, in φ\varphiφ, every free occurrence of xxx by ttt gives a formula written

φ(x/t)\varphi (x/t)φ(x/t)

called the substitution of ttt for xxx (bound occurrences are untouched — they are governed by quantifiers).

⚠ Substitution requires that ttt be, in φ\varphiφ, substitutable for xxx: variables occurring in ttt are not "captured" by quantifiers of φ\varphiφ after the substitution. This is called variable capture.

Notes

⚠ What capture looks like: take φ=∃y (y>x)\varphi = \exists y\, (y > x)φ=∃y(y>x), t=yt = yt=y. Direct substitution gives ∃y (y>y)\exists y\, (y > y)∃y(y>y) — the meaning is completely changed (it was "there is a number larger than xxx", and now it is a false statement). One then says that ttt, with respect to φ\varphiφ, is not substitutable for xxx.

⭐ Substitution is not a side note on the map: the whole content of the replacement axiom schema is "using one formula φ(x,y,p)\varphi (x, y, p)φ(x,y,p), collect the images in AAA of every xxx", and what happens there is exactly substitution.

⚠ The standard way to avoid capture is first to rename the conflicting bound variable (α\alphaα-renaming), then substitute. This is the most verbose but unavoidable step in logic textbooks.
