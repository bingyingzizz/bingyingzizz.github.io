Statement

Let M,NM, NM,N be RRR-modules. An extension of NNN by MMM is a short exact sequence

0→N→E→ π M→0.0 \to N \to E \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } M \to 0 .0→N→E π ​M→0.

Two extensions are equivalent if there is an isomorphism between them making both squares commute and inducing the identity on NNN and MMM. The equivalence classes are in bijection with Ext⁡R1(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(M, N)ExtR1​(M,N). The Baer sum makes these classes into an abelian group, and the bijection is a group isomorphism.

The split extension (E≅N⊕ME \cong N\oplus ME≅N⊕M) corresponds precisely to the zero element.

The zero element is N⊕MN\oplus MN⊕M (the split extension).

The inverse of EEE is obtained by replacing E→ME\to ME→M with the negative of N→EN\to EN→E, that is, in EEE reverse the sign of NNN ✓.

The bijection above sends the Baer sum to addition in Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1. When adding the classes of two extensions, the chosen splittings of the pullbacks can be combined, so the corresponding θ\thetaθ add ✓. ∎

⭐ This gives an interpretation of the elements of Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1: The group Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1 classifies actual middle terms. Thus Ext⁡1(M,N)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(M, N) = 0Ext1(M,N)=0 has a concrete meaning that can be checked by hand: every extension splits.

📌 Higher Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext groups also have an interpretation in terms of their elements. The group Ext⁡n(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, N)Extn(M,N) classifies extensions YYY of the form N→E1→⋯→En→MN \to E_{1}\to \cdots \to E_{n}\to MN→E1​→⋯→En​→M and of degree nnn (homotopy classes of exact sequences of length nnn).

The result for Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1 is the simplest case of n=1n = 1n=1.

⚠️ In the strict definition of equivalence, the isomorphism must be the identity at both ends. Allowing arbitrary isomorphisms at the ends gives a coarser classification by orbits of invertible elements in Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom, so the original correspondence is no longer one-to-one.
