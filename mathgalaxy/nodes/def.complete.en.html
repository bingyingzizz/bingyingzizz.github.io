Statement

A sequence {xn}\{x_{n}\}{xn​} is a Cauchy sequence if and only if

A metric space (X,d)(X, d)(X,d) is complete if and only if every Cauchy sequence in XXX converges in XXX:

{xn} Cauchy  ⟹  ∃x∈X:xn→x\{x_n\}\ \text{Cauchy} \implies \exists x \in X : x_n \to x{xn​} Cauchy⟹∃x∈X:xn​→x

Notes

“Convergent   ⟹  Cauchy\implies Cauchy⟹Cauchy” always holds; “Cauchy  ⟹  Cauchy \impliesCauchy⟹ converges” is the content of completeness.

Examples: Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, Cn\mathbb{C}^{n}Cn, and every Banach space are complete; Q\mathbb{Q}Q is not (3, 3.1, 3.14, …, a Cauchy sequence converging to 2\sqrt 22​ that does not converge in Q\mathbb{Q}Q).
