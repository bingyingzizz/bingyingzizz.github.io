Statement

The free module R(I)R^{(I)}R(I) is flat; more generally, projective modules are flat.

Concretely, M⊗RR(I)≅M(I)M \otimes_{R} R^{(I)} \cong M^{(I)}M⊗R​R(I)≅M(I) naturally (m⊗(ri)i↦(mri)im \otimes (r_{i})_{i} \mapsto (m r_{i})_{i}m⊗(ri​)i​↦(mri​)i​), and "taking the III-fold direct sum" detects exactness componentwise.

Notes

Proof: by the "unit" and "commutes with direct sums" properties in 张量积的函子性质,

(−)⊗RR(I)  ≅  ⨁i∈I(−),(-) \otimes_{R} R^{(I)} \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{i \in I} (-),(−)⊗R​R(I)≅i∈I⨁​(−),

the right-hand side is "taking the III-fold direct sum", which is exact (a direct sum of functors is exact, since injectivity is detected componentwise) ✓.

Projective modules are flat: a projective module is a direct summand of a free module (the remark after 投射 / 内射对象), and direct summands inherit flatness (平坦的直和项) ✓. ∎

⭐ Use: this guarantees that "projective resolutions are flat resolutions", so Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor can be computed either by projective or by flat resolutions; the latter are often much shorter (for example the flat resolution given by localization).

⚠️ The converse fails: flat does not imply projective (平坦但不投射).
