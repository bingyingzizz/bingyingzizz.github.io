Statement

Let A∈Mn(R)A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})A∈Mn​(R) be symmetric. The function it defines,

is called the quadratic form (it is a homogeneous quadratic function of xxx). One says that qqq (or AAA) is:

  • positive definite: x≠0  ⟹  q(x)>0x \ne 0 \implies q(x) > 0x=0⟹q(x)>0;
  • positive semidefinite: x≠0  ⟹  q(x)≥0x \ne 0 \implies q(x) \ge 0x=0⟹q(x)≥0;
  • negative definite / indefinite are defined similarly.

By the spectral theorem, qqq is positive definite   ⟺  \iff⟺ every eigenvalue of AAA is positive   ⟺  \iff⟺ there exists an invertible PPP with A=PTPA = P^{\mathsf{T}}PA=PTP (that is, AAA is congruent to a positive definite matrix).

Notes

⭐ A quadratic form is a relaxation of an inner product: an inner product requires positive definiteness, whereas a quadratic form may be indefinite (or even degenerate).

Hence every geometric conclusion for inner product spaces must be re-examined for quadratic forms ("length" may be negative).

⭐ Positive definite quadratic forms correspond one-to-one with inner products (⟨u,v⟩:=uTAv\langle u, v\rangle := u^{\mathsf{T}}Av⟨u,v⟩:=uTAv), and every inner product space can be turned into the standard Euclidean space by Gram–Schmidt — this is the meaning of "positive definite   ⟹  \implies⟹ realizable as PTPP^{\mathsf{T}}PPTP".

⭐ Indefinite quadratic forms appear in relativity (the Minkowski metric) and in Morse theory (the negative index of inertia of the Hessian = the type of a critical point).

Translating between "quadratic form / symmetric matrix / symmetric bilinear form" needs one polarization formula, and it requires char⁡≠2\operatorname{char} \ne 2char=2:

  • q(x)=xTAxq(x) = x^{\mathsf{T}}Axq(x)=xTAx (AAA symmetric): the convention "AAA symmetric" is already in force — a non-symmetric AAA gives the same qqq (because xTAxx^{\mathsf{T}}A xxTAx sees only the symmetric part), so quadratic forms correspond one-to-one with symmetric matrices.
  • q↔q \leftrightarrowq↔ a symmetric bilinear form BBB: by the polarization formula B(x,y)=12(q(x+y)−q(x)−q(y))B(x,y) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(q(x+y) - q(x) - q(y)\bigr)B(x,y)=21​(q(x+y)−q(x)−q(y)). ⚠️ The 12\frac{1}{2}21​ requires char⁡K≠2\operatorname{char} K \ne 2charK=2; in characteristic 222 quadratic forms and bilinear forms no longer correspond (which is why quadratic form theory in characteristic 2 needs separate treatment).
  • Positive definite   ⟺  \iff⟺ all eigenvalues positive   ⟺  \iff⟺ congruent to III: the first uses the spectral theorem (real symmetric); the third uses the law of inertia (惯性定理), which requires only invertible congruence, not orthogonality.

⚠️ Do not confuse "quadratic form" with "homogeneous quadratic polynomial": every homogeneous quadratic polynomial can be written as xTAxx^{\mathsf{T}}AxxTAx (take AAA symmetric), but in characteristic 222 the symmetrization of a cross term such as x1x2x_{1}x_{2}x1​x2​ degenerates.
