## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **It measures exactly “is adjoining one root enough”**: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is **not** normal — $x^{3} - 2$ has a root in it, but $\omega\sqrt[3]{2}$ does not. While $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is normal.

⭐ **Unrelated to “normal subgroup”**: here normal means a normal extension; the group-theoretic normal means a normal subgroup. They do meet in the Galois correspondence (an intermediate field normal $\iff$ a normal subgroup), but the **definitions are completely different**, so do not conflate the words.
