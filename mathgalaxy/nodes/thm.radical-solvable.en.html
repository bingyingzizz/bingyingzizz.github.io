Statement

Let FFF be a field of characteristic zero, let f∈F[x]f \in F[x]f∈F[x] be nonzero, and let KKK be the splitting field of fff. Then

The roots of fff can be expressed by radicals (arithmetic operations and extraction of nnnth roots) from elements of FFF   ⟺  \iff⟺ Gal⁡(K/F)\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)Gal(K/F) is a solvable group.

The extension K′/FK^{\prime}/FK′/F is normal, and every automorphism of KKK over FFF extends to K′K^{\prime}K′. A quotient of a solvable group is solvable, so Gal⁡(K/F)\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)Gal(K/F) is solvable. ∎

⭐ Why characteristic zero is required: Both directions use that xm−1x^{m}-1xm−1 has no repeated roots (so σ\sigmaσ is diagonalizable), as well as separability. In characteristic ppp, pure radical extensions must be replaced by Artin–Schreier extensions xp−x−ax^{p}-x-axp−x−a, and the conclusion must be reformulated.

📌 This theorem is the entry point to the quintic problem: It turns the question of whether a radical formula exists into a purely group-theoretic question (SnS_{n}Sn​ is solvable   ⟺  n≤4\iff n\le4⟺n≤4, see the solvability of symmetric groups). When applying it in reverse, distinguish the absence of a radical solution from the absence of any solution: Gal⁡(f)\operatorname{Gal}(f)Gal(f) is not solvable   ⟹  \implies⟹ has no solution by radicals.
