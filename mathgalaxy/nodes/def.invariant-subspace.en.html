Statement

Let T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V be linear. A subspace U⊆VU \subseteq VU⊆V is called TTT-invariant if and only if

T(U)⊆UT(U) \subseteq UT(U)⊆U

that is, u∈U  ⟹  T(u)∈Uu \in U \implies T(u) \in Uu∈U⟹T(u)∈U. In that case TTT restricted to UUU gives a linear map T∣U:U→UT|_U : U \to UT∣U​:U→U.

{0}\{0\}{0} and VVV are always TTT-invariant (trivial invariant subspaces); the kernel and image of TTT are also TTT-invariant.

Notes

⭐ "Invariance" is the prerequisite for "computing block by block": if V=U1⊕⋯⊕UkV = U_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus U_kV=U1​⊕⋯⊕Uk​ and each UiU_iUi​ is invariant, then TTT is block diagonal with respect to this decomposition — so eigenvalues, invertibility and powers can all be computed block by block.

⭐ The whole idea of Jordan decomposition is "split VVV into a direct sum of invariant subspaces as small (as fine) as possible", and the finest such decomposition is exactly the one given by Jordan blocks.

⚠ Note that TTT-invariance is not the same as "the image of TTT": invariance only requires T(U)⊆UT(U) \subseteq UT(U)⊆U, not equality.

Every subspace with ker⁡T⊆U\ker T \subseteq UkerT⊆U — that is, every such UUU — is automatically invariant.
