Statement

What the theorem says: This is the theorem that symplectic geometry has no local invariants. Around every point there are coordinates in which ω\omegaω becomes ω0\omega_{0}ω0​, so all the information in a symplectic manifold is global.

Let (M2n,ω)(M^{2n}, \omega)(M2n,ω) be a symplectic manifold and let p∈Mp \in Mp∈M. Then there is a coordinate neighborhood of ppp and coordinates (q1,…,qn,p1,…,pn)(q_{1}, \ldots, q_{n}, p_{1}, \ldots, p_{n})(q1​,…,qn​,p1​,…,pn​) such that

ω=∑i=1ndqi∧dpi\omega = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dq_{i} \wedge dp_{i}ω=i=1∑n​dqi​∧dpi​

holds on that neighborhood. These are called Darboux coordinates (canonical coordinates).

Contrast with Riemannian geometry: A Riemannian metric generally cannot be put in such a form. Curvature is a local invariant, and only metrics with identically zero curvature (flat metrics) can be put locally in the form δij\delta_{ij}δij​. A symplectic form always admits its local normal form.

⭐ This theorem marks a dividing line in symplectic geometry: Local coordinates cannot distinguish symplectic manifolds, so all symplectic invariants (Gromov–Witten invariants, Floer homology, and capacities) are global. Riemannian geometry starts from local data such as curvature, while symplectic geometry relies on global data.

⚠️ The theorem does not say that every symplectic manifold is diffeomorphic to R2n\mathbb{R}^{2n}R2n. It supplies only local coordinates and imposes no constraint on global topology (as illustrated by the area form on T2T^{2}T2). This is the most common misreading.

⚠️ Darboux's name appears in three places here: this theorem in symplectic geometry, the Darboux criterion and upper and lower sums (Darboux Criterion, Riemann integration), and Darboux's theorem in real analysis (derivatives have the intermediate value property). The three are unrelated.
