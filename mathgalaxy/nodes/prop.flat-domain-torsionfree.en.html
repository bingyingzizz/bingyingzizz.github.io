Statement

Let RRR be an integral domain and MMM a flat RRR-module. Then MMM is torsion-free: for r≠0r \ne 0r=0 and m∈Mm \in Mm∈M, rm=0r m = 0rm=0 implies m=0m = 0m=0. Equivalently, multiplication by rrr, M→  ⋅r  MM \xrightarrow{\;\cdot r\;} MM⋅r​M, is injective.

Conversely, if RRR is a PID, then torsion-free   ⟺  \iff⟺ flat. Over a general domain "torsion-free" is strictly weaker than "flat".

Notes

Proof (forward direction): take 0≠r∈R0 \ne r \in R0=r∈R.

Since RRR is a domain, rrr is not a zero divisor, so 0→R→  ⋅r  R0 \to R \xrightarrow{\;\cdot r\;} R0→R⋅r​R is exact (zero kernel).

Since MMM is flat, tensoring this sequence gives the exact sequence 0→M→  ⋅r  M0 \to M \xrightarrow{\;\cdot r\;} M0→M⋅r​M — that is, multiplication by rrr is injective ✓.

Converse over a PID: finitely generated submodules of a torsion-free module are free (finitely generated torsion-free over a PID is free), and MMM is the filtered colimit of its finitely generated submodules; free modules are flat, and (−)⊗(-)\otimes(−)⊗ preserves filtered colimits, so MMM is flat ✓.

⚠️ The converse fails over a general domain: take R=k[x,y]R = k[x, y]R=k[x,y] and I=(x,y)I = (x, y)I=(x,y).

III is an ideal of RRR, and visibly torsion-free as an RRR-module; but it is not flat — x,yx, yx,y is a regular sequence, and exactness of the Koszul complex gives x⊗y−y⊗x≠0x \otimes y - y \otimes x \ne 0x⊗y−y⊗x=0 (in I⊗RII \otimes_{R} II⊗R​I), while its image under I⊗I→II \otimes I \to II⊗I→I is xy−yx=0xy - yx = 0xy−yx=0, so the multiplication map is not injective.

📌 In one sentence: "torsion-free" is a divisibility property tested ideal by ideal, while "flat" demands it for ideals all at once. Over a domain the latter is stronger, and over a PID the two coincide.
