Statement

Let (K,δ)(K, \delta)(K,δ) be a metric space, (E,d)(E, d)(E,d) a metric space, and H⊆C(K,E)\mathcal{H} \subseteq C(K, E)H⊆C(K,E). Call H\mathcal{H}H uniformly equicontinuous if

∀ε>0 ∃η>0:δ(x,y)<η  ⟹  d(f(x),f(y))<ε(∀f∈H, ∀x,y∈K).\forall \varepsilon > 0\ \exists \eta > 0:\quad \delta(x, y) < \eta \implies d(f(x), f(y)) < \varepsilon \quad (\forall f \in \mathcal{H},\ \forall x, y \in K).∀ε>0 ∃η>0:δ(x,y)<η⟹d(f(x),f(y))<ε(∀f∈H, ∀x,y∈K).

that is, η\etaη does not depend on x,y,fx, y, fx,y,f.

Notes

Uniform equicontinuity   ⟹  \implies⟹ pointwise equicontinuity (take y=x0y = x_0y=x0​ to recover the pointwise definition), see imp.fa-uniform-eq-pointwise.

The converse holds when KKK is compact: 等度连续与一致等度连续 (the proof uses a finite cover or a Lebesgue number).

⚠️ Without compactness the converse fails: for K=RK = \mathbb{R}K=R and fn(x)=x/nf_n(x) = x/nfn​(x)=x/n, the family is pointwise equicontinuous (the oscillation near each point is uniformly small), but it is not uniformly equicontinuous on R\mathbb{R}R (∣fn(n)−fn(n+1)∣=1/n|f_n(n) - f_n(n+1)| = 1/n∣fn​(n)−fn​(n+1)∣=1/n, so η\etaη would have to depend on nnn).

⭐ The "equicontinuity" in Arzelà–Ascoli is exactly this notion on a compact metric space; 等度连续与一致等度连续 is the bridge that upgrades the pointwise version to it.
