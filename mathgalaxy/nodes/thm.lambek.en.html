Statement

Suppose RRR is a unital Noetherian ring and MMM is a left RRR-module. (The criterion holds for arbitrary unital rings, but the induction below uses the fact that submodules are still finitely generated. The non-Noetherian case requires a filtered colimit argument, which is omitted here.) Then MMM is flat if and only if, for every finitely generated right ideal I⊆RI \subseteq RI⊆R, the multiplication map

I⊗RM⟶M,a⊗m⟼amI \otimes_{R} M \longrightarrow M, \qquad a \otimes m \longmapsto a mI⊗R​M⟶M,a⊗m⟼am

is injective. Equivalently, Tor⁡1R(R/I, M)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(R/I,\ M) = 0Tor1R​(R/I, M)=0 holds for every finitely generated right ideal III.

(Over a commutative ring, there is no need to distinguish left and right ideals.)

Notes

Why this is a strengthening: The definition of flatness requires testing all injections. Item 4 of Equivalent Characterizations of Flatness already reduces the test to ideals. Lambek reduces it further to finitely generated ideals, which often form a countable family and can be checked individually.

Proof (converse, in three steps):

Step 1 (reduce the objects being tested to finitely generated ones): Keep the module MMM being tested fixed. The fact that MMM is a filtered colimit of its finitely generated submodules does not let us reduce MMM to the finitely generated case. Finite generation does not pass to submodules (R=k[x,y]R = k[x,y]R=k[x,y] is flat, but the ideal (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) is not). The reduction concerns the maps being tested. It suffices to verify that for every finitely generated submodule K⊆RnK \subseteq R^{n}K⊆Rn, the map K⊗M→Rn⊗MK \otimes M \to R^{n} \otimes MK⊗M→Rn⊗M is injective.

Step 2 (reduce to submodules of free modules): Take a surjection Rn↠M1R^{n} \twoheadrightarrow M_{1}Rn↠M1​ (where M1M_{1}M1​ is a finitely generated submodule of MMM). Let KKK be the preimage of M1M_{1}M1​, giving the short exact sequence 0→K→Rn→M1→00 \to K \to R^{n} \to M_{1} \to 00→K→Rn→M1​→0.

Tensor both sequences and compare them. Since K⊗M→Rn⊗M→M1⊗M→0K \otimes M \to R^{n} \otimes M \to M_{1} \otimes M \to 0K⊗M→Rn⊗M→M1​⊗M→0 is exact, K⊗M→Rn⊗MK \otimes M \to R^{n} \otimes MK⊗M→Rn⊗M is injective if and only if Rn⊗M→M1⊗MR^{n} \otimes M \to M_{1} \otimes MRn⊗M→M1​⊗M is injective, by the snake lemma.

Thus it suffices to show that for every finitely generated submodule K⊆RnK \subseteq R^{n}K⊆Rn, the map K⊗M→Rn⊗MK \otimes M \to R^{n} \otimes MK⊗M→Rn⊗M is injective.

Step 3 (induct on nnn): When n=1n = 1n=1, the submodule KKK is a finitely generated right ideal, so the hypothesis applies. When n>1n > 1n>1, write Rn=Rn−1⊕RR^{n} = R^{n-1} \oplus RRn=Rn−1⊕R and define K1:=K∩Rn−1K_{1} := K \cap R^{n-1}K1​:=K∩Rn−1, with K2K_{2}K2​ the projection of KKK onto the second component (a finitely generated right ideal of RRR, on the same side as the ideals in the criterion. Regard K⊆RnK \subseteq R^{n}K⊆Rn as a right submodule). By Noetherianity, K1K_{1}K1​ is finitely generated as a submodule of Rn−1R^{n-1}Rn−1, so the induction hypothesis applies directly (the non-Noetherian case requires a separate filtered colimit argument, omitted here). This gives the short exact sequence

0→K1→K→K2→0,0→Rn−1→Rn→R→0.0 \to K_{1} \to K \to K_{2} \to 0, \qquad 0 \to R^{n-1} \to R^{n} \to R \to 0 .0→K1​→K→K2​→0,0→Rn−1→Rn→R→0.

After tensoring, we obtain a 3×33 \times 33×3 diagram. The vertical maps in the left and right columns are injective by the induction hypothesis and the case n=1n = 1n=1, so the vertical map in the middle column is injective by the snake lemma ✓. ∎

⚠️ Testing injectivity only on principal ideals (a)(a)(a) gives torsion-freeness over an integral domain. This characterization holds only over integral domains: when R=Z/4R = \mathbb{Z}/4R=Z/4 and M=R/(2)M = R/(2)M=R/(2), non-zero-divisors act injectively, but (2)⊗M→M(2) \otimes M \to M(2)⊗M→M is the zero map. Torsion-freeness is weaker than flatness. See Flatness and Torsion-Freeness over a Domain for the counterexample (x,y)⊆k[x,y](x, y) \subseteq k[x, y](x,y)⊆k[x,y].

⭐ Practical use: To test whether a given RRR-module is flat, it suffices to do one division computation for each finitely generated ideal. Over a commutative Noetherian ring, every ideal is finitely generated, so the criterion simply applies to all ideals.
