Statement

  1. Realization F↦FX\mathcal{F} \mapsto \mathcal{F}_{X}F↦FX​ gives an equivalence between the crystalline sheaves on Top/X\mathbf{Top}_{/X}Top/X​ and the sheaves on XXX;
  2. Hence Et(X)\mathbf{Et}(X)Et(X) (the category of étale spaces over XXX) ≃\simeq≃ crystalline sheaves ≃\simeq≃ sheaves on XXX — in particular Et(X)\mathbf{Et}(X)Et(X) is a topos, because Et(X)≃Open(X)~\mathbf{Et}(X) \simeq \widetilde{\mathbf{Open}(X)}Et(X)≃Open(X)​.

Notes

Hypotheses used in each direction (the crystalline condition makes all data determined by the terminal object):

  • Crystalline sheaves F\mathcal FF to FX\mathcal F_XFX​: restricting to the identity map is free; only the crystalline transition isomorphisms for f:Y→Xf:Y\to Xf:Y→X yield f−1FX≅FYf^{-1}\mathcal F_X\cong\mathcal F_Yf−1FX​≅FY​.
  • Sheaves on XXX to crystalline sheaves: take FY=f−1F\mathcal F_Y=f^{-1}\mathcal FFY​=f−1F, and use the composite compatibility isomorphism of inverse images together with descent along open covers; no Hausdorff or local connectedness hypothesis is needed.
  • The reverse construction from sheaves to étale spaces uses the disjoint union of stalks with the germ topology; from étale spaces to sheaves one takes local sections. That they are mutually inverse is the standard theorem for sheaves and local homeomorphisms, not a mere change of terminology.
  • The crystalline condition cannot be dropped: transitions of a general compatible system need not be isomorphisms, so the whole system cannot be recovered from the data at the terminal object; crystalline also does not mean arbitrary homotopy invariance.
