Statement

Notes

⭐ ∂/∂z\partial/\partial z∂/∂z and ∂/∂zˉ\partial/\partial \bar z∂/∂zˉ are called Wirtinger derivatives.

They are not difference-quotient limits: zˉ\bar zzˉ behaves differently in different directions, so the difference quotient has no limit.

They are merely a pair of linear combinations of real partial derivatives, a bookkeeping device.

⭐ Mnemonic (a footnote in textbooks): substitute x=(z+zˉ)/2x = (z + \bar z)/2x=(z+zˉ)/2, y=−i(z−zˉ)/2y = -i(z - \bar z)/2y=−i(z−zˉ)/2 into f(x,y)f(x, y)f(x,y), and formally treat zzz and zˉ\bar zzˉ as independent variables, differentiating by the chain rule — this yields the two formulas above.

⚠ This only aids memory — the rigorous definition is the combination on the right, because zzz and zˉ\bar zzˉ are not independent as two coordinates on R2\mathbb{R}^2R2.

⭐ What is genuinely useful is its criterion property: fff is complex differentiable   ⟺  \iff⟺ ∂f/∂zˉ(z0)=0\partial f/\partial \bar z(z_0) = 0∂f/∂zˉ(z0​)=0.

That is, "whether fff can contain only zzz and no zˉ\bar zzˉ" becomes something verifiable term by term.
