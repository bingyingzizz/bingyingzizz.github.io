Statement

Let D:I→CD : I \to \mathcal{C}D:I→C be a diagram. A cone over DDD is an object X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C together with a family of morphisms

pi:X⟶D(i)(i∈I)p_i : X \longrightarrow D(i) \qquad (i \in I)pi​:X⟶D(i)(i∈I)

such that for every α:i→j\alpha : i \to jα:i→j in III,

D(α)∘pi=pjD(\alpha) \circ p_i = p_jD(α)∘pi​=pj​

Equivalently, a cone is a natural transformation from the constant diagram ΔX\Delta XΔX to DDD. Reversing all arrows gives a cocone.

Notes

Intuition: XXX stands outside the diagram and casts a beam toward each position of DDD, with the requirement that the projections be compatible (projecting first and then following an arrow in the diagram gives the same result as projecting directly to the target).

When XXX is fixed, the cones over DDD are exactly the set of natural transformations Nat⁡(ΔX,D)\operatorname{Nat}(\Delta X, D)Nat(ΔX,D), so a cone is nothing new — just a natural transformation under another name.
