# basic properties of extremally disconnected spaces　`prop.stonean-basic`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.stonean-basic)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.stonean-basic.md)

Since $X$ is extremally disconnected, $\overline{U}$ and $\overline{V}$ are both clopen, so their relation to their own interiors forces a contradiction — concretely, every neighbourhood of $x$ must meet both $U$ and $V$, while these two clopen sets separate $x$ from "the other".

**(b)** Let $x \ne y$. Hausdorffness gives open sets $U \ni x$, $V \ni y$ separating them; by (a) two disjoint clopen sets separate $x$ from $y$, hence $C(x) \subseteq U \not\ni y$, $y \notin C(x)$. So every connected component is a point.
