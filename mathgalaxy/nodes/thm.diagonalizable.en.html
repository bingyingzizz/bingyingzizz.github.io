Statement

Let dim⁡V=n<∞\dim V = n < \inftydimV=n<∞, let T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V be linear, suppose the characteristic polynomial of TTT splits completely over KKK, and let λ1,…,λm\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_mλ1​,…,λm​ be its distinct eigenvalues in KKK. Then the following are equivalent:

(1) TTT is diagonalizable: there is a basis in which the matrix of TTT is diagonal;

(2) V=Eλ1⊕⋯⊕EλmV = E_{\lambda_1} \oplus \cdots \oplus E_{\lambda_m}V=Eλ1​​⊕⋯⊕Eλm​​, that is, the eigenvectors span all of VVV;

(3) ∑idim⁡Eλi=n\sum_i \dim E_{\lambda_i} = n∑i​dimEλi​​=n (the sum of the geometric multiplicities reaches nnn);

(4) the geometric multiplicity of every eigenvalue equals its algebraic multiplicity;

(5) there exist distinct λi\lambda_iλi​ such that TTT satisfies ∏i(T−λiI)=0\prod_i (T - \lambda_i I) = 0∏i​(T−λi​I)=0 (that is, TTT is annihilated by a polynomial without repeated roots).

Notes

⭐ The most used criteria are (3)/(4): the existence of nnn linearly independent eigenvectors   ⟺  \iff⟺ diagonalizable.

Note that "the number of eigenvalues is =n= n=n" is not enough (repeated roots must be counted with multiplicity).

⭐ (5) is the deepest formulation: it translates "diagonalizable" into "the minimal polynomial has no repeated roots" (see "the minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial") — this is used in the discussion of the spectral theorem and of Jordan form.

⚠ The criterion depends on KKK: a rotation over R\mathbb{R}R has no real eigenvalue, so of course it is not diagonalizable;

but over C\mathbb{C}C it has eigenvalues ±i\pm i±i and is diagonalizable.

So "diagonalizable" is always relative to KKK.

(1)–(4): the first three are only restatements, only the fourth uses the characteristic polynomial; (5) is a separate entry point.

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (2): almost only a rewriting of the definition (diagonalizable = there is a basis consisting of eigenvectors).
  • (2)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (3): pure linear algebra (∑dim⁡Eλi=n\sum \dim E_{\lambda_i} = n∑dimEλi​​=n, and the sum is direct   ⟺  \iff⟺ spanning VVV). No characteristic polynomial, no algebraic closure needed.
  • (3)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (4): this step uses "geometric multiplicity ≤\le≤ algebraic multiplicity", and that needs the characteristic polynomial (computing the dimension of the nilpotent part of T−λIT - \lambda IT−λI). ∑\sum∑ geometric multiplicity =n= n=n is equivalent to "equality for every eigenvalue", because the total sum ≤∑\le \sum≤∑ algebraic multiplicity =n= n=n.

⚠️ Necessary hypotheses: finite dimension + the characteristic polynomial splits over KKK (all eigenvalues lie in KKK). Without the latter, TTT may have no eigenvalue at all over KKK (for example, over R\mathbb{R}R, the rotation by 90∘90^{\circ}90∘): then m=0m=0m=0, and (2)(3) are false statements — the right-hand side is the zero subspace 000, while V=R2V=\mathbb{R}^2V=R2 (an empty sum 0≠20\ne20=2); only (4) holds vacuously ("every eigenvalue" ranges over the empty family). So the chain of equivalences breaks already at (2)(3); do not think that only (4) fails. The correct statement is "diagonalizable over the algebraic closure   ⟺  \iff⟺ the minimal polynomial has no repeated roots".
