In Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond, every epimorphism f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is regular: it is the coequalizer of its own pair of projections,

Notes

What it says. In a general category, "epimorphism" (right-cancellative) and "coequalizer" (the quotient of some pair) are different notions. In a topos the two coincide — which is why one may freely "take quotients" in a topos: every epimorphism really does quotient out an equivalence relation.

What X×YX⇉XX \times_{Y} X \rightrightarrows XX×Y​X⇉X is: X×YX={ (x,x′):f(x)=f(x′) }X \times_{Y} X = \{\, (x, x') : f(x) = f(x') \,\}X×Y​X={(x,x′):f(x)=f(x′)} is the kernel pair of fff;

its two projections are that pair.

The coequalizer glues together "points identified by fff" — and the result is exactly YYY.
