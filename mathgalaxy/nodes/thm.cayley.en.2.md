That is, "group theory" and "permutation group theory" are the same subject — abstraction does not really create new objects, only a change of viewpoint.

⚠ The price is high: for $|G| = n$ one embeds into $S_n$ (of order $n!$), so Cayley's theorem is almost never used for computation.

Its real use is as a contrast with representation theory: replacing "acting on a set" by "acting on a vector space" is the beginning of that line.

⭐ Left multiplication is also **free** (every stabilizer is trivial: $gh = h \implies g = e$), so it is automatically injective — this is the core sentence of the proof.
