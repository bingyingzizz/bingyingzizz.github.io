Statement

What it says: the exterior derivative satisfies d2=0\mathrm{d}^{2} = 0d2=0, so kkk-forms line up in a "chain"; the part left after quotienting closed forms by exact ones is cohomology — it measures "how many closed forms are falsely closed".

Let MMM be a smooth manifold and Ωk(M)\Omega^{k}(M)Ωk(M) the space of kkk-forms. The exterior derivative on a manifold gives the de Rham complex

From d2=0\mathrm{d}^{2} = 0d2=0 one gets im⁡d⊆ker⁡d\operatorname{im}\mathrm{d} \subseteq \ker\mathrm{d}imd⊆kerd. Write the spaces of closed forms and exact forms as

Notes

What it measures: Hk=0H^{k} = 0Hk=0 means "closed implies exact"; Hk≠0H^{k} \ne 0Hk=0 says there is a closed but non-exact form.

On R2∖{0}\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}R2∖{0} the angle form x dy−y dxx2+y2\frac{x\,\mathrm{d}y - y\,\mathrm{d}x}{x^{2}+y^{2}}x2+y2xdy−ydx​ is closed but not exact; its class generates H1≅RH^{1} \cong \mathbb{R}H1≅R and measures "the number of turns around the origin".

The de Rham theorem (stated only, not proved): HdRk(M)≅Hsingk(M;R)H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M) \cong H^{k}_{\mathrm{sing}}(M;\mathbb{R})HdRk​(M)≅Hsingk​(M;R) — what is computed analytically (forms plus derivative) equals what is computed purely topologically (singular chains plus boundary).

This stitches differential geometry to algebraic topology and is the interface with the category/homology region of this graph.

Finite dimension: when MMM is compact, each HdRk(M)H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)HdRk​(M) is finite-dimensional and vanishes for k>dim⁡Mk > \dim Mk>dimM. So cohomology is a finite set of numbers (Betti numbers). Not proved here.

Locally it always vanishes: every point of any manifold has a contractible neighbourhood, so by the Poincaré lemma Hk=0H^{k} = 0Hk=0 for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1. Hence cohomology is purely global information — which is why it is more useful than local differential invariants.

H0H^{0}H0 must be read separately: Z0=ker⁡(d:C∞(M)→Ω1(M))Z^{0} = \ker(\mathrm{d} : C^{\infty}(M) \to \Omega^{1}(M))Z0=ker(d:C∞(M)→Ω1(M)) consists of locally constant functions, and B0=0B^{0} = 0B0=0, so H0H^{0}H0 counts connected components (零次上同调).

Only for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1 does one need a Poincaré-lemma-type argument.

It is not "homology" in the sense of a homotopy invariant: HkH^{k}Hk is cohomology (forms and pullback), dual to homology with chains and boundary. It is called cohomology because pullback is contravariant; the two are proved isomorphic in the de Rham theorem.
