Statement

What this is about: it brings the two operator-level spectral theorems already available (谱定理（实对称） for the real symmetric case, 谱定理（复正规） for the complex normal case) down to matrices, and gives the precise if-and-only-if conditions.

Let A∈Mn(C)A \in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})A∈Mn​(C). Then

AAA is normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is a unitary matrix UUU with U∗AU=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn)U^{*}AU = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}, \ldots, \lambda_{n})U∗AU=diag(λ1​,…,λn​)

that is: normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. In that case

A=UΛU∗,Λ=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn),A = U \Lambda U^{*}, \qquad \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1}, \ldots, \lambda_{n}) ,A=UΛU∗,Λ=diag(λ1​,…,λn​),

called the spectral decomposition of AAA (a sum of orthogonal projections: A=∑iλiuiui∗A = \sum_i \lambda_i u_i u_i^{*}A=∑i​λi​ui​ui∗​).

Real case: a real symmetric matrix   ⟺  \iff⟺ there is an orthogonal matrix QQQ with QTAQQ^{T}AQQTAQ real diagonal.

Notes

Four equivalent formulations; any one may serve as the definition: AAA is normal; AAA has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors; A=UΛU∗A = U\Lambda U^{*}A=UΛU∗; the spectral measure of AAA takes only atomic values. In matrix language the first is the most economical, the second the most intuitive (the whole content of 谱定理（矩阵版） is "the two are equivalent").

How to read the spectral decomposition: A=∑iλiPiA = \sum_i \lambda_i P_iA=∑i​λi​Pi​, where Pi=uiui∗P_i = u_i u_i^{*}Pi​=ui​ui∗​ is the orthogonal projection onto the eigen-direction, satisfying Pi2=PiP_i^2 = P_iPi2​=Pi​, PiPj=0P_iP_j = 0Pi​Pj​=0 (i≠ji \ne ji=j), ∑iPi=I\sum_i P_i = I∑i​Pi​=I.

Thus "diagonalization" = "splitting the operator into a bunch of mutually perpendicular directions, each multiplied by a number".

⭐ Connection with functional calculus: once the spectral decomposition is available, any function fff can be applied: f(A):=∑if(λi)Pif(A) := \sum_i f(\lambda_i)P_if(A):=∑i​f(λi​)Pi​. The operators A\sqrt{A}A​, etAe^{tA}etA, A1/2A^{1/2}A1/2 are all special cases — this is why "a positive definite matrix has a square root" needs no separate construction.

⚠️ A non-normal matrix may still be diagonalizable (可对角化判据), but its eigenvectors are not orthogonal, and the similarity matrix PPP is generally ill-conditioned. So "diagonalizable" and "unitarily diagonalizable" are two different things: the latter demands orthogonality and is much stronger.

The proof is on the outgoing edge: the full proof is the corollary edge "Schur triangularization ⟹\Longrightarrow⟹ spectral theorem (matrix version)" in this cluster — normality is preserved under unitary similarity, and an upper triangular normal matrix must be diagonal; the real symmetric case then follows by induction using "eigenvalues are real and eigenvectors can be chosen real".
