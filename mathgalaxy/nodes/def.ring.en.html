Statement

Let AAA be a set and +++, ⋅\cdot⋅ two binary operations on AAA. Then (A,+,⋅)(A, +, \cdot)(A,+,⋅) is a ring if and only if:

  • (A,+)(A, +)(A,+) is an abelian group;
  • (A,⋅)(A, \cdot)(A,⋅) satisfies associativity (no identity and no commutativity is required);
  • the distributive laws a(b+c)=ab+aca(b + c) = ab + aca(b+c)=ab+ac, (a+b)c=ac+bc(a + b)c = ac + bc(a+b)c=ac+bc hold.

A ring whose multiplication is also commutative is called a commutative ring; a ring that moreover has a multiplicative identity 1≠01 \ne 01=0 is called a ring with identity.

Notes

⭐ Again "set + operations + axioms", and this layer directly cites the abelian group below: the additive axioms are not rewritten, just mentioned in one phrase.

Contrast with a field: a field requires every non-zero element to be invertible ((F∖{0},⋅)(F\setminus\{0\}, \cdot)(F∖{0},⋅) is an abelian group), a ring does not.

So a field is a special case of a ring, and Z\mathbb{Z}Z is a ring but not a field — this is exactly where the counterexample Z\mathbb{Z}Z in the definition of a field comes from.

Which hypothesis each direction uses (here the definition is axiomatic; commutativity and identity are extra requirements):

  • ring   ⟺  \iff⟺ additive abelian group, associative multiplication, left and right distributivity: unwinding the definition adopted in this node; no axiom of choice is needed, nor is a field or PID required.
  • commutative ring   ⟹  \implies⟹ ring, ring with identity   ⟹  \implies⟹ ring: just forget extra structure; neither converse is free — M2(R)M_2(\mathbb{R})M2​(R) is not commutative, and 2Z2\mathbb{Z}2Z has no identity under the usual multiplication.
  • adding commutative multiplication   ⟺  \iff⟺ commutative ring; adding an identity together with the convention 1≠01\ne01=0 of this library   ⟺  \iff⟺ ring with identity: still definitional; a ring with identity here is not required to be commutative.
  • field   ⟹  \implies⟹ commutative ring with identity: expand the field axioms; the converse also needs every non-zero element invertible, with Z\mathbb{Z}Z as a counterexample. The zero ring satisfies the ring axioms but not the convention 1≠01\ne01=0 for rings with identity adopted in this node.
