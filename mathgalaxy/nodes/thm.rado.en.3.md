# Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable　`thm.rado`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.rado)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.rado.md)

**Surfaces with boundary.** The statement allows boundary, but both approaches below address only **closed** surfaces. The extension requires an argument. The standard method is **doubling along the boundary**. Take two copies of $S$ and glue them along $\partial S$ by the identity to obtain a closed surface $D(S)$. Triangulate $D(S)$ by either approach below, then restrict to $S$, provided the triangulation places $\partial S$ in the $1$-skeleton (see gap (vi)). One can also collapse each boundary circle to a point to obtain a closed surface.
