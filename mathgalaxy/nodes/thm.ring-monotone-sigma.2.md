## English abstract · reviewed by bingyingzizz (2026-10-16) · Chinese is authoritative（续）
For a ring $\mathfrak{A}$ of sets, the following are equivalent: $\mathfrak{A}$ is a $\sigma$-ring, $\mathfrak{A}$ is a monotone class, and $\mathfrak{A}$ is closed under countable disjoint unions. The hypothesis that $\mathfrak{A}$ is a ring cannot be dropped: a monotone class need not be closed under finite unions. This equivalence is the technical core of the monotone class theorem, since it reduces "true for the $\sigma$-algebra" to "check a ring, then take monotone limits".
