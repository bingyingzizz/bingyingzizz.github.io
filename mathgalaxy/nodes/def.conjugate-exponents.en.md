## Statement · **draft**
Let $1 \le p \le \infty$. One calls $q$ the **conjugate exponent** of $p$ iff

$$\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$$

(with the convention $1/\infty = 0$, so that $p = 1$ corresponds to $q = \infty$ and $p = \infty$ to $q = 1$.)

## Notes · **draft**
By symmetry $\dfrac{1}{q} + \dfrac{1}{p} = 1$, so conjugacy is mutual: $p$ and $q$ are conjugate to each other.
