prop.grothendieck-enough-injectives

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Every Grothendieck category (an AB5 category with a generator) has enough injective objects:

every object MMM embeds into an injective object; equivalently, every object has an injective resolution

0⟶M⟶I0⟶I1⟶⋯0 \longrightarrow M \longrightarrow I^{0} \longrightarrow I^{1} \longrightarrow \cdots0⟶M⟶I0⟶I1⟶⋯

Notes

⭐ This is the existence guarantee for "right derived functors". The standard definition of a right derived functor RnFR^{n}FRnF is "take an injective resolution, apply FFF, take cohomology" — and this definition makes sense only if injective resolutions always exist. This theorem says: as long as the category is Grothendieck, there is nothing to worry about.

What "enough" means: not "there are many injective objects", but "every object embeds into some injective object". This condition is about the category as a whole and cannot be checked object by object.

Where it is used: A-ModA\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}A-Mod, Ind⁡(C)\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{C})Ind(C), Ab(T)\mathbf{Ab}(\mathcal{T})Ab(T) on a topos, CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb — all are Grothendieck categories, so right derived functors, derived categories, and spectral sequences are all available on them.

This is the whole reason Grothendieck's generation singled out the two conditions "AB5 + generator".

📌 Another item that follows from the definition (same chapter): a Grothendieck category automatically satisfies AB3\* (has all limits) — existence of limits needs no extra hypothesis.

Proof (transfinite iteration; the categorical version of Baer's criterion). Let A\mathcal{A}A be an AB5 category with generator UUU.

  • Criterion: III is injective   ⟺  \iff⟺ for every monomorphism A↣BA\rightarrowtail BA↣B and every A→IA\to IA→I, the extension B→IB\to IB→I exists. Since UUU is a generator, it suffices to check subobjects A↣UA\rightarrowtail UA↣U (a general BBB is spread by a family of U→BU\to BU→B, extended piecewise, and pasted).
  • Step one: the evaluation map induced by UUU on   ⟹  \implies⟹, landing in M↪UHom⁡(U,M)M\hookrightarrow U^{\operatorname{Hom}(U,M)}M↪UHom(U,M) is a monomorphism (two morphisms equal on all of U→MU\to MU→M are equal), so MMM always embeds into a product of a family of UUU.
  • Step two (completing the extension): let M0:=MM_{0} := MM0​:=M.

Given MαM_{\alpha}Mα​, push out all the "data to be extended" (A↣U, A→Mα)\bigl(A\rightarrowtail U,\ A\to M_{\alpha}\bigr)(A↣U, A→Mα​) at once — let Mα+1M_{\alpha+1}Mα+1​ be the pushout of MαM_{\alpha}Mα​ and ∐(A,φ)U\coprod_{(A,\varphi)}U∐(A,φ)​U along ∐(A,φ)A\coprod_{(A,\varphi)}A∐(A,φ)​A.

Then every map from a subobject of UUU can be extended in Mα+1M_{\alpha+1}Mα+1​ to all of UUU.

  • Limit ordinals: take Mβ:=lim→⁡α<βMαM_{\beta} := \varinjlim_{\alpha<\beta}M_{\alpha}Mβ​:=lim​α<β​Mα​. A\mathcal{A}A is AB5 (filtered colimits are exact), and this layer-by-layer colimit preserves monomorphisms and exactness ✓.
  • Stabilization: the increment depends only on the set "subobjects of UUU" (the generator plus AB5 guarantees it is small), so after a sufficiently large ordinal Mα→Mα+1M_{\alpha}\to M_{\alpha+1}Mα​→Mα+1​ is already an isomorphism;

the stabilized I:=M∞I := M_{\infty}I:=M∞​ satisfies the criterion, hence is injective ✓.

Repeating the process with M↪IM\hookrightarrow IM↪I gives an injective resolution.

∎ Standard source: Freyd, Abelian Categories; a shorter route goes through the Gabriel–Popescu embedding (regard A\mathcal{A}A as an exact reflective subcategory of R-ModR\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}R-Mod, then reflect the injectives of R-ModR\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}R-Mod over).
