## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This theorem is the **certificate of legitimacy for elementary functions**: it guarantees that $\exp$ has a continuous inverse $\log$, and that $\sin$ on $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$ has a continuous inverse $\arcsin$.

Without it, the inverse trigonometric and inverse hyperbolic functions could only be waved through as “obviously continuous”.

⚠ Strict monotonicity is **necessary**: a constant function has no inverse;

$\sin$ is not monotone, so one must first restrict to a monotone interval and then invert.
