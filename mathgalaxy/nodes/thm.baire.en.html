Statement

Let XXX be a complete metric space (or a locally compact Hausdorff space). Then the intersection of countably many dense open sets is still dense. Equivalently:

  1. the union of countably many nowhere dense closed sets has empty interior;
  2. every nonempty open set is of the second category.

⭐ All three equivalent formulations should be usable; passing between them is just taking complements.

⚠️ Completeness cannot be dropped: in Q\mathbb{Q}Q every singleton is closed and nowhere dense, and Q\mathbb{Q}Q is countable, so Q\mathbb{Q}Q is of the first category — it is not a Baire space.

Which hypothesis each direction uses (the translations between the three formulations are free; their validity rests on Baire's theorem):

  • Countable intersection of dense open sets is dense   ⟺  \iff⟺ countable union of nowhere dense closed sets has empty interior: take complements; holds in any topological space, no completeness needed.
  • The closed nowhere dense formulation   ⟺  \iff⟺ every nonempty open set is not of the first category in the whole space: replace each nowhere dense set by its closure and unwind the definition of first category.
  • Complete metric space   ⟹  \implies⟹ the above properties: recursively choose nested closed balls with radii tending to zero and use completeness to get a common point; in the usual foundational setting this recursion uses dependent choice.
  • The locally compact Hausdorff version replaces completeness by nested compact neighbourhoods and compactness. A metric space alone is not enough: Q\mathbb QQ is a countable union of nowhere dense singletons; but completeness is sufficient, not necessary, for the Baire property.
