# Mean Value Theorem for Integrals　`thm.mean-value-integral`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.mean-value-integral)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.mean-value-integral.md)

Taking $g \equiv 1$ gives: there is $\xi \in [a, b]$ with $\int_a^b f = f(\xi)(b - a)$; in this case $f(\xi)$ is called the **mean value** of $f$ on $[a, b]$.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Geometric reading: the area under the curve equals "some height" times the base — that is, the **mean value** is actually attained at some point (continuity plus the extreme value theorem).

⚠ The hypothesis that $g$ has **constant sign** cannot be dropped: if $g$ changes sign, the weighted mean may lie outside the range of $f$.
