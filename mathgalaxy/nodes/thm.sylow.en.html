Statement

Let GGG be a finite group with ∣G∣=pam|G| = p^{a} m∣G∣=pam, where ppp is prime and p∤mp \nmid mp∤m. Then

(1) Existence: For every 0≤i≤a0 \le i \le a0≤i≤a, the group GGG has a subgroup of order pip^{i}pi. In particular, a subgroup of order pap^{a}pa exists and is called a Sylow ppp-subgroup.

(2) Conjugacy: Any two Sylow subgroups of GGG for the prime ppp are conjugate. More generally, every ppp-subgroup is contained in a Sylow ppp-subgroup.

(3) Counting: The number of Sylow ppp-subgroups, denoted by npn_pnp​, satisfies

np≡1(modp),np∣m,np=[G:NG(P)]n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}, \qquad n_p \mid m, \qquad n_p = [G : N_G(P)]np​≡1(modp),np​∣m,np​=[G:NG​(P)]

(Here PPP is any Sylow ppp-subgroup and NG(P)N_G(P)NG​(P) is its normalizer.) In particular, np=1  ⟺  P⊴Gn_p = 1 \iff P \trianglelefteq Gnp​=1⟺P⊴G.

Notes

⭐ Sylow's theorem is a partial converse to Lagrange's theorem. Lagrange says that ∣H∣|H|∣H∣ must divide ∣G∣|G|∣G∣. The claim that d∣∣G∣d \mid |G|d∣∣G∣   ⟹  \implies⟹ has a subgroup of order ddd is false. Sylow guarantees a subgroup of the largest possible ppp-power order, unique up to conjugacy.

⚠ Galois theory does not require Sylow's theorem (the Galois correspondence uses normality rather than counting).
