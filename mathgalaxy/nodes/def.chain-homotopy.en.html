Why "chain homotopy   ⟹  \implies⟹ equal on homology": let z∈Cnz \in C_{n}z∈Cn​ be a cycle (∂z=0\partial z = 0∂z=0). Then

⭐ It is the algebraic translation of "homotopy": a homotopy in topology is "a continuous deformation between two maps"; a chain complex has no space to deform, so the deformation is turned into a homomorphism PPP raising degree by one. The combination ∂P+P∂\partial P + P\partial∂P+P∂ is exactly what "homotopy of chain complexes" should look like: it commutes with ∂\partial∂ (∂T=T∂\partial T = T\partial∂T=T∂, so TTT is a chain map) and therefore sends cycles to boundaries; the anticommuting relation is the termwise relation between PPP and ∂\partial∂ (∂P=T−P∂\partial P = T - P\partial∂P=T−P∂).

✅ Chain homotopy is an equivalence relation: reflexive with 000, symmetric with −P-P−P, transitive with P+QP + QP+Q (∂(P+Q)+(P+Q)∂=(g−f)+(h−g)=h−f\partial(P+Q) + (P+Q)\partial = (g - f) + (h - g) = h - f∂(P+Q)+(P+Q)∂=(g−f)+(h−g)=h−f). So "chain homotopy classes" are the real morphisms in the homotopy category; formally inverting chain homotopy classes gives the homotopy category.
