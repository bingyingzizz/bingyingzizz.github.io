Notes

⭐ This theorem says: integrate, then differentiate, and you return to the original function (at the points where fff is continuous).

Thus “finding an antiderivative” acquires a concrete construction, rather than relying on reciting the derivative table backwards.

⚠ (b) only requires fff to be continuous at that point: where fff has a jump, FFF has a corner (FFF is not differentiable there), but FFF is still continuous everywhere.

⭐ The Lipschitz estimate in (a) is exactly “∫abf\int_a^b f∫ab​f depends continuously on the upper limit”.

On the Lebesgue side (a) still holds, while (b) must be replaced by “differentiable a.e.” (the Lebesgue differentiation theorem).
