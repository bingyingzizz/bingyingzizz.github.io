Statement

A group G≠{e}G \ne \{e\}G={e} is simple iff it has only two normal subgroups: {e}\{e\}{e} and GGG itself (that is, it has no nontrivial proper normal subgroup).

Notes

⭐ A simple group is “the prime of group theory”: the factors of a composition series are all simple, and Jordan–Hölder says these factors are unique — simple groups play exactly the role of prime factors in integer factorization.

⭐ Basic examples and counterexamples:

  • Z/p\mathbb{Z}/pZ/p (ppp prime) is simple: every subgroup is normal (abelian) and there are only the two trivial ones;
  • AnA_nAn​ (n≥5n \ge 5n≥5) is simple and non-abelian;
  • SnS_nSn​ (n≥3n \ge 3n≥3) is not simple: An⊴SnA_n \trianglelefteq S_nAn​⊴Sn​ is a proper normal subgroup;
  • A4A_4A4​ is not simple: V4={e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)}V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}V4​={e,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)} is normal.

⭐ The relation between “simple” and “non-solvable” is a clean chain: simple and solvable   ⟺  \iff⟺ simple abelian   ⟺  \iff⟺ cyclic of prime order.

So non-abelian simple = the unsolvable brick, and A5A_5A5​ is the smallest one.
