# Generalized Eigenvector　`def.generalized-eigenvector`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.generalized-eigenvector)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.generalized-eigenvector.md)

**Meaning of the order**: $k = 1$ is an ordinary eigenvector; $k = 2$ means $T(v) = \lambda v + u$ ($u$ is an eigenvector) — this is the second term of a Jordan chain. So "generalized eigenvector" = "a point on a Jordan chain".

⚠️ The generalized eigenspace is **not** the eigenspace of $T$, but it is $T$-invariant; and $T$ is not diagonalizable on it (unless it equals $V_{\lambda}$).

⚠️ Only **eigenvalues** have generalized eigenvectors ($\lambda$ must satisfy $\det(T - \lambda I) = 0$), otherwise $T - \lambda I$ is invertible and no power ever vanishes.
