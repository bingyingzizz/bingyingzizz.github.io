Statement

Let (V,⟨⋅,⋅⟩)(V, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)(V,⟨⋅,⋅⟩) be an inner product space. Define

∥v∥:=⟨v,v⟩,d(u,v):=∥u−v∥\|v\| := \sqrt{\langle v, v \rangle}, \qquad d(u, v) := \|u - v\|∥v∥:=⟨v,v⟩​,d(u,v):=∥u−v∥

Then ∥⋅∥\|\cdot\|∥⋅∥ is a norm on VVV (positive definite, homogeneous, triangle inequality), ddd is a metric, and

∥v∥=0  ⟺  v=0,∥cv∥=∣c∣ ∥v∥,∥u+v∥≤∥u∥+∥v∥\|v\| = 0 \iff v = 0, \qquad \|cv\| = |c|\,\|v\|, \qquad \|u + v\| \le \|u\| + \|v\|∥v∥=0⟺v=0,∥cv∥=∣c∣∥v∥,∥u+v∥≤∥u∥+∥v∥

(the triangle inequality comes from Cauchy–Schwarz.)

Notes

⭐ Thus an inner product space is automatically a metric space, so convergence, completeness and compactness make sense — the whole toolbox of analysis becomes available.

A complete inner product space is called a Hilbert space.

⭐ Conversely: a norm need not come from an inner product.

The criterion is the parallelogram law

∥u+v∥2+∥u−v∥2=2∥u∥2+2∥v∥2\|u + v\|^{2} + \|u - v\|^{2} = 2\|u\|^{2} + 2\|v\|^{2}∥u+v∥2+∥u−v∥2=2∥u∥2+2∥v∥2

(ℓp\ell^{p}ℓp fails it when p≠2p \ne 2p=2, so the norm on ℓp\ell^{p}ℓp does not come from any inner product — this is also why L2L^{2}L2 is special.)
