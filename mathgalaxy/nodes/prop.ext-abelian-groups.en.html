Statement

Take A=Ab\mathcal{A} = \mathbf{Ab}A=Ab (abelian groups). Then

  1. for every abelian group M,NM, NM,N and all n≥2n \ge 2n≥2:
Ext⁡Zn(M, N)=0,Ext⁡Z0(M, N)=Hom⁡Z(M, N);\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\ N) = 0, \qquad \operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\ N) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\ N);ExtZn​(M, N)=0,ExtZ0​(M, N)=HomZ​(M, N);
  1. for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1:
Ext⁡Z1(Z/kZ, N)  ≅  N/kN,Hom⁡Z(Z/kZ, N)=N[k].\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z},\ N\bigr) \;\cong\; N/kN, \qquad \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z},\ N\bigr) = N[k].ExtZ1​(Z/kZ, N)≅N/kN,HomZ​(Z/kZ, N)=N[k].

(N[k]:={n∈N:kn=0}N[k] := \{n \in N : kn = 0\}N[k]:={n∈N:kn=0}.)

Notes

⭐ Together these say one thing: the homological algebra of abelian groups has only two layers (n=0n = 0n=0 and n=1n = 1n=1). So "higher Ext" is a phenomenon that truly appears only after changing the category (sheaves, condensed abelian groups) — exactly what the condensed cohomology below does.
