Statement

The functor Cond→kTop\mathrm{Cond} \to k\mathbf{Top}Cond→kTop, X↦X(⋅)X \mapsto X(\cdot)X↦X(⋅), preserves finite products: for any X,Y∈CondX, Y \in \mathrm{Cond}X,Y∈Cond,

(X×Y)(⋅)  ≅  k(X(⋅)×Y(⋅)).(X \times Y)(\cdot) \;\cong\; k\bigl(X(\cdot) \times Y(\cdot)\bigr).(X×Y)(⋅)≅k(X(⋅)×Y(⋅)).

(The right-hand side must first be kkk-ified: the product in Top\mathbf{Top}Top is usually not compactly generated.)

Notes

⭐ Why it matters: it says the functor "condensed set ↦\mapsto↦ underlying topological space" does not break finite products, so a product computed in Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond is still that product on spaces (after kkk-ification). This is the most-used step when translating algebraic constructions in Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond back to topological ones.

⚠️ Passing to Top\mathbf{Top}Top (i.e. applying kkk-ification) makes it false — the root cause is that Top\mathbf{Top}Top is not cartesian closed.

📌 Example: projection formulas such as S×X(⋅)≅(S×X)(⋅)S \times X(\cdot) \cong (S \times X)(\cdot)S×X(⋅)≅(S×X)(⋅) (with SSS locally compact Hausdorff) are another face of the same fact.
