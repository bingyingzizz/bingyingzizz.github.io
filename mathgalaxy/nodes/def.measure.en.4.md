**Which hypothesis each direction uses** (the two measure axioms are the definition; countable additivity is stronger than finite additivity):
- measure $\iff$ non-negative extended real-valued function satisfying (i), (ii): unwinding the definition; no $\sigma$-finiteness or completeness of the measure is needed; the domain must be a $\sigma$-algebra.
- countable additivity $\implies$ finite additivity: also uses $\mu(\emptyset)=0$, padding a finite disjoint sequence with empty sets; conversely, finite additivity plus continuity from below recovers countable additivity by taking limits of partial unions.
- (ii) alone cannot imply (i): the constant $+\infty$ is countably additive in the extend
