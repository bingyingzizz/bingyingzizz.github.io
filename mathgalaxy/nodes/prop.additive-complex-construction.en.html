Statement

An additive functor F:A→A′F : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}'F:A→A′, applied termwise, gives a functor

C(F):C(A)⟶C(A′),(Kn,dn)⟼(FKn, Fdn),C(F) : C(\mathcal{A}) \longrightarrow C(\mathcal{A}'), \qquad (K^{n}, d^{n}) \longmapsto \bigl(FK^{n},\ Fd^{n}\bigr),C(F):C(A)⟶C(A′),(Kn,dn)⟼(FKn, Fdn),

that is, F(C(A))≅C(F(A))F(C(\mathcal{A})) \cong C(F(\mathcal{A}))F(C(A))≅C(F(A)): taking the category of complexes first and then applying FFF is the same as applying FFF first and then taking the category of complexes.

Notes

Proof. The data of a complex consist only of "a family of objects + a family of morphisms + d∘d=0d \circ d = 0d∘d=0" ✓, all of it in the language of additive categories: an additive functor preserves zero morphisms, composites and addition, hence F(dn+1)∘F(dn)=F(dn+1∘dn)=0F(d^{n+1}) \circ F(d^{n}) = F(d^{n+1} \circ d^{n}) = 0F(dn+1)∘F(dn)=F(dn+1∘dn)=0 ✓, and C(F)C(F)C(F) is a functor (FFF preserves commutative diagrams) ✓. ∎
