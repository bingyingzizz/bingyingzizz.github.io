def.associated-bundle

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

What is being done: the fibre of a principal bundle is the group GGG, but the fibre you want is often some other space FFF (a vector space, a sphere, a flag manifold...). The associated bundle is the standard surgery "replace GGG by FFF": use the action of GGG on FFF to glue the two together.

Let P→MP \to MP→M be a principal GGG-bundle (@@def.principal-bundle@@) and FFF a manifold with a left GGG-action. On the product P×FP \times FP×F let GGG act on the right by

(u,y)⋅g:=(u⋅g, g−1⋅y),(u, y)\cdot g := (u \cdot g,\ g^{-1}\cdot y) ,(u,y)⋅g:=(u⋅g, g−1⋅y),

and write its orbit space as

P×GF:=(P×F)/G,P \times_{G} F := (P \times F)\big/ G ,P×G​F:=(P×F)/G,

The projection [u,y]↦π(u)[u, y] \mapsto \pi(u)[u,y]↦π(u) makes it a fibre bundle with fibre FFF, called the associated bundle.

The most important case: taking F=RkF = \mathbb{R}^{k}F=Rk with the standard action of G=GL(k,R)G = \mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})G=GL(k,R) gives

Fr(E)×GL(k)Rk  ≅  E,\mathrm{Fr}(E) \times_{\mathrm{GL}(k)} \mathbb{R}^{k} \;\cong\; E ,Fr(E)×GL(k)​Rk≅E,

i.e. every vector bundle is the associated bundle of its frame bundle — combined with @@def.frame-bundle@@ this is the one-to-one correspondence "vector bundle ↔\leftrightarrow↔ principal bundle of rank kkk with structure group GL(k)\mathrm{GL}(k)GL(k)" (classification is meant up to isomorphism).

Notes

⭐ g−1g^{-1}g−1 is not a typo: for the right action to really be an action one must use ggg on one side and g−1g^{-1}g−1 on the other (otherwise (u,y)⋅g⋅h(u,y)\cdot g\cdot h(u,y)⋅g⋅h would become (u⋅gh,h−1g−1y)(u\cdot gh, h^{-1}g^{-1}y)(u⋅gh,h−1g−1y), inconsistent with (u,y)⋅(gh)(u,y)\cdot(gh)(u,y)⋅(gh)). This is the notation most easily gotten wrong in associated bundles.

Why use GGG rather than "some arbitrary isomorphism": in the definition of the associated bundle, the GGG-action is the only gluing data. Thus "associated bundle" writes "a fibre bundle together with its structure group living in GGG" as one concrete construction — two ways of saying the same thing as "the structure group is GGG" in @@def.fiber-bundle@@.

⚠️ Sections versus equivariant maps: sections M→P×GFM \to P\times_{G}FM→P×G​F of the associated bundle correspond one-to-one to "GGG-equivariant maps P→FP \to FP→F".

This correspondence is the mathematical form of "gauge fields versus equivariant functions on a principal bundle" in physics, and the source of "sections of a vector bundle = GL(k)\mathrm{GL}(k)GL(k)-equivariant Rk\mathbb{R}^{k}Rk-valued functions on the frame bundle".

⚠️ The associated bundle does not need GGG discrete, nor FFF linear. Taking F=G/HF = G/HF=G/H gives "homogeneous space bundles" (for example F=S2=SO(3)/SO(2)F = S^{2} = \mathrm{SO}(3)/\mathrm{SO}(2)F=S2=SO(3)/SO(2) gives "sphere bundles"); taking F=GF = GF=G with the action given by left/right multiplication produces associated principal bundles — the latter being the kind of construction "twistors of GGG-bundles".
