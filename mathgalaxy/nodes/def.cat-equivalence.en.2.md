# equivalence of categories　`def.cat-equivalence`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.cat-equivalence)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.cat-equivalence.md)

If the two $\cong$ are strengthened to equalities $F \circ G = 1_{\mathcal{C}'}$ and $G \circ F = 1_{\mathcal{C}}$, it is called an **isomorphism of categories**, written $\mathcal{C} \cong \mathcal{C}'$.

## Notes · **draft**
An isomorphism requires the two composites to **equal** the identity functors; an equivalence only requires them to be **naturally isomorphic** to the identities. The latter is much weaker and much more useful: for instance, "finite-dimensional vector spaces" is equivalent to "matrices over $\mathbb{F}$", but can never be isomorphic (even the cardinalities of the object classes are wrong).
