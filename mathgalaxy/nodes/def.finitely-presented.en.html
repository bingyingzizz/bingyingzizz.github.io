def.finitely-presented

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

An object X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C is called finitely presented (also called compact) if the representable functor

hX=Hom⁡C(X,−)h^{X} = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, -)hX=HomC​(X,−)

preserves filtered colimits. That is, for every filtered diagram DDD, we have Hom⁡(X,lim→⁡D)≅lim→⁡Hom⁡(X,D(−))\operatorname{Hom}(X, \varinjlim D) \cong \varinjlim \operatorname{Hom}(X, D(-))Hom(X,lim​D)≅lim​Hom(X,D(−)).

Notes

Intuition. To probe with XXX, there is no need to wait until the colimit has been formed — maps out of XXX are already determined at a finite stage, and the filtered colimit just assembles them. So “compact” means “no need to reach out to infinity.”

Examples. Finite sets (in Set\mathbf{Set}Set), finitely generated modules (in A-ModA\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}A-Mod), and finite CW complexes (in topology).

R\mathbb{R}R is not compact in Top\mathbf{Top}Top (R\mathbb{R}R is a filtered colimit of finite intervals, but Hom⁡(R,−)\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{R},-)Hom(R,−) does not preserve that colimit).

⭐ Use in homological algebra. XXX is finitely presented   ⟺  \iff⟺ hXh^{X}hX preserves filtered colimits   ⟺  \iff⟺ XXX is a “small” test object. This is the idea behind the statement in condensed mathematics that “CondAb is generated by finitely presented projective objects” — finitely presented projective objects are generators of CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb.

⚠️ “Finitely presented” differs from “finitely generated.” Finite generation requires a surjection An↠MA^{n} \twoheadrightarrow MAn↠M. Finite presentation also requires its kernel to be finitely generated (that is, Am→An→M→0A^{m} \to A^{n} \to M \to 0Am→An→M→0 is exact).
