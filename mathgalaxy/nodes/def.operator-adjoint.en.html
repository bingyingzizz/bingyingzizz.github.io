Statement

Let V,WV, WV,W be finite-dimensional inner product spaces and T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W linear. Then there is a unique linear map T∗:W→VT^{\ast} : W \to VT∗:W→V such that

⟨Tv,w⟩W=⟨v,T∗w⟩V(∀v∈V,w∈W)\langle T v, w \rangle_W = \langle v, T^{\ast} w \rangle_V \qquad (\forall v \in V, w \in W)⟨Tv,w⟩W​=⟨v,T∗w⟩V​(∀v∈V,w∈W)

T∗T^{\ast}T∗ is called the adjoint of TTT. In an orthonormal basis, [T∗]=[T]†[T^{\ast}] = [T]^{\dagger}[T∗]=[T]† (conjugate transpose).

Properties: (T+S)∗=T∗+S∗(T + S)^{\ast} = T^{\ast} + S^{\ast}(T+S)∗=T∗+S∗, (cT)∗=cˉ T∗(cT)^{\ast} = \bar{c}\,T^{\ast}(cT)∗=cˉT∗, (TS)∗=S∗T∗(TS)^{\ast} = S^{\ast}T^{\ast}(TS)∗=S∗T∗, T∗∗=TT^{\ast\ast} = TT∗∗=T.

⚠️ This is not the same as an adjoint functor in category theory: that is a relation between two functors, whereas here we have an operator on the same space.

Notes

⭐ The way it is defined is worth noting: T∗T^{\ast}T∗ is not constructed but uniquely determined by an identity (existence comes from Riesz representation: w↦⟨T⋅,w⟩w \mapsto \langle T\cdot, w\ranglew↦⟨T⋅,w⟩ is a bounded linear functional on VVV).

This is the finite-dimensional inner-product version of "defining an object by a universal property".

⭐ In the complex case the conjugate in (cT)∗=cˉT∗(cT)^{\ast} = \bar c T^{\ast}(cT)∗=cˉT∗ cannot be dropped — it comes from the conjugate-linearity of the inner product in its second argument.
