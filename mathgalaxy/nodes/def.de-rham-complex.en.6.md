**Locally it always vanishes**: every point of any manifold has a contractible neighbourhood, so by the Poincaré lemma $H^{k} = 0$ for $k \ge 1$. Hence cohomology is **purely global** information — which is why it is more useful than local differential invariants.

**$H^{0}$ must be read separately**: $Z^{0} = \ker(\mathrm{d} : C^{\infty}(M) \to \Omega^{1}(M))$ consists of locally constant functions, and $B^{0} = 0$, so $H^{0}$ counts connected components (`thm.de-rham-h0`).

Only for $k \ge 1$ does one need a Poincaré-lemma-type argument.
