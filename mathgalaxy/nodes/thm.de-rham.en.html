Abstract

Integrating forms pairs de Rham cohomology, closed forms modulo exact ones, with singular homology, and the de Rham theorem says the pairing is perfect: for every kkk there is an isomorphism HdRk(M)→Hsingk(M;R)H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\to H^{k}_{\mathrm{sing}}(M;\mathbb{R})HdRk​(M)→Hsingk​(M;R) sending [ω][\omega][ω] to the functional [c]↦∫cω[c]\mapsto\int_{c}\omega[c]↦∫c​ω. The maps are compatible with pullbacks, so they form a natural isomorphism of graded algebras. The theorem welds analysis, geometry and topology into a single object.

Statement

In plain language: The cohomology defined using calculus (closed forms modulo exact forms) is the same as the topological cohomology built from simplices (by dualizing singular chains). This theorem connects analysis, geometry, and topology.

Let MMM be a smooth manifold. For each k≥0k \ge 0k≥0 there is an isomorphism

where Hsingk(M;R):=Hk(Hom⁡(C∙sing(M),R))H^{k}_{\mathrm{sing}}(M;\mathbb{R}) := H^{k}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}(C^{\mathrm{sing}}_{\bullet}(M),\mathbb{R})\bigr)Hsingk​(M;R):=Hk(Hom(C∙sing​(M),R)) is the cohomology of the dual of the singular chain complex (Singular Chain), and ∫cω\int_{c}\omega∫c​ω is integration of forms over chains.
