Statement

Let M,NM, NM,N be discrete abelian groups, also regarded as the corresponding constant condensed abelian groups. Then

RHom⁡Zcond(M, N)  ≅  c(RHom⁡Zabs(M, N)),M⊗ZL,condN  ≅  c(M⊗ZL,absN),\operatorname{RHom}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\ N) \;\cong\; \mathrm{c}\bigl(\operatorname{RHom}^{\mathrm{abs}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\ N)\bigr), \qquad M \otimes^{\mathbf{L},\mathrm{cond}}_{\mathbb{Z}} N \;\cong\; \mathrm{c}\bigl(M \otimes^{\mathbf{L},\mathrm{abs}}_{\mathbb{Z}} N\bigr),RHomZcond​(M, N)≅c(RHomZabs​(M, N)),M⊗ZL,cond​N≅c(M⊗ZL,abs​N),

where the superscript cond\mathrm{cond}cond means "computed in condensed abelian groups", abs\mathrm{abs}abs means "computed in abstract abelian groups", and c(A)\mathrm{c}(A)c(A) denotes the constant condensed group.

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: discrete groups "cannot feel" the condensed layer — on them, condensed homological algebra and abstract homological algebra give the same answer (up to a constant sheaf). So to really use the condensed structure one must test it on non-discrete objects such as R\mathbb{R}R, Z[S]\mathbb{Z}[S]Z[S].
