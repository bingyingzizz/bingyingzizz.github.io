It uses exactly the two requirements on a functor: preserve composition, preserve identities.

⚠ The converse fails: F(f)F(f)F(f) being an isomorphism does not imply that fff is one (a forgetful functor identifies linear isomorphisms with invertible linear maps, but also sends non-invertible square matrices to maps).

To go backwards one needs FFF fully faithful, which is the content of propositions such as fully faithful iff the unit is an isomorphism.

⭐ Small as it is, this is the first brick of "isomorphism is a categorical property": no functor distinguishes isomorphic objects, which is why invariants of isomorphism (rank, trace, determinant, homology) make sense.
