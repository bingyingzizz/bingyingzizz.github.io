Notes

Why free abelian: we want to add the simplices of a chain with signs — adding σ\sigmaσ three times gives 3σ3\sigma3σ, cancelling it against its reverse gives 000, and no other relations are allowed. The free abelian group is exactly the minimal structure that permits arbitrary addition on a basis with no extra relations.

⚠️ Exactly enough freedom: a group homomorphism is determined by its values on a basis, so to define the boundary operator later it suffices to write a formula on a single simplex and extend linearly — no compatibility check is required. That is the entire use of the word "free".

Other coefficient rings support the same theory: coefficients in Q\mathbb{Q}Q or R\mathbb{R}R give Betti numbers (torsion is invisible), while Z/2\mathbb{Z}/2Z/2 makes all signs (−1)i(-1)^{i}(−1)i disappear (no orientation worries). This region fixes coefficients in Z\mathbb{Z}Z.
