# isometry and orthogonal transformation　`def.isometry`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.isometry)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.isometry.md)

In the real case it is called an **orthogonal transformation**, in the complex case a **unitary transformation**; their matrices are called orthogonal matrices ($Q^{\mathsf{T}}Q = I$) and unitary matrices ($U^{\dagger}U = I$).

Equivalent characterization of an isometry: $T^{\ast}T = I$ ($\iff T$ invertible and $T^{\ast} = T^{-1}$). All orthogonal (unitary) transformations form a group $O(n)$ ($U(n)$).

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ An isometry preserves **all of geometry**: lengths, angles, orthogonality, volume ($|\det| = 1$).

Thus "two figures are congruent" is, algebraically, "they differ by an orthogonal transformation".
