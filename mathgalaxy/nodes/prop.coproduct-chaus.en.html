Statement

In the category of compact Hausdorff spaces (and likewise between free compact Hausdorff spaces), the disjoint union is the coproduct:

Hom⁡(S⊔T, Z)  ≅  Hom⁡(S,Z)×Hom⁡(T,Z).\operatorname{Hom}(S \sqcup T,\ Z) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}(S, Z) \times \operatorname{Hom}(T, Z).Hom(S⊔T, Z)≅Hom(S,Z)×Hom(T,Z).

Notes

Proof. A continuous map S⊔T→ZS \sqcup T \to ZS⊔T→Z is determined by its restrictions to the two halves ✓; conversely, given two continuous maps, gluing them remains continuous (the topology of the disjoint union is the sum of the two topologies) ✓. Hence the natural bijection above holds, i.e. the universal property of the coproduct ✓. Compact Hausdorff spaces are closed under finite disjoint unions (both pieces compact, both Hausdorff) ✓. ∎

⚠️ An infinite disjoint union is in general not compact, so the coproduct stays in the category only for finite families; this is also why the pretopology below uses finite disjoint unions rather than arbitrary ones.
