Statement

The following are equivalent:

  1. XXX is a Baire space (the intersection of countably many dense open sets is dense);
  2. every non-empty open subset of XXX is of the second category;
  3. a countable union of nowhere dense closed sets contains no non-empty open set (has empty interior).

Notes

The three statements are one statement after taking complements: dense open ↔\leftrightarrow↔ nowhere dense closed, intersection ↔\leftrightarrow↔ union, "dense" ↔\leftrightarrow↔ "empty interior".

⭐ How category is used (the lecture notes' formulation): to prove "there exists a point satisfying property PPP", prove that {x:P(x)}\{x : P(x)\}{x:P(x)} is a dense GδG_\deltaGδ​. What this yields is not merely existence but "almost everywhere existence" (genericity).

⚠️ The "second category" in (2) applies to non-empty open sets, not to arbitrary sets.

Why the three are equivalent, and what each step uses (this is the one that "looks obvious but needs complements and countability once each"):

  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): contradiction. Suppose some non-empty open UUU is meager: U⊆⋃nFnU \subseteq \bigcup_{n} F_{n}U⊆⋃n​Fn​, with each FnF_{n}Fn​ nowhere dense (closed, without loss). Put Gn:=X∖FnG_{n} := X \setminus F_{n}Gn​:=X∖Fn​; then each GnG_{n}Gn​ is dense open. By (1), G:=⋂nGnG := \bigcap_{n} G_{n}G:=⋂n​Gn​ is dense, hence G∩U≠∅G \cap U \ne \emptysetG∩U=∅; but x∈G∩Ux \in G \cap Ux∈G∩U would give x∈Ux \in Ux∈U and x∉⋃nFnx \notin \bigcup_n F_nx∈/⋃n​Fn​, contradicting U⊆⋃nFnU \subseteq \bigcup_{n} F_{n}U⊆⋃n​Fn​. This step uses only "a countable intersection of dense open sets is dense".
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): the same in reverse, still only complements: if a countable union of nowhere dense closed sets contains a non-empty open UUU, then UUU is meager, contradicting (2).
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): this step uses the construction of countable intersections. Let OnO_{n}On​ be dense open and put Fn:=X∖OnF_{n} := X \setminus O_{n}Fn​:=X∖On​ (nowhere dense closed). To prove that ⋂On\bigcap O_{n}⋂On​ is dense is to prove that its complement ⋃Fn\bigcup F_{n}⋃Fn​ has empty interior — which is exactly (3).
  • Note that "countable" in (1) cannot be replaced by "finite": the intersection of finitely many dense open sets is dense in every topological space (no hypothesis needed), so Baire-ness is entirely a phenomenon of countability.

Hypotheses that must be remembered beyond the three:

  • The equivalence of the three needs no hypothesis (it is purely topological, valid in any space); what needs hypotheses is "which spaces satisfy them": complete metric spaces (@@thm.baire@@) and locally compact Hausdorff spaces (@@thm.locally-compact-baire@@). The two proofs use completely different tools (nested balls/completeness versus compactness, finite subcovers and local compactness).
  • ⚠️ "Baire" is not a consequence of "complete" by itself, but the theorem "complete   ⟹  \implies⟹ Baire"; Q\mathbb{Q}Q is a (non-complete) metric space that is not Baire (each singleton is nowhere dense and Q\mathbb{Q}Q is countable), which shows precisely that completeness cannot be dropped.
