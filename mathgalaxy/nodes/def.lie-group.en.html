Statement

What it is about: a set that is both a group and a smooth manifold, with the two structures respecting each other — multiplication and inversion are both smooth. Group operations can therefore be studied with calculus.

A Lie group is a set GGG carrying at the same time

The group structure comes from "the product of orthogonal matrices is again orthogonal".

Topological restrictions: not every group can be a Lie group (second countability and local Euclideanity are required). Cartan's closed-subgroup theorem says that a closed subgroup of a Lie group is automatically a submanifold — this generalizes "SL⁡\operatorname{SL}SL and O⁡\operatorname{O}O are Lie groups"; this library only mentions it.

Why it matters: Lie groups turn "symmetry" into a differentiable object, so differentiating at the identity gives a Lie algebra (Lie 代数, 左不变向量场) — linearizing a nonlinear problem, the technical core of the whole theory.
