def.branched-cover

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let X,YX, YX,Y be a compact Riemann surface (Riemann surface), and let p:Y→Xp : Y \to Xp:Y→X be a nonconstant holomorphic map (Holomorphic Function). Then

  • There is a finite set B⊂XB \subset XB⊂X such that ppp restricts to a covering map on Y∖p−1(B)→X∖BY \setminus p^{-1}(B) \to X \setminus BY∖p−1(B)→X∖B (covering space);
  • The number of sheets ddd (independent of the point, by the sum identity for ramification indices below) is called the degree of ppp;
  • BBB is called the branch locus, the points of p−1(B)p^{-1}(B)p−1(B) are called ramification points, and ppp is called a branched covering of XXX of degree ddd.

Notes

⭐ Why compactness and holomorphicity are needed for this definition: Compactness ensures a finite number of sheets and a finite BBB. Without compactness, one point can have infinitely many preimages and branch points can accumulate.

Holomorphicity (the open mapping theorem and isolation of zeros) ensures that the only local model is z↦zez \mapsto z^{e}z↦ze. For purely topological branched coverings, finitely many sheets and a finite branch locus must be imposed as additional hypotheses.

⚠️ A branched covering becomes a covering map upon removal of BBB. The most common example is

p:P1→P1,z↦z2p : \mathbb{P}^{1} \to \mathbb{P}^{1}, \qquad z \mapsto z^{2}p:P1→P1,z↦z2

This is a branched covering of degree 222 with branch points 000 and ∞\infty∞ (at these points it is 222-to-111), and everywhere else it is 222-to-111.

📌 In brief, a branched covering is a finite-sheeted covering +++ finitely many points where ramification is allowed.
