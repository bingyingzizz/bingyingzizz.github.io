Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category and 0→K→L→M→00 \to K \to L \to M \to 00→K→L→M→0 a short exact sequence of complexes. Then there is a long exact sequence

⋯→Hn(K)→Hn(L)→Hn(M)→ δ Hn+1(K)→⋯\cdots \to H^{n}(K) \to H^{n}(L) \to H^{n}(M) \xrightarrow{\ \delta\ } H^{n+1}(K) \to \cdots⋯→Hn(K)→Hn(L)→Hn(M) δ ​Hn+1(K)→⋯

where δ\deltaδ is called the connecting homomorphism.

Corollary: Hn:K(A)→AH^{n} : \mathbf{K}(\mathcal{A}) \to \mathcal{A}Hn:K(A)→A is a cohomology functor.

Notes

What it says: a short exact sequence of complexes 0→K→L→M→00 \to K \to L \to M \to 00→K→L→M→0 yields a long exact sequence in cohomology, and the connecting homomorphism δ:Hn(M)→Hn+1(K)\delta : H^{n}(M) \to H^{n+1}(K)δ:Hn(M)→Hn+1(K) in the middle raises the degree by one.

⭐ How δ\deltaδ is built: take a representative of m∈Hn(M)m \in H^{n}(M)m∈Hn(M), lift it along the surjection Ln→MnL^{n} \to M^{n}Ln→Mn, then apply dLd_{L}dL​ and land in Kn+1K^{n+1}Kn+1 — one must show the result is independent of the choice of lift (two lifts differ by a coboundary), and this step is exactly diagram chasing.

⚠ This is not the same as the long exact sequence of derived functors (the short exact sequence →\to→ RnFR^{n}FRnF gives a long exact sequence): that one concerns derived functors, this one concerns the cohomology of the complexes themselves.
