Proof. There are five steps. ① Take the chains whose simplices each lie entirely in UUU or VVV to obtain a subcomplex. ② Use barycentric subdivision to prove that the inclusion of this subcomplex into the full complex induces an isomorphism in homology (this is the main difficulty). ③ Express the subcomplex as the sum of the chains in UUU and the chains in VVV. ④ Obtain a long exact sequence from the short exact sequence. ⑤ Substitute the isomorphism from step ②.

Reduced version. Apply the same argument to the augmented chain complexes. When U∩V≠∅U \cap V \ne \emptysetU∩V=∅, choose a basepoint x0∈U∩Vx_{0} \in U \cap Vx0​∈U∩V. When U∩V=∅U \cap V = \emptysetU∩V=∅, exactness fails in the segment H~0\tilde{H}_{0}H~0​. See the statement for a counterexample.

Why no good pair hypothesis is needed. The argument uses only the open cover and Lebesgue numbers. It requires no neighborhood deformation retraction of AAA in XXX. This is one way singular homology is easier to work with than homotopy groups.

⭐ The main difficulty is passing from simplices that lie locally in UUU or VVV to a global isomorphism in homology. The chain homotopy in Step 4 does this, and the exact sequence then follows by algebra. The signs are all in the cone construction, sd⁡p:=[bp,sd⁡p−1(∂Δp)]\operatorname{sd}_{p} := [b_{p}, \operatorname{sd}_{p-1}(\partial\Delta^{p})]sdp​:=[bp​,sdp−1​(∂Δp)] and tp:=[bp,cp]t_{p} := [b_{p}, c_{p}]tp​:=[bp​,cp​].

⚠️ Difference from Mayer–Vietoris Sequence. That node gives the de Rham version for smooth manifolds with real coefficients and the complex of differential forms (de Rham Complex and Cohomology). This is the singular-chain version for arbitrary spaces with Z\mathbb{Z}Z coefficients. The sequences have the same form, but only one retains torsion information. They are distinct results and should not be cited as proofs of each other.
