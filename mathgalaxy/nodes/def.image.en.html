Statement

For a morphism f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y, its image im⁡f\operatorname{im} fimf is a subobject of YYY that is initial in the category of all factorizations f:X→I↣Yf : X \to I \rightarrowtail Yf:X→I↣Y (that is, every other factorization factors uniquely through it).

Dually, coim⁡f\operatorname{coim} fcoimf is the co-universal object of factorizations X↠I→YX \twoheadrightarrow I \to YX↠I→Y.

There is always a morphism

coim⁡f⟶im⁡f\operatorname{coim} f \longrightarrow \operatorname{im} fcoimf⟶imf

When it is an isomorphism, fff is called strict.

Notes

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set, Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab, Top\mathbf{Top}Top and similar categories coim⁡f→im⁡f\operatorname{coim} f \to \operatorname{im} fcoimf→imf is always an isomorphism, so one need not distinguish the two; in a general category this fails.

Compared with Set\mathbf{Set}Set, where "the image is {f(x)}\{f(x)\}{f(x)}", what is added here is universality: the image is no longer a set of points but "the smallest intermediate object through which everything factors". Looking at subobjects, monomorphisms, and images together is a sample of "replacing the language of elements by the language of arrows".
