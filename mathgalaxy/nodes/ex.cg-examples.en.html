Statement

Example 1 A locally compact Hausdorff space is compactly generated.

Example 2 A sequential space is compactly generated: A⊆XA \subseteq XA⊆X is closed   ⟺  \iff⟺ whenever xn→xx_{n} \to xxn​→x and xn∈Ax_{n} \in Axn​∈A, one has x∈Ax \in Ax∈A.

Example 3 If III is uncountable, then RI\mathbb{R}^{I}RI and ZI\mathbb{Z}^{I}ZI (product topology) are not compactly generated.

Notes

Example 1 is the most used one: a compact Hausdorff space is of course compactly generated (it is its own colimit); a general locally compact Hausdorff space has its topology determined by maps "take a compact neighbourhood".

Example 3 is the counterexample — it is precisely the source of "products behave badly": RI\mathbb{R}^{I}RI is too fine, so fine that there are not enough compact Hausdorff spaces to detect its topology. kkk-ification replaces it by the coarser k(RI)k(\mathbb{R}^{I})k(RI), which behaves well.
