## Notes · **draft**
**Examples**: $(\mathbb Z,+)$, $(\mathbb Q,+)$, $(\mathbb R,+)$ and $(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,+)$ are abelian groups, and so is $(\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$. The $n\times n$ invertible matrices do not form one under multiplication ($n\ge 2$).

**Why a separate entry**: @@def.group@@ keeps only the group axioms; abelian groups recur in module theory, homological algebra, and the definition of $\mathbf{Ab}$, so a separate entry can be cited directly by @@def.module@@ and @@def.category-of-abelian-groups@@.
