Statement

In plain words: the value of a harmonic function at a point equals its average over the circle centred at that point of any radius; it also equals the area average over the disc.

⭐ The converse also holds: if uuu is continuous and has the circle mean property, then uuu is harmonic. So the "mean property" can serve as another definition of harmonic function — many books do exactly this, to avoid second-order differentiability.

⚠ The mean must be taken over the whole circle, not just the few points straight above; nor over an arbitrary closed curve — that is precisely what the Poisson formula answers.

⚠ uuu need only be harmonic in the interior of B(a,r)B(a, r)B(a,r); it is not required to be harmonic on the closed disc.

📌 The mean property immediately yields the maximum principle (see the entry on the maximum principle for harmonic functions), and it is also the starting point for "holomorphic functions are real-analytic" (expand the integral into a power series).
