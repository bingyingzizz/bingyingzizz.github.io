Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a site and F:Cop→SetF : \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{Set}F:Cop→Set a presheaf.

  • FFF is called separated if for every XXX and every R∈J(X)R \in J(X)R∈J(X), the restriction map
Hom⁡(hX,F)⟶Hom⁡(R,F)\operatorname{Hom}(h_{X}, F) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(R, F)Hom(hX​,F)⟶Hom(R,F)

is injective;

  • FFF is called a sheaf if this map is bijective.

Notes

By the Yoneda lemma Hom⁡(hX,F)≅F(X)\operatorname{Hom}(h_{X}, F) \cong F(X)Hom(hX​,F)≅F(X), this definition can be read as two sentences: the left-hand side is F(X)F(X)F(X) (global sections), while the right-hand side is "a compatible family of sections along RRR":

  • Injective = separatedness: two global sections that agree on RRR must be equal;
  • Bijective = gluing: any compatible family of sections glues uniquely into a global section.

In the old language of topological spaces (C=Open⁡(X)op\mathcal{C} = \operatorname{Open}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}C=Open(X)op), these two conditions read: if s∣Ui=t∣Uis|_{U_{i}} = t|_{U_{i}}s∣Ui​​=t∣Ui​​ holds everywhere then s=ts = ts=t; and local sections sis_{i}si​ that agree on overlaps glue into a global section.
