Statement

All fractional linear transformations form a group under composition, denoted PGL⁡(2,C)\operatorname{PGL}(2, \mathbb{C})PGL(2,C); it is isomorphic to the group of 2×22 \times 22×2 complex matrices modulo nonzero scalars.

Moreover, it is exactly the group of holomorphic automorphisms of the extended plane C^\widehat{\mathbb{C}}C: every biholomorphism C^→C^\widehat{\mathbb{C}} \to \widehat{\mathbb{C}}C→C is fractional linear.

Notes

Group structure: matrix multiplication corresponds to composition of transformations; (abcd)\begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix}(ac​bd​) and λ(abcd)\lambda\begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix}λ(ac​bd​) give the same transformation, so one must quotient by scalars. The matrix of the inverse transformation is the inverse matrix (up to a scalar).

Why there are no other automorphisms: a holomorphic automorphism of C^\widehat{\mathbb{C}}C — look at the image of ∞\infty∞. If it is ∞\infty∞, the map is entire and bijective — by a Liouville-type argument it must be az+baz + baz+b. Otherwise — when the image is not ∞\infty∞ — compose first with z↦1/(z−z0)z \mapsto 1/(z - z_{0})z↦1/(z−z0​) to reduce to the previous case. ∎

⭐ Three kinds of subgroups correspond to three geometries: the real fractional linear transformations preserving the upper half-plane, ≅PGL(2,R)≅PSL(2,R)\cong \mathrm{PGL}(2, \mathbb{R}) \cong \mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{R})≅PGL(2,R)≅PSL(2,R), form the isometry group of the hyperbolic plane; those preserving the unit disc are conjugate to them. Schwarz–Pick (Schwarz–Pick 定理) says precisely that "a holomorphic self-map does not increase the hyperbolic distance", and equality holds exactly for elements of this group.
