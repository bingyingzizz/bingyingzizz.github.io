def.subcategory

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A subcategory of a category C\mathcal{C}C, written C′\mathcal{C}'C′, consists of:

  1. a subclass Ob⁡(C′)⊆Ob⁡(C)\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C}') \subseteq \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})Ob(C′)⊆Ob(C) of the objects;
  2. for each pair X,Y∈C′X, Y \in \mathcal{C}'X,Y∈C′, a subset Hom⁡C′(X,Y)⊆Hom⁡C(X,Y)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}'}(X, Y) \subseteq \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, Y)HomC′​(X,Y)⊆HomC​(X,Y);

these morphisms must be closed under composition and contain the identity of every object. (When all morphisms between X,YX, YX,Y are taken, it is called a full subcategory.)

Notes

⚠️ A subcategory is not a full subcategory: a full subcategory takes some objects but drops no morphisms; a general subcategory may drop morphisms as well.

Strict subcategory: the identities must also be the identities inside the subcategory. Almost all contexts assume this.

Examples: Ab↪Grp\mathbf{Ab} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Grp}Ab↪Grp is a full subcategory (homomorphisms of abelian groups are exactly their homomorphisms as groups); CHaus↪Top\mathbf{CHaus} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Top}CHaus↪Top is a full subcategory;

Grp→Set\mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Set}Grp→Set is not a subcategory (not even the objects are the same sets).

⭐ “Reflective” and “coreflective” subcategories add one more condition to a full subcategory: the inclusion functor has an adjoint.
