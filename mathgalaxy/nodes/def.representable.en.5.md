- Let $k \in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathbf{CRng})$ and let $F : k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg} \to \mathbf{Set}$ be the forgetful functor. Then $(k[t],\ t)$ represents $F$: for $A \in k\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}$ and $a \in F(A)$, sending $t \mapsto a$ induces a unique $k$-algebra homomorphism $k[t] \to A$.
- Let $F : \mathbf{Ring} \to \mathbf{Set}$ be the constant functor at the one-point set. Then $\mathbb{Z}$ represents $F$: every ring has a unique $\mathbb{Z} \to R$.
