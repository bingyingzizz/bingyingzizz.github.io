Statement

The Gaussian curvature KKK depends only on the first fundamental form III. Precisely: if φ:S1→S2\varphi : S_{1}\to S_{2}φ:S1​→S2​ is a local isometry, then

Notes

Why it is called "remarkable": KKK is the product of the two principal curvatures, which come from dn\mathrm{d}ndn, where nnn is the unit normal in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 — extrinsic data by any account. Yet it can be read off from the metric inside the surface alone. All of intrinsic geometry (Gauss–Bonnet, uniformization, the topological meaning of KKK) grows from this.

An operational reading: an inhabitant of the surface who can only measure lengths can still determine KKK. Measure the circumference ρ\rhoρ of a small geodesic circle of radius L(ρ)L(\rho)L(ρ); then

K(p)=3πlim⁡ρ→02πρ−L(ρ)ρ3.K(p) = \frac{3}{\pi}\lim_{\rho\to0}\frac{2\pi\rho-L(\rho)}{\rho^{3}}.K(p)=π3​ρ→0lim​ρ32πρ−L(ρ)​.

This turns the remarkable theorem into an experiment: curvature is the third-order rate at which the circumference falls short of 2πρ2\pi\rho2πρ.

Two checks: the cylinder (K=0K = 0K=0) is locally isometric to the plane, so the curvatures must agree; the sphere (K=1/R2K = 1/R^{2}K=1/R2) is not locally isometric to any surface with K=0K = 0K=0, so curvature really is an isometry invariant.

⚠️ What is not intrinsic: k1k_{1}k1​, k2k_{2}k2​ and HHH are all extrinsic (the cylinder versus the plane is the counterexample: k1k_{1}k1​ is 1/R1/R1/R and 000 respectively). The remarkable theorem holds for the product, not for the factors. This also explains why KKK appears everywhere in intrinsic geometry while HHH hardly ever does.
