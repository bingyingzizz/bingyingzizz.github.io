## Notes · **draft**
**Intuition.** To probe with $X$, there is no need to wait until the colimit has been formed — maps out of $X$ are **already determined at a finite stage**, and the filtered colimit just assembles them. So “compact” means “no need to reach out to infinity.”

**Examples.** Finite sets (in $\mathbf{Set}$), finitely generated modules (in $A\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$), and finite CW complexes (in topology).

$\mathbb{R}$ is **not** compact in $\mathbf{Top}$ ($\mathbb{R}$ is a filtered colimit of finite intervals, but $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{R},-)$ does not preserve that colimit).
