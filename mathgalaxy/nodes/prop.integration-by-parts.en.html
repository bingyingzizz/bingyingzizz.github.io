Statement

Notes

⭐ It is again the product rule read backwards: (FG)′=F′G+FG′(FG)' = F'G + FG'(FG)′=F′G+FG′; integrate both sides and use Newton–Leibniz.

⚠ A common error when checking the boundary terms: writing F(b)G(b)−F(a)G(a)F(b)G(b) - F(a)G(a)F(b)G(b)−F(a)G(a) as (FG)(b−a)(FG)(b - a)(FG)(b−a) — that means "the value of FGFGFG at the point b−ab - ab−a" (or (b−a)(b-a)(b−a) times FGFGFG), which is neither "the difference of the product at the endpoints" nor "the integral of the product".

⭐ The most valuable form of integration by parts is "moving a term": ∫FG′=FG∣ab−∫F′G\int F G' = FG\bigr|_a^b - \int F'G∫FG′=FG​ab​−∫F′G moves the hard-to-integrate side to the other side.

On the Lebesgue side it corresponds to the whole package of "weak derivatives / differentiation in the distributional sense".
