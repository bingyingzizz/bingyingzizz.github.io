Abstract

A smooth manifold is a Hausdorff second countable space covered by charts (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ) into Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn whose transition maps ψ∘φ−1\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}ψ∘φ−1 are C∞C^{\infty}C∞, equipped with a maximal atlas. Maximising the atlas is what makes smoothness a property of the manifold rather than of a chosen family of charts. Hausdorff separates points, while second countability gives paracompactness and hence partitions of unity, the tool that glues local constructions into global ones. A smooth structure is extra data, not a topological invariant: one topological space can carry inequivalent smooth structures.

Statement

What this does: it moves calculus from Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn to spaces that "look locally like Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn" — build a stack of coordinate charts, require that changes of chart be smooth, and only then do "smooth functions" acquire meaning.

Let MMM be a Hausdorff, second-countable topological space.

An nnn-dimensional chart on MMM is (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), where U⊆MU\subseteq MU⊆M is open and φ:U→φ(U)⊆Rn\varphi : U\to\varphi(U)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}φ:U→φ(U)⊆Rn is a homeomorphism.

Two charts (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), (V,ψ)(V,\psi)(V,ψ) are C∞C^{\infty}C∞-compatible if the transition map

ψ∘φ−1:φ(U∩V)⟶ψ(U∩V)\psi\circ\varphi^{-1} : \varphi(U\cap V)\longrightarrow\psi(U\cap V)ψ∘φ−1:φ(U∩V)⟶ψ(U∩V)

is C∞C^{\infty}C∞ (it is defined on an open subset of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, so it is tested by partial derivatives). A family of pairwise compatible charts covering MMM is an atlas; adding to an atlas all charts compatible with it gives a maximal atlas, also called a smooth structure on MMM. An nnn-dimensional manifold equipped with a smooth structure is an nnn-dimensional smooth manifold.

Smooth functions: f:M→Rf : M\to\mathbb{R}f:M→R is smooth if for every chart (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), the map f∘φ−1f\circ\varphi^{-1}f∘φ−1 is smooth on φ(U)\varphi(U)φ(U).

Examples: Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn (one chart); Sn⊆Rn+1S^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}Sn⊆Rn+1 (stereographic projection gives two charts); RPn\mathbb{RP}^{n}RPn (n+1n+1n+1 affine charts); any open subset; products of manifolds.

Notes

Why one must pass to the maximal atlas: if one only says "there is a family of compatible charts", "fff is smooth" depends on the choice of that family; after maximalization, every chart compatible with the atlas is automatically in it, so smoothness becomes a property of the manifold rather than of one chart.

Division of labour among the three topological conditions: Hausdorff rules out "two points glued together and inseparable" (otherwise limits are not unique); second countability gives Lindelöf and paracompactness, hence partitions of unity (the 仿紧空间 line) — only with them can local metrics and local forms be glued into global ones.

⚠ A smooth structure is not part of the topology: the same topological space can carry inequivalent smooth structures (Milnor's exotic 7-sphere; R4\mathbb{R}^{4}R4 carries uncountably many pairwise inequivalent smooth structures). So a "smooth manifold" is a topological space plus structure, not a property of the space.

Interface with existing content: the implicit function theorem gives that level sets in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn are manifolds (正则水平集定理); conversely, every manifold embeds into some RN\mathbb{R}^{N}RN (Whitney), but NNN may be much larger than nnn, so the abstract definition is not superfluous.

⚠ The surfaces of this map (曲面与参数化) are the concrete two-dimensional model: the overlap of two charts is a change of parameters, and "C∞C^{\infty}C∞-compatible" is exactly "smoothness is unchanged after reparametrization".
