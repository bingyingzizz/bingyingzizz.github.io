Statement

Let KKK be compact, (E,d)(E, d)(E,d) a metric space, and H⊆C(K,E)\mathcal{H} \subseteq C(K, E)H⊆C(K,E). Then the following are equivalent:

  1. H\mathcal{H}H is totally bounded in (C(K,E),Δ)(C(K,E), \Delta)(C(K,E),Δ);
  2. (a) H\mathcal{H}H is equicontinuous; (b) H(x):={f(x):f∈H}\mathcal{H}(x) := \{f(x) : f \in \mathcal{H}\}H(x):={f(x):f∈H} is totally bounded for every x∈Kx \in Kx∈K.

Notes

"1   ⟹  \implies⟹ 2": total boundedness gives a finite ε\varepsilonε-net f1,…,fnf_1, \ldots, f_nf1​,…,fn​, and then H∪{f1,…,fn}\mathcal{H} \cup \{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}H∪{f1​,…,fn​} is still equicontinuous (finitely many continuous functions plus one equicontinuous family).

⭐ This is the same theorem as Arzelà–Ascoli 定理 in the graph (R\mathbb{R}R-valued, compact metric space, uniformly bounded), in a second form: here EEE may be any metric space, and "uniformly bounded" is refined to "pointwise totally bounded".

⚠️ "Pointwise totally bounded" cannot be replaced by "pointwise bounded": when EEE is incomplete, relative compactness needs "coverable", whereas boundedness only says "does not diverge".

Together with "C(K,E)C(K,E)C(K,E) is complete" this is the standard form: totally bounded + complete === relatively compact.

Which hypothesis each direction uses (the characterisation of total boundedness does not require completeness of the target):

  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ pointwise total boundedness: the evaluation map is 1-Lipschitz for the uniform distance, so a finite net maps to a finite net; this is an elementary check.
  • (1) 111 equicontinuity: first take a uniform finite net, then use the finitely many continuous functions in the net and a common neighbourhood of each point together with the triangle inequality; one must not assume circularly that the original family is already equicontinuous.
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): equicontinuity gives local oscillation control, compactness extracts finitely many controlling neighbourhoods, and the pointwise finite nets give finitely many value patterns; this is where the converse pays for compactness.
  • Pointwise boundedness cannot replace pointwise total boundedness: let   ⟹  \implies⟹ be a single point and KKK; the family of orthonormal vectors is bounded but not totally bounded, and this fails even though E=ℓ2E=\ell^2E=ℓ2 is complete.
  • To upgrade total boundedness to compactness of the closure one still needs completeness of the ambient space; for EEE a single point and KKK, the set Q∩[0,1]\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]Q∩[0,1] (inside E=QE=\mathbb QE=Q) is totally bounded but not relatively compact.
