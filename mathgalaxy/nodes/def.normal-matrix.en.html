def.normal-matrix

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

What it does: collects unitary matrices, Hermitian matrices, and those matrices for which A∗AA^{*}AA∗A and AA∗AA^{*}AA∗ do not matter in which order, into a single class — and this class is exactly the set of unitarily diagonalizable matrices.

Let A∈Mn(C)A \in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})A∈Mn​(C). Call AAA a normal matrix if

A∗A=AA∗.A^{*}A = AA^{*} .A∗A=AA∗.

Inclusions (all strict):

{\{{ unitary }⊂{\} \subset \{}⊂{ normal }\}}, {\{{ Hermitian }⊂{\} \subset \{}⊂{ normal }\}}, {\{{ real symmetric }⊂{\} \subset \{}⊂{ normal }\}}.

Core result (thm.spectral-matrix): AAA normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ there exists a unitary matrix UUU such that U∗AUU^{*}AUU∗AU is diagonal.

The real case corresponds to real normal matrices (ATA=AATA^{T}A = AA^{T}ATA=AAT): they are orthogonally diagonalizable, but as a block diagonal with 1×11\times 11×1 and 2×22\times 22×2 blocks, not necessarily purely diagonal — because non-real eigenvalues can only occur in conjugate pairs.

Notes

Why normality is the "right" class: in the proof of the spectral theorem, the only things really used are "eigenvectors are orthogonal + there are enough eigenvectors". Together these two are exactly equivalent to A∗A=AA∗A^{*}A = AA^{*}A∗A=AA∗ — so "normal" is not an artificial condition, but the algebraic translation of "unitarily diagonalizable".

Why real normal matrices can only be block-diagonalized: (0−110)\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}(01​−10​) is real normal (it is a rotation by 90∘90^\circ90∘), but it has no real eigenvector, so it certainly cannot be orthogonally diagonalized. Its two eigenvalues are ±i\pm i±i — conjugate pairs, the source of those 2×22\times 22×2 blocks.

⚠ Relation to def.normal-operator (normal operator): that entry speaks about an operator T∗T=TT∗T^{*}T = TT^{*}T∗T=TT∗ and is coordinate-free; this entry speaks about the matrix condition in an orthonormal basis. The two translate into each other, but do not mix them (after a non-orthonormal change of basis the matrix of the operator is no longer "normal").

⭐ It is enough to remember this table: normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ unitarily diagonalizable; adding further conditions yields the special classes — adding "all eigenvalues real" gives Hermitian, adding "all eigenvalues on the unit circle" gives unitary, and adding both gives the self-adjoint unitary with "eigenvalues only ±1\pm 1±1".
