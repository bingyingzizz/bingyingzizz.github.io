Statement

Let AAA be a ring and I,J⊆AI, J \subseteq AI,J⊆A ideals. Then:

(i) I∩JI \cap JI∩J and

I+J:={ x+y:x∈I, y∈J }I + J := \{\, x + y : x \in I,\ y \in J \,\}I+J:={x+y:x∈I, y∈J}

(ii) the set of finite sums

Notes

⭐ Compare groups: to quotient a group into a group, one quotients by a normal subgroup; to quotient a ring into a ring, one quotients by an ideal.

The reason is exactly the same — the operations on the quotient set must be independent of the representative.

⚠ The union of ideals is not generally an ideal: 2Z∪3Z2\mathbb{Z} \cup 3\mathbb{Z}2Z∪3Z does not contain 2+3=52 + 3 = 52+3=5.

So the substitute for "union" is the sum I+JI + JI+J, which is the smallest ideal containing both.

⭐ The universal property of the quotient ring is the other face of the "first isomorphism theorem": when ker⁡φ⊇I\ker \varphi \supseteq Ikerφ⊇I, φ\varphiφ factors through A/IA/IA/I, and A/ker⁡φ≅im⁡φA/\ker\varphi \cong \operatorname{im}\varphiA/kerφ≅imφ.
