Statement

In plain words: as long as fff is holomorphic on and inside a triangle (nothing else is required, not even continuity of f′f'f′), the integral along the boundary of the triangle is 000. This is the "minimal core" of Cauchy's theorem.

Let TTT be a closed triangle and fff holomorphic on some open set containing TTT. Then

∫∂Tf(z) dz=0.\int_{\partial T} f(z)\,\mathrm{d}z = 0.∫∂T​f(z)dz=0.

Notes

⭐ The method is bisection plus shrinking the triangle to a point, with no Green's theorem and no continuity of f′f'f′.

Step 1 (quartering): join the midpoints of the three sides to split TTT into four small triangles.

The integral over the whole boundary equals the sum of the four small boundaries (the three interior edges are each traversed twice in opposite directions and cancel), so at least one satisfies ∣I∣≤4 ∣I(T1)∣|I| \le 4\,|I(T_1)|∣I∣≤4∣I(T1​)∣.
