# Companion Matrix　`def.companion-matrix`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.companion-matrix)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.companion-matrix.md)

From $d(C_d)=0$ we get $\mu_{C_d}\mid d$; and since $1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}$ are linearly independent in $K[x]/(d)$, we get $\deg\mu_{C_d}=m=\deg d$, hence $\mu_{C_d}=d$; also, $\chi_{C_d}$ and $d$ are both monic of degree $m$ and $\mu\mid\chi$, so $\chi_{C_d}=d$.

Convention: some textbooks put the $1$ on the superdiagonal and the coefficients in the first row; the two conventions differ by a transpose, and the characteristic and minimal polynomials are unchanged.

When $d=1$, we have $m=0$, $C_d$ is the empty matrix, and this definition only discusses $m\ge1$.
