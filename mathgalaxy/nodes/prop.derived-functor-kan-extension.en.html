prop.derived-functor-kan-extension

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let F:A→A′F : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}'F:A→A′ be a left exact additive functor. Then RFRFRF is the composite map

K+(A)→ F K+(A′)⟶D+(A′)\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{A}) \xrightarrow{\ F\ } \mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{A}') \longrightarrow \mathbf{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A}')K+(A) F ​K+(A′)⟶D+(A′)

along the localization K+(A)→D+(A)\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{A}) \to \mathbf{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})K+(A)→D+(A) (dually: the LFLFLF of a right exact functor along the other localization).

Notes

Proof. Localization (turning quasi-isomorphisms into isomorphisms) is left adjoint to precomposition ✓, and a left exact functor behaves well on quasi-isomorphisms, so the composite functor "first take FFF, then localize" has a left Kan extension along the localization, and it is precisely RFRFRF ✓ — this is the universal characterization of the derived functor: it is the best extension of FFF in the derived category ✓. ∎

⭐ How to read it: the "derived functor" is nothing new; it is the Kan extension along localization — paired with "Kan extension is adjunction".
