Statement

An additive category is a preadditive category satisfying the following two conditions:

  1. it has a zero object (End⁡(M)=1\operatorname{End}(M) = 1End(M)=1, i.e. an object with 1M=0M1_{M} = 0_{M}1M​=0M​, see @@def.zero-object@@);
  2. any two objects have a direct sum M=M1⊕M2M = M_{1} \oplus M_{2}M=M1​⊕M2​: there exist morphisms pk:M→Mkp_{k} : M \to M_{k}pk​:M→Mk​, ik:Mk→Mi_{k} : M_{k} \to Mik​:Mk​→M such that p1i1=1M1p_{1} i_{1} = 1_{M_{1}}p1​i1​=1M1​​, p2i2=1M2p_{2} i_{2} = 1_{M_{2}}p2​i2​=1M2​​, i1p1+i2p2=1Mi_{1} p_{1} + i_{2} p_{2} = 1_{M}i1​p1​+i2​p2​=1M​.

Equivalently: an additive category is a preadditive category in which finite products and finite coproducts exist and coincide (biproducts).

Notes

Direct sum and direct product are the same object in an additive category (a biproduct): M1⊕M2M_{1} \oplus M_{2}M1​⊕M2​ carries both the projections of the product and the inclusions of the coproduct, satisfying the three identities above.

Examples: A-ModA\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}A-Mod, Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab, and the category of complexes C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C) (with termwise direct sums) are all additive.

⚠️ An additive category supplies only "addition" and "direct sums"; to speak of kernels, cokernels, and exact sequences one still needs pre-abelian (see @@def.pre-abelian-category@@).
