Statement

On the square [0,1]×[0,1][0,1] \times [0,1][0,1]×[0,1], take the equivalence relation that glues the left and right sides in opposite directions, namely

(0,t)∼(1,1−t),0≤t≤1.(0, t) \sim (1, 1 - t), \qquad 0 \le t \le 1.(0,t)∼(1,1−t),0≤t≤1.

The quotient space M:=([0,1]×[0,1])/∼M := ([0,1]\times[0,1])/\simM:=([0,1]×[0,1])/∼ is called the Möbius band.

Notes

Comparison with the cylinder: The cylinder glues the two sides in the same direction, (0,t)∼(1,t)(0, t) \sim (1, t)(0,t)∼(1,t). The Möbius band reverses one end before gluing (t↦1−tt \mapsto 1 - tt↦1−t). This single change gives very different results.

⭐ Nonorientability: After one circuit along the centerline t=1/2t = 1/2t=1/2, the normal vector reverses direction, so the Möbius band admits no orientation. It is also a standard building block for nonorientable surfaces: M∪∂M≅M \cup_{\partial} M \congM∪∂​M≅ the Klein bottle (see Klein bottle).

The boundary is one circle: ∂M≅S1\partial M \cong S^{1}∂M≅S1 (one circle, whereas the cylinder has two). The subspace ∂M\partial M∂M is connected, which also gives an intuitive view of nonorientability.

⚠ It cannot embed in the plane. There is no Möbius band in R2\mathbb{R}^{2}R2. It can embed without self-intersection in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3, as a strip of paper given a half twist and glued. The picture that requires self-intersection is an immersion of RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2. The realization in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 is an example of a nonorientable surface (compare Orientable Surface).

📌 Euler characteristic: MMM is homotopy equivalent to the cylinder (both are homotopy equivalent to S1S^{1}S1), so χ(M)=χ(S1)=0\chi(M) = \chi(S^{1}) = 0χ(M)=χ(S1)=0. Homotopy invariants cannot detect the twist, while orientability can.
