## Notes · **draft**
⭐ $n = 2$, $a = 2$ is exactly the **existence of $\sqrt{2}$**: $\{q \in \mathbb{Q} : q^{2} < 2\}$ has no least upper bound in $\mathbb{Q}$, but does in $\mathbb{R}$ — that least upper bound is $\sqrt{2}$.

So this theorem is the first concrete construction of "what $\mathbb{R}$ has beyond $\mathbb{Q}$".

⭐ The proof is a **template for the least upper bound principle**: let $S = \{t \ge 0 : t^{n} \le a\}$, take $b = \sup S$, then prove $b^{n} = a$ (neither inequality may be strict: if too small, raise a little; if too large, lower a little).
