Statement

Let VVV be a KKK-vector space. The dual space of VVV is defined as the set of all linear maps from VVV to KKK:

V∗:=Hom⁡K(V,K)V^{\ast} := \operatorname{Hom}_K(V, K)V∗:=HomK​(V,K)

its elements are called linear functionals. V∗V^{\ast}V∗ becomes a vector space under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication.

If T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W is linear, define the transpose (or dual) map

T∗:W∗→V∗,φ⟼φ∘TT^{\ast} : W^{\ast} \to V^{\ast}, \qquad \varphi \longmapsto \varphi \circ TT∗:W∗→V∗,φ⟼φ∘T

It is linear, and (S∘T)∗=T∗∘S∗(S \circ T)^{\ast} = T^{\ast} \circ S^{\ast}(S∘T)∗=T∗∘S∗ (contravariance).

Notes

⭐ Mind the direction: the dual T∗T^{\ast}T∗ of T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W is W∗→V∗W^{\ast} \to V^{\ast}W∗→V∗, the arrow is reversed.

This is the entire meaning of the word "dual", and it is consistent with the status of (−)∗(-)^{\ast}(−)∗ as a contravariant functor.

⭐ In finite dimension V≅V∗V \cong V^{\ast}V≅V∗ (equal dimensions), but there is no natural isomorphism — to find one, a basis must first be chosen.

Whereas V≅V∗∗V \cong V^{\ast\ast}V≅V∗∗ has the natural isomorphism v↦(φ↦φ(v))v \mapsto (\varphi \mapsto \varphi(v))v↦(φ↦φ(v)); this is "the dual of the dual returns to itself".

⚠ In infinite dimension V∗V^{\ast}V∗ is strictly larger than VVV: dim⁡V∗=#(K)dim⁡V\dim V^{\ast} = \#(K)^{\dim V}dimV∗=#(K)dimV is a cardinality theorem of Erdős–Kaplansky (it uses AC; #(V∗)=#(K)dim⁡V\#(V^{\ast}) = \#(K)^{\dim V}#(V∗)=#(K)dimV is elementary, but "the dimension equals this cardinal" is not), so the above isomorphism fails.
