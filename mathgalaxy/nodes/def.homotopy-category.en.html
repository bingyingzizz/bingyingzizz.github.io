Statement

The homotopy category K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C) has the same objects as C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C), and morphisms

Hom⁡K(C)(K,L)  :=  Hom⁡C(C)(K,L)/ ∼,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})}(K, L) \;:=\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})}(K, L) \big/\ \sim,HomK(C)​(K,L):=HomC(C)​(K,L)/ ∼,

where ∼\sim∼ is generated by the null-homotopic maps.

Its isomorphisms are called homotopy equivalences; a complex that in K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C) becomes 000 is called homotopy trivial.

Notes

  • (a) Equivalent formulation: f:K→Lf : K \to Lf:K→L is a homotopy equivalence - (b) there exists g:L→Kg : L \to Kg:L→K with g∘f∼1Kg \circ f \sim 1_{K}g∘f∼1K​, f∘g∼1Lf \circ g \sim 1_{L}f∘g∼1L​; KKK is homotopy trivial - (c) 1K∼0K1_{K} \sim 0_{K}1K​∼0K​.

K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C) is an additive category, and C(C)→K(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})C(C)→K(C) is additive;

any additive functor F:C→C′F : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'F:C→C′ induces K(C)→K(C′)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C}')K(C)→K(C′).

Why work in the homotopy category: homology functors are invariant under homotopy, so quotienting out homotopy first leaves exactly the information homology can see.
