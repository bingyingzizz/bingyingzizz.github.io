Statement

Let (X,T)(X, \mathcal{T})(X,T) be a topological space and B⊆T\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{T}B⊆T. Then B\mathcal{B}B is a base for the topology T\mathcal{T}T if and only if every open set is a union of members of B\mathcal{B}B. If one further requires T\mathcal{T}T to be exactly the topology generated by B\mathcal{B}B (that is, B⊆T\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{T}B⊆T, and T\mathcal{T}T is precisely the family of all unions of members of B\mathcal{B}B), this is equivalent to:

  1. ⋃B=X\bigcup \mathcal{B} = X⋃B=X;
  2. for B1,B2∈BB_1, B_2 \in \mathcal{B}B1​,B2​∈B and x∈B1∩B2x \in B_1 \cap B_2x∈B1​∩B2​, there exists B3∈BB_3 \in \mathcal{B}B3​∈B with x∈B3⊆B1∩B2x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2x∈B3​⊆B1​∩B2​.

A family S⊆P(X)\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)S⊆P(X) is a subbase if and only if all its finite intersections form a base.

Notes

⭐ Why bases are useful: a condition checked on a base holds automatically for all open sets.

  • In metric spaces: all open balls form a base;
  • On R\mathbb{R}R: open intervals form a base; the half-open intervals {(a,b]}\{ (a, b] \}{(a,b]} generate a base for another topology (the upper limit topology) — they are not the usual open sets, hence not a base for the usual topology (although they are handier when constructing the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure);
  • The cylinder sets of the product topology and of the σ\sigmaσ-algebra follow the same pattern: first fix a convenient family of sets, then take what it generates.

⚠ A base is not unique: the same topology can have many bases.

Which hypothesis each direction uses (generating a topology and being a base for a specified topology must be kept distinct):

  • Being a base for the given T\mathcal TT   ⟺  \iff⟺ every T\mathcal TT-open set is a union of basic elements: this is definition unpacking; the covering and local-intersection conditions then follow using XXX and the fact that the intersection of two basic elements is open.
  • Covering plus local-intersection conditions   ⟹  \implies⟹ the arbitrary unions of basic elements form a topology: finite intersections are handled by local refinement, the remaining axioms are checked directly, no choice is needed, and one may take all basic elements satisfying the conditions.
  • The two conditions listed in the node do not by themselves imply being a base for the given T\mathcal TT: one also needs T\mathcal TT to equal the generated topology. For example, on the discrete two-point space take B={X}\mathcal B=\{X\}B={X}; it satisfies both conditions yet does not generate the singleton open sets.
  • A subbase   ⟺  \iff⟺ the family of all finite intersections is a base: by convention this is definition unpacking, and finite intersections should include the empty intersection XXX; for the usual topology on the reals the basic elements must be usual open sets, while half-open intervals generate another topology.
