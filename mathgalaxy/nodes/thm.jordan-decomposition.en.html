Statement

Let ν\nuν be a signed measure. Then there exist measures μ1,μ2\mu_1, \mu_2μ1​,μ2​ such that

ν=μ1−μ2,μ1⊥μ2,\nu = \mu_1 - \mu_2, \quad \mu_1 \perp \mu_2, ν=μ1​−μ2​,μ1​⊥μ2​,

and at least one of them is finite. Such a decomposition is unique.

Concretely, take a Hahn decomposition X=P⊔NX = P \sqcup NX=P⊔N of ν\nuν and set

ν+:=ν(⋅∩P),ν−:=−ν(⋅∩N)\nu^+ := \nu(\cdot \cap P), \quad \nu^- := -\nu(\cdot \cap N)ν+:=ν(⋅∩P),ν−:=−ν(⋅∩N)

Then ν=ν+−ν−\nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-ν=ν+−ν− and ν+⊥ν−\nu^+ \perp \nu^-ν+⊥ν−.

Notes

The positive variation ν+\nu^+ν+ and the negative variation ν−\nu^-ν− are both genuine measures; their sum is called the total variation:

∣ν∣:=ν++ν−|\nu| := \nu^+ + \nu^-∣ν∣:=ν++ν−

⭐ "Signed measure" is thereby fully dissected: every signed measure is the difference of a pair of mutually singular measures.

⚠ "At least one finite" corresponds to the clause "takes at most one of ±∞\pm \infty±∞" in the definition of a signed measure.
