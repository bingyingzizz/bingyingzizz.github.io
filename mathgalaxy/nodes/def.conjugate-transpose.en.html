def.conjugate-transpose

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

What it does: over the complex field the transpose alone is not enough — the inner product is conjugate-linear in the second variable, so moving AAA to the other side moves the conjugate along with it. This is the conjugate transpose.

Let A∈Mm×n(C)A \in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{C})A∈Mm×n​(C). The conjugate transpose (also called the adjoint matrix) is

A∗:=A‾T=(AT‾),(A∗)ij:=aji‾.A^{*} := \overline{A}^{T} = \bigl(\overline{A^{T}}\bigr), \qquad (A^{*})_{ij} := \overline{a_{ji}} .A∗:=AT=(AT),(A∗)ij​:=aji​​.

Properties: (A+B)∗=A∗+B∗(A+B)^{*} = A^{*} + B^{*}(A+B)∗=A∗+B∗, (cA)∗=c‾ A∗(cA)^{*} = \overline{c}\,A^{*}(cA)∗=cA∗, (AB)∗=B∗A∗(AB)^{*} = B^{*}A^{*}(AB)∗=B∗A∗, (A∗)∗=A(A^{*})^{*} = A(A∗)∗=A.

Relation to the inner product (this is its defining property): for all x,yx, yx,y one has

⟨Ax,y⟩=⟨x,A∗y⟩.\langle Ax, y\rangle = \langle x, A^{*}y\rangle .⟨Ax,y⟩=⟨x,A∗y⟩.

For a real matrix A∗=ATA^{*} = A^{T}A∗=AT, and the two notions coincide.

Notes

It is the matrix of the adjoint operator: if matrices are taken in an orthonormal basis, then for the linear map TTT the adjoint operator T∗T^{*}T∗ (def.operator-adjoint) has as matrix the conjugate transpose of the matrix of TTT. ⚠️ In a non-orthonormal basis these two are no longer equal — that relation is a privilege of orthonormal bases, not a coordinate-free identity.

Why the conjugate is necessary: the standard inner product on Cn\mathbb{C}^{n}Cn is ⟨x,y⟩=∑xiyi‾\langle x, y\rangle = \sum x_i \overline{y_i}⟨x,y⟩=∑xi​yi​​ (conjugate in the second variable), so when moving AAA from the left side to the right, A‾\overline{A}A appears naturally.

With the "transpose only" version, ⟨Ax,y⟩=⟨x,ATy⟩\langle Ax,y\rangle = \langle x, A^{T}y\rangle⟨Ax,y⟩=⟨x,ATy⟩ is false over the complex field.

⚠️ Three notations coexist: A∗A^{*}A∗, A†A^{\dagger}A†, AHA^{H}AH. This entry uses A∗A^{*}A∗ throughout (the same star as for adjoint operators).

⚠️ The term "adjoint matrix" in some books means the cofactor matrix (the notation of the def.determinant branch). Here it is used in the inner-product sense; when reading Chinese textbooks, take care to distinguish the two.
