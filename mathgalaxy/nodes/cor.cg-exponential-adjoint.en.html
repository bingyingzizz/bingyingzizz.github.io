Statement

On the category of compactly generated spaces, the functor

X⟼k(X×Y)⊣Z⟼kC(kY,Z).X \longmapsto k(X \times Y) \qquad \dashv \qquad Z \longmapsto kC(kY, Z).X⟼k(X×Y)⊣Z⟼kC(kY,Z).

that is, kC(kY,−)kC(kY, -)kC(kY,−) is the right adjoint of "multiplying by YYY" (k(Y×−)k(Y \times -)k(Y×−) is called the exponential object ZYZ^{Y}ZY).

Notes

Two immediate corollaries: a left adjoint preserves colimits, so X↦k(X×Y)X \mapsto k(X \times Y)X↦k(X×Y) preserves colimits of compactly generated spaces; a right adjoint preserves limits, so Z↦kC(kY,Z)Z \mapsto kC(kY,Z)Z↦kC(kY,Z) preserves limits.

⭐ Colimits in kTopk\mathbf{Top}kTop are universal. Because "multiplying by YYY" has a right adjoint, it commutes with colimits, and this is exactly what is required for colimits to be universal (pulling a colimit back along any morphism is again a colimit). This fails in Top\mathbf{Top}Top — another reason one "has to work in kTopk\mathbf{Top}kTop".

taking A=X×YA = X\times YA=X×Y gives the left-hand side =Hom⁡Top(X×Y, Z)= \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Top}}(X\times Y,\ Z)=HomTop​(X×Y, Z).

  • Right-hand side: similarly Hom⁡kTop(X,kC(kY,Z))=Hom⁡Top(X,C(kY,Z))\operatorname{Hom}_{k\mathbf{Top}}(X, kC(kY,Z)) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Top}}(X, C(kY,Z))HomkTop​(X,kC(kY,Z))=HomTop​(X,C(kY,Z)).
  • The currying in the middle: at the level of sets there is always a bijection Hom⁡Top(X×Y,Z)≅Hom⁡Top(X,C(Y,Z))\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Top}}(X\times Y, Z) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Top}}(X, C(Y,Z))HomTop​(X×Y,Z)≅HomTop​(X,C(Y,Z)) (sending fff to x↦f(x,−)x\mapsto f(x,-)x↦f(x,−));

at the level of topology, when ZZZ is compactly generated "continuity can be tested on compact Hausdorff pieces", and the compact pieces of kYkYkY are the compact pieces of YYY, so the compact-open topologies on C(Y,Z)C(Y,Z)C(Y,Z) and C(kY,Z)C(kY,Z)C(kY,Z) agree. Combining the two gives a natural bijection, i.e. k(X×Y)⊣kC(kY,−)k(X\times Y) \dashv kC(kY, -)k(X×Y)⊣kC(kY,−). ∎ Two corollaries (general properties of adjunctions): the left adjoint X↦k(X×Y)X\mapsto k(X\times Y)X↦k(X×Y) preserves colimits; the right adjoint Z↦kC(kY,Z)Z\mapsto kC(kY,Z)Z↦kC(kY,Z) preserves limits.
