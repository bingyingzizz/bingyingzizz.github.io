These two are equivalent in $\mathbb{Z}$, but not in a general integral domain: in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$, $2 \mid (1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5}) = 6$ while $2$ divides neither factor — the element is "irreducible" but not "prime". Ring theory abstracts the latter as a **prime element**, the former as an **irreducible element**.

$1$ is not prime: otherwise unique factorization would fail ($6 = 2\cdot 3 = 1\cdot 2\cdot 3$).
