Statement

Let (u,v,w)(u, v, w)(u,v,w) be a morphism between derived triangles in K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C). Then

  1. if it is a morphism from a triangle to itself and its first two steps vanish (u=v=0u = v = 0u=v=0), then www of the third step satisfies w2=0w^{2} = 0w2=0;
  2. if u,vu, vu,v are both homotopy equivalences, then so is www.

Notes

What item 1 says: a morphism between triangles is tightly controlled by its first two steps — once they vanish, the third is forced to have zero square and is squeezed between two exact sequences. This is the source of the least obvious axiom of triangulated categories.

Hypotheses used in each direction (these two are consequences of the triangle-morphism axioms, not converses of one another):

  • In K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C), homotopy equivalence   ⟺  \iff⟺ isomorphism: expand the definition of morphisms in the homotopy category; the rest must be discussed in this category.
  • Self-morphism with u=v=0u=v=0u=v=0   ⟹  \implies⟹ w2=0w^2=0w2=0: use the long exact Hom sequence of a derived triangle, factor www through the connecting morphism, and use that adjacent composites vanish; this is not an unfolding of the definition of a zero morphism.
  • u,vu,vu,v homotopy equivalences   ⟹  \implies⟹ www homotopy equivalence: apply the long exact Hom sequence to each object, the five lemma and the Yoneda criterion; no termwise finiteness or boundedness of complexes is needed.
  • An isomorphism on the third component does not imply that the first two are isomorphisms: between two identity triangles K→1K→0→K[1]K\xrightarrow{1}K\to0\to K[1]K1​K→0→K[1] take (0,0,10)(0,0,1_0)(0,0,10​); for nonzero KKK the first two terms are not isomorphisms; "to itself" is also the type condition needed to write w2w^2w2.

Item 2 is the five lemma inside the homotopy category: once the first two steps are pinned down, the third is pinned down too.
