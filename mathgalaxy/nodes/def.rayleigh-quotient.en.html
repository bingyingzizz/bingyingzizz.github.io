Statement

What it does: turns "eigenvalue" into an extremal problem. In this way eigenvalues can be "optimized" instead of only "solved for".

Let AAA be a Hermitian matrix of order nnn. For x≠0x \ne 0x=0 define the Rayleigh quotient

RA(x):=x∗Axx∗x.R_{A}(x) := \frac{x^{*}Ax}{x^{*}x} .RA​(x):=x∗xx∗Ax​.

(for a unit vector xxx this is just x∗Axx^{*}Axx∗Ax, so it is really "the average stretch factor of AAA in the direction xxx".)

Core properties:

  1. RA(x)R_{A}(x)RA​(x) is always real and lies in [λmin⁡,λmax⁡][\lambda_{\min}, \lambda_{\max}][λmin​,λmax​];
  2. λmin⁡=min⁡x≠0RA(x)\lambda_{\min} = \min_{x\ne 0} R_A(x)λmin​=minx=0​RA​(x), λmax⁡=max⁡x≠0RA(x)\lambda_{\max} = \max_{x\ne 0} R_A(x)λmax​=maxx=0​RA​(x), with the extrema attained at the corresponding eigenvectors;
  3. more generally, all critical points are exactly the eigenvectors, and the critical values are exactly the eigenvalues.

Notes

Why it is real: x∗Ax‾=x∗A∗x=x∗Ax\overline{x^{*}Ax} = x^{*}A^{*}x = x^{*}Axx∗Ax=x∗A∗x=x∗Ax (using Hermitian), and the denominator is real.

⭐ It gives the theoretical basis for steepest descent/power iteration: moving along the gradient of RAR_ARA​ is approximating the largest eigenvector. Numerically xk+1∝Axkx_{k+1} \propto Ax_kxk+1​∝Axk​ converges to the dominant eigenvector at a rate governed by ∣λ2/λ1∣\lvert\lambda_2/\lambda_1\rvert∣λ2​/λ1​∣ — this is the convergence rate of the power method.

Its generalization is thm.courant-fischer: restricting RAR_ARA​ to a kkk-dimensional subspace and taking the extremum gives the kkk-th eigenvalue. So the Rayleigh quotient is the gateway to the "variational characterization of the spectrum", not an isolated notation.

⚠️ AAA must be Hermitian (or real symmetric) for RAR_ARA​ to be real and to have an extremal meaning; for a general matrix x∗Axx^{*}Axx∗Ax is complex, the Rayleigh quotient degenerates into a complex-valued function, and the standard theory does not apply.
