- **Non-degeneracy** $d(x,y)=0 \iff x=y$: **read it as two halves.** "$x=y \implies d(x,y)=0$" is nearly free (often built into how $d$ is given); "$d(x,y)=0 \implies x=y$" is the half with content (weakening it gives a **pseudometric**).
- **Symmetry**: **can be dropped** — what is left is a **quasi-metric** (e.g. a "one-way shortest path"); the topology is still definable, but balls are asymmetric (the ball for $d$ and the ball for the reversed quasi-metric differ).
