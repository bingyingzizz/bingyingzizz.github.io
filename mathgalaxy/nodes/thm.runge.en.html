Statement

Let K⊂CK \subset \mathbb{C}K⊂C be compact, and let fff be holomorphic on a neighborhood of KKK. If C∖K\mathbb{C} \setminus KC∖K is connected (that is, KKK has no holes), then for each ε>0\varepsilon > 0ε>0 there is a polynomial ppp such that

sup⁡z∈K∣f(z)−p(z)∣<ε.\sup_{z \in K} |f(z) - p(z)| < \varepsilon.z∈Ksup​∣f(z)−p(z)∣<ε.

In the general case (when C∖K\mathbb{C}\setminus KC∖K has holes), fff can be approximated by rational functions whose poles are restricted to one point chosen in advance in each connected component of C∖K\mathbb{C}\setminus KC∖K.

Proof: There are four steps. ① Express fff as a Cauchy integral over a grid boundary outside KKK. ② Discretize the integral to obtain rational functions with poles outside KKK. ③ Move the poles along paths in C∖K\mathbb{C}\setminus KC∖K to the prescribed locations. ④ If there are no holes, move them out to infinity and expand to obtain polynomials.

⭐ Runge's theorem, Mittag-Leffler's theorem, and the Weierstrass product theorem (Weierstrass product theorem) belong together: Runge concerns approximation, Mittag-Leffler constructs functions with prescribed principal parts, and Weierstrass constructs functions with prescribed zeros. All three answer the same question: What kinds of holomorphic or meromorphic functions can be constructed on a domain?
