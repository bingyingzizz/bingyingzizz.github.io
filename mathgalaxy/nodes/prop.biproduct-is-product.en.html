Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a preadditive category and M,M1,M2∈CM, M_{1}, M_{2} \in \mathcal{C}M,M1​,M2​∈C.

p1i1=1M1,p2i2=1M2,i1p1+i2p2=1M.p_{1}i_{1} = 1_{M_{1}}, \qquad p_{2}i_{2} = 1_{M_{2}}, \qquad i_{1}p_{1} + i_{2}p_{2} = 1_{M}.p1​i1​=1M1​​,p2​i2​=1M2​​,i1​p1​+i2​p2​=1M​.

Notes

⭐ This is where the name biproduct comes from: product and coproduct coincide in a preadditive category, so the direct sum needs only one object.

Why an additive category must have biproducts: precisely because the two coincide, "addition" (f+gf + gf+g) and "the inclusions of the coproduct" can be written in one notation — this is the foundation of all notation in homological algebra.
