def.filtered-category · filtered

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A category III is called filtered if:

  1. nonempty: III has at least one object;
  2. upper bounds: for any i,j∈Ii, j \in Ii,j∈I there exist k∈Ik \in Ik∈I and morphisms i→ki \to ki→k, j→kj \to kj→k;
  3. coequalization: for any parallel u,v:i⇉ju, v : i \rightrightarrows ju,v:i⇉j, there exists w:j→kw : j \to kw:j→k with w∘u=w∘vw \circ u = w \circ vw∘u=w∘v.

Equivalently: every finite diagram in III has a cocone. A colimit taken along a filtered category is called a filtered colimit.

Notes

The three items   ⟺  \iff⟺ finite cocones: the three correspond exactly to the three cases of "a cocone for a finite diagram exists" — the empty diagram (item 1), two points (item 2), and a pair of parallel arrows (item 3). So "filtered" = "every finite diagram has a cocone" = all information can later be caught up.

Relation to directed sets: a directed set (a poset in which any two elements have an upper bound), regarded as a category, is filtered; conversely, any filtered category can be replaced by a cofinal directed set (see "a filtered category can be replaced by a directed set"). So "filtered colimits" and "colimits along directed sets" are the same thing.

⭐ Why it is required everywhere: filtered colimits are exact (commute with finite limits) in Set\mathbf{Set}Set, A-ModA\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}A-Mod, CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb; this is Grothendieck's AB5 axiom and the key condition when homological algebra selects categories. Colimits along a general category lack this good property.

⚠️ Distinguish from "chain": a chain is a totally ordered subset, stronger than filtered; filtered only requires "a common upper bound", and any two elements need not be comparable.

Hypotheses used in each direction (finite cocones and the finite combinations of the three axioms):

  • Every finite diagram has a cocone   ⟹  \implies⟹ the three items: take respectively the empty diagram, two discrete objects, and a pair of parallel arrows, and only unfold the definition of a cocone.
  • The three items   ⟹  \implies⟹ finite cocones: first take common upper bounds finitely many times, then coequalize the incompatibilities created by arrows one by one; this needs finite induction, not Zorn's lemma or infinite choice.
  • For a poset regarded as a category, upper bounds and nonemptiness   ⟹  \implies⟹ filteredness: there is at most one parallel arrow, so item 3 is automatic; a general category cannot drop item 3, and a nontrivial group regarded as a one-object category is a counterexample.
  • Nonemptiness cannot be dropped: the empty category vacuously satisfies the last two items but has no cocone for the empty diagram. The further theorem about replacing by a cofinal directed set needs smallness conventions and the corresponding set-theoretic constructions, which are not part of this group of equivalent definitions.
