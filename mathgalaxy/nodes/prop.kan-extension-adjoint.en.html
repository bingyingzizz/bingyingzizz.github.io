Statement

Let p:C→C′p : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'p:C→C′ be a functor between small categories and D\mathcal{D}D a category. Then the precomposition functor

p−1:Hom⁡(C′,D)⟶Hom⁡(C,D),G⟼G∘pp^{-1} : \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}', \mathcal{D}) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}), \qquad G \longmapsto G \circ pp−1:Hom(C′,D)⟶Hom(C,D),G⟼G∘p

has a left adjoint p!p_{!}p!​   ⟺  \iff⟺ left Kan extensions along ppp with values in D\mathcal{D}D always exist.

Dually: p−1p^{-1}p−1 has a right adjoint p∗p_{*}p∗​   ⟺  \iff⟺ right Kan extensions always exist.

Notes

Proof. Compare the two sides verbatim. "p−1p^{-1}p−1 has a left adjoint p!p_{!}p!​" says: for every F∈Hom⁡(C,D)F \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D})F∈Hom(C,D) there are p!F∈Hom⁡(C′,D)p_{!}F \in \operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C}', \mathcal{D})p!​F∈Hom(C′,D) and a natural isomorphism

Hom⁡(p!F, G)  ≅  Hom⁡(F, p−1G)=Hom⁡(F, G∘p).\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(p_{!}F,\ G\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(F,\ p^{-1}G\bigr) = \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(F,\ G \circ p\bigr).Hom(p!​F, G)≅Hom(F, p−1G)=Hom(F, G∘p).

The data on the right is precisely the defining universal property of "the left Kan extension of FFF along ppp" ✓: the unit of the adjunction ηF:F  ⟹  p!F∘p\eta_{F} : F \implies p_{!}F \circ pηF​:F⟹p!​F∘p is the α\alphaα in the definition, and the isomorphism itself gives the universal property of α\alphaα ✓. So "every FFF has a left Kan extension" and "p−1p^{-1}p−1 has a left adjoint" are the same statement; dually, reversing the arrows gives the right Kan extension clause ✓. ∎

⭐ Reading: Kan extensions are nothing new — they are adjoints of precomposition functors. This proposition merges the two languages "Kan extensions everywhere" and "adjoints everywhere" into one.

⚠️ The proposition itself does not guarantee existence; it only says that "adjoint" and "Kan extension" live and die together. Existence needs extra hypotheses (see "Kan extensions exist when the target has enough colimits").
