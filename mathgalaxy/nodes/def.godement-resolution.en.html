def.godement-resolution · Godement

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let F\mathcal{F}F be an abelian sheaf on XXX. The Godement resolution is the complex 0→F→C0(F)→C1(F)→⋯0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathcal{F})\to\cdots0→F→C0(F)→C1(F)→⋯, where Cq(F)(U)=∏x∈UFx\mathcal{C}^{q}(\mathcal{F})(U)=\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal{F}_{x}Cq(F)(U)=∏x∈U​Fx​ (q≥0q\ge0q≥0), with differential given by the alternating sum of stalks. Each Cq(F)\mathcal{C}^{q}(\mathcal{F})Cq(F) is a flabby sheaf. That it is an acyclic resolution is conditional on flabby acyclicity (flabby sheaves are acyclic, Godement's theorem); this is not proved in this graph, see step 3 of @@thm.soft-sheaf-acyclic@@.

Notes

Construction: C0(F)(U)=∏x∈UFx\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathcal{F})(U)=\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal{F}_{x}C0(F)(U)=∏x∈U​Fx​ records sections pointwise as elements of stalks; the restriction maps are projections, so sections over any open set extend (flabbiness). Cq\mathcal{C}^{q}Cq takes the alternating version of the qqq-fold "stalk product", with the Čech-type alternating differential.

Use (conditional on flabby acyclicity): if flabby sheaves are acyclic (Godement's theorem, not proved here), the Godement resolution computes sheaf cohomology: Hq(X,F)≅Hq(Γ(X,C∙(F)))H^{q}(X,\mathcal{F})\cong H^{q}(\Gamma(X,\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F})))Hq(X,F)≅Hq(Γ(X,C∙(F))). This is the standard construction of a canonical acyclic resolution for an arbitrary sheaf, without injective sheaves.
