Statement

A distinguished triangle in K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C) is a diagram of the shape

K⟶L⟶M⟶K[1]K \longrightarrow L \longrightarrow M \longrightarrow K[1]K⟶L⟶M⟶K[1]

It is called distinguished if it is isomorphic in K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C) to some mapping-cone triangle

K→ f L⟶M(f)⟶K[1]K \xrightarrow{\ f\ } L \longrightarrow M(f) \longrightarrow K[1]K f ​L⟶M(f)⟶K[1]

A morphism of triangles is a family of maps (u,v,w,u[1])(u, v, w, u[1])(u,v,w,u[1]) making all squares commute.

Notes

A distinguished triangle is the homotopy version of a "short exact sequence": it does not require MMM to be a quotient, only that K→L→M→K[1]K \to L \to M \to K[1]K→L→M→K[1] fits together in shape. After adding the shift, a single morphism can be embedded in a sequence of arbitrary length — precisely the form a triangulated category wants.
