# the minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial　`thm.minimal-poly-divisor`

[in graph](../#n=thm.minimal-poly-divisor)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.minimal-poly-divisor.md)

⭐ The proof of the second (same roots) is worth remembering: if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue with eigenvector $v$, then $\mu_T(T)v = \mu_T(\lambda) v$, while $\mu_T(T) = 0$ and $v \ne 0$, hence $\mu_T(\lambda) = 0$;

the converse follows from $\mu_T \mid \chi_T$.

⚠ The phrase "viewed over the algebraic closure" in the third assertion cannot be omitted: whether $\mu_T$ has repeated roots must be decided in an extension field (the rotation over $\mathbb{R}$: $\mu_T = x^{2} + 1$ has no repeated roots, is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{C}$, but not over $\mathbb{R}$).
