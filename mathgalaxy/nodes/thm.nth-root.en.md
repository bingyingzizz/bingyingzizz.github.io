## Statement · **draft**
Let $K$ be a complete ordered field (e.g. $\mathbb{R}$), $a > 0$, $n \ge 1$. Then there exists a **unique** $b > 0$ such that

$$b^{n} = a;$$

Write $b = \sqrt[n]{a} = a^{1/n}$. Moreover:

- $a \mapsto \sqrt[n]{a}$ is strictly increasing on $(0, +\infty)$;
- $\sqrt[n]{ab} = \sqrt[n]{a}\,\sqrt[n]{b}$, $\sqrt[n]{a^{m}} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{m}$ ($m \in \mathbb{Z}$).
