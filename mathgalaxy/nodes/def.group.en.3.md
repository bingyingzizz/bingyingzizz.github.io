**Examples and counterexamples**: $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ is an abelian group; $(\mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}, \cdot)$ is an abelian group; $(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)$ is **not** a group (only $\pm 1$ have multiplicative inverses);

$n \times n$ invertible matrices form a group under multiplication but **not** an abelian one ($n \ge 2$).

📌 There is a weaker layer: requiring only associativity and an identity, but not inverses, gives a **monoid**. ($\mathbb{N}$ under addition is a monoid but not a group.)
