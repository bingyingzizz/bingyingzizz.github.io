# group　`def.group`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.group)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.group.md)

**The identity and inverses are unique**: if $e, e'$ are both identities, then $e = e e' = e'$; if $a^{-1}, a'$ are both inverses of $a$, then $a' = a' e = a' a a^{-1} = e a^{-1} = a^{-1}$. Hence the notations "$e$" and "$a^{-1}$" are legitimate.

**Examples and counterexamples**: $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ is an abelian group; $(\mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}, \cdot)$ is an abelian group; $(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)$ is **not** a group (only $\pm 1$ have multiplicative inverses);

$n \times n$ invertible matrices form a group under multiplication but **not** an abelian one ($n \ge 2$).
