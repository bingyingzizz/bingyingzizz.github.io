Statement

The limit of a diagram of shape X1→f1X0←f2X2X_1 \xrightarrow{f_1} X_0 \xleftarrow{f_2} X_2X1​f1​​X0​f2​​X2​ is called the fibred product, written X1×X0X2X_1 \times_{X_0} X_2X1​×X0​​X2​, also called the pullback of f1f_1f1​ along f2f_2f2​. It completes to a commutative square

X1×X0X2⟶X2↓↓f2X1→f1X0\begin{array}{ccc} X_1 \times_{X_0} X_2 & \longrightarrow & X_2 \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow f_2 \\ X_1 & \xrightarrow{f_1} & X_0 \end{array}X1​×X0​​X2​↓X1​​⟶f1​​​X2​↓f2​X0​​

Such a square is called cartesian. Dually, the colimit of such a diagram is the fibred coproduct, also called the pushout.

Notes

Intuition: the pullback is “the product after matching over the base X0X_0X0​” — solving f1(x1)=f2(x2)f_1(x_1) = f_2(x_2)f1​(x1​)=f2​(x2​); the pushout is “gluing X1,X2X_1, X_2X1​,X2​ together along their images in X0X_0X0​”.

Example (Set\mathbf{Set}Set):

X1×X0X2={(x1,x2)∈X1×X2:f1(x1)=f2(x2)},X1∪X0X2=(X1⊔X2)/ ⁣∼X_1 \times_{X_0} X_2 = \{(x_1, x_2) \in X_1 \times X_2 : f_1(x_1) = f_2(x_2)\}, \qquad X_1 \cup_{X_0} X_2 = (X_1 \sqcup X_2)/\!\simX1​×X0​​X2​={(x1​,x2​)∈X1​×X2​:f1​(x1​)=f2​(x2​)},X1​∪X0​​X2​=(X1​⊔X2​)/∼

where ∼\sim∼ is generated by f1(x0)∼f2(x0)f_1(x_0) \sim f_2(x_0)f1​(x0​)∼f2​(x0​) (x0∈X0x_0 \in X_0x0​∈X0​). In Top\mathbf{Top}Top, the pullback carries the subspace topology and the pushout the quotient topology.

In Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab both reduce to kernels and cokernels: combine f:X→Sf: X \to Sf:X→S and g:Y→Sg: Y \to Sg:Y→S into φ:X⊕Y→S\varphi : X \oplus Y \to Sφ:X⊕Y→S, (x,y)↦f(x)−g(y)(x,y) \mapsto f(x) - g(y)(x,y)↦f(x)−g(y), then the pushout is =coker⁡φ= \operatorname{coker} \varphi=cokerφ and the pullback is =ker⁡φ= \ker \varphi=kerφ — first take the direct sum, then quotient out the differences of f,gf, gf,g.

📌 The “pullback” of category theory is the fibred product (here); the pullback of differential forms is 拉回.
