Statement

Let K⊆RnK \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}K⊆Rn, regarded as a metric space with the Euclidean distance. Then KKK is compact if and only if KKK is bounded and closed.

("Compact" means "every open cover has a finite subcover", or equivalently "every sequence has a subsequence converging to a point of KKK".)

Notes

⭐ This is a corollary of a corollary: Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn complete + bounded sets are totally bounded   ⟹  \implies⟹ bounded closed sets are sequentially compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ compact (see "three equivalent characterizations of compactness in metric spaces").

What is really used is the coordinatewise version of Bolzano–Weierstrass.

⚠ It is not a theorem for general metric spaces: in a general metric space "compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ bounded closed" always holds, but not conversely (the discrete space is a sufficient example, as is the unit ball of ℓ2\ell^{2}ℓ2).

⭐ An immediately usable consequence: the closed interval [a,b][a, b][a,b] is compact — hence a continuous function attains its extrema on it, is uniformly continuous, and is integrable.

The three existence theorems of elementary calculus all rest on this.
