## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Note the order of the quantifiers: $\exists N$ comes **after** $\forall x$ — so $N$ here may depend on $\varepsilon$ **and $x$**.

Each point gets its own $N$, independently of the others.

⚠ Pointwise convergence is too weak: the limit function may be **discontinuous**, and limit and integral need **not commute**.

Example: on $[0, 1]$, $f_n(x) = x^{n}$ converges pointwise to the discontinuous function that equals $0$ for $x < 1$ and $1$ for $x = 1$.

⭐ Pointwise convergence is a "pointwise" notion, and checking it point by point suffices;
