## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **An $F$-automorphism must send roots to roots**: if $\alpha$ is a root of $f \in F[x]$, then

$$f(\sigma(\alpha)) = \sigma(f(\alpha)) = \sigma(0) = 0$$

(because $\sigma$ fixes the coefficients of $F$). So $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ **acts** on the set of roots of $f$ — all of Galois theory is the counting of this action.

⭐ **This gives the bridge between groups and fields**: on one side the subfields (the intermediate layer of the field), on the other this group (a group-theoretic object); the Galois correspondence says they are **two faces of the same lattice**.
