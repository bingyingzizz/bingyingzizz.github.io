Statement

Let K∙,∙K^{\bullet,\bullet}K∙,∙ be a bounded-below double complex with injective terms, whose total complex I∙I^{\bullet}I∙ together with the augmentation gives a resolution K∙K^{\bullet}K∙. Then K∙→I∙K^{\bullet} \to I^{\bullet}K∙→I∙ is still an injective resolution.

Notes

Proof. Each term of the total complex is a finite direct sum of injective objects (bounded below guarantees only finitely many terms in each degree), and a direct sum of injectives is injective ✓; and the total complex is compatible with the homology of the double complex: Hn(Tot⁡)≅HnH^{n}(\operatorname{Tot}) \cong H^{n}Hn(Tot)≅Hn (this uses convergence from "bounded below + every column a resolution") ✓, so K∙→I∙K^{\bullet} \to I^{\bullet}K∙→I∙ is a quasi-isomorphism ✓.

∎

⭐ This is the legitimacy behind the Cartan–Eilenberg device "double complex →\to→ total complex".
