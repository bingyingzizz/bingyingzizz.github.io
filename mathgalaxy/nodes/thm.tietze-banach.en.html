thm.tietze-banach · Tietze

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a normal topological space, let K⊆XK \subseteq XK⊆X be a compact subset, and let VVV be a real Banach space. Then the restriction map

C(X, V)↠C(K, V)C(X,\ V) \twoheadrightarrow C(K,\ V)C(X, V)↠C(K, V)

is surjective. Every continuous map K→VK \to VK→V extends to XXX.

Notes

Reduction in stages.

(1) Finite-dimensional case: V=RmV = \mathbb{R}^{m}V=Rm. Apply the classical Tietze extension theorem coordinatewise (extension of continuous real-valued functions on normal spaces, based on Urysohn's lemma).

(2) General separable Banach case: Every separable Banach space is isomorphic to a closed subspace of ℓ∞\ell^{\infty}ℓ∞, and ℓ∞\ell^{\infty}ℓ∞ is a Lipschitz retract. First extend the map to ℓ∞\ell^{\infty}ℓ∞ and then retract.

(3) In the nonseparable case, the compact image lies in a separable closed subspace, reducing the problem to (2).

⭐ Why condensed mathematics uses this. The main theorem of §8.2, acyclicity of real Banach spaces on compact Hausdorff spaces, covers a general compact Hausdorff space SSS by a Stonean space S0S_{0}S0​ and then eliminates the discrepancy;

the discrepancy term lies in a function space C(K,V)C(K, V)C(K,V), and eliminating it requires extension, supplied by this Tietze theorem.

⚠️ General topology also has a real-valued version (the Tietze extension theorem). Here the target can be any real Banach space.

Proof (Dugundji extension theorem). Dugundji's theorem is the version of Tietze for maps into topological vector spaces. Suppose XXX is paracompact (a metric space suffices), A⊆XA \subseteq XA⊆X is closed, and VVV is a locally convex topological vector space. Then there is a linear extension operator C(A,V)→C(X,V)C(A, V) \to C(X, V)C(A,V)→C(X,V), so the restriction map is surjective.
