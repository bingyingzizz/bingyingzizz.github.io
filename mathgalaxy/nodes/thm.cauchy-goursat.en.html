Statement

In plain words: on a simply connected region, the integral of a holomorphic function along any closed curve is 000 — this is the engine of all of complex analysis.

Let Ω\OmegaΩ be a simply connected plane region, fff holomorphic on Ω\OmegaΩ, and γ\gammaγ a piecewise smooth closed curve in Ω\OmegaΩ. Then

∮γf(z) dz=0.\oint_\gamma f(z)\,\mathrm{d}z = 0.∮γ​f(z)dz=0.

The multiply connected case: let γ0,γ1,⋯ ,γn\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \cdots, \gamma_nγ0​,γ1​,⋯,γn​ be piecewise smooth simple closed curves in Ω\OmegaΩ, with γ1,⋯ ,γn\gamma_1, \cdots, \gamma_nγ1​,⋯,γn​ lying inside γ0\gamma_0γ0​ and pairwise disjoint, all oriented counterclockwise, and fff holomorphic on some open set containing the closed region they bound (holomorphy "on the region" alone is not enough: the curves themselves must lie in the domain). Notes

⚠ "Simply connected" cannot be dropped.

f(z)=1/zf(z) = 1/zf(z)=1/z is holomorphic on C∖{0}\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}C∖{0}, yet ∮∣z∣=1dz/z=2πi≠0\oint_{|z| = 1} \mathrm{d}z/z = 2\pi i \ne 0∮∣z∣=1​dz/z=2πi=0.

That hole is the obstruction — its presence makes the winding number detectable.

⭐ Equivalent statement: contour integrals of holomorphic functions are homotopy invariant (continuous deformation of the path inside the region leaves the integral unchanged);

simply connected = every closed curve in the region is homotopic to a point.

⭐ Immediate corollary: a holomorphic function on a simply connected region has a primitive (take F(z)=∫z0zf dzF(z) = \int_{z_0}^{z} f\,\mathrm{d}zF(z)=∫z0​z​fdz, which is path-independent by Cauchy's theorem, and then F′=fF' = fF′=f).

There is no clean counterpart in real analysis: F(x)=log⁡∣x∣F(x) = \log|x|F(x)=log∣x∣ is indeed a primitive of 1/x1/x1/x on R∖{0}\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}R∖{0} — the difference is that R∖{0}\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}R∖{0} is disconnected, so "path independence" fails there (the integral around 000 is nonzero).

⚠ The "Goursat" in the name: Cauchy's original 1825 proof required f′f'f′ to be continuous (via Green's formula);

Goursat later removed this hypothesis by subdividing into triangles.

Modern textbooks therefore treat it as the fundamental theorem of holomorphy: the conclusion is not bought with a stronger hypothesis.
