Statement

What it says: a manifold has no built-in coordinates, so coordinates must be issued chart by chart — a chart is a piece of ground plus a coordinate sign, and changing signs has to be smooth.

Let MMM be a topological space and n≥0n \ge 0n≥0.

A coordinate chart on MMM, of dimension nnn, is a pair (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), where U⊆MU \subseteq MU⊆M is open and φ:U→φ(U)⊆Rn\varphi : U \to \varphi(U) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n}φ:U→φ(U)⊆Rn is a homeomorphism (with φ(U)\varphi(U)φ(U) open in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn).

UUU is the coordinate neighbourhood and φ\varphiφ the coordinate map; the components xi:=φix^{i} := \varphi^{i}xi:=φi are the local coordinates, i.e. p↦(x1(p),…,xn(p))p \mapsto \bigl(x^{1}(p),\ldots,x^{n}(p)\bigr)p↦(x1(p),…,xn(p)).

When n=0n = 0n=0, R0\mathbb{R}^{0}R0 is a single point, so a chart only records whether the point lies in UUU.

The transition map between two charts (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), (V,ψ)(V,\psi)(V,ψ) is

ψ∘φ−1:φ(U∩V)⟶ψ(U∩V),\psi \circ \varphi^{-1} : \varphi(U \cap V) \longrightarrow \psi(U \cap V),ψ∘φ−1:φ(U∩V)⟶ψ(U∩V),

which turns φ\varphiφ-coordinates into ψ\psiψ-coordinates. The charts are C∞C^{\infty}C∞-compatible when the transition map is C∞C^{\infty}C∞ everywhere (it is defined on an open subset of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, so partial derivatives test it); if U∩V=∅U \cap V = \emptysetU∩V=∅ they are declared compatible.

Covering MMM, a family of pairwise compatible charts A={(Uα,φα)}\mathcal{A} = \{(U_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})\}A={(Uα​,φα​)} is an atlas of MMM of dimension nnn.

Example: on Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn take U=RnU = \mathbb{R}^{n}U=Rn and φ=id⁡\varphi = \operatorname{id}φ=id; this is a one-chart atlas.

For Sn⊆Rn+1S^{n} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{n+1}Sn⊆Rn+1 use the two stereographic charts (from the north and south poles to Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn); their transition map is x↦x/∥x∥2x \mapsto x/\lVert x\rVert^{2}x↦x/∥x∥2, which is smooth.

Notes

Why a homeomorphism and not merely a bijection: coordinates must carry the topological notions — continuity, convergence, compactness — otherwise what is computed is only a property of sets. A homeomorphism makes the chart a coordinate chart in the topological sense; this is why a topological space is part of the definition of a manifold.

The transition map is the only thing to manage: calculus on Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn knows only Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn-coordinates. Every derivative on a manifold descends into some chart, and the chain-rule factor when changing charts is the Jacobian of the transition map. Thus "calculus on a manifold" is equivalent to "transition maps are smooth" — this single technical assumption is the whole subject.

Coordinates are not part of the manifold: one chart admits infinitely many descriptions (any diffeomorphism of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn gives new coordinates); what belongs to the manifold is the existence of a pairwise compatible family. Distinguishing these two facts motivates the maximal atlas.

Dimension is pinned by charts: if two charts on MMM have overlapping domains of dimensions nnn and mmm, then open subsets of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn and Rm\mathbb{R}^{m}Rm are homeomorphic, so Brouwer's invariance of domain gives n=mn = mn=m. Hence "nnn-dimensional" is not an extra choice.

Interface with surface theory: the inverse of a regular parametrization r:U→Sr : U \to Sr:U→S is a chart, and a change of parametrization is precisely the transition map — "smoothness is independent of the parametrization" and "C∞C^{\infty}C∞-compatible" are the same sentence (R³ 中的可定向曲面).
