Statement

Let a,n≥1a, n \ge 1a,n≥1 with gcd⁡(a,n)=1\gcd(a, n) = 1gcd(a,n)=1. Then

aφ(n)≡1(modn).a^{\varphi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod n .aφ(n)≡1(modn).

Notes

The proof is one line, but it is group-theoretic: [a][a][a] lies in the unit group G=(Z/nZ)×G = (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}G=(Z/nZ)×, with ∣G∣=φ(n)|G| = \varphi(n)∣G∣=φ(n), so Lagrange's theorem gives [a]∣G∣=1[a]^{|G|} = 1[a]∣G∣=1.

So the real content of the theorem is "the unit group is a finite group" — the congruence is merely its coordinate form.
