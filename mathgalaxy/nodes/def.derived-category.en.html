def.derived-category

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category. Its derived category D(A)D(\mathcal{A})D(A) is the localization of the homotopy category K(A)K(\mathcal{A})K(A) at the quasi-isomorphisms.

Thus the objects of D(A)D(\mathcal{A})D(A) are the same as those of K(A)K(\mathcal{A})K(A) (equivalently C(A)C(\mathcal{A})C(A)), while a morphism of K∙→L∙K^{\bullet} \to L^{\bullet}K∙→L∙ is a "roof":

K∙← ∼ K′∙⟶L∙,K^{\bullet} \xleftarrow{\ \sim\ } K'^{\bullet} \longrightarrow L^{\bullet},K∙ ∼ ​K′∙⟶L∙,

The arrow on the left is a quasi-isomorphism (now turned into an isomorphism).

Notes

⭐ Why quotient once more in K(A)K(\mathcal{A})K(A): homotopic morphisms are identified in K(A)K(\mathcal{A})K(A), but quasi-isomorphisms are not yet isomorphisms — and what derived functors, Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext and spectral sequences really want is the category where "quasi-isomorphism = isomorphism".

D(A)D(\mathcal{A})D(A) was built for exactly this.

What a "roof" is: localization cannot in general be done by "one step to the right", because quasi-isomorphisms do not always admit a common denominator; hence a morphism is written as "invert one quasi-isomorphism, then take one step". This is also why the Hom of D(A)D(\mathcal{A})D(A) need not be a set — it is under control only when A\mathcal{A}A has enough injectives (or projectives).

📌 The bounded-above, bounded-below and bounded versions are written D+D^{+}D+, D−D^{-}D−, DbD^{b}Db; the injective-complex version is written K+(I)K^{+}(\mathcal{I})K+(I) and so on.
