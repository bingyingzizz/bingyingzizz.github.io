# bilinear form　`def.bilinear-form`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.bilinear-form)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.bilinear-form.md)

Examples: the **real** inner product (positive definite symmetric), symplectic forms (non-degenerate alternating), and polarizations of quadratic forms (symmetric) are all bilinear forms. ⚠ The **complex** inner product is not bilinear but **Hermitian sesquilinear** (for instance $h(i,1)=-i$).

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Bilinear is "weaker than linear": $B$ is **in general not** a linear map $V \times W \to U$ (the zero map is the only exception; linearity on $V \times W$ would require $B(v + v', w + w') = B(v,w) + B(v',w')$, which usually fails).

This is exactly the problem the tensor product solves — **translating bilinear into linear**.
