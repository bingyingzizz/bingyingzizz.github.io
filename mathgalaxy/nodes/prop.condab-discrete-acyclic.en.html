Statement

Let MMM be a condensed abelian group. Then

  1. when EEE is a discrete topological space, Hcondn(E,M)=0H^{n}_{\mathrm{cond}}(E, M) = 0Hcondn​(E,M)=0 (n>0n > 0n>0);
  2. more generally, when EEE is discrete and SSS is Stonean, Hcondn(E×S, M)=0H^{n}_{\mathrm{cond}}(E \times S,\ M) = 0Hcondn​(E×S, M)=0 (n>0n > 0n>0).

Notes

Proof. 1. A discrete space is a disjoint union of points: E=∐e∈E{e}E = \coprod_{e \in E}\{e\}E=∐e∈E​{e}. And cohomology turns disjoint unions into products:

Hn(∐iXi, M)≅∏iHn(Xi, M)✓H^{n}\Bigl(\coprod_{i} X_{i},\ M\Bigr) \cong \prod_{i} H^{n}(X_{i},\ M) ✓Hn(i∐​Xi​, M)≅i∏​Hn(Xi​, M)✓

The one-point case vanishes: {e}=∗\{e\} = *{e}=∗, whose free object is Z[∗]=Z\mathbb{Z}[*] = \mathbb{Z}Z[∗]=Z (projective), so Hn(∗,M)=Ext⁡Zn(Z,M)=0H^{n}(*, M) = \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, M) = 0Hn(∗,M)=ExtZn​(Z,M)=0 (n>0n > 0n>0) ✓. Hence on a discrete space all higher terms vanish ✓. 2. When EEE is discrete, E×S=∐e∈ESE \times S = \coprod_{e \in E} SE×S=∐e∈E​S is the disjoint union of SSS, and again use "disjoint union   ⟹  \implies⟹ product":

Hn(E×S, M)≅∏e∈EHn(S, M)=0(n>0),H^{n}(E \times S,\ M) \cong \prod_{e \in E} H^{n}(S,\ M) = 0 \qquad (n > 0),Hn(E×S, M)≅e∈E∏​Hn(S, M)=0(n>0),

The last step uses "condensed abelian groups are acyclic on Stonean spaces" ✓. ∎

⭐ In one sentence: acyclicity passes from Stonean to "discrete ×\times× Stonean". This is why mixed spaces such as N×S\mathbb{N} \times SN×S, Z×S\mathbb{Z} \times SZ×S have no higher cohomology.
