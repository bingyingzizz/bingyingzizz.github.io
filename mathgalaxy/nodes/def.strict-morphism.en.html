def.strict-morphism

Statement

A morphism f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is called strict if the middle step of its factorization through the coimage, X↠coim⁡f→im⁡f↪YX \twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{coim} f \to \operatorname{im} f \hookrightarrow YX↠coimf→imf↪Y, is an isomorphism — that is, the natural map "from coimage to image" is invertible.

Notes

⭐ "Strict" is the watershed between abelian and general categories: in an abelian category every morphism is strict; in Top\mathbf{Top}Top it is not.

Why the counterexample appears in topology: Rdisc→R\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{disc}} \to \mathbb{R}Rdisc→R is a monomorphism (isomorphic to its image), but its coimage is trivial (because the image is quotiented away in the cokernel), so the step coimage →\to→ image is not an isomorphism.

This is exactly why "topological abelian groups do not form an abelian category" over Top\mathbf{Top}Top.

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set and Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab every morphism is strict: there coimage and image both compute concretely as factorizations, and the middle map is the identity.
