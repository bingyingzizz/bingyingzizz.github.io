Statement

Let FFF be a family of sets with ∅∉F\emptyset \notin F∅∈/F.

A function fff is a choice function for FFF iff both of the following hold:

(i) fff is a function and dom⁡f=F\operatorname{dom} f = Fdomf=F; (ii) ∀X∈F\forall X \in F∀X∈F, f(X)∈Xf(X) \in Xf(X)∈X.

That is, fff picks one element from each member of FFF.

The Axiom of Choice can therefore be stated concisely as: every family FFF with ∅∉F\emptyset \notin F∅∈/F has a choice function.

Notes

If FFF is finite, one can pick the elements one by one, and no AC is needed. The difficulty lies only with infinite families, especially uncountable ones.
