Statement

A morphism of topoi f:T→T′f : \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{T}'f:T→T′ is a pair of functors

f∗:T′⟶T,f∗:T⟶T′f^{*} : \mathcal{T}' \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}, \qquad f_{*} : \mathcal{T} \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}'f∗:T′⟶T,f∗​:T⟶T′

satisfying f∗⊣f∗f^{*} \dashv f_{*}f∗⊣f∗​, with f∗f^{*}f∗ exact (preserving finite limits). f∗f^{*}f∗ is called the pullback functor and f∗f_{*}f∗​ the pushforward functor.

Notes

The motivation comes from topological spaces: a continuous map φ:X→Y\varphi : X \to Yφ:X→Y gives a pair of functors between categories of sheaves — pull sheaves back along φ\varphiφ (f∗f^{*}f∗), then push them forward (f∗f_{*}f∗​).

This pair satisfies f∗⊣f∗f^{*} \dashv f_{*}f∗⊣f∗​, and f∗f^{*}f∗ is exact.

So a "map" between topoi is defined on this pattern: not a functor, but a pair of adjoint functors, with the direction opposite to geometric intuition (the left one is the pullback).
