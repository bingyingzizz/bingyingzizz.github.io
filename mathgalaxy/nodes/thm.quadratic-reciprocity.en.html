Statement

Let p,qp, qp,q be two distinct odd primes. Then

(pq)(qp)=(−1)p−12⋅q−12.\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\frac{q-1}{2}} .(qp​)(pq​)=(−1)2p−1​⋅2q−1​.

Notes

This is the most important theorem in number theory (Gauss gave eight proofs in his lifetime, the first in 1796 at age 19). It turns the difficult question of whether ppp is a quadratic residue modulo qqq into the easier question of whether qqq is a quadratic residue modulo ppp, since one can recursively reduce ppp to <q< q<q.

⭐ A mainstream approach today uses point counting on curves over finite fields. (pq)\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)(qp​) is related to the number of solutions of x2≡p(modq)x^{2} \equiv p \pmod qx2≡p(modq), and these solutions can be counted in stages using elliptic curves or multiplicative groups over Fp\mathbb{F}_{p}Fp​ (Zolotarev's lemma and the signs of permutations). This is one modern approach. Elementary proofs also exist, including Gauss's third proof and proofs using Gauss's lemma or Zolotarev's lemma, all based on elementary counting.

Application. To evaluate (ap)\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)(pa​), factor it into primes, use reciprocity to swap the arguments for each prime factor, and recurse. This takes on the order of O(log⁡p)O(\log p)O(logp) divisions. The two supplementary formulas above handle −1-1−1 and 222, the two cases where the arguments cannot be swapped.
