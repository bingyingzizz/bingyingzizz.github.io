prop.stereographic-circle

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Stereographic projection maps circles on S2S^{2}S2 to circles on C^\widehat{\mathbb{C}}C:

  1. a circle not passing through the north pole NNN ↦\mapsto↦ a genuine circle in the complex plane;
  2. a circle passing through the north pole NNN ↦\mapsto↦ a line in the complex plane (adding ∞\infty∞ makes it a circle through ∞\infty∞).

Moreover it is conformal: the angle between two curves on the sphere equals the angle between them in the plane.

Notes

The algorithm for circle preservation: a circle on the sphere is “the intersection of a plane with the sphere”, i.e. ax+by+cw=dax + by + cw = dax+by+cw=d. Substitute the stereographic projection formula and rearrange into a linear expression in u,v,∣z∣2u, v, |z|^{2}u,v,∣z∣2:

(d−c)∣z∣2−2au−2bv=(d+c),(d - c)|z|^{2} - 2au - 2bv = (d + c),(d−c)∣z∣2−2au−2bv=(d+c),

When c≠dc \ne dc=d this is the equation of a circle; when c=dc = dc=d it degenerates into a line — exactly matching “whether the circle passes through the north pole”. ∎

⭐ The meaning of conformality: it shows that “doing complex analysis on the sphere” and “doing it on the plane” are the same subject, only with a different metric. This is also why the conformal structure on C^\widehat{\mathbb{C}}C can be defined as “z↦1/zz \mapsto 1/zz↦1/z is also holomorphic” — 1/z1/z1/z is precisely what “rotation by 180° about the north pole” looks like on the plane.

⚠️ Orientation: the sphere and the plane have different orientation conventions, and different textbooks differ by a sign in saying “conformal or anticonformal”. This graph only asserts “conformal (angles preserved)”; orientation is left to each book's convention.
