# Hermitian and Symmetric Matrices　`def.hermitian-matrix`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.hermitian-matrix)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.hermitian-matrix.md)

**Relation to quadratic forms**: symmetric matrices correspond one-to-one to quadratic forms ($q(x) = x^{T}Ax$, def.quadratic-form); Hermitian matrices correspond to Hermitian forms. "Positive definite" is exactly "$q > 0$" under this correspondence (def.positive-definite-matrix).

⚠️ Do not treat "Hermitian" and "symmetric" as two unrelated things: real symmetric $\subset$ Hermitian. But a **complex symmetric** matrix ($A^{T} = A$ while $A^{*} \ne A$) is neither Hermitian nor has real eigenvalues — over the complex field one must use $A^{*}$, not $A^{T}$.
