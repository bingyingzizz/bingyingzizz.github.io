# shrinking　`def.shrinks-nicely`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.shrinks-nicely)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.shrinks-nicely.md)

$\cdot$ $E_r \subseteq B(r, x)$ holds for every $r$;
$\cdot$ there is $\alpha > 0$ with $m(E_r) > \alpha\cdot m(B(r, x))$ for every $r$.

## Notes · **reviewed**
What the two conditions say: **it fits inside** ($E_r$ lies in the ball) and **it is not too flat** (its volume is at least $alpha$ times that of the ball).

Typical examples: take $E_r = B(r, x)$ itself ($alpha = 1$); or take $E_r$ to be a fixed shape inside the ball (a cube, say), rescaled proportionally.

Counterexample: let $E_r$ be a long thin sliver inside the ball — the volume ratio can tend to 0, and such a family does not shrink nicely to $x$.
