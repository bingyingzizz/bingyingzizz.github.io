Statement

If there are injections f:A→Bf : A \to Bf:A→B and g:B→Ag : B \to Ag:B→A, then there is a bijection A→BA \to BA→B; that is,

#A≤#B ∧ #B≤#A  ⟹  #A=#B\#A \le \#B \ \wedge \ \#B \le \#A \implies \#A = \#B#A≤#B ∧ #B≤#A⟹#A=#B

Notes

Its value is convenience: to prove that two sets are equinumerous it suffices to build one injection in each direction; the bijection itself need not be written down. (Compare the other route to Cantor's theorem — an injection but no surjection — which is the dual statement.)

It does not need the Axiom of Choice: the proof is constructive (see the proof on the edge), which is what makes it more usable than "any two cardinals are comparable".
