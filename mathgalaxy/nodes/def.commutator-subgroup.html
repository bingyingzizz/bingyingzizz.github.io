Statement draft

Let GGG be a group. For x,y∈Gx, y \in Gx,y∈G, the element [x,y]:=xyx−1y−1[x, y] := x y x^{-1} y^{-1}[x,y]:=xyx−1y−1 is the commutator of xxx and yyy. The subgroup generated by all commutators,

[G,G]:=⟨ [x,y]:x,y∈G ⟩≤G[G, G] := \langle\, [x, y] : x, y \in G \,\rangle \le G[G,G]:=⟨[x,y]:x,y∈G⟩≤G

is the commutator subgroup (also called the derived subgroup) of GGG.

[G,G] is normal, and the quotient G/[G,G]G/[G,G]G/[G,G] is abelian. More strongly, if N⊴GN \trianglelefteq GN⊴G is such that G/NG/NG/N is abelian, then [G,G]⊆N[G,G] \subseteq N[G,G]⊆N; hence G/[G,G]G/[G,G]G/[G,G] is the maximal abelian quotient of GGG.

⭐ Commutators themselves are not associative: in general [[x,y],z]≠[x,[y,z]][[x,y],z] \ne [x,[y,z]][[x,y],z]=[x,[y,z]]; the subgroup they generate is the useful object.

⭐ Relation to solvability: set G(0)=GG^{(0)} = GG(0)=G and G(i+1)=[G(i),G(i)]G^{(i+1)} = [G^{(i)}, G^{(i)}]G(i+1)=[G(i),G(i)] (the derived series). Then GGG is solvable if and only if some G(i)={e}G^{(i)} = \{e\}G(i)={e}.
