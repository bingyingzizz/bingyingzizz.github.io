Statement

Let MMM be an abelian group in a topos (for example M∈CondAbM \in \mathrm{CondAb}M∈CondAb). Then there is a natural left resolution

F(M)∙↠M,F(M)^{\bullet} \twoheadrightarrow M,F(M)∙↠M,

each term of which is a finite direct sum of "free abelian groups Z⋅Ms\mathbb{Z} \cdot M^{s}Z⋅Ms":

F(M)n=⨁i=1rnZ⋅Msn,i.F(M)_{n} = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_{n}} \mathbb{Z} \cdot M^{s_{n,i}}.F(M)n​=i=1⨁rn​​Z⋅Msn,i​.

Notes

What it says. In a general topos "free object" is not finite (the sss in MsM^{s}Ms is a set and may have arbitrarily large cardinality). The theorem says that nevertheless any abelian group MMM can be resolved by a termwise free complex, and the resolution is natural (independent of choices, functorial in MMM).

What Z⋅X\mathbb{Z} \cdot XZ⋅X is: the free abelian group generated by the set XXX (in a topos, Z[X]\mathbb{Z}[X]Z[X]). MsM^{s}Ms is "the product of sss copies of MMM" — the power set is taken so that "elements of MMM" can be written as a countable combination.

Why it is called the Breen–Deligne decomposition: the construction was proposed by Deligne but lacked a complete proof for a long time; Scholze completed the proof in an appendix to the fourth lecture of his condensed mathematics course. So it is a modern theorem.

⭐ It is the engine of all Ext computations in CondAb\mathrm{CondAb}CondAb: with this termwise free resolution, any Ext⁡n(M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, N)Extn(M,N) can be expanded into Exts of free terms, which can be computed (see the spectral sequence below).

⚠️ The proof is heavy, and the explicit formulas for the construction (combinations of d0,d1,d2d_{0}, d_{1}, d_{2}d0​,d1​,d2​) are quite technical — in practice one only needs to know that it exists and is natural.
