## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The three exponent laws are all "induction on $n$", with no trick.

What is really useful is **monotonicity**: for $a > 1$, $n \mapsto a^{n}$ is strictly increasing, so in an **Archimedean ordered field** (e.g. $\mathbb{R}$), $q^{n} \to 0$ ($0 < q < 1$) can be written as $q^{n} \le 1/(1+nh)$ and squeezed (it fails in a general ordered field: in $\mathbb{Q}(t)$, $t$ is infinite, and powers of $q = 1/2$ are never less than $1/t$) — the proof that the geometric series converges rests on this.
