Statement

What it does: it makes precise "integrating an nnn-form over an oriented region" — the only thing that can be integrated naturally, because under a change of variables only the transformation factor of a top-degree exterior form is exactly the determinant (without absolute value).

Let U⊆RnU\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}U⊆Rn be open and let ω=f dx1∧⋯∧dxn\omega = f\,\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}ω=fdx1∧⋯∧dxn be a compactly supported nnn-form. Define

∫Uω:=∫Uf(x) dx1⋯dxn,\int_{U}\omega := \int_{U}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x^{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x^{n},∫U​ω:=∫U​f(x)dx1⋯dxn,

where the right-hand side is the usual (Lebesgue or Riemann) integral and dx1⋯dxn\mathrm{d}x^{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x^{n}dx1⋯dxn denotes Lebesgue measure. Well-definedness: if y=ψ(x)y = \psi(x)y=ψ(x) is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, then

ω=(f∘ψ−1)det⁡(∂x∂y) dy1∧⋯∧dyn,\omega = (f\circ\psi^{-1})\det\Bigl(\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{n},ω=(f∘ψ−1)det(∂y∂x​)dy1∧⋯∧dyn,

Notes

Why nnn-forms specifically: under a change of variables the coefficient of dx1∧⋯∧dxn\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}dx1∧⋯∧dxn transforms by det⁡\detdet, which matches the det⁡\detdet in the change-of-variables formula exactly (no absolute value when orientation is preserved).

To integrate a form of degree k<nk<nk<n, one must first choose a kkk-dimensional submanifold and pull it back — that is "integration of forms on submanifolds".

Orientation cannot be dropped: replacing ψ\psiψ by an orientation-reversing map changes the sign of det⁡\detdet and hence of the integral. Without an orientation the only integrable objects are densities ∣ω∣\lvert\omega\rvert∣ω∣ — the area element dA\mathrm{d}AdA is a density (面积元).

Interface with the area element: on a surface dA=det⁡g du∧dv\mathrm{d}A = \sqrt{\det g}\,\mathrm{d}u\wedge\mathrm{d}vdA=detg​du∧dv, and integrating over an oriented surface gives area.

Here det⁡g\sqrt{\det g}detg​ is the factor on the "density" side and du∧dv\mathrm{d}u\wedge\mathrm{d}vdu∧dv the orientation on the "form" side; both signs must be right to avoid errors.

Why partitions of unity suffice: ω\omegaω is compactly supported, so only finitely many ραω\rho_{\alpha}\omegaρα​ω are non-zero, each supported in an oriented chart, reducing to the case of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn. Without compact support one replaces this by absolute integrability; this library does not elaborate.

⚠️ Notation: ∫Mω\int_{M}\omega∫M​ω depends only on ω\omegaω and the orientation, not on the atlas or the partition of unity (the difference between two choices cancels term by term). This is the prerequisite for being able to write Stokes' theorem.
