Statement

If M,NM, NM,N are two topological abelian groups and MMM is compactly generated, then there is a natural isomorphism of condensed abelian groups CZ(M,N)≅Hom⁡Z(M,N)C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, N)CZ​(M,N)≅HomZ​(M,N).

Notes

Proof. Denote the condensed abelian group on the left by S↦C(S,CZ(M,N))S \mapsto C(S, C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,N))S↦C(S,CZ​(M,N)). For compact Hausdorff SSS, evaluate both sides objectwise:

  • Right-hand side:
Hom⁡Z(M‾,N‾)(S)=Hom⁡(S,Hom⁡Z(M‾,N‾))=Hom⁡Z(M‾, Hom⁡(S,N‾))=Hom⁡Z(M‾, C(S,N))=CZ(M, C(S,N)),\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\underline{M},\underline{N})(S) = \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(S, \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\underline{M},\underline{N})\bigr) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\underline{M},\ \operatorname{Hom}(S, \underline{N})\bigr) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\underline{M},\ C(S, N)\bigr) = C_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(M,\ C(S,N)\bigr),HomZ​(M​,N​)(S)=Hom(S,HomZ​(M​,N​))=HomZ​(M​, Hom(S,N​))=HomZ​(M​, C(S,N))=CZ​(M, C(S,N)),

The second step uses the adjunction between (S,−)(S, -)(S,−) and internal Hom. The last step uses that, when MMM is compactly generated, M‾\underline{M}M​ is a fully faithful embedding (the preceding proposition);

  • Left-hand side: CZ(M,N)(S)=Hom⁡(S,CZ(M,N))=C(S, CZ(M,N))C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,N)(S) = \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(S, C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,N)\bigr) = C\bigl(S,\ C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,N)\bigr)CZ​(M,N)(S)=Hom(S,CZ​(M,N))=C(S, CZ​(M,N));
  • The exponential law (valid under compact generation or local compactness) relates the two sides:
C(M, C(S,N))≅C(M×S, N)≅C(S, C(M,N)).C\bigl(M,\ C(S,N)\bigr) \cong C(M\times S,\ N) \cong C\bigl(S,\ C(M,N)\bigr).C(M, C(S,N))≅C(M×S, N)≅C(S, C(M,N)).

Combining these gives the required natural isomorphism, since the exponential law preserves the homomorphism condition pointwise. ∎ ⭐ Role of the compact generation hypothesis on MMM: Under this hypothesis, Hom⁡Z(M‾,−)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\underline{M}, -)HomZ​(M​,−) measures continuous homomorphisms. For general topological abelian groups, condensed Hom can contain more maps than the continuous homomorphisms, because maps may be continuous on the test pieces and fail to be continuous globally.
