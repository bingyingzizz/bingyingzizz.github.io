Statement

A real (or complex) series ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ is called conditionally convergent if and only if it converges but ∑∣an∣\sum |a_n|∑∣an​∣ diverges.

The typical example is the alternating harmonic series:

∑n=1∞(−1)n−1n=ln⁡2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n} = \ln 2n=1∑∞​n(−1)n−1​=ln2

It converges, while after taking absolute values it becomes the divergent harmonic series.

Notes

⭐ A conditionally convergent series is fragile: Riemann's rearrangement theorem says its terms can be reordered to converge to any prescribed value (even to diverge to ±∞\pm\infty±∞).

So any "interchange of order" must first be justified.

⚠ The alternating harmonic series converges to ln⁡2\ln 2ln2, but after the rearrangement "one positive term, two negative terms" it converges to 12ln⁡2\frac{1}{2}\ln 221​ln2 — the same series, the same terms, a different sum.

⭐ At the level of tests: conditional convergence can only be detected by the Abel–Dirichlet family (all positive-term tests fail).
