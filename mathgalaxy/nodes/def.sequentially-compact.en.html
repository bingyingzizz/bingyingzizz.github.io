Statement

A metric space (X,d)(X, d)(X,d) is sequentially compact iff every sequence in XXX has a subsequence converging in XXX:

Notes

In metric spaces, "sequentially compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ compact" (see the arrows below), but in general topological spaces the two are not equivalent — sequential compactness is strictly stronger. This is exactly where metric spaces are convenient.

Note that the subsequence converges to a point of XXX: sequential compactness is an intrinsic property and does not refer to any ambient space.
