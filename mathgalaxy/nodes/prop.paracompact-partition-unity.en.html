prop.paracompact-partition-unity

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Abstract

For a topological space XXX: if XXX is paracompact Hausdorff then every open cover has a subordinate partition of unity, and if XXX is normal then every locally finite open cover has one. Neither converse holds without a separation hypothesis: on the trivial topology with at least two points the constant function 111 is a partition of unity subordinate to every cover, yet the space is neither Hausdorff nor T1T_1T1​. In the forward direction one shrinks each open set twice, applies Urysohn's lemma and normalizes the locally finite sum; conversely the supports of a partition of unity give a locally finite open refinement. Such partitions turn local data into global objects, which is what integrations and extension theorems on manifolds rely on.

Statement

For a topological space XXX:

  1. XXX is paracompact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ every open cover admits a subordinate partition of unity (the converse requires a separation hypothesis. In the indiscrete topology on at least two points, the constant function 111 gives a partition of unity, but the space is neither Hausdorff nor T1T_1T1​).
  2. XXX is normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ every locally finite open cover admits a subordinate partition of unity (the converse likewise requires T1T_1T1​ or Hausdorffness).

⭐ This result underlies extension theorems: A partition of unity combines locally defined data by a weighted sum to produce a globally defined function. Tietze- and Dugundji-type extensions, integration on manifolds, and Stokes' theorem all use this construction.

⚠️ Local finiteness is essential: Without local finiteness, ∑ρi=1\sum \rho_{i} = 1∑ρi​=1 is merely a pointwise assertion, and the pointwise sum need not be continuous.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the construction uses normality, and the converses require separate separation hypotheses):

  • Paracompact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ every open cover admits a subordinate partition of unity: Use normality of paracompact Hausdorff spaces, a locally finite refinement, the shrinking lemma, and Urysohn's lemma. A second shrinking ensures the supports lie in the original open sets.
  • Every open cover admits a subordinate partition of unity   ⟹  \implies⟹ a locally finite open refinement exists: Take the sets on which the functions are positive, using local finiteness of supports and the fact that the sum is one. This gives paracompactness. Hausdorffness must be assumed separately.
  • Normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ every locally finite open cover admits a subordinate partition of unity: Use locally finite shrinking, Urysohn's lemma, and normalization of a locally finite sum of functions. There is no requirement that every cover admit a locally finite refinement.
  • For the converse, use the cover {X∖A,X∖B}\{X\setminus A,X\setminus B\}{X∖A,X∖B} to separate the disjoint closed sets A,BA,BA,B. This gives the separation property for closed sets alone. To obtain normality under the definition that includes T1T_1T1​, one must also assume T1T_1T1​.
  • The missing separation hypothesis has a counterexample: In the indiscrete topology on at least two points, every open cover contains the entire space. The constant function one gives a subordinate partition of unity, yet the space is neither Hausdorff nor T1T_1T1​. Both converses require the additional hypotheses.
