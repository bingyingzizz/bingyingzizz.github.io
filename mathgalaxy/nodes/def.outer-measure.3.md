## English abstract · **draft** · machine-drafted, pending author review; Chinese is authoritative（续）
An outer measure on a set $X$ is a function $\mu^* : \mathcal{P}(X) \to [0, +\infty]$ with $\mu^*(\emptyset) = 0$ that is monotone and countably subadditive; $X$ may be empty. It is defined on every subset, and the price is exactly subadditivity: it need not be additive, hence need not be a measure. Lebesgue's construction $\mu^*(A) = \inf \sum (b_n - a_n)$ over countable interval covers is the model, and Carathéodory's condition singles out the sets on which $\mu^*$ becomes additive.
