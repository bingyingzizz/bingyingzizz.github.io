# group　`def.group`

[in graph](../#n=def.group)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.group.md)

A group that also satisfies the **commutative law** $a b = b a$ is called an **abelian group**, or **commutative group**.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **The format is again "set + operation + axioms"**: a group is not an object out of nowhere — one set, one function and three identities determine it. To decide whether something is a group, check these identities one by one.

**The identity and inverses are unique**: if $e, e'$ are both identities, then $e = e e' = e'$; if $a^{-1}, a'$ are both inverses of $a$, then $a' = a' e = a' a a^{-1} = e a^{-1} = a^{-1}$. Hence the notations "$e$" and "$a^{-1}$" are legitimate.
