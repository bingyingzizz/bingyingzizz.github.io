def.bicomplex

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A bicomplex in an additive category is a complex of complexes: that is, on (Z,⪯)2(\mathbb{Z}, \preceq)^{2}(Z,⪯)2 a diagram (Kp,q,dp,q,d′p,q)(K^{p,q}, d^{p,q}, d'^{p,q})(Kp,q,dp,q,d′p,q) such that for all p,qp, qp,q

dp+1,q∘dp,q=0,d′p,q+1∘d′p,q=0,d′p+1,q∘dp,q=dp,q+1∘d′p,q.d^{p+1,q} \circ d^{p,q} = 0, \qquad d'^{p,q+1} \circ d'^{p,q} = 0, \qquad d'^{p+1,q} \circ d^{p,q} = d^{p,q+1} \circ d'^{p,q}.dp+1,q∘dp,q=0,d′p,q+1∘d′p,q=0,d′p+1,q∘dp,q=dp,q+1∘d′p,q.

Notes

The first two identities say "each direction is separately a complex"; the third says the differentials of the two directions commute (up to a sign, depending on conventions).

⭐ How to turn it into an ordinary complex: take the total complex Kn:=⨁p+q=nKp,qK^{n} := \bigoplus_{p+q=n} K^{p,q}Kn:=⨁p+q=n​Kp,q (assuming the direct sum exists: in general A\mathcal{A}A need not have countable direct sums — in the category of finite-dimensional kkk-vector spaces every diagonal can have infinitely many nonzero terms, and then the total complex does not exist; alternatively use the version "each diagonal has only finitely many nonzero terms"), with differential d+d′d + d'd+d′ (with signs). Then the relation between the homology of the bicomplex and "compute row by row, then column by column" is exactly what produces spectral sequences.

Typical source: resolving a complex by injectives naturally produces a bicomplex (in Ip,qI^{p,q}Ip,q, ppp is the degree of the original complex and qqq is the degree of the resolution). The Grothendieck spectral sequence comes from it.
