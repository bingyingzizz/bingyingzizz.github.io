Statement

Let (X,T)(X, \mathcal{T})(X,T) be a topological space and Y⊆XY \subseteq XY⊆X a subset. The subspace topology on YYY is

TY:={ U∩Y:U∈T }\mathcal{T}_{Y} := \{\, U \cap Y : U \in \mathcal{T} \,\}TY​:={U∩Y:U∈T}

The resulting topology is the coarsest topology making the inclusion map Y↪XY \hookrightarrow XY↪X continuous.

Notes

Intuition: the open sets of the subspace are exactly the open sets of the big space cut down. So "closed in YYY" and "compact in YYY" must be judged with respect to the cut-down piece, not by looking at the big space — for example (0,1)⊆R(0, 1) \subseteq \mathbb{R}(0,1)⊆R is closed in itself but not in R\mathbb{R}R.

📌 诱导拓扑 is a different notion sharing the same Chinese name: it denotes the Grothendieck topology on a site induced by a functor, and shares only the name with the subspace topology (the two are not merged).
