## Notes · **draft**
The name "projective" comes from module theory: the projective objects of $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ are exactly the projective modules, and the injective objects are exactly the injective modules — the two standard criteria ($P$ is a direct summand, $I$ is a direct summand) are this definition specialised to modules.

Two immediate corollaries: **if $P$ is projective, every epimorphism $Y \twoheadrightarrow P$ has a section**; **if $X \to Y$ has a retraction and $Y$ is projective, then $X$ is projective too** (the injective case is dual).
