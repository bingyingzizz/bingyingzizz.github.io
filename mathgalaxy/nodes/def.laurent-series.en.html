Statement

In plain words: relax a power series into a series “on both sides” — the negative powers describe the blow-up of the function near the centre, the positive powers describe its behaviour far away.

that converges in Ar,R(a)A_{r,R}(a)Ar,R​(a) (on every compact subset) is called a Laurent series centred at aaa. The first block on the right is called the regular part, the second (the sum of negative powers) the principal part.

Notes

⭐ The two blocks have their own domains of convergence: the regular part converges in ∣z−a∣<R|z - a| < R∣z−a∣<R, the principal part in ∣z−a∣>r|z - a| > r∣z−a∣>r, and their intersection is exactly the annulus — this is why the natural stage for Laurent series is an annulus rather than a disc.

⚠ The trichotomy below requires aaa to be an isolated singularity (holomorphic on some punctured disc 0<∣z−a∣<R0 < |z - a| < R0<∣z−a∣<R); otherwise the shape of the principal part does not reflect the type of singularity — for example on ∣z∣>1|z| > 1∣z∣>1 the Laurent expansion has infinitely many principal terms, while 000 is holomorphic at fff. A vanishing principal part corresponds to a removable singularity;

a principal part with only finitely many terms corresponds to a pole (the order = the absolute value of the largest negative power in the principal part: if it starts from (z−a)−m(z - a)^{-m}(z−a)−m one says order mmm — not the number of nonzero terms; z−3z^{-3}z−3 has only one term yet is a pole of order three);

an infinite principal part corresponds to an essential singularity.

So the trichotomy of isolated singularities is, in essence, a classification by the shape of the principal part.

📌 The book first does the “Laurent decomposition”: a holomorphic function on an annulus splits into “a term holomorphic on the inner disc” plus “a term holomorphic on the outer domain (including infinity)”, and the Laurent series is assembled from their expansions.
