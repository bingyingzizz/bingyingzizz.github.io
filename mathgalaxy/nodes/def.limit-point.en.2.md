# limit point　`def.limit-point`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.limit-point)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.limit-point.md)

Replacing $S$ by the set of terms of a sequence gives "$x$ is a limit point of $\{x_n\}$", which means "$\{x_n\}$ has a subsequence converging to $x$".

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ "Every bounded sequence has a limit point" is the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem — one of the six faces of the completeness of the real numbers.

⚠ A limit point **need not belong** to $S$: the set of all rationals $\mathbb{Q}$ has limit-point set all of $\mathbb{R}$ (this is precisely what incompleteness of $\mathbb{Q}$ looks like).
