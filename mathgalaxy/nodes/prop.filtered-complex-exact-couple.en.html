Statement

Notes

This proposition connects the two constructions.

  • One approach (already in the graph) defines Zrp,qZ^{p,q}_{r}Zrp,q​ and Brp,qB^{p,q}_{r}Brp,q​ directly for the filtered complex, verifies Er+1=H(Er,dr)E_{r+1} = H(E_{r}, d_{r})Er+1​=H(Er​,dr​), and proves convergence to Gr⁡pHp+q\operatorname{Gr}^{p}H^{p+q}GrpHp+q. See a filtered complex gives a spectral sequence.
  • The other approach (used here) packages the three-term exactness of the long exact sequences into an exact couple and obtains the spectral sequence by deriving the couple. See exact couples give spectral sequences.

Why the two constructions agree.

① The first pages agree. Both are given by E1p,q=Hp+q(Gr⁡pK)E_{1}^{p,q} = H^{p+q}(\operatorname{Gr}^{p}K)E1p,q​=Hp+q(GrpK).

∎

⭐ Practical use. When computing a spectral sequence, choose whichever construction is easier to work with. For a double complex, the direct construction is usually convenient. Each filtration direction gives a spectral sequence, and it suffices to compare them at the E2E_{2}E2​ page. See a bicomplex gives a spectral sequence. For a general exact couple, such as one arising from homotopy groups or from a Bockstein or Gysin sequence, the exact couple is the only construction available, since there is no complex to filter.

⚠️ Convergence still requires boundedness on both sides (or at least stabilization for each (p,q)(p,q)(p,q), with rrr large enough that both ZrZ_{r}Zr​ and BrB_{r}Br​ stabilize). Boundedness on the p≪0p \ll 0p≪0 side alone is insufficient. The spectral sequence of an unbounded complex may fail to converge to anything, so convergence cannot be inferred from the notation alone.
