Statement

Let E,FE, FE,F be Banach spaces and u:E→Fu : E \to Fu:E→F linear. If the graph of uuu,

G(u):={(x,u(x)):x∈E}G(u) := \{(x, u(x)) : x \in E\}G(u):={(x,u(x)):x∈E}

is closed in the product space E×FE \times FE×F, then uuu is bounded (hence continuous).

Unwinding convergence in the product topology, the criterion is one sentence: for every sequence xn→xx_n \to xxn​→x, if u(xn)→yu(x_n) \to yu(xn​)→y, then necessarily y=u(x)y = u(x)y=u(x).

The practical value is that "closed" is easier to verify than "bounded". To prove uuu bounded directly one must estimate ∥ux∥\|u x\|∥ux∥; to see that the graph is closed one only needs: take convergent xn→xx_n \to xxn​→x and u(xn)→yu(x_n) \to yu(xn​)→y, and prove y=u(x)y = u(x)y=u(x) — no norm estimates in between. This is the route by which distribution theory proves that differential operators and the Fourier transform are continuous.

Why linearity and completeness at both ends are needed. "Continuous   ⟹  \implies⟹ closed graph" is elementary (the graph of a continuous map into a product is closed, for any map), so the two properties look similar; the whole difference lies in the converse.
