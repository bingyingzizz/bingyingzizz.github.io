Statement

Let MMM be an abelian sheaf on a site C\mathcal{C}C and let X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C. Take a hypercover of MMM (that is, a complex F∙→MF_{\bullet} \to MF∙​→M in which each FnF_{n}Fn​ is a direct sum of representable sheaves and which is exact everywhere after augmentation). Then the cohomology of MMM equals the homotopy groups of the simplicial abelian group obtained by taking sections levelwise:

Hn(X, M)≅πn(Γ(X, F∙)).H^{n}(X,\ M) \cong \pi^{n}\bigl(\Gamma(X,\ F_{\bullet})\bigr).Hn(X, M)≅πn(Γ(X, F∙​)).

Notes

What is being said. Sheaf cohomology is defined as "take an injective resolution, take sections, take cohomology", and an injective resolution is not computable (injective objects are too large). This gives a computable substitute: replacing the injective resolution by a resolution by free / representable objects, taking sections levelwise produces a simplicial abelian group (or chain complex) whose homotopy groups are HnH^{n}Hn.

Why it works: when FnF_{n}Fn​ consists of representable objects, the sections Γ(X,Fn)\Gamma(X, F_{n})Γ(X,Fn​) can be written down directly (they are the values of FnF_{n}Fn​ on that object). And a map between two resolutions gives a chain homotopy, so the resulting homotopy groups do not depend on the choice of resolution.

⭐ This is the modern form of the Godement decomposition, and the entry point of the ∞\infty∞-categorical method: in ∞\infty∞-categorical language, "hypercover" is replaced by "simplicial decomposition by XXX", and cohomology is the homotopy groups of the mapping space of that simplicial object. The whole computation of sheaf cohomology in derived algebraic geometry follows this route.

📌 Three inputs: simplicial objects, the definition of the simplex category (already available), and the Čech complex (the existing "cheap algorithm"). The lemma wires them into a machine that computes cohomology.
