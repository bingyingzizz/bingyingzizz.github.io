The reason is exactly the same — the operations on the quotient set must be independent of the representative.

⚠ The **union** of ideals is not generally an ideal: $2\mathbb{Z} \cup 3\mathbb{Z}$ does not contain $2 + 3 = 5$.

So the substitute for "union" is the **sum** $I + J$, which is the smallest ideal containing both.

⭐ The universal property of the quotient ring is the other face of the "first isomorphism theorem": when $\ker \varphi \supseteq I$, $\varphi$ factors through $A/I$, and $A/\ker\varphi \cong \operatorname{im}\varphi$.
