def.one-point-compactification · Alexandrov

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

What it does: the most economical way of adding a single point — add one point ∞\infty∞ so that every sequence running off to infinity converges to it. The passage from Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn to SnS^{n}Sn, and from C\mathbb{C}C to the Riemann sphere, are both instances of this construction.

Let XXX be a topological space. Take X∗:=X⊔{∞}X^{*} := X \sqcup \{\infty\}X∗:=X⊔{∞}, with the topology whose open sets are

  1. the open sets of XXX;
  2. sets of the form U∪{∞}U \cup \{\infty\}U∪{∞}, where U⊆XU \subseteq XU⊆X is open and X∖UX \setminus UX∖U is compact in XXX.

X∗X^{*}X∗ is called the one-point compactification of XXX (also the Alexandrov compactification).

Theorem:

  • X∗X^{*}X∗ is compact (for arbitrary XXX);
  • X∗X^{*}X∗ is Hausdorff   ⟺  \iff⟺ XXX is a locally compact Hausdorff space.

Notes

⭐ Topological intuition: U∪{∞}U \cup \{\infty\}U∪{∞} is open   ⟺  \iff⟺ its complement is compact. So "the complement of XXX is compact" is "a neighbourhood of ∞\infty∞" — the space around the point at infinity is exactly "what remains after XXX escapes". Thus the compactification of Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn has its point ∞\infty∞ with neighbourhoods that are "outside a ball", and SnS^{n}Sn is the north pole.

⭐ Standard examples: (Rn)∗≅Sn(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*} \cong S^{n}(Rn)∗≅Sn (stereographic projection; for n=1n = 1n=1, R∪{∞}≅S1\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\} \cong S^{1}R∪{∞}≅S1);

C∗≅S2\mathbb{C}^{*} \cong S^{2}C∗≅S2 is the Riemann sphere, on which complex analysis treats ∞\infty∞ as an ordinary point;

the one-point compactification of N\mathbb{N}N is {1/n}∪{0}\{1/n\} \cup \{0\}{1/n}∪{0}.

⚠️ Why local compactness is required: when XXX is not locally compact, X∗X^{*}X∗ is not Hausdorff — intuitively "a neighbourhood of ∞\infty∞" is so large that it meets a neighbourhood of some point forever. So existence and Hausdorffness are two separate matters: X∗X^{*}X∗ is always compact, but Hausdorffness costs local compactness.

⚠️ Relation to "locally compact Hausdorff implies completely regular": that proof uses exactly "X∗X^{*}X∗ is compact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ one can build a Urysohn function on X∗X^{*}X∗ and pull it back". The one-point compactification is the standard interface that transfers the good properties of compact spaces to XXX.

The two conclusions rest on completely different hypotheses (which is why this deserves a separate node):

  • X∗X^{*}X∗ is compact: no hypothesis is needed. Take any open cover of X∗X^{*}X∗; some member contains ∞\infty∞ (call it U∪{∞}U\cup\{\infty\}U∪{∞}), so X∖UX\setminus UX∖U is compact; it is covered by the remaining open sets, and compactness gives a finite subcover, which together with U∪{∞}U\cup\{\infty\}U∪{∞} itself gives a finite subcover. The whole argument uses only "an open cover of a compact set has a finite subcover", not Hausdorff, not local compactness.
  • X∗X^{*}X∗ is Hausdorff   ⟺  \iff⟺ XXX is locally compact Hausdorff: both directions cost something.

⋅\cdot⋅ (⇐\Leftarrow⇐) needs local compactness: given x∈Xx \in Xx∈X, separating it from ∞\infty∞ requires a compact neighbourhood of xxx, call it KKK, so X∖KX\setminus KX∖K is open and K∘K^{\circ}K∘ is an open neighbourhood of xxx, disjoint. Without local compactness this compact neighbourhood cannot be built. ⋅\cdot⋅ (  ⟹  \implies⟹): going back from X∗X^{*}X∗ being Hausdorff to XXX being Hausdorff is free (subspaces inherit it); going back to local compactness uses closedness of compact sets: the open neighbourhood of ∞\infty∞, say U∪{∞}U\cup\{\infty\}U∪{∞}, satisfies X∖UX\setminus UX∖U compact, so the closure of UUU in XXX... This is exactly the use of "in a compact Hausdorff space compact sets are closed".

⚠️ So "the one-point compactification always exists" must be read in two parts: X∗X^{*}X∗ always exists as a compact space; but it is Hausdorff only when XXX is locally compact Hausdorff. Many textbooks state only the first, so beginners think "every space can be compactified" — false: those that admit a Hausdorff compactification are the locally compact Hausdorff ones.

⚠️ Why the neighbourhoods of ∞\infty∞ have that form: "complement compact" in the second axiom is not arbitrary — if the neighbourhood is U∪{∞}U\cup\{\infty\}U∪{∞}, then points "running to infinity" must eventually lie in UUU, i.e. eventually leave X∖UX\setminus UX∖U; requiring X∖UX\setminus UX∖U compact is exactly what aligns "leaving it" with "compactness", so that X∗X^{*}X∗ is compact.
