That is why "finding an antiderivative" makes sense and is determined only up to a constant (the other half of the fundamental theorem of calculus).

⚠ The domain must be an **interval**: on $(0, 1) \cup (2, 3)$ a function whose derivative vanishes everywhere can take different constants on the two pieces.

⭐ In measure theory its status changes: $f' = 0$ a.e. does not force $f$ to be constant (the Cantor function) — "almost everywhere" is not enough to pin down a function, and absolute continuity is exactly the missing hypothesis.
