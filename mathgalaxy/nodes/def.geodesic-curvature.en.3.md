## Notes · **draft**
**Sign**: $k_{g}$ depends on orientation. With $(T,u,n)$ positively oriented, $u = n\times T$, and the curve bends towards $u$ exactly when $k_{g} > 0$. Replacing $u$ by $-u$ reverses all signs — when discussing "the integral of $k_{g}$" the orientation must be fixed first; this matters especially in the Gauss–Bonnet theorem.

**Why it is intrinsic**: the tangential part of $\ddot\gamma$ uses only $\nabla$ (that is, $I$), and needs neither $n$ nor the second fundamental form. So $k_{g}$ is intrinsic, in contrast to $\kappa_{n}$.
