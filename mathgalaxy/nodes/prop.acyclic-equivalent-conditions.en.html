Statement

Let MMM be an abelian sheaf on the site C\mathcal{C}C. The following four conditions are equivalent:

  1. MMM is acyclic: Hn(M)=0H^{n}(M) = 0Hn(M)=0 (n≠0n \ne 0n=0);
  2. for every X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C: Hn(X,M)=0H^{n}(X, M) = 0Hn(X,M)=0 (n≠0n \ne 0n=0);
  3. for every cover family X\mathfrak{X}X: Hˇn(X,M)=0\check{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{X}, M) = 0Hˇn(X,M)=0 (n≠0n \ne 0n=0);
  4. for every X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C: Hˇn(X,M)=0\check{H}^{n}(X, M) = 0Hˇn(X,M)=0 (n≠0n \ne 0n=0).

Notes

Among the four, (1)  ⟺  \iff⟺(2) is only a change of notation, (2)  ⟺  \iff⟺(3) uses the comparison theorem, and (3)  ⟺  \iff⟺(4) uses limits:

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (2): tautology. The cohomology of a sheaf is the cohomology of its values pointwise; Hn(C,M)H^{n}(\mathcal{C}, M)Hn(C,M) is by definition the derived functor of Hn(X↦Γ(X,M))H^{n}(X \mapsto\Gamma(X,M))Hn(X↦Γ(X,M)).
  • (2)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (4): this step is the comparison between Čech and derived functors: for any XXX and any cover, Čech cohomology and sheaf cohomology are connected by a spectral sequence; when acyclic the spectral sequence degenerates and the two agree. ⚠️ In general they do not agree (one needs C\mathcal{C}C paracompact / the open cover to be good enough, see @@thm.paracompact-cech-equals-sheaf@@).
  • (2)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (3): uses "the colimit over cover families": Hn(X,M)=lim→⁡XHˇn(X,M)H^{n}(X, M) = \varinjlim_{\mathfrak{X}} \check{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{X}, M)Hn(X,M)=lim​X​Hˇn(X,M) (take the limit along refinements of covers). Since every Čech group vanishes, the limit vanishes; the converse follows from "every cover can be refined to a point cover". ⚠️ This step requires that the colimit really be sheaf cohomology (i.e. the comparison above holds).

⚠️ The correct use of this proposition: it is a tool for deciding whether a particular sheaf is acyclic, but it cannot be used to decide whether a site is good enough — conversely, it is precisely "Čech = derived" that needs extra hypotheses on the site. Do not mix up the two directions.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the original node's notation and the range of validity of the Čech comparison need verification):

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (2): this is a definitional unfolding only when Hn(M)H^n(M)Hn(M) is explicitly defined as the presheaf X↦Hn(X,M)X\mapsto H^n(X,M)X↦Hn(X,M); if it denotes the cohomology sheaf after sheafification, or cohomology at the terminal object, it cannot be read this way. This step is unverified; recorded as a gap.
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): needs the Čech-to-derived-cohomology spectral sequence for covers and their finite fibred products, and needs the vanishing of higher cohomology on the overlaps to make it degenerate; the precise hypotheses on covers and overlaps are unverified here and recorded as a gap.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (4): if Čech cohomology on an object is defined as the colimit along refinements of covers, then vanishing of each term gives vanishing of the colimit; only the definition and colimit properties are used.
  • (4)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3) cannot be deduced from vanishing of the colimit alone: classes may disappear only after refinement; nor can (4)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2) simply declare the spectral sequence degenerate — the needed comparison or acyclicity theorem is unverified and recorded as a gap.
  • The original note's claim "every cover can be refined to a point cover" is not a general fact; the identity cover of a single object is usually the thing being refined by other covers. The text is retained for manual verification, and this paragraph does not treat the unconditional equivalence of the four as proved.
