Statement

Let T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W be linear. Define

ker⁡T:={ v∈V:T(v)=0 }⊆V,im⁡T:={ T(v):v∈V }⊆W\ker T := \{\, v \in V : T(v) = 0 \,\} \subseteq V, \qquad \operatorname{im} T := \{\, T(v) : v \in V \,\} \subseteq WkerT:={v∈V:T(v)=0}⊆V,imT:={T(v):v∈V}⊆W

called the kernel and the image of TTT. Both are subspaces, and

TTT is injective iff

ker⁡T={0}\ker T = \{0\}kerT={0}

TTT is surjective iff im⁡T=W\operatorname{im} T = WimT=W.

Notes

⭐ "Injective iff the kernel is zero" is the most useful fact about linear maps:   ⟺  \iff⟺, because T(u)=T(v)  ⟺  T(u−v)=0T(u) = T(v) \iff T(u - v) = 0T(u)=T(v)⟺T(u−v)=0 — linearity turns "two points coincide" into "their difference lies in the kernel".

General maps have no such convenience.

⭐ The kernel and the image are "the information lost" and "the information retained": the rank–nullity theorem says precisely that together they account for VVV.

⚠ ker⁡T\ker TkerT is a subspace of VVV, while im⁡T\operatorname{im} TimT is a subspace of WWW — do not swap them.
