Statement

What it does**: rewrite convergence in the language of families of sets**. A filter abstracts the phrase "eventually falls into a given set", so limits can be discussed without mentioning indices, only families of sets — the handiest tool at the interface of set theory and topology.

Let XXX be a topological space. A nonempty family F⊆P(X)\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)F⊆P(X) is a **filter** if

  1. ∅∉F\emptyset \notin \mathcal{F}∅∈/F, X∈FX \in \mathcal{F}X∈F;
  2. upward closed: F∈FF \in \mathcal{F}F∈F and F⊆GF \subseteq GF⊆G   ⟹  \implies⟹ G∈FG \in \mathcal{F}G∈F;
  3. closed under finite intersections: F,G∈FF, G \in \mathcal{F}F,G∈F   ⟹  \implies⟹ F∩G∈FF \cap G \in \mathcal{F}F∩G∈F.

**Convergence**: F→x\mathcal{F} \to xF→x if every neighbourhood of xxx belongs to F\mathcal{F}F (i.e. N(x)⊆F\mathcal{N}(x) \subseteq \mathcal{F}N(x)⊆F).

An ultrafilter** is a maximal filter. Equivalently, for every A⊆XA \subseteq XA⊆X, exactly one** of AAA and X∖AX \setminus AX∖A belongs to U\mathcal{U}U.

**Ultrafilter lemma**: every filter is contained in an ultrafilter (using Zorn's lemma, Zorn 引理).

Notes

⭐ **What filters are for: a clean characterization of compactness**: XXX is compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ every ultrafilter converges (equivalently, every filter has a cluster point). This is shorter than the net version, because the "subnet" step is absorbed by maximality of the ultrafilter.

⭐ The ultrafilter lemma cannot be dispensed with**: it is not a theorem but a small piece of the axiom of choice** (strictly weaker than AC, but equivalent to the Boolean prime ideal theorem). So every proof that "takes an ultrafilter" is secretly using choice — in contrast with the family of arguments in 仿紧 Hausdorff 空间正规 that use **only compactness**.

⚠️ The neighbourhood system of the filter ≠\ne=: N(x)\mathcal{N}(x)N(x) itself is a filter (the neighbourhood filter), whereas a general filter may **fail to converge**. Convergence depends on whether it contains some N(x)\mathcal{N}(x)N(x).

⚠️ Name clash with "filter" in category theory**: 覆盖筛构成滤子 concerns the filteredness of sieves in a Grothendieck topology, and is unrelated** to the set-theoretic filter here.

**Of the three axioms, "closed under finite intersections" is the only one with content** (the other two are almost mere normalization):

  • ∅∉F\emptyset \notin \mathcal{F}∅∈/F and X∈FX \in \mathcal{F}X∈F: **normalization**. Requiring the family to be nonempty and to omit the empty set, together with upward closure, automatically gives XXX.
  • **Upward closure**: part of the definition; dropping it leaves a "filter base", for which one must additionally require that any two members have a smaller member.
  • Closure under finite intersections**: this is the real condition**, and the reason filters can be translated into nets (eq.net-filter).

Two ways of writing convergence**: F→x\mathcal{F} \to xF→x   ⟺  \iff⟺ N(x)⊆F\mathcal{N}(x) \subseteq \mathcal{F}N(x)⊆F   ⟺  \iff⟺ F\mathcal{F}F, which is finer** than the neighbourhood filter. ⚠️ The direction is easy to invert: **the larger (finer) the filter, the easier it converges**.

The ultrafilter lemma (every filter lies in an ultrafilter) needs Zorn's lemma** — the only dependence on AC in this chain. It also makes the characterization "compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ every ultrafilter converges" non-constructive**.

⚠️ Unrelated to "filteredness" in category theory (滤过范畴); the two share only the word "filter".
