Statement

Let 0≤an≤bn0 \le a_n \le b_n0≤an​≤bn​ hold for all sufficiently large nnn. Then

  • ∑bn\sum b_n∑bn​ converges   ⟹  \implies⟹ ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ converges;
  • ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ diverges   ⟹  \implies⟹ ∑bn\sum b_n∑bn​ diverges.

Limit form: if an,bn>0a_n, b_n > 0an​,bn​>0 and anbn→L\dfrac{a_n}{b_n} \to Lbn​an​​→L, then for L∈(0,+∞)L \in (0, +\infty)L∈(0,+∞) the two converge or diverge together; for L=0L = 0L=0, convergence of ∑bn\sum b_n∑bn​ implies convergence of ∑an\sum a_n∑an​.

Notes

⭐ This is the foundation of all criteria for series with positive terms, and it is itself just a direct translation of "a monotone bounded partial-sum sequence   ⟹  \implies⟹ converges": the partial sums of ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ are dominated by those of ∑bn\sum b_n∑bn​.

⚠ The hypothesis is nonnegativity (or eventual nonnegativity): with mixed signs the partial sums are no longer monotone, and "dominated" means nothing.

⚠ It is not a corollary of Kummer's test — the direction is the opposite: the proof of Kummer's test itself uses this "monotone bounded partial sums" idea (i.e. comparison).

Deleting this would leave the whole pack of criteria without a foothold.
