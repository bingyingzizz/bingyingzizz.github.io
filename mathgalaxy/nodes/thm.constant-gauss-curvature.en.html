Statement

What this does: it pushes "KKK constant" to its limit — the answer is extremely clean: locally there are only three possibilities. This is where intrinsic geometry really shows its power (only the first fundamental form is used throughout).

On a surface Σ\SigmaΣ, let the Gauss curvature KKK be constant. At any point take geodesic polar coordinates (r,θ)(r, \theta)(r,θ); then

g=dr2+f(r,θ)2 dθ2,g = \mathrm{d}r^{2} + f(r,\theta)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{2} ,g=dr2+f(r,θ)2dθ2,

and Cartan's structure equation dω2=K ω1∧ω2\mathrm{d}\omega^{2} = K\,\omega^{1}\wedge\omega^{2}dω2=Kω1∧ω2 gives that fff satisfies

∂2f∂r2+Kf=0,\frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial r^{2}} + K f = 0 ,∂r2∂2f​+Kf=0,

with initial conditions f(0,θ)=0f(0,\theta) = 0f(0,θ)=0, ∂rf(0,θ)=1\partial_{r}f(0,\theta) = 1∂r​f(0,θ)=1, yielding three solutions:

  • K=0K = 0K=0: f=rf = rf=r, i.e. g=dr2+r2dθ2g = \mathrm{d}r^{2} + r^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}g=dr2+r2dθ2 (Euclidean metric);
  • K>0K > 0K>0: f=1Ksin⁡(K r)f = \frac{1}{\sqrt K}\sin(\sqrt K\, r)f=K​1​sin(K​r) (spherical metric);
  • K<0K < 0K<0: f=1−Ksinh⁡(−K r)f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-K}}\sinh(\sqrt{-K}\, r)f=−K​1​sinh(−K​r) (hyperbolic metric).

Theorem: a surface of constant Gauss curvature is locally isometric to one of the plane, the sphere or the hyperbolic plane.

Corollary: if it is simply connected and complete, only the plane, the sphere and the hyperbolic plane occur.

Notes

Why it deserves its own entry: it translates a metric condition (KKK constant) into three concrete metric forms using only the first fundamental form — how the surface sits in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 plays no role. This is the first big victory of intrinsic geometry after Gauss's theorema egregium.

Where the three solutions come from: the characteristic roots of the constant-coefficient equation f′′+Kf=0f'' + Kf = 0f′′+Kf=0 are ±−K\pm\sqrt{-K}±−K​, and the sign of KKK decides whether the solutions are trigonometric (oscillating), linear, or hyperbolic (exponentially growing). The initial conditions f(0)=0f(0) = 0f(0)=0, f′(0)=1f'(0) = 1f′(0)=1 are the geometric requirements of geodesic polar coordinates (circumference zero at r=0r = 0r=0, and rrr is arc length).

⭐ The row K=0K = 0K=0 is exactly the developable case (可展曲面): dr2+r2dθ2\mathrm{d}r^{2} + r^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}dr2+r2dθ2 is the polar-coordinate metric of the plane, so a surface with K≡0K \equiv 0K≡0 is locally isometric to the plane — this is the analytic basis of "developable".

⚠ Locally only three, globally many: flat tori, cylinders and hyperbolic surfaces all have constant curvature. The corollary's "simply connected + complete" excludes them, and both hypotheses are needed.

⚠ For K>0K > 0K>0, rrr is bounded: fff returns to zero at K r=π\sqrt K\,r = \piK​r=π, so geodesics meet again at a finite distance (antipodal points); for K≤0K \le 0K≤0, fff stays positive and geodesics never meet again.
