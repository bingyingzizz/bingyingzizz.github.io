Statement

Let {fn}⊆L+\{f_n\} \subseteq L^+{fn​}⊆L+ be a pointwise increasing sequence:

f1≤f2≤⋯ ,fn↑ff_1 \le f_2 \le \cdots, \quad f_n \uparrow ff1​≤f2​≤⋯,fn​↑f

Then the integral and the limit can be exchanged:

∫f=lim⁡n→∞∫fn\int f = \lim_{n\to\infty} \int f_n∫f=n→∞lim​∫fn​

Notes

⭐ This is the engine of the whole Lebesgue integration theory: term-by-term integration, Fatou, and the DCT are all corollaries.

Compare with the Riemann integral: there "exchanging integral and limit" needs a strong condition such as uniform convergence; here monotonicity alone suffices — that is the entire advantage of the Lebesgue integral.

⚠ Both sides may be ∞\infty∞; the identity is understood in the extended sense.
