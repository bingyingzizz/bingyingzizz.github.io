Statement

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V self-adjoint (that is, real symmetric). Then there is an orthonormal basis of VVV in which TTT is diagonal:

V=Eλ1⊕⋯⊕EλmV = E_{\lambda_1} \oplus \cdots \oplus E_{\lambda_m}V=Eλ1​​⊕⋯⊕Eλm​​

(these eigenspaces are pairwise orthogonal.)

Moreover, all eigenvalues λi\lambda_iλi​ are real. Matrix form: for a real symmetric matrix AAA there is an orthogonal matrix QQQ such that QTAQ=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn)Q^{\mathsf{T}} A Q = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n)QTAQ=diag(λ1​,…,λn​).

Notes

⭐ Difference from Jordan form: Jordan guarantees only "similarity", while the spectral theorem guarantees "unitary/orthogonal similarity", at the price of tightening the hypothesis to self-adjoint (normal).

These are the answers to two different classification problems in finite dimension.

⭐ The two steps of the proof: (1) there is at least one (real) eigenvalue (by the extreme value theorem: ∣Tv∣|Tv|∣Tv∣ or ⟨Tv,v⟩\langle Tv, v\rangle⟨Tv,v⟩ attains an extremum on the compact unit sphere);

(2) induct on the orthogonal complement of the eigenspace (invariance comes from self-adjointness: T(U)⊆U  ⟹  T(U⊥)⊆U⊥T(U) \subseteq U \implies T(U^{\perp}) \subseteq U^{\perp}T(U)⊆U⟹T(U⊥)⊆U⊥).

⭐ The infinite-dimensional generalization is the spectral theorem on Hilbert spaces (spectral measures / projection-valued measures for self-adjoint operators) — that belongs to functional analysis, and L2L^{2}L2 in the analysis.lp cluster on the graph is its stage.
