## Notes · **draft**
⚠️ **Why sequences are not enough**: in an uncountable space a singleton can be closed without being “sequentially closed” (for example $\omega_{1}$ in $[0,\omega_{1}]$: no sequence approaches it from below, yet it lies in the closure).

So “$x \in \overline{A}$ $\iff$ there is a sequence in $A$ converging to $x$” **holds only in first-countable spaces**.

Nets fix this at once by replacing $\mathbb{N}$ with “the reverse order of the neighbourhoods of $x$”.
