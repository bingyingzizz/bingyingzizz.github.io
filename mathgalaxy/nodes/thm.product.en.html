Statement

Given any two sets, the collection of all ordered pairs is again a set:

∀A∀B∃C∀z[z∈C↔∃a∈A,∃b∈B,z=(a,b)]\forall A \forall B \exists C \forall z [ z \in C \leftrightarrow \exists a \in A, \exists b \in B, z = (a, b) ]∀A∀B∃C∀z[z∈C↔∃a∈A,∃b∈B,z=(a,b)]

It is written A×B={(a,b):a∈A,b∈B}A \times B = \{ (a, b) : a \in A, b \in B \}A×B={(a,b):a∈A,b∈B}.

Notes

It is the prerequisite for defining relations and functions as sets: without A×BA \times BA×B there is no such thing as R⊆A×BR \subseteq A \times BR⊆A×B.
