## Notes · **draft**
The advantage of Tukey's lemma is that it **saves verification**: as soon as the family in question has finite character, the tedious check that "every chain has an upper bound" can be skipped.

Example: the family of linearly independent sets has finite character ($X$ is linearly independent $\iff$ every finite subset of $X$ is linearly independent), so "every linearly independent set extends to a maximal one" holds automatically.

History: Teichmüller (1939) and Tukey (1940) stated it independently.
