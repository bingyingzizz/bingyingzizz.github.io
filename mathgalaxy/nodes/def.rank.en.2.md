# rank　`def.rank`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.rank)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.rank.md)

It is the **only** numerical invariant of linear algebra: two matrices are equivalent under a change of bases (not necessarily the same basis) just when their ranks agree.

⚠ Does rank depend on the **choice** of basis? No: no basis appears in the definition ($\dim \operatorname{im} T$ is basis-independent, because dimension is well defined).

That is exactly why rank is useful.

⭐ The later section on determinants gives a way to compute the rank and test invertibility from matrix entries alone ($\det \ne 0 \iff$ full rank).
