## Abstract · **reviewed**
For measure spaces $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ and $(Y, \mathcal{N}, \nu)$: rectangles $A \times B$ generate $\mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{N}$; on finite disjoint unions of rectangles set $\mu_0(\bigsqcup_j A_j \times B_j) := \sum_j \mu(A_j)\nu(B_j)$, which is a premeasure; extend it to an outer measure and restrict to $\mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{N}$. That restriction is the product measure $\mu \times \nu$. Disjointness is exactly what makes $\mu_0$ well defined. When both factors are $\sigma$-finite so is the product, and Carathéodory uniqueness makes it the only such measure.
