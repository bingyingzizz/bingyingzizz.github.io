Yet the original definition of $K$ as $k_{1}k_{2}$ uses the second fundamental form (an extrinsic quantity) — so this is not a consequence of the definition but a theorem.

## Notes · **draft**
**Why it is called "remarkable"**: $K$ is the product of the two principal curvatures, which come from $\mathrm{d}n$, where $n$ is the unit normal in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ — extrinsic data by any account. Yet it can be read off from the metric inside the surface alone. All of intrinsic geometry (Gauss–Bonnet, uniformization, the topological meaning of $K$) grows from this.
