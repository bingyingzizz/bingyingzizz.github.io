Statement

Let MMM be a GGG-module. The nnn-th cohomology group of the group GGG with coefficients in MMM is the right derived functor of the invariant functor:

Hn(G,M):=Rn(−)G(M),H^{n}(G, M) := R^{n}(-)^{G}(M),Hn(G,M):=Rn(−)G(M),

equivalently

Hn(G,M)=Ext⁡R[G]n(R, M),H^{n}(G, M) = \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R[G]}\bigl(R,\ M\bigr),Hn(G,M)=ExtR[G]n​(R, M),

where RRR carries the trivial GGG-action. In particular H0(G,M)=MGH^{0}(G, M) = M^{G}H0(G,M)=MG and Hn(G,M)=0H^{n}(G, M) = 0Hn(G,M)=0 (for n<0n < 0n<0).

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: H0H^{0}H0 is only "fixed points"; HnH^{n}Hn for n≥1n \ge 1n≥1 measures how far the invariant functor is from exact — that is, how nontrivial the action of GGG is.

Two algorithms:

  • Injective resolution (the definition itself): take an injective R[G]R[G]R[G]-module resolution M↣I∙M \rightarrowtail I^{\bullet}M↣I∙ of MMM, take invariants, take homology. Cleanest, but injective R[G]R[G]R[G]-modules are invisible.
  • Standard (bar) resolution (computable): take the free R[G]R[G]R[G]-module resolution B∙→RB_{\bullet} \to RB∙​→R of RRR, then take Hom⁡R[G](B∙,M)\operatorname{Hom}_{R[G]}(B_{\bullet}, M)HomR[G]​(B∙​,M), obtaining an explicit cochain complex. This gives a concrete formula for HnH^{n}Hn.

Why it equals Ext⁡\operatorname{Ext}Ext: (−)G=Hom⁡R[G](R,−)(-)^{G} = \operatorname{Hom}_{R[G]}(R, -)(−)G=HomR[G]​(R,−), and computing RnHom⁡R[G](R,−)R^{n}\operatorname{Hom}_{R[G]}(R, -)RnHomR[G]​(R,−) with an injective resolution of RRR is exactly Ext⁡R[G]n(R,−)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R[G]}(R, -)ExtR[G]n​(R,−).

The same right derived functor under another name.

Hn=0H^{n} = 0Hn=0 (n<0n < 0n<0) is likewise a direct consequence of "right derived functor".

Two immediate consequences:

  • If MMM is a Q\mathbb{Q}Q-vector space and GGG acts trivially, then (−)G(-)^{G}(−)G is the identity functor (exact), so Hn(G,M)=0H^{n}(G, M) = 0Hn(G,M)=0 for n≥1n \ge 1n≥1.
  • If GGG acts trivially on MMM, then H1(G,M)=Hom⁡(G,M)H^{1}(G, M) = \operatorname{Hom}(G, M)H1(G,M)=Hom(G,M); in general one replaces \(\operatorname{Hom}\) by "crossed homomorphisms".
