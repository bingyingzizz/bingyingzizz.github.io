# Injective Objects Are Exactly the Acyclic Ones　`prop.injective-iff-acyclic`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.injective-iff-acyclic)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.injective-iff-acyclic.md)

Using it to compute the right derived functor: $RF(I) = F(I)$ is concentrated in degree $0$, so $R^{n}F(I) = 0$ ($n > 0$) ✓, that is, $I$ is $F$-acyclic.
$(2) \implies (3)$: obvious ($\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)$ is left exact).
$(3) \implies (1)$: for every $M$, $I$ being acyclic for $\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)$ means $R^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(M, I) = \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, I) = 0$ ($n > 0$); in particular $\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M, I) = 0$ holds for all $M$ ✓.
