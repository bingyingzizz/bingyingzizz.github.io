Statement

Let F:C↪DF : \mathcal{C} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}F:C↪D be a fully faithful functor, with D\mathcal{D}D additive (resp. abelian). If FFF has an adjoint or a coadjoint (resp. and FFF is exact), then C\mathcal{C}C is also an additive (resp. abelian) category.

Notes

Proof.

(i) Biproducts. An adjoint (left or right) is additive: a right adjoint preserves products, a left adjoint preserves coproducts ✓ (see "adjoint functors are additive").

Hence in D\mathcal{D}D the object F(X)⊕F(Y)F(X) \oplus F(Y)F(X)⊕F(Y) is a biproduct, and the fully faithful FFF carries it back "faithfully" to C\mathcal{C}C: by Yoneda's lemma, the two identities on Hom⁡C(Z,⋅)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Z, \cdot)HomC​(Z,⋅) agree completely with those in D\mathcal{D}D ✓.

(ii) Abelianness. Once biproducts exist, kernels and cokernels are carried back along the exact functor from D\mathcal{D}D, where every morphism is strict (coker⁡(ker⁡u)≅ker⁡(coker⁡u)\operatorname{coker}(\ker u) \cong \ker(\operatorname{coker} u)coker(keru)≅ker(cokeru)) ✓. ∎

⭐ Reading: a fully faithful "inclusion" functor that still has an adjoint gives the subcategory additive structure for free — this answers "why subcategories of Ab\mathbf{Ab}Ab are automatically additive".
