# sequence of morphisms　`def.sequence-of-morphisms`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.sequence-of-morphisms)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.sequence-of-morphisms.md)

$$\cdots \longrightarrow K^{n-1} \xrightarrow{d^{n-1}} K^{n} \xrightarrow{d^{n}} K^{n+1} \longrightarrow \cdots$$

Since between any two objects of $(\mathbb{Z}, \le)$ there is at most one morphism, commutativity is automatic: $d^{m-1} \circ \cdots \circ d^{n}$ is the unique morphism $K^{n} \to K^{m}$. It is also called a **sequence of morphisms**.

## Notes · **draft**
A sequence **does not require** $d^{n} \circ d^{n-1} = 0$; adding this condition gives a (co)chain complex (see @@def.cochain-complex@@).
