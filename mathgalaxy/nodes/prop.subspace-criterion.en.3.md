## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The "merged form" compresses three conditions into one operation $cu + v$: it governs $0$ (take $c = -1$, $v = u$), addition (take $c = 1$), and scalar multiplication (take $v = 0$, where $0 \in U$ has already been obtained) all at once.

It saves the most space when writing proofs.

⭐ The "ascending chain form" is the one place where an **infinite** union works: an arbitrary finite union need not be closed, but the union of an increasing chain is closed — because any two elements lie in the same $U_n$ of the chain (take the maximum of finitely many indices).
