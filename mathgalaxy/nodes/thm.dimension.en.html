Statement

Let VVV be a KKK-vector space, and B\mathcal{B}B, B′\mathcal{B}'B′ two bases of VVV. Then B\mathcal{B}B and B′\mathcal{B}'B′ are **equipotent** (there is a bijection).

Hence the **dimension** of VVV is well defined:

dim⁡KV:=#B\dim_K V := \#\mathcal{B}dimK​V:=#B

(by convention dim⁡V=0\dim V = 0dimV=0 when V={0}V = \{0\}V={0}; when there is no finite basis, VVV is called **infinite-dimensional**.)

Notes

⭐ The only content of this theorem is **well-definedness**: there may be many bases, but their cardinality is the same number — that is what makes the word "dimension" meaningful.

The proof is entirely Steinitz: two bases are each independent and generating for the other, so two applications of the exchange lemma give #B≤#B′\#\mathcal{B} \le \#\mathcal{B}'#B≤#B′ and the reverse.

⚠ In the finite-dimensional case the exchange lemma suffices;

the infinite-dimensional case requires proving "infinite bases are equipotent" (using the cardinal arithmetic of #(A×N)=#A\#(A \times \mathbb{N}) = \#A#(A×N)=#A), which is much more involved than the finite case.

⭐ Basic properties of dimension: dim⁡(U+W)+dim⁡(U∩W)=dim⁡U+dim⁡W\dim(U + W) + \dim(U \cap W) = \dim U + \dim Wdim(U+W)+dim(U∩W)=dimU+dimW;

when VVV is **finite-dimensional**, dim⁡(V/U)=dim⁡V−dim⁡U\dim(V/U) = \dim V - \dim Udim(V/U)=dimV−dimU; in infinite dimension subtraction is meaningless (cardinals do not subtract), and only cardinal addition dim⁡V=dim⁡U+dim⁡(V/U)\dim V = \dim U + \dim(V/U)dimV=dimU+dim(V/U) remains.
