where $Z^{n}(K) := \ker d^{n}$ is the group of **cocycles** and $B^{n}(K) := \operatorname{im} d^{n-1}$ the group of **coboundaries** (see @@def.cycle@@, @@def.boundary@@); $d^{2} = 0$ gives $B^{n} \subseteq Z^{n}$, so the quotient makes sense.

For a chain complex $K_{n}$, its degree $n$ homology is, on the associated cochain complex $\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}$, the degree $(1-n)$ cohomology.

## Notes · **draft**
Examples: $H^{n}(K[k]) = H^{n+k}(K)$; for $M \in \mathcal{A}$ one has $H^{0}([M]) = M$ and all others $0$; $H^{0}([M \to N]) = \ker f$, $H^{1}([M \to N]) = \operatorname{coker} f$.
