## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This is the most useful property of “convergence in measure”: **convergence itself does not guarantee convergence at any single point, but one can always extract a subsequence converging almost everywhere**.

The tool in the proof is a Borel–Cantelli type argument: build a family of “bad sets” with $\sum_k \mu(E_k) < \infty$; then almost every point lies in only finitely many bad sets.
