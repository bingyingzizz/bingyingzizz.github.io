Statement

Let AAA be a commutative ring with unit, P,M⊂AP, M \subset AP,M⊂A a proper ideal (proper; if P=AP = AP=A were allowed, "A/PA/PA/P is an integral domain" would fail; if M=AM = AM=A were allowed, "maximal" would fail).

(i) PPP is called prime if ab∈P  ⟹  a∈Pab \in P \implies a \in Pab∈P⟹a∈P or b∈Pb \in Pb∈P. Equivalently: A/PA/PA/P is an integral domain.

(ii) MMM is called maximal if there is no ideal JJJ with M⊂J⊂AM \subset J \subset AM⊂J⊂A. Equivalently: A/MA/MA/M is a field.

(iii) Maximal   ⟹  \implies⟹ prime (A/MA/MA/M a field   ⟹  \implies⟹ an integral domain).

Notes

⭐ A prime ideal is the abstraction of "divisibility": in Z\mathbb{Z}Z (p≥2p \ge 2p≥2), (p)(p)(p) is prime   ⟺  \iff⟺ ppp is prime ((0)(0)(0) is also prime, but 000 is not prime); in k[x]k[x]k[x] (kkk a field, f≠0f \ne 0f=0), (f)(f)(f) is prime   ⟺  \iff⟺ fff is irreducible ((0)(0)(0) is also prime, while 000 is irreducible).

⭐ A maximal ideal is "the simplest after quotienting": Z/pZ\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}Z/pZ is a field, Z/(6)\mathbb{Z}/(6)Z/(6) is not (it has zero divisors).

⚠ Maximal   ⟹  \implies⟹ prime, but not conversely: in k[x,y]k[x,y]k[x,y], (x)(x)(x) is prime (k[x,y]/(x)≅k[y]k[x,y]/(x) \cong k[y]k[x,y]/(x)≅k[y] is an integral domain), yet not maximal ((x)⊂(x,y)⊂k[x,y](x) \subset (x, y) \subset k[x,y](x)⊂(x,y)⊂k[x,y]).

In a one-dimensional integral domain, the nonzero prime ideals are all maximal (e.g. Z\mathbb{Z}Z, k[x]k[x]k[x]); (0)(0)(0) is prime but not maximal (Z\mathbb{Z}Z in (0)⊂(2)⊂Z(0) \subset (2) \subset \mathbb{Z}(0)⊂(2)⊂Z).

📌 Existence (every proper ideal lies in a maximal ideal) requires Zorn's lemma; that is an application of Zorn 引理, not part of this entry.
