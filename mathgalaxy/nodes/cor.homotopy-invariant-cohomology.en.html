cor.homotopy-invariant-cohomology

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let MMM be a constant abelian group.

  1. If f≃g:X→Yf \simeq g : X \to Yf≃g:X→Y are homotopic continuous maps, then fff and ggg induce the same map on cohomology;
  2. If f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is a homotopy equivalence, then Hn(Y,M)≅Hn(X,M)H^{n}(Y, M) \cong H^{n}(X, M)Hn(Y,M)≅Hn(X,M).

Notes

Hypotheses used in each direction (homotopy invariance is a substantive theorem; deriving the isomorphism from a homotopy inverse is a formal consequence):

  • That homotopic maps   ⟹  \implies⟹ induce the same map is not an unfolding of the definition; for singular cohomology one uses the prism chain homotopy. To use it for sheaf cohomology, one also needs an applicable homotopy-invariance theorem or a comparison theorem between sheaf and singular cohomology.
  • A safe sufficient setting is a paracompact Hausdorff, locally contractible space with constant abelian coefficients: the comparison theorem transfers the problem to singular cohomology. The unconditional version for arbitrary spaces in the original text was not verified at this step and is recorded as a gap; one cannot assert it from the general Čech model alone.
  • Once homotopy invariance is known, the cohomology isomorphism for a homotopy equivalence   ⟹  \implies⟹ uses only contravariant functoriality and a homotopy inverse; this is a free formal step. Do not reverse the contravariant order of the composite.
  • Constant coefficients cannot be replaced by an arbitrary fixed nonconstant sheaf: on the real line take the skyscraper at a point with nonzero group; the identity and a constant map avoiding that point are homotopic, but their inverse-image sheaves are respectively nonzero and zero, so the comparison under constant coefficients cannot be copied.

⭐ This is the source of the phrase "sheaf cohomology is a topological invariant" — with constant coefficients, Hn(−,M)H^{n}(-, M)Hn(−,M) is homotopy invariant, so it can be used to compute invariants of homotopy classes of spaces (singular cohomology and de Rham cohomology both fall under this statement).
