def.cocontinuous-functor

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let g:C′→Cg : \mathcal{C}' \to \mathcal{C}g:C′→C be a functor between two sites. It is called cocontinuous if the functor g♯g^{\sharp}g♯ (pushing presheaves forward along ggg and then sheafifying) sends sheaves to sheaves.

Notes

What it says: ggg is cocontinuous   ⟺  \iff⟺ after pushing a presheaf forward along ggg and sheafifying (that is, g♯g^{\sharp}g♯) it is still a sheaf.

⭐ Why needed: a morphism of sites requires a pair of functors (f−1⊣f∗f^{-1} \dashv f_{*}f−1⊣f∗​), and the existence of the direct image f∗f_{*}f∗​ relies on exactly this "pushing forward stays in sheaves".

⚠ Different direction and different condition from a continuous functor (@@def.continuous-functor@@: f−1f^{-1}f−1 pulls sheaves back to sheaves) — one handles pullback, the other pushforward, and a morphism of sites needs both; ⛔ do not treat the two as dual to one statement.
