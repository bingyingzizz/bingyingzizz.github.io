- Open map $\implies$ nonconstant follows because a constant map on a nonempty domain has a singleton image. Continuity alone is insufficient. For example, the image of $f(z)=\operatorname{Re}z$ has empty interior in the complex plane.
- Holomorphic injection $\implies$ biholomorphism: The open mapping theorem first gives continuity of the inverse. The local zero-multiplicity argument then gives $f'\ne0$, and the inverse function theorem gives holomorphicity of the inverse. This uses more than the definition of injectivity.
