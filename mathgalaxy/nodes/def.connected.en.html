Statement

A topological space XXX is disconnected if it can be written as the union of two disjoint non-empty open sets, X=U⊔VX = U \sqcup VX=U⊔V; otherwise it is connected.

For a fixed x∈Xx \in Xx∈X, the connected component C(x)C(x)C(x) of xxx is the union of all connected subsets containing xxx — it is itself connected, and it is maximal.

Notes

The connected components form a partition of XXX, and each component is closed. Subsets that are both open and closed (called clopen sets) are the other face of connectedness: XXX is connected iff its only clopen subsets are ∅\emptyset∅ and XXX itself — so "is there a non-trivial clopen set?" is the same question as "can the space be split?".

Four equivalent formulations; the cost lies in "taking complements" and "continuous images":

  • connected   ⟺  \iff⟺ not expressible as the union of two disjoint non-empty open sets: this is the definition.
  • connected   ⟺  \iff⟺ no non-trivial clopen subsets: (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) use V=X∖UV = X \setminus UV=X∖U, which is closed; (⇐)(\Leftarrow)(⇐) one line. This step uses only the duality of open and closed sets.
  • connected   ⟺  \iff⟺ every continuous map X→{0,1}X \to \{0,1\}X→{0,1} (discrete two points) is constant: (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) the image is connected and {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1} is not; (⇐)(\Leftarrow)(⇐) apply the previous item to a characteristic function. This turns connectedness into "there is no surjection onto two points", the standard form in the internal language.
  • the union of a family of connected subsets with a common point is connected (pairwise intersecting suffices, then induct to arbitrarily many; to avoid AC take the subfamily containing the fixed point). Conversely, the union of all connected components is XXX, which of course need not be connected — in the discrete two-point space the union of the two singleton components is XXX, and it is disconnected.

Connected ≠\ne= path-connected: path-connected   ⟹  \implies⟹ connected, but not conversely (the topologist's sine curve). The gap between the two is exactly the gap between "π0\pi_{0}π0​ and path π0\pi_{0}π0​".
