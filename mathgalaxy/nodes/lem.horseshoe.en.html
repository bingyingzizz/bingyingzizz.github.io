Statement

Let 0→M1→ i M→ p M2→00 \to M_{1} \xrightarrow{\ i\ } M \xrightarrow{\ p\ } M_{2} \to 00→M1​ i ​M p ​M2​→0 be a short exact sequence of RRR-modules, with projective resolutions of the two end terms given by

⋯→P1→ d1 P0→ ε M1→0,⋯→Q1→ e1 Q0→ η M2→0.\cdots \to P_{1} \xrightarrow{\ d_{1}\ } P_{0} \xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ } M_{1} \to 0, \qquad \cdots \to Q_{1} \xrightarrow{\ e_{1}\ } Q_{0} \xrightarrow{\ \eta\ } M_{2} \to 0 .⋯→P1​ d1​ ​P0​ ε ​M1​→0,⋯→Q1​ e1​ ​Q0​ η ​M2​→0.

Then there is a projective resolution of MMM, denoted by R∙↠MR_{\bullet} \twoheadrightarrow MR∙​↠M, such that

  1. Rn=Pn⊕QnR_{n} = P_{n} \oplus Q_{n}Rn​=Pn​⊕Qn​ (a termwise direct sum).
  2. The inclusions and projections form a termwise split short exact sequence of complexes 0→P∙→R∙→Q∙→00 \to P_{\bullet} \to R_{\bullet} \to Q_{\bullet} \to 00→P∙​→R∙​→Q∙​→0.
  3. The augmentation R0=P0⊕Q0→MR_{0} = P_{0}\oplus Q_{0} \to MR0​=P0​⊕Q0​→M combines iεi\varepsiloniε with a lift of η\etaη.

The diagram formed by the three resolutions is a double complex. The resolution of MMM runs horizontally, and the two end terms have their respective resolutions running vertically. The diagram is shaped like a horseshoe.

⭐ Practical use. When an explicit resolution of a module MMM is unavailable, place it in the middle of a short exact sequence and resolve the two end terms. This gives a resolution of MMM that can be written explicitly as a termwise direct sum. Koszul complexes, resolutions of tensor products, and double complexes in spectral sequences are all assembled this way.

Dual version. Reverse all the arrows to obtain the horseshoe lemma for injective resolutions. It expresses the same principle as “The Total Complex Is Still an Injective Resolution” (the total complex of the double complex is again a resolution).

⚠️ The three resolutions fit together on an equal footing to form a double complex. The horseshoe lemma thus provides a double complex and two associated spectral sequences. Taking horizontal homology first or vertical homology first gives the two different spectral sequences. See “Double Complexes Give Rise to Spectral Sequences.”
