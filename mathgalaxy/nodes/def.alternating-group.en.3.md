## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The sign is the **only** nontrivial homomorphism from $S_n$ to $\{\pm 1\}$ (for $n \ge 3$), so $A_n$ is, among the subgroups of $S_n$, the **only** one of index $2$ — this uniqueness is the source of "$S_n$ is not simple" later, and of "the quadratic subextension is unique" in Galois theory.

⭐ Why the product formula is clean: in each factor $\frac{\sigma(j)-\sigma(i)}{j-i}$ the denominator is fixed and the numerator just permutes the same differences, so the sign of the product depends only on the parity of the permutation induced by $\sigma$.
