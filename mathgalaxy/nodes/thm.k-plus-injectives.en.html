thm.k-plus-injectives

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

If A\mathcal{A}A has enough injectives, then the inclusion functor

K+(I)→  ∼  D+(A)\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{I}) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} D^{+}(\mathcal{A})K+(I)∼​D+(A)

is a category equivalence. Dually: with enough projectives, K−(P)→  ∼  D−(A)\mathbf{K}^{-}(\mathcal{P}) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} D^{-}(\mathcal{A})K−(P)∼​D−(A).

Notes

Write K+(I)\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{I})K+(I) for the category of bounded-above injective complexes modulo homotopy. The theorem says: to compute things in D+(A)D^{+}(\mathcal{A})D+(A), it suffices to look at injective complexes.

Proof: fully faithful + essentially surjective.

  • Fully faithful: source and target are complexes in C+(I)C^{+}(\mathcal{I})C+(I); "Hom in bounded-above injective complexes is derived Hom" gives directly Hom⁡K(I∙,J∙)=Hom⁡D(I∙,J∙)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(I^{\bullet}, J^{\bullet}) = \operatorname{Hom}_{D}(I^{\bullet}, J^{\bullet})HomK​(I∙,J∙)=HomD​(I∙,J∙), so the inclusion is bijective on Hom.
  • Essentially surjective: given K∙∈C+(A)K^{\bullet} \in C^{+}(\mathcal{A})K∙∈C+(A), "enough injectives" allows lifting term by term, producing an injective resolution K∙↣I∙K^{\bullet} \rightarrowtail I^{\bullet}K∙↣I∙ made of monomorphisms (in the homotopy category one extends the morphism at each step using injectivity, recursing downward).

The resolution is a quasi-isomorphism, hence an isomorphism in D+(A)D^{+}(\mathcal{A})D+(A) — K∙K^{\bullet}K∙ is isomorphic to I∙I^{\bullet}I∙.

⭐ Practical value: this is the source of legitimacy for "computing right derived functors by injective resolutions" — the definition of the right derived functor RFRFRF already requires replacing complexes by injective ones, and this theorem guarantees that the category on that side is the derived category itself, so nothing is lost.
