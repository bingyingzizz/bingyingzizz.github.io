So "$\omega^{k}_{j}(e_{i})$ equals some $\Gamma$" holds literally only when the metric has already been flattened by the coordinates (flat Cartesian coordinates).

**Two ways of writing along a curve**, the most useful comparison:

- coordinate frame: $\dot\gamma = \dot x^{k}\partial_{k}$, $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = \left(\ddot x^{k}+\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\dot x^{i}\dot x^{j}\right)\partial_{k}$;
- orthonormal frame: $\dot\gamma = y^{j}e_{j}$, $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = \left(\dot y^{k}+\omega^{k}_{j}(\dot\gamma)y^{j}\right)e_{k}$.
