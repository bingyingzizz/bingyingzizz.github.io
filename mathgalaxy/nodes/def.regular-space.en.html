Statement

What this is: the T3T_{3}T3​ property together with T1T_{1}T1​. Only after adding T1T_{1}T1​ does it automatically become T2T_{2}T2​ — this is the real purpose of the name "regular".

A topological space XXX is a regular space if

  1. XXX is T3T_{3}T3​: a point and a closed set not containing it can be separated by disjoint open sets (T3 空间);
  2. XXX is T1T_{1}T1​: singletons are closed (T1 空间).

Why T1T_{1}T1​ is indispensable: the indiscrete topology is T3T_{3}T3​ but not T1T_{1}T1​, and certainly not T2T_{2}T2​. After adding T1T_{1}T1​, the set {y}\{y\}{y} is closed, so "xxx and {y}\{y\}{y} can be separated" yields two disjoint open sets — exactly T2T_{2}T2​.

Its place in the chain: regular space   ⟹  T2  ⟹  T1  ⟹  T0\implies T_{2} \implies T_{1} \implies T_{0}⟹T2​⟹T1​⟹T0​ (all reverse implications fail).

Notes

⭐ Convention: the T-number denotes the bare property; only the named notion carries T1T_{1}T1​.

So T3 means a point and a closed set can be separated (without T1T_{1}T1​), while a regular space means T3 plus T1T_{1}T1​.

⚠️ Munkres uses the opposite convention (he takes "regular" without T1T_{1}T1​ and T3T_{3}T3​ with T1T_{1}T1​) — when reading another book, fix the convention first; otherwise a statement such as "T3  ⟹  T2T_{3} \implies T_{2}T3​⟹T2​" is sometimes true and sometimes false.

One-line proof that regular   ⟹  \implies⟹ T2T_{2}T2​: given x≠yx \ne yx=y, T1T_{1}T1​ makes {y}\{y\}{y} closed and x∉{y}x \notin \{y\}x∈/{y}; by T3T_{3}T3​ choose disjoint open sets U∋xU \ni xU∋x and V⊇{y}V \supseteq \{y\}V⊇{y}. This is exactly T2T_{2}T2​.

⚠️ Regular does not imply normal: the Sorgenfrey plane is regular but not normal. So "regular" is not a weaker version of "normal" on the same axis — they separate differently shaped objects (point/closed set vs closed set/closed set).

⭐ Relation to 仿紧 Hausdorff 空间正规: "step 1" of that theorem proves "paracompact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ regular", using precisely the form U‾⊆W\overline{U} \subseteq WU⊆W of T3T_{3}T3​.
