Statement

In C\mathcal{C}C, all limits indexed by III exist   ⟺  \iff⟺ the constant-diagram functor Δ:C→CI\Delta : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}^{I}Δ:C→CI has a right adjoint, given by

D↦lim⁡DD \mapsto \lim DD↦limD

Dually, all colimits indexed by III exist   ⟺  \iff⟺ Δ\DeltaΔ has a left adjoint, given by D↦colim⁡DD \mapsto \operatorname{colim} DD↦colimD.

Notes

The proof is the definition of a limit itself:

Hom⁡CI(ΔX,D)  ≅  Hom⁡C(X,lim⁡D)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{I}}(\Delta X, D) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X, \lim D)HomCI​(ΔX,D)≅HomC​(X,limD)

The left side is the set of cones, the right side the set of morphisms into the limit. Comparing with the definition of an adjunction word by word, both sides are the same sentence.

So “does this category have limits” asks “does Δ\DeltaΔ have an adjoint” — nearly every later construction of an adjunction reduces to constructing some limit.
