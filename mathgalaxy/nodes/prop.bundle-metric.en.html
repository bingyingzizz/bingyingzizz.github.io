Statement

What it does: it builds "an inner product on every fibre" globally — locally such products always exist, and a partition of unity glues them. This is the general version of "every manifold carries a Riemannian metric".

Let π:E→M\pi : E \to Mπ:E→M be a smooth vector bundle of rank kkk. A fibre metric is an inner product on each fibre EpE_{p}Ep​, namely ⟨⋅,⋅⟩p\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{p}⟨⋅,⋅⟩p​, such that for any two smooth sections s,t∈Γ(E)s, t \in \Gamma(E)s,t∈Γ(E) the function

p⟼⟨s(p),t(p)⟩pp \longmapsto \langle s(p), t(p)\rangle_{p}p⟼⟨s(p),t(p)⟩p​

is smooth. Then every smooth vector bundle admits a fibre metric.

Special case: for E=TME = TME=TM a fibre metric is a Riemannian metric (Riemann 度量); for a line bundle EEE it is a "everywhere positive-definite symmetric bilinear form".

Notes

The proof is a partition of unity: take a locally trivializing cover {Uα}\{U_{\alpha}\}{Uα​}, on each UαU_{\alpha}Uα​, identify EEE with Uα×RkU_{\alpha} \times \mathbb{R}^{k}Uα​×Rk, and take the standard inner product ⟨⋅,⋅⟩α\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}⟨⋅,⋅⟩α​;

then take a smooth partition of unity {ρα}\{\rho_{\alpha}\}{ρα​} (光滑单位分解) and set ⟨s,t⟩:=∑αρα⟨s,t⟩α\langle s,t\rangle := \sum_{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha}\langle s,t\rangle_{\alpha}⟨s,t⟩:=∑α​ρα​⟨s,t⟩α​.

This is a convex combination: symmetry and bilinearity are clear;

positive definiteness follows from ρα≥0\rho_{\alpha} \ge 0ρα​≥0 and the sum being identically 111 — at each point this is a weighted average of inner products with non-negative weights summing to 111, so ⟨s,s⟩=0\langle s,s\rangle = 0⟨s,s⟩=0 forces s=0s = 0s=0.

Why metrics face no obstruction while other structures do: positive-definite symmetric forms form a convex set, and convex combinations stay positive definite; complex structures (J2=−id⁡J^{2} = -\operatorname{id}J2=−id) and symplectic structures (ω\omegaω non-degenerate and closed) are not convex conditions, cannot be glued, and have topological obstructions. So "a Riemannian metric on TMTMTM always exists, an almost complex structure need not" is not a technical gap but the difference between convex and non-convex.

Corollary: the structure group reduces to O⁡(n)\operatorname{O}(n)O(n): with a fibre metric, the transition functions of TMTMTM can be pointwise Gram–Schmidt orthogonalized, reducing the structure group from GL⁡(n,R)\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})GL(n,R) to O⁡(n)\operatorname{O}(n)O(n). This is the technical prerequisite for doing Riemannian geometry on an arbitrary manifold.

Dual and tensor bundles work the same way: the metrics on E∗E^{\ast}E∗, ⋀kE\bigwedge^{k}E⋀kE and E⊗FE \otimes FE⊗F are induced pointwise from those on E,FE, FE,F (the inner product of a tensor product). Hence "every tensor bundle carries a fibre metric" is a corollary.

A fibre metric is not a Riemannian metric on EEE: EEE itself is a manifold and can carry a Riemannian metric, but that measures both fibre and base directions, whereas here only the fibre direction is measured. For TMTMTM the Riemannian metric happens to serve both roles, which is the easiest place to confuse them.
