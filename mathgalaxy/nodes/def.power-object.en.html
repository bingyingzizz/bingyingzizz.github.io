Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C have finite products and X,Y∈CX, Y \in \mathcal{C}X,Y∈C. If there are an object YXY^{X}YX and a morphism ev⁡:YX×X→Y\operatorname{ev} : Y^{X} \times X \to Yev:YX×X→Y such that for every ZZZ and every f:Z×X→Yf : Z \times X \to Yf:Z×X→Y there is a unique f~:Z→YX\tilde f : Z \to Y^{X}f~​:Z→YX with

ev⁡∘(f~×id⁡X)=f,\operatorname{ev} \circ (\tilde f \times \operatorname{id}_{X}) = f,ev∘(f~​×idX​)=f,

then YXY^{X}YX is called the power object (exponential object) of YYY with respect to XXX, and ev⁡\operatorname{ev}ev is called the evaluation map.

Notes

Equivalent description (more useful): YXY^{X}YX is the right adjoint of the functor −×X:C→C-\times X : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}−×X:C→C, that is,

Hom⁡(Z×X, Y)  ≅  Hom⁡(Z, YX).\operatorname{Hom}(Z \times X,\, Y) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}(Z,\, Y^{X}).Hom(Z×X,Y)≅Hom(Z,YX).

So "has power objects" = "the product functor has a right adjoint".

⭐ Why it is the second half of "cartesian closed": cartesian closed = finite products + power objects. @@prop.cg-cartesian-closed@@ here says exactly that "the compactly generated side has power objects, only the compact-open topology has to be kkk-ified once more".

⚠ Do not confuse it with "internal Hom": when C\mathcal{C}C is a general category, YXY^{X}YX is the object that really exists in C\mathcal{C}C; the internal Hom (@@def.internal-hom@@, the one in a topos) is built by first taking Hom sets and then using colimits or sheafification. In a cartesian closed category the two agree; in a general topos only the internal Hom exists.

📌 What it looks like in Set\mathbf{Set}Set: YXY^{X}YX is just the function set Hom⁡Set(X,Y)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Set}}(X,Y)HomSet​(X,Y), with ev⁡(f,x)=f(x)\operatorname{ev}(f, x) = f(x)ev(f,x)=f(x) — the power object is the objectification of "collecting functions into a set".
