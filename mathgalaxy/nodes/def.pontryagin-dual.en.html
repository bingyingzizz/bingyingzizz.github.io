Statement

Write the circle group as

T:={ z∈C:∣z∣=1 }.\mathbb{T} := \{\, z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1 \,\}.T:={z∈C:∣z∣=1}.

The Pontryagin dual of a topological abelian group MMM is

M^:=CZ(M,T),\widehat{M} := C_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{T}),M:=CZ​(M,T),

that is, the group of all continuous homomorphisms from MMM to T\mathbb{T}T, equipped with the compact-open topology.

Notes

Why T\mathbb{T}T: It is a compact T1T_{1}T1​ group and the simplest group that can accommodate all characters. The dual group is therefore automatically compact (when MMM is discrete). This is how duality reverses size.

Two basic examples (both can be computed directly): Z^≅T\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \mathbb{T}Z≅T, T^≅Z\widehat{\mathbb{T}} \cong \mathbb{Z}T≅Z. Duality exchanges the discrete group of integers and the compact circle group.

The third example R^≅R\widehat{\mathbb{R}} \cong \mathbb{R}R≅R comes from the Fourier transform: every continuous character of y↦e2πixyy \mapsto e^{2\pi i xy}y↦e2πixy has this form.

⭐ Two independent propositions describe what duality exchanges:

  • The exchange between discrete and compact Hausdorff groups concerns topological properties;
  • The correspondences between torsion and Stonean spaces, and between torsion-freeness and connectedness, concern algebraic properties.

Together these two dictionaries give the full picture of how topology encodes algebra. They also motivate the condensed mathematics approach of using compact Hausdorff spaces to probe other objects.
