# classification of discontinuities　`prop.discontinuity-types`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.discontinuity-types)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.discontinuity-types.md)

⭐ The typical second-kind example: $\sin(1/x)$ at $0$ (oscillation, the limit does not exist).

**Proof** (the classification is exhaustive).

At a discontinuity $x_0$, each one-sided limit has only two states: exists and is finite, or does not exist / is infinite.

Thus “both one-sided limits are finite” and “at least one fails” are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, and the former splits according to whether the two limits are equal into removable and jump — the three cases are mutually exclusive and all possibilities are listed.

∎
