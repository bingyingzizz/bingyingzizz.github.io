## Statement · **draft**
Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $K$. Then $U \subseteq V$ is called a **(linear) subspace** of $V$ if and only if

- $0 \in U$;
- $U$ is closed under addition: $u, v \in U \implies u + v \in U$;
- $U$ is closed under scalar multiplication: $u \in U$, $c \in K \implies cu \in U$.

Equivalently, $U$ together with the operations restricted from $V$ is itself a $K$-vector space.
