def.equinumerous · 定义 · 基数

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Sets AAA and BBB are equinumerous (written #A=#B\#A = \#B#A=#B or A≈BA \approx BA≈B) iff there is a bijection

f:A→Bf : A \to Bf:A→B

Equinumerosity is an equivalence relation on sets: reflexive (identity map), symmetric (inverse map), transitive (composition).

Notes

This is the right way to compare sizes: instead of counting (an infinite set cannot be counted out), ask whether the two sets can be paired off one-to-one.

Classical results:

  • N\mathbb{N}N is equinumerous with Q\mathbb{Q}Q and with N×N\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}N×N (both are countable);
  • (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1) is equinumerous with R\mathbb{R}R (via something like x↦tan⁡π(x−1/2)x \mapsto \tan \pi(x - 1/2)x↦tanπ(x−1/2));
  • N\mathbb{N}N and R\mathbb{R}R are not equinumerous (Cantor's theorem).

A⊆BA \subseteq BA⊆B has nothing to do with equinumerosity: N⊂Q\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Q}N⊂Q yet the two are equinumerous — this is precisely the dividing line between infinite and finite sets (Dedekind's characterization: equinumerous with a proper subset   ⟺  \iff⟺ infinite).
