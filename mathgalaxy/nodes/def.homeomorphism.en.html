Statement

Let XXX and YYY be topological spaces. f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is a homeomorphism if and only if

  • fff is a bijection;
  • both fff and f−1f^{-1}f−1 are continuous.

In that case we write X≅YX \cong YX≅Y and call the two spaces homeomorphic. Homeomorphism is an equivalence relation between topological spaces (identity, inverse and composition all preserve it).

Notes

⭐ A homeomorphism means "topologically indistinguishable": every topological property (compactness, connectedness, Hausdorff, closure) is preserved.

⚠ The converse also deserves care: metric properties change under a homeomorphism.

R\mathbb{R}R is homeomorphic to the open interval (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1) (x↦12+1πarctan⁡xx \mapsto \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan xx↦21​+π1​arctanx), yet the former is complete and the latter is not — the classic example that completeness is not a topological property, and the dividing line between the "compact / closed" and the "complete / totally bounded" families of properties in metric spaces.

Three equivalent formulations, differing in which side (open/closed) is used:

  • definition (bijection + fff, f−1f^{-1}f−1 continuous)   ⟺  \iff⟺ fff is a continuous open bijection   ⟺  \iff⟺ fff is a continuous closed bijection: (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) continuity of f−1f^{-1}f−1 makes fff open; (⇐)(\Leftarrow)(⇐) openness of fff makes f−1f^{-1}f−1 continuous. Both steps use only the duality between open and closed sets; no extra hypothesis is needed.
  • a continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism: extra hypotheses are required, the two most common being "compact →\to→ Hausdorff" (arguments of the 紧子集是闭的 type) and the open mapping theorem (开映射定理（Banach 版）, a continuous linear bijection between Banach spaces). ⚠️ So the incorrect slogan "homeomorphism = continuous bijection" is true only in the compact Hausdorff and Banach settings.

⚠️ A homeomorphism is an isomorphism in the category of topological spaces: it preserves every topological property (connectedness, compactness, countability, separation), which is exactly its meaning as an "equivalence relation".
