## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Thus **an inner product space is automatically a metric space**, so convergence, completeness and compactness make sense — the whole toolbox of analysis becomes available.

A complete inner product space is called a **Hilbert space**.

⭐ Conversely: a norm need not come from an inner product.

The criterion is the **parallelogram law**

$$\|u + v\|^{2} + \|u - v\|^{2} = 2\|u\|^{2} + 2\|v\|^{2}$$

($\ell^{p}$ fails it when $p \ne 2$, so the norm on $\ell^{p}$ does not come from any inner product — this is also why $L^{2}$ is special.)
