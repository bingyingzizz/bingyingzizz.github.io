prop.slice-topos

Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be a site and T∈C^T \in \widehat{\mathcal{C}}T∈C. Then

  1. jTj_{T}jT​ is cocontinuous, hence gives a topos morphism jT:C/T~→C~j_{T} : \widetilde{\mathcal{C}/T} \to \widetilde{\mathcal{C}}jT​:C/T​→C;
  2. C/T~  ≃  C~/T~\widetilde{\mathcal{C}/T} \;\simeq\; \widetilde{\mathcal{C}}/\widetilde{T}C/T​≃C/T;
  3. under this equivalence, jT!j_{T!}jT!​ corresponds to the forgetful functor C~/T~→C~\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}/\widetilde{T} \to \widetilde{\mathcal{C}}C/T→C, and jT−1j_{T}^{-1}jT−1​ to F↦(F×T~→T~)\mathcal{F} \mapsto (\mathcal{F} \times \widetilde{T} \to \widetilde{T})F↦(F×T→T).

Notes

Skeleton. The slice equivalence and the adjunction correspondence only give the route; the mutually inverse natural isomorphisms and the lifting of covers are not expanded item by item.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the slice equivalence depends on the induced topology and sheafification):

  • The map from slice sheaves to sheaves on the base: sheafify the data on the category of elements accordingly, obtaining a sheaf over T~\widetilde TT; when TTT is merely a presheaf, this step cannot be described as objects being literally the same.
  • Conversely obtain slice sheaves from F→T~F\to\widetilde TF→T: pull back along sections; the slice uses the covers induced by the base, and only then is the sheaf condition compatible; the inverse property uses descent for sheaves and sheafification.
  • Once the equivalence is established, the adjunction between the forgetful functor and pullback along the product needs only universal properties and is a free formal check; the base topology need not be subcanonical.
  • The lifting of covers along the projection can be checked directly for the induced topology, but "sending covers to covers" must not be conflated with the general definition of cocontinuity; away from the induced topology this equivalence gives no such guarantee.
