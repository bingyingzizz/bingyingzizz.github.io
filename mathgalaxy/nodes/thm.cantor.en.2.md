# diagonal argument　`thm.cantor`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.cantor)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.cantor.md)

Taking $A = \mathbb{N}$ gives "$\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable" ($\#\mathbb{R} = 2^{\aleph_0}$).

The proof uses the **diagonal method** (see the proof on the edge): the "diagonal set" $D = \{ a \in A : a \notin f(a) \}$ is not in the image of $f$.

This is the most theorem-producing construction in the history of mathematics; Cantor–Bernstein, Gödel's incompleteness theorem and the halting problem all use it.
