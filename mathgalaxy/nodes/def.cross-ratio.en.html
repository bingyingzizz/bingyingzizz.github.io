Notes

Why it is invariant: a fractional linear map T(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d)T(z) = (az+b)/(cz+d)T(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d) can be written as a composite of four-point cross-ratios. A direct check: each difference Tzi−Tzj=(ad−bc)(zi−zj)(czi+d)(czj+d)Tz_{i} - Tz_{j} = \dfrac{(ad - bc)(z_{i} - z_{j})}{(cz_{i} + d)(cz_{j} + d)}Tzi​−Tzj​=(czi​+d)(czj​+d)(ad−bc)(zi​−zj​)​; substituting into the numerator and denominator of the cross-ratio, (ad−bc)(ad-bc)(ad−bc) cancels pairwise with the factors (czk+d)(cz_{k}+d)(czk​+d), leaving the original cross-ratio.

⭐ It is the coordinate of "four-point geometry": a triangle is described by side lengths and angles, while four points need one complex quantity — the cross-ratio being real or taking a special value corresponds to the two classical facts "concyclic" and "harmonic division".

⚠️ The value depends on the order of the points: swapping two positions gives 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ, 1−λ1-\lambda1−λ, and four more values — six ways of writing the same "non-harmonic ratio". Fix the order before writing a formula.
