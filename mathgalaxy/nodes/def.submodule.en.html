def.submodule · 定义 · 代数 · 模 · 子模

Translated for an earlier content version and not updated since. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let AAA be a ring with identity and MMM an AAA-module. If an additive subgroup N⊆MN \subseteq MN⊆M satisfies

a⋅n∈Nfor all a∈A, n∈N,a \cdot n \in N \qquad \text{for all } a \in A,\ n \in N,a⋅n∈Nfor all a∈A, n∈N,

then NNN is a submodule of MMM, namely an AAA-submodule. Equivalently, NNN is itself an AAA-module under the restricted action, and the inclusion N↪MN \hookrightarrow MN↪M is a module homomorphism.

Kernels and images of module homomorphisms are submodules; a left ideal of AAA is exactly a submodule of the left AAA-module AAA.

Notes

⭐ Three-level comparison: subgroup (group) ⊂ submodule (module) ⊂ subspace (module over a field). A submodule is required to be closed under the ring action, one condition more than "additive subgroup".

⭐ Examples: for A=ZA = \mathbb{Z}A=Z, submodules are the subgroups nZn\mathbb{Z}nZ; for A=K[x]A = K[x]A=K[x], submodules are the K[x]K[x]K[x]-subspaces.

⭐ Submodule lattice: any intersection is again a submodule, but a union in general is not; the submodule generated by SSS is the smallest submodule containing SSS. Quotient modules are built as M/NM/NM/N (see 商模, a later node).
