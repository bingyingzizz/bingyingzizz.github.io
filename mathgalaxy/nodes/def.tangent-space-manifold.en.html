Statement

What this does: it defines "all directions at a point" on a manifold. There is no ambient space to use, so directions are captured by "curves through the point": two curves point in the same direction if they have the same velocity in some chart.

Let MMM be an nnn-dimensional smooth manifold and p∈Mp\in Mp∈M.

Two curves γ1,γ2:(−ε,ε)→M\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2} : (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to Mγ1​,γ2​:(−ε,ε)→M through ppp (γi(0)=p\gamma_{i}(0) = pγi​(0)=p) are called tangent at ppp if there exists (equivalently: for every) chart (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ) containing ppp with

(φ∘γ1)′(0)=(φ∘γ2)′(0).(\varphi\circ\gamma_{1})'(0) = (\varphi\circ\gamma_{2})'(0) .(φ∘γ1​)′(0)=(φ∘γ2​)′(0).

"Exists one chart" and "for every chart" are equivalent, because changing charts only introduces a chain-rule factor. Such an equivalence class is a tangent vector at ppp, and the set of all of them is TpMT_{p}MTp​M.

In a chart (U,φ)(U,\varphi)(U,φ), write xi:=φix^{i} := \varphi^{i}xi:=φi, ∂i:=∂/∂xi\partial_{i} := \partial/\partial x^{i}∂i​:=∂/∂xi.

Take the class of the curve t↦φ−1(φ(p)+t ei)t\mapsto\varphi^{-1}\bigl(\varphi(p)+t\,e_{i}\bigr)t↦φ−1(φ(p)+tei​), denoted ∂i(p)\partial_{i}(p)∂i​(p).

These nnn tangent vectors are pairwise distinct and linearly independent, forming a basis of TpMT_{p}MTp​M, hence dim⁡TpM=n\dim T_{p}M = ndimTp​M=n.

Derivation characterization (equivalent definition): TpMT_{p}MTp​M is in bijection with the linear functionals D:C∞(M)→RD : C^{\infty}(M)\to\mathbb{R}D:C∞(M)→R satisfying the Leibniz rule, i.e. D(fg)=f(p) Dg+g(p) DfD(fg) = f(p)\,Dg+g(p)\,DfD(fg)=f(p)Dg+g(p)Df, the correspondence being v=[γ]↦(f↦(f∘γ)′(0))v = [\gamma]\mapsto\bigl(f\mapsto (f\circ\gamma)'(0)\bigr)v=[γ]↦(f↦(f∘γ)′(0)).

The tangent bundle TM:=⨆p∈MTpMTM := \bigsqcup_{p\in M}T_{p}MTM:=⨆p∈M​Tp​M also carries a smooth manifold structure (dimension 2n2n2n).

Notes

Why difference quotients do not work: in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, a tangent vector is the limit of (γ(t)−γ(0))/t(\gamma(t)-\gamma(0))/t(γ(t)−γ(0))/t, which needs subtraction; on a manifold there is no subtraction, only "coordinates + chain rule", so the definition must be written in the transformation-compatible form "same velocity in some chart".

Why the two definitions are equivalent: curve classes give directional derivatives (Leibniz follows from the product rule); conversely, given a derivation DDD, apply it to an expansion and use Leibniz to kill terms of order at least two, obtaining D=∑iD(xi) ∂i(p)D = \sum_{i}D(x^{i})\,\partial_{i}(p)D=∑i​D(xi)∂i​(p) — exactly the directional derivative of some curve.

Coordinate change: ∂/∂xi=∑j∂yj∂xi ∂/∂yj\partial/\partial x^{i} = \sum_{j}\frac{\partial y^{j}}{\partial x^{i}}\,\partial/\partial y^{j}∂/∂xi=∑j​∂xi∂yj​∂/∂yj, with the Jacobian as coefficients.

So tangent vectors are contravariant (they move opposite to the coordinates), while cotangent vectors such as df\mathrm{d}fdf are covariant.

⚠ Notation trap: ∂i(p)\partial_{i}(p)∂i​(p) is a vector, while dxi(p)\mathrm{d}x^{i}(p)dxi(p) is its dual covector (dxi(∂j)=δ ji\mathrm{d}x^{i}(\partial_{j}) = \delta^{i}_{\ j}dxi(∂j​)=δ ji​).

On a surface these two are the basis of the tangent plane and the dual coframe (活动标架).

Agreement with the tangent plane of a surface: for S⊆R3S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}S⊆R3, the space TpST_{p}STp​S (spanned by ru,rvr_{u},r_{v}ru​,rv​) is the same space as the abstract definition — take the chart φ=r−1\varphi = r^{-1}φ=r−1 and compare; the velocity of a curve is exactly the velocity in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3.
