thm.freyd-mitchell · Freyd · Mitchell

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

If C\mathcal{C}C is a small abelian category, then there exist a ring AAA and a fully faithful exact functor

C⟶A-Mod.\mathcal{C} \longrightarrow A\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}.C⟶A-Mod.

Notes

⭐ Its meaning: why chasing elements is legitimate. A statement in a small abelian category can first be embedded along a fully faithful exact functor into A-ModA\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}A-Mod, proved there (where elements make sense), and carried back. "Chasing elements in a concrete category" is therefore a legitimate method of proof.

⚠️ But it is not the usual tool for proving the snake lemma or the five lemma — those are big theorems whose standard proofs first build exactness tools inside an abelian category; using this to prove them is overkill. Those two have elementary universal-property / generalized-element proofs (see their nodes).

Why smallness is required: the proof has to make the objects of C\mathcal{C}C into modules, using precisely the colimit construction over "the set of all hom⁡\homhom"; if the category is not small these "sets" do not exist and the construction fails.

⚠️ Dual version: the same argument gives a version embedding into Mod-A\mathbf{Mod}\text{-}AMod-A (right modules); AAA is not unique and the construction depends on the chosen generator.

Compared with Giraud's theorem: Giraud describes a topos as "an exact reflective subcategory of a presheaf category", Freyd–Mitchell describes a small abelian category as "a fully faithful exact subcategory of a module category" — the two theorems are the same idea ("find a concrete large category to house oneself in") for different structures.
