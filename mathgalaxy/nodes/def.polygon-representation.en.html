Statement

Let n≥2n\ge 2n≥2, and let PPP be a regular polygon in the plane with 2n2n2n sides, whose boundary edges are paired by a fixed-point-free involution τ\tauτ. Glue each pair of edges according to x∼τ(x)x\sim\tau(x)x∼τ(x). The quotient space S=P/∼S=P/{\sim}S=P/∼ is called a polygonal representation. If every pair of edges is glued with opposite orientations, then SSS is orientable. If some pair is glued with the same orientation, then SSS is nonorientable.

Notes

Standard forms: A closed orientable surface has a polygonal representation with 4g4g4g sides, a1b1a1−1b1−1⋯agbgag−1bg−1a_{1}b_{1}a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\cdots a_{g}b_{g}a_{g}^{-1}b_{g}^{-1}a1​b1​a1−1​b1−1​⋯ag​bg​ag−1​bg−1​. A closed nonorientable surface has a polygonal representation with 2k2k2k sides, c1c1⋯ckckc_{1}c_{1}\cdots c_{k}c_{k}c1​c1​⋯ck​ck​.

Examples: The torus is represented by a polygon with 444 sides, aba−1b−1aba^{-1}b^{-1}aba−1b−1. The projective plane is represented by a polygon with 222 sides, aaaaaa. The Klein bottle is represented by a polygon with 444 sides, abab−1abab^{-1}abab−1.

Why use it: A polygonal representation encodes a surface by finite combinatorial data, namely the edge pairings. These data are the input to the classification theorem. Gluing the edges is the inverse step that reconstructs the surface.

⚠ Gluing need not produce a manifold. If a vertex link is not a circle, the quotient may be a pseudomanifold (for example, when the two endpoints of the 222-gon aaaaaa are identified, this needs a separate check). The definition of a polygonal representation gives the quotient space. Whether it is a surface requires verification.
