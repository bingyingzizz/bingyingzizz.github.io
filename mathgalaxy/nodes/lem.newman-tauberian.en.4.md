# Newman's Tauberian lemma　`lem.newman-tauberian`

[in graph](../#n=lem.newman-tauberian)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./lem.newman-tauberian.md)

**Remark 2 (strength of the hypotheses and Tauberian phenomena)**: the proof only requires $g$ to be holomorphic on a neighbourhood of **every** closed half-disc $\{|z|\le R,\ \operatorname{Re}z\ge0\}$; for the $g$ of the prime number theorem this follows from the non-vanishing of $\zeta$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ together with compactness. If only "$g$ holomorphic on $\operatorname{Re}z>0$" is assumed, the conclusion generally fails: $f(t)=\sin t$ is bounded, $g(z)=1/(z^{2}+1)$ has poles on the imaginary axis, and $\int_{0}^{T}\sin t\,\mathrm{d}t$ does not converge.
