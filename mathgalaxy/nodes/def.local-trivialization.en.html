Statement

**What it does**: this is the whole reason a bundle is called a bundle — locally it can always be flattened into a product. The clause "every point has a neighbourhood such that…" in the definitions of fiber bundles, vector bundles, principal bundles and covering spaces is exactly this statement.

Let π:E→B\pi : E\to Bπ:E→B be a continuous surjection, FFF a topological space and U⊆BU\subseteq BU⊆B an open set. A continuous map

Φ:π−1(U)⟶U×F\Phi : \pi^{-1}(U)\longrightarrow U\times FΦ:π−1(U)⟶U×F

is called a **local trivialization** of π\piπ over UUU if Φ\PhiΦ is a homeomorphism and commutes with the projection:

pr⁡1∘Φ=π.\operatorname{pr}_{1}\circ\Phi = \pi .pr1​∘Φ=π.

Thus Φ\PhiΦ flattens the piece of the bundle over UUU into the trivial product** U×FU\times FU×F: each fiber Eb:=π−1(b)E_{b} := \pi^{-1}(b)Eb​:=π−1(b) is sent to {b}×F\{b\}\times F{b}×F, and is therefore homeomorphic to FFF. If every point of BBB has such an open neighbourhood with a local trivialization, π\piπ is called locally trivial**.

Smooth case**: when E,B,FE, B, FE,B,F are smooth manifolds and π\piπ is smooth, one replaces "homeomorphism" by "diffeomorphism**" in the definition; that is the local trivialization of a smooth bundle — without it even the tangent bundle would not be a smooth bundle.
