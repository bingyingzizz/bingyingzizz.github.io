Statement

A topological space XXX is a Hausdorff space (or a T2T_{2}T2​ space) if any two distinct points can be separated by open sets:

∀x≠y∈X, ∃U∋x, V∋y open:U∩V=∅\forall x \ne y \in X,\ \exists U \ni x,\ V \ni y\ \text{open}:\quad U \cap V = \emptyset∀x=y∈X, ∃U∋x, V∋y open:U∩V=∅

Notes

In one phrase: limits are unique. A sequence that converges can converge to only one point — this is the kind of space analysis tacitly assumes.

A stronger separation property is normal (T4T_{4}T4​): any two disjoint closed sets can be separated by open sets. Every compact Hausdorff space is normal (the proof uses compactness twice, building open covers and passing to finite subcovers).

T3T_{3}T3​ and T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ sit in between: T3T_{3}T3​ says "a point and a closed set not containing it can be separated by open sets", and T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​ upgrades this to separation by continuous functions (the statement that "there are enough continuous functions").

The injectivity of the Stone–Čech compactification requires exactly T3.5T_{3.5}T3.5​.

Convention. The TTT-number names the bare property; the adjective adds T1T_{1}T1​: "regular" = T3+T1T_{3} + T_{1}T3​+T1​ (正则空间), "completely regular" = T3.5+T1T_{3.5} + T_{1}T3.5​+T1​ (完全正则空间).

Bare T3T_{3}T3​ does not imply T1T_{1}T1​.
