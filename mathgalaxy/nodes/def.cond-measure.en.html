Statement

In plain words: the continuous functions on SSS with values in Z\mathbb{Z}Z form a group under pointwise addition, denoted C(S,Z)C(S,\mathbb{Z})C(S,Z); an integer-valued measure is "something that can take weighted sums (that is, 'integrals') of these functions", i.e. a group homomorphism from C(S,Z)C(S,\mathbb{Z})C(S,Z) to Z\mathbb{Z}Z.

Let SSS be a profinite set (compact Hausdorff totally disconnected space), and denote the set of all Z\mathbb{Z}Z-linear homomorphisms on C(S,Z)C(S, \mathbb{Z})C(S,Z) by

M(S,Z):=Hom⁡Z(C(S,Z), Z).M(S, \mathbb{Z}) := \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(C(S, \mathbb{Z}),\ \mathbb{Z}\bigr).M(S,Z):=HomZ​(C(S,Z), Z).

More generally, for any abelian group AAA write

M(S,A):=Hom⁡Z(C(S,Z), A),M(S, A) := \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(C(S, \mathbb{Z}),\ A\bigr),M(S,A):=HomZ​(C(S,Z), A),

in particular M(S,R)M(S, \mathbb{R})M(S,R) and M(S,R/Z)M(S, \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})M(S,R/Z) (R\mathbb{R}R-valued and R/Z\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}R/Z-valued measures).

Notes

⭐ Where the name comes from: the elements of C(S,Z)C(S,\mathbb{Z})C(S,Z) are "integer-valued continuous functions on SSS"; applying a measure μ\muμ gives ∫f dμ\int f\,d\mu∫fdμ.

When SSS is finite, M(S,Z)=ZSM(S,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}^{S}M(S,Z)=ZS, i.e. "put one integer at each point";

when SSS is an infinite profinite set, M(S,Z)M(S,\mathbb{Z})M(S,Z) is a formal infinite sum, in general much larger than Z[S]\mathbb{Z}[S]Z[S].

⭐ The key step is 连续函数群是自由的: C(S,Z)C(S,\mathbb{Z})C(S,Z) is a free abelian group, so M(S,Z)≅∏IZM(S,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \prod_I \mathbb{Z}M(S,Z)≅∏I​Z is a large product beyond countable products — exactly the completion wanted in solid theory (not a topological "completion of Cauchy sequences", see 固态阿贝尔群).

⚠ Take C(S,Z)C(S,\mathbb{Z})C(S,Z) with the discrete topology: SSS is compact and Z\mathbb{Z}Z is discrete, so the image of a continuous function is finite; hence as an abstract group it is free (Nöbeling's theorem), but in general not finitely generated.
