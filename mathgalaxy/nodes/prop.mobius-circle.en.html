Statement

A Möbius transformation sends circles and lines (collectively "circles") in C^\widehat{\mathbb{C}}C to circles or lines. Equivalently: the image of R∪{∞}\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}R∪{∞} is always a circle or a line.

Corollary: between any two disks (or half-planes) there is a Möbius transformation carrying one to the other; in particular every disk is conformally equivalent to the upper half-plane and to the unit disk.

Notes

Proof: by 交比为实 ⟺ 四点共圆, four points are concyclic   ⟺  \iff⟺ the cross-ratio is real. The cross-ratio is invariant under Möbius transformations, so "concyclic" is preserved — hence the image of a circle (or line) stays in the same class.

More concretely: TTT is a composite of three translations/rotations/dilations and z↦1/zz \mapsto 1/zz↦1/z; the first three obviously preserve circles, and 1/z1/z1/z sends the circle ∣z−a∣=r|z - a| = r∣z−a∣=r to a circle (compute that w=1/zw = 1/zw=1/z satisfies ∣1/w−a∣=r|1/w - a| = r∣1/w−a∣=r, which rearranges to an equation of the form ∣w−aˉ/σ∣2=r2/σ2|w - \bar a/\sigma|^{2} = r^{2}/\sigma^{2}∣w−aˉ/σ∣2=r2/σ2, still a circle).

∎

⭐ "A line counts as a circle" is not wordplay: on the extended plane a line is a circle through ∞\infty∞, and after unification the theorem is a single sentence — half the code and half the proof.

⚠️ Circles are preserved but not their interiors: whether a disk maps to a disk or to its exterior depends on orientation. A real-coefficient Möbius transformation (real coefficients, ad−bcad - bcad−bc ≠ 0) sends the upper half-plane to the upper or lower half-plane; to decide the direction, look at the image of one test point.
