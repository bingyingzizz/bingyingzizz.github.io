Abstract

Let (P,⪯)(P, \preceq)(P,⪯) be a non-empty poset in which every chain has an upper bound in PPP. Then PPP has a maximal element. Both hypotheses are needed: the empty poset has none, and an upper bound for every chain yields maximality only, never a greatest element. It is the standard engine of existence proofs — bases, maximal ideals, compactness, Hahn–Banach — and it is non-constructive: an element is produced without telling you which one.

Statement

Let (P,⪯)(P, \preceq )(P,⪯) be a non-empty poset. If every chain in PPP has an upper bound in PPP, then PPP has a maximal element.

Notes

Neither hypothesis can be dropped: the empty poset has no maximal element, and "every chain has an upper bound" does not give a greatest element — only a maximal one.

It is "the universal engine of existence proofs": every vector space has a basis, every ring has a maximal ideal, the compactness theorem, Hahn–Banach — all of them rely on it.

The name is a historical accident: Zorn popularised the statement in the 1940s, while Kuratowski had already proved an equivalent form in 1922, so it is also called the Kuratowski–Zorn lemma.

⚠ Non-constructive: what one obtains is "there exists a maximal element", and usually one cannot say which.
