⭐ The key step is `thm.csz-free`: $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ is a free abelian group, so $M(S,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \prod_I \mathbb{Z}$ is a large product **beyond countable products** — exactly the completion wanted in solid theory (not a topological "completion of Cauchy sequences", see `def.solid-group`).

⚠ Take $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ with the **discrete** topology: $S$ is compact and $\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete, so the image of a continuous function is finite; hence as an abstract group it is free (Nöbeling's theorem), but in general not finitely generated.
