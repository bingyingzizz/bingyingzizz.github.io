Statement

(i) On Solid\mathbf{Solid}Solid there is a unique symmetric monoidal tensor product ⊗■\otimes^{\blacksquare}⊗■ making the solidification functor M↦M■M \mapsto M^{\blacksquare}M↦M■ symmetric monoidal. Explicitly,

M⊗■N:=(M⊗N)■.M \otimes^{\blacksquare} N := (M \otimes N)^{\blacksquare}.M⊗■N:=(M⊗N)■.

(ii) On D(Solid)D(\mathbf{Solid})D(Solid) there is a unique symmetric monoidal structure ⊗L■\otimes^{L\blacksquare}⊗L■ making C↦CL■C \mapsto C^{L\blacksquare}C↦CL■ symmetric monoidal. It is the left-derived functor of ⊗■\otimes^{\blacksquare}⊗■.

Notes

⭐ This is the object the whole theory aims to construct: a tensor product compatible with completion.

For comparison, the classical Zp⊗^R\mathbb{Z}_p \hat\otimes \mathbb{R}Zp​⊗^​R is not well-defined, whereas Zp⊗L■R=0\mathbb{Z}_p \otimes^{L\blacksquare} \mathbb{R} = 0Zp​⊗L■R=0 (see computations with solid tensor products).

⚠ Uniqueness follows because colimit-preserving functors are determined by their values on free objects.

For existence, verify that (M⊗N)■→(M■⊗N■)■(M \otimes N)^{\blacksquare} \to (M^{\blacksquare} \otimes N^{\blacksquare})^{\blacksquare}(M⊗N)■→(M■⊗N■)■ is an isomorphism by reducing to M=Z[S]M = \mathbb{Z}[S]M=Z[S] and N=Z[T]N = \mathbb{Z}[T]N=Z[T] and using a computation of the kind in part (iv) of compact projective objects in solid modules.
