Hypotheses used in each direction (existence has a completeness cost; the variational characterization is a local algebraic argument):

  • Nearest point   ⟹  \implies⟹ variational inequality: first require y∈Cy\in Cy∈C, use convexity to guarantee y+t(z−y)∈Cy+t(z-y)\in Cy+t(z−y)∈C, expand the square and let t→0t\to0t→0; this is not a definition unfolding, and needs no completeness.
  • Variational inequality   ⟹  \implies⟹ nearest point: still requires y∈Cy\in Cy∈C, and is just a square expansion; this direction does not even need convexity, closedness, or completeness.
  • Existence and uniqueness for all xxx: use nonemptiness, convexity, the parallelogram identity, completeness, and closedness; a nonconvex circle has non-unique nearest points to its centre, and an open interval does not attain the distance to an exterior point.
  • Nonexpansiveness follows from the variational inequality twice and Cauchy–Schwarz; idempotence follows directly from zero distance and uniqueness, and the four items are not mutually equivalent.
