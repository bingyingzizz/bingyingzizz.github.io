Statement

A real sequence {xn}\{x_n\}{xn​} is a Cauchy sequence iff

∀ε>0, ∃N, ∀m,n≥N: ∣xm−xn∣<ε\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ \exists N,\ \forall m, n \ge N :\ |x_m - x_n| < \varepsilon∀ε>0, ∃N, ∀m,n≥N: ∣xm​−xn​∣<ε

That is: once the index is far enough out, any two terms are close to each other.

Notes

⭐ The difference from convergence is the direction of information: to check convergence one needs xxx first in order to compute ∣xn−x∣|x_n - x|∣xn​−x∣, whereas a Cauchy sequence lets the terms compare among themselves — someone who does not know the limit can still verify it.

⭐ In R\mathbb{R}R the two are equivalent (the Cauchy criterion);

in Q\mathbb{Q}Q they are not (1,1.4,1.41,…1, 1.4, 1.41, \ldots1,1.4,1.41,… is Cauchy but has no rational limit).

R\mathbb{R}R is constructed precisely to fill this gap.

⚠ Do not confuse this with Cauchy 列与零列: that one is about rational Cauchy sequences, a building block in the construction of R\mathbb{R}R;

the present notion is the tool for testing convergence once R\mathbb{R}R is available.
