Statement

Let SSS be a closed orientable surface of genus g=12rank⁡H1(S;Z)g = \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank} H_{1}(S;\mathbb{Z})g=21​rankH1​(S;Z) (genus). Then

χ(S)=2−2g.\chi(S) = 2 - 2g .χ(S)=2−2g.

Thus χ\chiχ and ggg determine each other: g=(2−χ)/2g = (2 - \chi)/2g=(2−χ)/2.

The other previous gap, orientability for topological surfaces, now has its own node (topological orientability). This proposition no longer uses the classification theorem. The elementary argument below replaces both routes in the previous notes, namely (a) using classification of closed surfaces and (b) using the fundamental class or orientation duality. 7. Status and remaining gap. (1) The only external theorem unproved here is Radó's theorem (Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable, skeleton). (2) Orientability now has its own node, topological orientability (oriented atlases and the orientation double cover). The graph also has a smooth version (Orientable Manifold, Orientation and Volume Forms) and an extrinsic version in R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 (Orientable Surface). Applying the smooth definition to a topological surface first requires a smoothing theorem, which in turn requires Radó or Whitehead. Section 2 of these notes restates the notion in topological terms. (3) This proposition therefore carries status: 'skeleton': the proof depends on Radó's theorem (Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable is still a skeleton). Once Radó's theorem is proved, the status can be removed (orientability is covered by topological orientability and does not affect the conclusion). (4) Another option, with the statement unchanged for now and the decision left to the parent session, is to assume a triangulable closed orientable surface. That would make the proof immediately complete by making triangulability a hypothesis.
