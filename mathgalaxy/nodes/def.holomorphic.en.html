Notes

⭐ The only difference from real differentiability: hhh is a complex number.

In the real case it suffices that the left and right directions agree; in the complex case infinitely many directions (h=teiθh = t e^{i\theta}h=teiθ, θ\thetaθ arbitrary) must all agree. The requirement is very strong and the reward is very large: one derivative gives infinitely many.

⚠ This requirement is not automatic.

f(z)=zˉf(z) = \bar zf(z)=zˉ is nowhere complex differentiable: along the real axis, h=th = th=t gives difference quotient 111; along the imaginary axis, h=ith = ith=it gives quotient −1-1−1.

The two one-sided limits differ.

So "real differentiable" is far from implying "complex differentiable".

⭐ An equivalent formulation of complex differentiability is Δf=A Δz+o(Δz)\Delta f = A\,\Delta z + o(\Delta z)Δf=AΔz+o(Δz) — the increment is only a linear function of Δz\Delta zΔz, and Δz‾\overline{\Delta z}Δz may not appear.

In partial-derivative language this is ∂f/∂zˉ=0\partial f/\partial \bar z = 0∂f/∂zˉ=0 (see the Cauchy–Riemann equations).

📌 Sums, products, quotients (with nonzero denominator) and composites of holomorphic functions are holomorphic, with the same derivative formulas as in the real case — because these are only the arithmetic of limits and never use the realness of hhh.
