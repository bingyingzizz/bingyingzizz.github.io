## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Where the name comes from: the elements of $C(S,\mathbb{Z})$ are "integer-valued continuous functions on $S$"; applying a measure $\mu$ gives $\int f\,d\mu$.

When $S$ is finite, $M(S,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}^{S}$, i.e. "put one integer at each point";

when $S$ is an infinite profinite set, $M(S,\mathbb{Z})$ is a **formal infinite sum**, in general much larger than $\mathbb{Z}[S]$.
