Statement

In plain words: the integral along a closed curve equals 2πi2\pi i2πi times the sum of the residues at all singularities inside the curve — a two-dimensional integration problem is compressed into finitely many algebraic computations.

Let Ω\OmegaΩ be a bounded region whose boundary ∂Ω\partial\Omega∂Ω consists of finitely many piecewise smooth simple closed curves, oriented positively. Let fff be holomorphic on Ω\OmegaΩ except at finitely many points z1,⋯ ,znz_1, \cdots, z_nz1​,⋯,zn​, all isolated singularities not lying on ∂Ω\partial\Omega∂Ω; and let fff be holomorphic on some neighbourhood of Ω‾\overline{\Omega}Ω except at these points. (⚠ The last condition cannot be omitted: saying only "the singularities are not on the boundary" is not enough — for example Ω=D\Omega = \mathbb{D}Ω=D, f=1/(1−z)f = 1/(1-z)f=1/(1−z), where z=1z = 1z=1 lies on ∂D\partial\mathbb{D}∂D and fff is undefined there, so ∮∂Ωf dz\oint_{\partial\Omega} f\,dz∮∂Ω​fdz does not exist at all.) Then

∮∂Ωf(z) dz=2πi∑k=1nRes⁡(f,zk).\oint_{\partial\Omega} f(z)\,\mathrm{d}z = 2\pi i \sum_{k=1}^{n} \operatorname{Res}(f, z_k).∮∂Ω​f(z)dz=2πik=1∑n​Res(f,zk​).
