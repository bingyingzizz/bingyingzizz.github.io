Statement

Let f:K∙→L∙f : K^{\bullet} \to L^{\bullet}f:K∙→L∙ be a morphism of complexes. Then

  1. fff is a homotopy equivalence (that is, an isomorphism in K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C))   ⟺  \iff⟺ there exists g:L∙→K∙g : L^{\bullet} \to K^{\bullet}g:L∙→K∙ with gf∼1Kg f \sim 1_{K}gf∼1K​, fg∼1Lf g \sim 1_{L}fg∼1L​;
  2. a complex K∙K^{\bullet}K∙ is homotopy trivial   ⟺  \iff⟺ 1K∼0K1_{K} \sim 0_{K}1K​∼0K​.

Notes

⚠️ Note that "homotopy equivalence" is not "termwise isomorphism": 1∼01 \sim 01∼0 means that between two adjacent layers of K∙K^{\bullet}K∙ there is a family of maps sns^{n}sn contracting the identity; such a complex (for example 0→M→1M→00 \to M \xrightarrow{1} M \to 00→M1​M→0) has every layer nonzero yet is homotopy trivial.

Hypotheses used in each direction (invertibility in the homotopy category is entirely an unfolding of the quotient-category definition):

  • [f][f][f] invertible   ⟹  \implies⟹ there is a homotopy inverse ggg: take a representative of the inverse morphism; morphisms in the quotient category are homotopy classes, so the composite class equals the identity class precisely in the two homotopies listed. No injectivity or boundedness is required.
  • gf∼1Kgf\sim1_Kgf∼1K​ and fg∼1L  ⟹  [f]fg\sim1_L\implies[f]fg∼1L​⟹[f] invertible: take [g][g][g] as the inverse; this is a definitional unfolding and needs no axiom of choice to select a family of representatives at once.
  • KKK isomorphic to the zero complex   ⟺  \iff⟺ 1K∼01_K\sim01K​∼0: morphisms to and from a zero object are unique, composites are zero, and both directions are checked directly against the definition of an inverse; only an additive category in which complexes and homotopies are defined is needed.
  • Homotopy trivial cannot be replaced by acyclic: the nonsplit exact complex of abelian groups 0→Z→2Z→Z/2→00\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2\to00→Z2​Z→Z/2→0 is acyclic, but a contracting homotopy would force the terminal surjection to split.
