## Abstract · **reviewed**
In a metric space the following are equivalent: compact, sequentially compact, and complete together with totally bounded. The skeleton lives in the implications: compact implies sequentially compact; sequentially compact implies totally bounded (the one place dependent choice is used) and complete; sequentially compact implies compact by the "bad ball" argument; complete and totally bounded implies sequentially compact by diagonalisation. In general topological spaces only the first implication survives — sequential compactness is a metric-space luxury.
