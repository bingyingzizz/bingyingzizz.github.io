Statement

Let {fn}⊆L+\{f_n\} \subseteq L^+{fn​}⊆L+. Then

∫lim inf⁡n→∞fn≤lim inf⁡n→∞∫fn\int \liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n \le \liminf_{n\to\infty} \int f_n∫n→∞liminf​fn​≤n→∞liminf​∫fn​

Notes

⚠ Mind the direction of the inequality: the lower limit outside the integral is at most the lower limit of the integrals. In other words, passing to the limit can only make the integral smaller, never larger.

This is what remains of MCT once monotonicity is dropped — without monotonicity, equality degenerates into an inequality.

A counterexample in the other direction: fn=χ[n,n+1]f_n = \chi_{[n, n+1]}fn​=χ[n,n+1]​ (on R\mathbb{R}R) has ∫fn=1\int f_{n} = 1∫fn​=1 for every index, but ∫lim inf⁡fn=0\int \liminf f_{n} = 0∫liminffn​=0.
