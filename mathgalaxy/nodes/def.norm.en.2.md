## Notes · **draft**
What a norm has beyond a distance is **linearity**: it records only "how far from the origin", and distances are obtained by translation (see "the distance induced by a norm"). So a normed space is a metric space "with an origin, a scale, and translation invariance".

⚠️ A seminorm is not a norm: one must pass to the quotient by the zero-norm subspace to get a norm (in the construction of $L^p$, "$\|f\|_p = 0$ only says $f = 0$ a.e." is exactly this; see "the $L^p$ norm").
