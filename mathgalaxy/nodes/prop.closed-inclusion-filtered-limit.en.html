Statement

Let X=lim→⁡i∈IXiX = \varinjlim_{i \in I} X_{i}X=lim​i∈I​Xi​ be a filtered colimit of compact Hausdorff spaces along closed inclusions. Then

  1. XXX is compactly generated and weak Hausdorff;
  2. every XiX_{i}Xi​ is closed in XXX;
  3. the topology of XXX is the colimit topology of these compact pieces: U⊆XU \subseteq XU⊆X is open   ⟺  \iff⟺ every U∩XiU \cap X_{i}U∩Xi​ is open in XiX_{i}Xi​.

Conversely, every compactly generated weak Hausdorff space is the filtered colimit of its own compact Hausdorff subspaces along closed inclusions.

Notes

Item 3 is the substance of this proposition. When taking a colimit of topological spaces, the topology may be coarser or finer than the "piecewise" topology; this item says that in this situation the two coincide exactly — a set is open iff its trace on every piece is open. Hence "continuous functions on XXX" = "a compatible family of functions continuous on each piece", and one can work piece by piece.

Why the pieces must be compact Hausdorff. Compactness is the geometric incarnation of the finiteness condition "finite subcover"; in condensed mathematics, compact Hausdorff spaces are exactly the "probes". Writing a large object as a filtered colimit of compact pieces amounts to writing it as a limit of "larger and larger finite information" — every computation can stop at some piece.

⭐ Where it is used: to prove "the underlying space of a quasi-separated condensed set is weak Hausdorff", first write the quasi-separated XXX as a filtered colimit of compact Hausdorff pieces along inclusions, then take underlying spaces of this chain — by this item the colimit is still compactly generated weak Hausdorff.

📌 Division of labour with "closure of CGWH\mathrm{CGWH}CGWH": that one says that a general CGWH space glued along closed inclusions is still CGWH; this one replaces the pieces by compact Hausdorff ones and additionally gives an explicit description of the topology and the reverse decomposition.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the topological description is the definition of colimit; the separation conclusion needs a separate argument):

  • In a topological space, a colimit   ⟹  \implies⟹ has open sets detected piecewise: this is the definition of the final topology, and does not require proving compact generation or weak Hausdorffness first; the note above calling item 3 extra substance should be qualified in this way.
  • In a filtered system of closed inclusions   ⟹  \implies⟹, each piece is closed in the colimit: the intersection of two pieces is closed in a common successor, and the final topology detects it; filteredness and closed inclusions are used in this step.
  • The final topology on compact Hausdorff pieces   ⟹  \implies⟹ is compactly generated: the structure maps of the pieces are precisely the compact Hausdorff probes; the weak Hausdorff conclusion does not follow from this definition alone, and the full proof of this step for an arbitrary filtered index has not been verified, so it is recorded as a gap.
  • A compactly generated weak Hausdorff space   ⟹  \implies⟹ is a filtered union of compact Hausdorff subspaces: weak Hausdorffness guarantees that the images of compact Hausdorff probes are closed and Hausdorff, finite unions give common upper bounds, and compact generation guarantees that the final topology recovers the original topology.
  • Compact generation cannot be dropped: the cocountable topology on an uncountable set is weak Hausdorff, its compact Hausdorff subspaces are all finite, and their final topology is discrete, so it cannot recover the original topology.
