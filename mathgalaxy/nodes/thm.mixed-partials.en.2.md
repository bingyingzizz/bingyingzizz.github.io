In particular, when $f \in C^{2}$ the matrix $H_f(a)$ is symmetric.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This theorem compresses "$n^{2}$ second partial derivatives" into "$\binom{n+1}{2}$" — it is the entire source of the symmetry of the Hessian, and the reason $D^{2}f$ can be written as a **symmetric** bilinear form in the multivariable Taylor formula.

⚠ **Continuity cannot be dropped**: $f(x,y) = xy(x^{2}-y^{2})/(x^{2}+y^{2})$ (with value $0$ at the origin) satisfies $\partial_1\partial_2 f(0,0) = 1 \ne -1 = \partial_2\partial_1 f(0,0)$.
