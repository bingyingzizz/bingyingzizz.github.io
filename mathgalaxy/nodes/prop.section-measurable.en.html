Abstract

Sections of measurable sets are measurable: if E∈M⊗NE \in \mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{N}E∈M⊗N then Ex∈NE_x \in \mathcal{N}Ex​∈N for every xxx and Ey∈ME^y \in \mathcal{M}Ey∈M for every yyy; likewise the sections of an M⊗N\mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{N}M⊗N-measurable fff are measurable, because fx−1(B)=(f−1(B))xf_x^{-1}(B) = (f^{-1}(B))_xfx−1​(B)=(f−1(B))x​ and dually. The proof is the standard one: the class of sets all of whose sections are measurable contains every rectangle and is a σ\sigmaσ-algebra. Note that this holds for every point, whereas Fubini can only promise almost every point once integrability enters.

The proof is the standard pattern "rectangles first, then σ\sigmaσ-algebras". Let

R={E⊆X×Y:Ex∈N ∧ Ey∈M∀(x,y)}\mathcal{R} = \{E \subseteq X \times Y : E_x \in \mathcal{N} \ \wedge\ E^y \in \mathcal{M}\quad \forall(x, y)\}R={E⊆X×Y:Ex​∈N ∧ Ey∈M∀(x,y)}
