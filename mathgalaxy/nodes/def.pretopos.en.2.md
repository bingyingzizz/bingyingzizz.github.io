## Notes · **draft**
Item 4 **is not independent** — it can be deduced from the first three, so some textbooks write only three. The advantage of writing it is to recall one thing: **here epimorphisms are automatically regular**, so "epimorphism" and "quotient map" are the same thing.

Standard fact: **monomorphisms can be defined by limits** ($i$ is a monomorphism $\iff$ that square is cartesian), and pullbacks and limits preserve monomorphisms; this guarantees that pushouts also preserve monomorphisms — which is what makes the "disjoint" and "effective" conditions in items 2 and 3 meaningful.
