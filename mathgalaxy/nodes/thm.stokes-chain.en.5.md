# Stokes' Theorem on Chains　`thm.stokes-chain`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.stokes-chain)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.stokes-chain.md)

**Why no orientation is required**: For chains, the parametrization already encodes the orientation (`def.integral-on-chain`), so orientability does not enter the statement. An orientation is needed when passing back from the chain version to the manifold version. The signs then come from `prop.boundary-sign-convention`.

**Four in one**: $k = 1$ gives the fundamental theorem of calculus. $k = 2$ gives Green's theorem. The two forms of $k = 3$ are Gauss–Ostrogradsky (flux, i.e., the divergence theorem `thm.divergence-theorem`) and Kelvin–Stokes (circulation). These are readings of the same theorem for different $k$.
