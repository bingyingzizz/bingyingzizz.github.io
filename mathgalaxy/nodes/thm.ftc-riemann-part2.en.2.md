It also explains why tables of indefinite integrals are worth memorising — every derivative formula, read backwards, is an integration formula.

⚠ The hypothesis “$F'$ is integrable” cannot be dropped: there are differentiable functions whose derivative is **not** integrable (the $F(x) = x^{2}\sin(1/x^{2})$ type).

⚠ The converse is not strong enough either: Newton–Leibniz requires $F$ to be an antiderivative;

the Lebesgue version (`thm.ftc-lebesgue`) relaxes the hypothesis to **absolute continuity** and makes “$F'$ exists” part of the conclusion.
