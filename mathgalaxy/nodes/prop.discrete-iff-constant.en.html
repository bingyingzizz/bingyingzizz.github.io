prop.discrete-iff-constant

Statement

For a condensed set XXX, the following three are equivalent:

  1. XXX is discrete: there is a discrete topological space EEE with X≅E‾X \cong \underline{E}X≅E​;
  2. XXX is constant: there is a set EEE with X≅E~X \cong \widetilde{E}X≅E (a constant sheaf);
  3. for every compact Hausdorff space SSS,
X(S)  ≅  lim←⁡S↠S′X(S′),X(S) \;\cong\; \varprojlim_{S \twoheadrightarrow S'} X(S'),X(S)≅S↠S′lim​​X(S′),

where S′S'S′ runs over the discrete quotients of SSS.

Notes

Hypotheses used in each direction (discrete representation agrees with constant sheaves, but the quotient formula needs checking):

  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2) and (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): sheafifying the constant presheaf gives locally constant maps, not the original constant presheaf; finite clopen partitions of a compact Hausdorff probe give this identification.
  • Discreteness   ⟹  \implies⟹ factors through a finite discrete quotient: the image of a continuous map S→ES\to ES→E is finite by compactness, and its fibres are clopen; without compactness, the identity map of an infinite discrete space does not factor through a finite quotient.
  • Step lim←⁡\varprojlimlim​ of item (3) in the source has not been verified, and is recorded as a gap: a contravariant functor along refinements of discrete quotients gives pullbacks toward finer quotients, and locally constant maps should usually be expressed as a filtered colimit, not an equivalence asserted from the present projective-limit formula.
  • If replaced by the natural filtered-colimit formula, then conversely one uses that the value of a sheaf on a finite discrete set is the finite product of X(∗)X(*)X(∗), recovering the constant sheaf; this step needs the sheaf condition and naturality of the formula in SSS.
