**In metric spaces**: on a metric space, "compact $\iff$ sequentially compact $\iff$ complete + totally bounded" (`thm.metric-compact-equiv`) — that is a **theorem** (provable only with the metric), not part of the definition. In a general topological space **compactness and sequential compactness imply neither of the other**: $[0, \omega_{1})$ with the order topology is sequentially compact but not compact (the cover $\{[0, \alpha) : \alpha < \omega_{1}\}$ has no finite subcover); $\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})}$ is compact by Tychonoff but not sequentially compact (take $x_{n}(A) = 1 \iff n \in A$; every subsequence fails to converge at the coordinate $A = \{n_{2k
