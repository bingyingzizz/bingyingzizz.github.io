Notes

⭐ What it is for: to compute the derived functors Rn(G∘F)R^{n}(G \circ F)Rn(G∘F) of a composite, one may first compute RqFR^{q}FRqF, then RpGR^{p}GRpG, stitching the two layers together with a spectral sequence. This is the most basic "two-step" tool in homological algebra.

Proof (double complex + two spectral sequences). Take an injective resolution of MMM, say I∙I^{\bullet}I∙, and put A∙:=FI∙A^{\bullet} := F I^{\bullet}A∙:=FI∙.

  • By hypothesis InI^{n}In is injective, so FInFI^{n}FIn is GGG-acyclic, and by left exactness of FFF the sequence 0→FM→FI∙0 \to FM \to FI^{\bullet}0→FM→FI∙ is exact; hence A∙=FI∙A^{\bullet} = FI^{\bullet}A∙=FI∙ is a right resolution of FMFMFM whose terms are GGG-acyclic (⚠ this step uses left exactness; without it FI∙FI^{\bullet}FI∙ only computes RF(M)RF(M)RF(M) and need not resolve FMFMFM), so R(G∘F)M=GA∙R(G\circ F)M = G A^{\bullet}R(G∘F)M=GA∙ in the derived category (Leray acyclicity), i.e. Rn(G∘F)M=Hn(GA∙)R^{n}(G\circ F)M = H^{n}(GA^{\bullet})Rn(G∘F)M=Hn(GA∙);
  • then take a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution of A∙A^{\bullet}A∙, say J∙,∙J^{\bullet,\bullet}J∙,∙ (each Jp,∙J^{p,\bullet}Jp,∙ is a resolution of ApA^{p}Ap that is GGG-acyclic, and the homologies in both directions are computable);

the other spectral sequence (horizontal then vertical) degenerates at its E2E_{2}E2​ page because every column is an acyclic resolution, and both converge to the same thing.

∎

The details are in the first two references: existence of the Cartan–Eilenberg resolution, and convergence of the double-complex spectral sequence together with the identification of the E2E_{2}E2​ page.

Typical applications: sheaf cohomology — sheafify then take sections, Čech cohomology to de Rham cohomology, the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence in group homology — all are special cases.

Why require FFF to send injectives to GGG-acyclics: this is what lets an injective resolution under FFF serve directly as a GGG-acyclic resolution, so that the two sides match up.
