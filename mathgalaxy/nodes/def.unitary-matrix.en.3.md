Thus a unitary matrix preserves the inner product, hence the norm ($\lVert Ux\rVert = \lVert x\rVert$) and distances; $\lvert\det U\rvert = 1$. All unitary matrices form a group $U(n)$, and the orthogonal ones form $O(n)$.

## Notes · **draft**
**The name "unitary"**: $U^{*}U = I$ degenerates to orthogonal in the real case (which is what $\perp$ means); in the complex case the term is unitary (from Latin unus, "one"). The terminology does not matter; the condition is the same.
