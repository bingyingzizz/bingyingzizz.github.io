Statement

  1. A condensed set XXX is quasi-compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ there are a free compact Hausdorff space FFF and an epimorphism
F↠X.F \twoheadrightarrow X.F↠X.
  1. the functor S↦S‾S \mapsto \underline{S}S↦S​ (regarding a compact Hausdorff space as the condensed set it represents) gives an equivalence of categories
CHaus ≃ CHaus ⟶CondSetqcqs.\mathbf{CHaus}\ \simeq\ \mathrm{CHaus}\ \longrightarrow \mathrm{CondSet}_{\mathrm{qcqs}}.CHaus ≃ CHaus ⟶CondSetqcqs​.

that is: compact Hausdorff spaces and quasi-compact quasi-separated condensed sets are equivalent.

Notes

What this says. A condensed set is "a sheaf probed by compact Hausdorff spaces". This theorem says: the most regular of the probed objects (quasi-compact quasi-separated) are the probers themselves. Thus the category of compact Hausdorff spaces sits unchanged inside Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond, as its "compact-piece part".

Why item 1 uses a "free" FFF. The general definition of "quasi-compact" is "every cover has a finite subcover", a purely logical condition. This item replaces it by a geometric condition: one can map onto the object from a (possibly very large) free compact Hausdorff piece. Hence quasi-compact becomes "can be covered by a compact Hausdorff piece".

What "free" means: things like Z[S]\mathbb{Z}[S]Z[S], generated by a set of points with no extra relations — being able to fit means "there are enough points to cover".

⭐ The corresponding statement for a general topos is "quasi-compact quasi-separated object ≅\cong≅ compact Hausdorff object"; here we do the special case Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond.

Proof (both items are special cases of theorems for general toposes, applied to Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond).

  • Item 1: the topos-theoretic characterization of "quasi-compact" is "there is an epimorphism from a projective / free object" — the reverse is clear (an epimorphism F↠XF \twoheadrightarrow XF↠X gives a cover, and the parametrization by FFF extracts a finite subcover, because the free object F=βIF = \beta IF=βI is "generated by a set of points", so finiteness is pointwise);

the forward direction takes the free objects FCHaus\mathbf{FCHaus}FCHaus and shows that they span CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus, then compresses the parameters of the cover into one free object.

  • Item 2: a quasi-compact quasi-separated object is determined by "its values on the compact Hausdorff pieces", and in Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond, for a compact Hausdorff space SSS its represented sheaf S‾=hS\underline{S} = h_{S}S​=hS​ is precisely quasi-compact quasi-separated (quasi-compactness from S→SS \to SS→S itself;

qs from "the intersection of two compact Hausdorff pieces is again compact Hausdorff").

Hence S↦S‾S \mapsto \underline{S}S↦S​ is fully faithful;

essential surjectivity is obtained by combining the previous item with the characterization of quasi-separatedness.

∎

Hypotheses used in each direction (representability comes from the theory of compact Hausdorff quotients, not from an arbitrary topos):

  • A compact Hausdorff representable object   ⟹  \implies⟹ is quasi-compact quasi-separated: finiteness of covers gives quasi-compactness, and the fact that fibred products of maps between compact Hausdorff spaces are again compact Hausdorff gives quasi-separatedness; full faithfulness comes from the subcanonical topology and the Yoneda lemma.
  • Quasi-compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ is covered by a compact Hausdorff object, then cover it by the Stone–Čech compactification βI\beta IβI of its underlying discrete set, obtaining a free compact Hausdorff cover; this uses existence of Stone–Čech and the usual choice background. "Free" is not the abelian group Z[I]\mathbb Z[I]Z[I].
  • A free compact Hausdorff object that is an epimorphic cover   ⟹  \implies⟹ is quasi-compact: epimorphic images preserve quasi-compactness, substantively because of pullbacks of covers, finite subcovers, and descent of covers, not pointwise finiteness on an infinite point set.
  • Quasi-compact quasi-separated   ⟹  \implies⟹ compact Hausdorff representable is the direction with a cost: from a cover S→XS\to XS→X one gets a compact relation S×XSS\times_XSS×X​S, identifies its image as a closed equivalence relation in S×SS\times SS×S, and then uses the theorem on quotients by closed relations of compact Hausdorff spaces together with effective descent.
  • Quasi-compactness cannot be dropped: the infinite discrete condensed set is quasi-separated but not quasi-compact and cannot be represented by a compact Hausdorff space; this equivalence of categories depends on the condensed site and does not generalize to an arbitrary topos.
