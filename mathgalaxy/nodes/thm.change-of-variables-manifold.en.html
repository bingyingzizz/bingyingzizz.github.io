Statement

What it does: the change-of-variables formula on a manifold — pushing a form along a diffeomorphism leaves the integral unchanged, and reversing the direction changes the sign. It is another way of saying that "an nnn-form is the only thing that integrates naturally".

Let f:M→Nf : M\to Nf:M→N be a diffeomorphism between oriented nnn-dimensional smooth manifolds and ω∈Ωn(N)\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)ω∈Ωn(N) integrable (NNN compact, or ω\omegaω compactly supported). Then

if fff preserves orientation

∫Mf∗ω=∫Nω;\int_{M}f^{\ast}\omega = \int_{N}\omega ;∫M​f∗ω=∫N​ω;

if fff reverses orientation

∫Mf∗ω=−∫Nω.\int_{M}f^{\ast}\omega = -\int_{N}\omega .∫M​f∗ω=−∫N​ω.

Both cases combine as ∫Mf∗ω=ε∫Nω\int_{M}f^{\ast}\omega = \varepsilon\int_{N}\omega∫M​f∗ω=ε∫N​ω, where ε=+1\varepsilon = +1ε=+1 means orientation-preserving and −1-1−1 orientation-reversing.

The classical change of variables is the coordinate version: take M,U⊆RnM,U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}M,U⊆Rn, N=g(U)N = g(U)N=g(U) and ω=F dy1∧⋯∧dyn\omega = F\,\mathrm{d}y^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{n}ω=Fdy1∧⋯∧dyn; then f∗ω=(F∘g)det⁡(∂g/∂x) dx1∧⋯∧dxnf^{\ast}\omega = (F\circ g)\det(\partial g/\partial x)\,\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}f∗ω=(F∘g)det(∂g/∂x)dx1∧⋯∧dxn, and the formula becomes ∫g(U)F dy=∫U(F∘g)det⁡(∂g/∂x) dx\int_{g(U)}F\,\mathrm{d}y = \int_{U}(F\circ g)\det(\partial g/\partial x)\,\mathrm{d}x∫g(U)​Fdy=∫U​(F∘g)det(∂g/∂x)dx — the only difference from a calculus textbook is the absence of absolute values.

Notes

The presence or absence of the absolute value is the presence or absence of orientation: without orientation only densities integrate, and the change of variables carries ∣det⁡∣|\det|∣det∣ (密度与定向无关); with orientation the nnn-form carries its own sign, and the change of variables carries det⁡\detdet. Two bookkeepings of one fact.

Why it holds only for nnn-forms: a form with k<nk<nk<n transforms by k×kk\times kk×k minors under a diffeomorphism, which does not match the nnn-dimensional volume element, so there is no "change-of-variables formula for kkk-forms on a manifold". To integrate a kkk-form one first chooses a kkk-dimensional submanifold and pulls back (形式的积分, opening sentence).

The two pieces of the proof: first, "the integral is invariant under an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism" (well-definedness of 形式的积分, i.e. the matching in Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn of det⁡\detdet with the change-of-variables formula); second, a partition of unity (仿紧性与单位分解) to split the whole into charts. Both pieces are proved elsewhere, so the theorem itself is an assembly.

⚠️ A diffeomorphism cannot be weakened to a smooth surjection: that would require degrees and multiplicities (∫Mf∗ω=deg⁡(f)∫Nω\int_{M}f^{\ast}\omega = \deg(f)\int_{N}\omega∫M​f∗ω=deg(f)∫N​ω), and ε\varepsilonε would become an integer rather than ±1\pm1±1. Only the diffeomorphism case is stated here.

⚠️ Choosing orientations: if MMM and NNN each carry an orientation, ε\varepsilonε is decided by whether fff preserves these two orientations; reversing the orientation of NNN changes both ε\varepsilonε and the right-hand side, and the identity survives. So "orientation-preserving" must be read relative to two fixed orientations.
