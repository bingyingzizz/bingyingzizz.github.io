Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be an additive category. A (co)chain complex is a (long) sequence in C\mathcal{C}C (see @@def.sequence-of-morphisms@@) KKK such that dn∘dn−1=0d^{n} \circ d^{n-1} = 0dn∘dn−1=0 holds for every nnn; ddd is called the differential.

Reversing the arrows (equivalently, working in Cop\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}Cop) gives a chain complex, written Kn:=K−nK_{n} := K^{-n}Kn​:=K−n, dn:=d−nd_{n} := d^{-n}dn​:=d−n.

Notes

KKK is called bounded below (n≪0n \ll 0n≪0, with Kn=0K^{n} = 0Kn=0), bounded above, or bounded (both).

The category of complexes is written C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C) (see @@def.category-of-complexes@@); its full subcategories of bounded-below, bounded-above and bounded complexes are written C+(C)\mathcal{C}^{+}(\mathcal{C})C+(C), C−(C)\mathcal{C}^{-}(\mathcal{C})C−(C), Cb(C)\mathcal{C}^{b}(\mathcal{C})Cb(C);

the homotopy category receives the same decorations +++, −-−, bbb.

Example: when KKK is a (semi)simplicial object, put dn:=∑i(−1)idind^{n} := \sum_{i} (-1)^{i} d_{i}^{n}dn:=∑i​(−1)idin​; then KKK is a chain complex — the sign (−1)i(-1)^{i}(−1)i is exactly what makes consecutive composites cancel, in complete agreement with the classical simplicial boundary operator.
