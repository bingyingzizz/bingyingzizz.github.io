# dominated convergence　`thm.dct`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.dct)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.dct.md)

$\cdot \text{a.e.}$ convergence — cannot be removed (on a one-point probability space $f_{n} = (-1)^{n}$: dominated by $g \equiv 1$, yet neither the functions nor the integrals converge);

$\cdot$ the dominating function $g \in L^{1}$ — cannot be removed ($n\chi_{[0,1/n]}$ tends to $0$ **almost everywhere** on $[0,1]$ (it fails to converge at $x = 0$), yet its integral is constantly $1$; another example is $\chi_{[n,n+1]}$, which converges to $0$ **everywhere** while its integral is constantly $1$ and does not tend to $0$ — the defect is the absence of an integrable dominating function, since any dominating function would have to be $\ge 1$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$);
