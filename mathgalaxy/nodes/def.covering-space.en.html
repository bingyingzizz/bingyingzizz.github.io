Statement

Let p:E→Bp : E \to Bp:E→B be continuous and surjective. An open set U⊆BU \subseteq BU⊆B is evenly covered if p−1(U)p^{-1}(U)p−1(U) is a union of a family {Vj}j∈J\{V_{j}\}_{j \in J}{Vj​}j∈J​ of pairwise disjoint open sets in EEE, and for every jjj the restriction

Notes

Why "local homeomorphism + discrete fibre" rather than "locally trivial fibre bundle": a covering is stiffer than a fibre bundle — each sheet VjV_{j}Vj​ is required to be homeomorphic to UUU, not merely fibre-homotopy equivalent. Hence the fibres of a covering are necessarily discrete. This stiffness is exactly what makes the lifting theorem work.

Number of sheets = index: if BBB is path connected and locally path connected and the number of sheets is finite, then the index of π1(E)\pi_{1}(E)π1​(E) in π1(B)\pi_{1}(B)π1​(B) equals the number of sheets (覆叠的指数). This is the source of "count the sheets to compute the fundamental group".

⚠️ A common counterexample: p:(0,2)→S1p : (0, 2) \to S^{1}p:(0,2)→S1, p(t)=e2πitp(t) = e^{2\pi i t}p(t)=e2πit — this is not a covering, because every neighbourhood of 1∈S11 \in S^{1}1∈S1 is covered only halfway ((0,2)(0,2)(0,2) does not "continue" at the endpoint). A covering must be even at every point, including limit points.
