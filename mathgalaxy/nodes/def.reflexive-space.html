Statement draft

Let XXX be a Banach space and JX:X→X∗∗J_X : X \to X^{**}JX​:X→X∗∗ the natural embedding JX(x)(φ)=φ(x)J_X(x)(\varphi) = \varphi(x)JX​(x)(φ)=φ(x). Call XXX reflexive if JXJ_XJX​ is surjective (hence an isometric isomorphism).

Notes draft

The natural embedding is always isometric (Hahn–Banach): ∥JXx∥=sup⁡∥φ∥≤1∣φ(x)∣=∥x∥\|J_X x\| = \sup_{\|\varphi\| \le 1}|\varphi(x)| = \|x\|∥JX​x∥=sup∥φ∥≤1​∣φ(x)∣=∥x∥. Hence reflexivity   ⟺  \iff⟺ every element of X∗∗X^{**}X∗∗ comes from XXX.

Examples: Hilbert spaces; LpL^pLp and ℓp\ell^pℓp (1<p<∞1 < p < \infty1<p<∞, see L^p 自反); finite-dimensional spaces.

Counterexamples: c0c_0c0​ (c0∗∗=ℓ∞≠c0c_0^{**} = \ell^\infty \ne c_0c0∗∗​=ℓ∞=c0​), ℓ1\ell^1ℓ1 (ℓ1∗∗=(ℓ∞)∗⊋ℓ1\ell^{1**} = (\ell^\infty)^* \supsetneq \ell^1ℓ1∗∗=(ℓ∞)∗⊋ℓ1), C[0,1]C[0,1]C[0,1].

⭐ The core use of reflexivity: bounded sequences have weakly convergent subsequences (Eberlein–Šmulian; the graph has no weak-topology node yet), which is the entry point to variational existence and to Milman–Pettis.

📌 Relation to pairing language: XXX is reflexive   ⟺  \iff⟺ both embeddings of the perfect pairing (X,X∗)(X, X^*)(X,X∗) are surjective (完美配对, Riesz 表示的配对版).
