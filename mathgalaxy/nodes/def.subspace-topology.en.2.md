## Notes · **draft**
Intuition: **the open sets of the subspace are exactly the open sets of the big space cut down**. So "closed in $Y$" and "compact in $Y$" must be judged with respect to the cut-down piece, not by looking at the big space — for example $(0, 1) \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is closed in itself but not in $\mathbb{R}$.

📌 `def.induced-topology` is a different notion sharing the same Chinese name: it denotes the Grothendieck topology on a site induced by a functor, and shares only the name with the subspace topology (the two are not merged).
