Statement

Let M\mathcal{M}M be a σ\sigmaσ-algebra on XXX. A function μ:M→[0,+∞]\mu : \mathcal{M} \to [0, +\infty]μ:M→[0,+∞] is a measure if and only if

  • (i) μ(∅)=0\mu(\emptyset) = 0μ(∅)=0
  • (ii) countable additivity: if {Ej}\{E_{j}\}{Ej​} is a sequence of pairwise disjoint sets in M\mathcal{M}M, then
μ(⋃j=1∞Ej)=∑j=1∞μ(Ej)\mu( \bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty} E_j ) = \sum_{j=1}^{\infty} \mu(E_j)μ(j=1⋃∞​Ej​)=j=1∑∞​μ(Ej​)

(X,M,μ)(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu )(X,M,μ) is called a measure space.

Notes

(i) is independent of (ii), and (ii) alone does not imply it: the constant function +∞+\infty+∞ is countably additive yet does not vanish on the empty set (taking Ej≡∅E_j \equiv \emptysetEj​≡∅ yields μ(∅)=+∞⋅μ(∅)\mu(\emptyset) = +\infty \cdot \mu(\emptyset)μ(∅)=+∞⋅μ(∅), and both sides are legitimate). The axiom is there to exclude the meaningless solution that assigns ∞\infty∞ to everything, and at the same time to ensure that the series in (ii) is never ∞−∞\infty - \infty∞−∞.

Countable additivity is the only requirement in this definition with real content: it upgrades "size" from finite additivity to countable additivity, at the price of allowing the value ∞\infty∞.

⚠ Allowing μ(E)=+∞\mu (E) = +\inftyμ(E)=+∞ is necessary: Lebesgue measure on R\mathbb{R}R is ∞\infty∞.

Which hypothesis each direction uses (the two measure axioms are the definition; countable additivity is stronger than finite additivity):

  • measure   ⟺  \iff⟺ non-negative extended real-valued function satisfying (i), (ii): unwinding the definition; no σ\sigmaσ-finiteness or completeness of the measure is needed; the domain must be a σ\sigmaσ-algebra.
  • countable additivity   ⟹  \implies⟹ finite additivity: also uses μ(∅)=0\mu(\emptyset)=0μ(∅)=0, padding a finite disjoint sequence with empty sets; conversely, finite additivity plus continuity from below recovers countable additivity by taking limits of partial unions.
  • (ii) alone cannot imply (i): the constant +∞+\infty+∞ is countably additive in the extended non-negative real sense but does not vanish on the empty set; if some set has finite measure, one can deduce (i) from its disjoint union with the empty set.
  • finite additivity is not enough for a measure: on the finite-cofinite algebra of N\mathbb{N}N, assigning zero to finite sets and one to cofinite sets is finitely additive, but the disjoint union of singletons breaks countable additivity; this also shows that the domain there is not yet a σ\sigmaσ-algebra.
