## Notes · **draft**
Writing $F_{i} \cong X_{i}$, $F_{i} \times_{F} F_{j} \cong X_{i,j}$, the object $R := \coprod_{i,j} X_{i,j}$ defines an equivalence relation on $X := \coprod_{i} X_{i}$, so $F \cong X/R$ — quotients exist in a topos (equivalence relations are effective), hence $F$ is isomorphic to a representable sheaf.

This is the hardest step in Giraud's theorem $(1) \implies (2)$.
