Statement

For every unital ring homomorphism f:R→Sf : R \to Sf:R→S there is an adjunction

S⊗R−  ⊣  f∗:R-Mod⇄S-Mod,S \otimes_{R} - \;\dashv\; f_{\ast} : R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod} \rightleftarrows S\text{-}\mathbf{Mod},S⊗R​−⊣f∗​:R-Mod⇄S-Mod,

that is, for every left RRR-module MMM and left SSS-module PPP a natural isomorphism

Hom⁡S(S⊗RM, P)  ≅  Hom⁡R(M, f∗P),g⟼(m↦g(1⊗m)).\operatorname{Hom}_{S}\bigl(S \otimes_{R} M,\ P\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{R}\bigl(M,\ f_{\ast} P\bigr), \qquad g \longmapsto \bigl(m \mapsto g(1 \otimes m)\bigr).HomS​(S⊗R​M, P)≅HomR​(M, f∗​P),g⟼(m↦g(1⊗m)).

The unit is ηM:M→f∗(S⊗RM)\eta_{M} : M \to f_{\ast}(S \otimes_{R} M)ηM​:M→f∗​(S⊗R​M), m↦1⊗mm \mapsto 1 \otimes mm↦1⊗m; the counit is εP:S⊗Rf∗P→P\varepsilon_{P} : S \otimes_{R} f_{\ast} P \to PεP​:S⊗R​f∗​P→P, s⊗p↦sps \otimes p \mapsto s ps⊗p↦sp.

Notes

Proof: an SSS-linear map g:S⊗RM→Pg : S \otimes_{R} M \to Pg:S⊗R​M→P is first of all RRR-linear, hence determined by m↦g(1⊗m)m \mapsto g(1 \otimes m)m↦g(1⊗m) (because s⊗m=s(1⊗m)s \otimes m = s(1 \otimes m)s⊗m=s(1⊗m));

conversely, given an RRR-linear map φ:M→P\varphi : M \to Pφ:M→P, the SSS-balanced formula (s,m)↦sφ(m)(s, m) \mapsto s \varphi(m)(s,m)↦sφ(m) is well defined, and the universal property gives a unique SSS-linear map S⊗RM→PS \otimes_{R} M \to PS⊗R​M→P.

The two directions are inverse, and nothing is chosen in the formula, so it is natural ✓.

Triangle identities: εS⊗M∘(S⊗ηM)=id\varepsilon_{S \otimes M} \circ (S \otimes \eta_{M}) = \mathrm{id}εS⊗M​∘(S⊗ηM​)=id and f∗εP∘ηf∗P=idf_{\ast}\varepsilon_{P} \circ \eta_{f_{\ast}P} = \mathrm{id}f∗​εP​∘ηf∗​P​=id, checked on pure tensors (or elements of MMM) and extended linearly ✓.

∎

⭐ The precise meaning of "most free": every map that sends MMM into some SSS-module and is compatible with the RRR-action factors uniquely through 1⊗−1 \otimes -1⊗−. So S⊗RMS \otimes_{R} MS⊗R​M is the standard answer to "the image of MMM in the category of SSS-modules".

Immediate consequences: S⊗R−S \otimes_{R} -S⊗R​− is right exact and preserves colimits (left adjoint); f∗f_{\ast}f∗​ is left exact (right adjoint), in fact exact.
