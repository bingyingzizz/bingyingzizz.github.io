Statement

For a square matrix A=(aij)∈Mn(K)A = (a_{ij}) \in M_n(K)A=(aij​)∈Mn​(K), define the trace as the sum of the diagonal entries:

tr⁡A:=∑i=1naii\operatorname{tr} A := \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ii}trA:=i=1∑n​aii​

Basic properties:

  • tr⁡(A+B)=tr⁡A+tr⁡B\operatorname{tr}(A + B) = \operatorname{tr} A + \operatorname{tr} Btr(A+B)=trA+trB, tr⁡(cA)=ctr⁡A\operatorname{tr}(cA) = c\operatorname{tr} Atr(cA)=ctrA (linearity);
  • tr⁡(AB)=tr⁡(BA)\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \operatorname{tr}(BA)tr(AB)=tr(BA), hence tr⁡(P−1AP)=tr⁡A\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}AP) = \operatorname{tr} Atr(P−1AP)=trA (similarity invariance);
  • tr⁡(ABC)=tr⁡(BCA)\operatorname{tr}(ABC) = \operatorname{tr}(BCA)tr(ABC)=tr(BCA) (cyclic invariance).

Notes

⭐ The trace is the cheapest invariant: O(n)O(n)O(n) computes it, yet it already distinguishes many matrices (far cheaper than the determinant).

⭐ Its deeper meaning: tr⁡\operatorname{tr}tr is the unique (up to a scalar) central linear functional on the matrix algebra, and also the "trace form" from the KnK^{n}Kn-algebra to KKK — it reappears in Galois theory and characteristic classes.
