rization in the polynomial ring over a field; uniqueness uses $\mathbb C[z]$, roots counted with multiplicity, multiplicities positive integers, order of factors irrelevant, constants by the empty product.
- complex polynomials really have roots: this page's proof uses compactness of the closed disk, the extreme value theorem for continuous functions, and the polar form of complex numbers to choose a direction; over $\mathbb R$, $z^2+1$ has no root, so the base-field hypothesis cannot be dropped.
