Statement

Let T\mathcal{T}T be a topos, X∈TX \in \mathcal{T}X∈T, and M∙M^{\bullet}M∙ a complex of abelian groups in T\mathcal{T}T. Then

R⁡Γ(X, M∙)  ≅  RHom⁡Z(Z⋅X, M∙),Hn(X, M∙)  ≅  Ext⁡Zn(Z⋅X, M∙).\operatorname{R}\Gamma(X,\ M^{\bullet}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X,\ M^{\bullet}\bigr), \qquad H^{n}(X,\ M^{\bullet}) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(\mathbb{Z}\cdot X,\ M^{\bullet}\bigr).RΓ(X, M∙)≅RHomZ​(Z⋅X, M∙),Hn(X, M∙)≅ExtZn​(Z⋅X, M∙).

Notes

⭐ This is the bridge between two languages: on one side the geometric "sections" (Γ(X,−)\Gamma(X,-)Γ(X,−)), on the other the algebraic "derived Hom". With it, every computation in sheaf cohomology translates into an Ext computation in the derived category — and conversely the geometric meaning of Ext becomes clear: it is cohomology on some space.

📌 Compare with "the section formula on Stonean spaces": that one writes Ext as Ext⁡n(M⋅S,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M \cdot S, N)Extn(M⋅S,N), this one writes Ext as Hn(X,N)H^{n}(X, N)Hn(X,N), and together they say exactly that "Hn(X,N)H^{n}(X, N)Hn(X,N) can be computed from a resolution of Z⋅X\mathbb{Z}\cdot XZ⋅X".
