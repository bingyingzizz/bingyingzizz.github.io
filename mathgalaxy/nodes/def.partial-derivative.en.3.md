## Notes · **draft**
⚠️ The existence of partial derivatives **guarantees nothing**: for $f(x, y) = xy/(x^{2} + y^{2})$ (with $f(0,0) = 0$ at the origin), both partial derivatives at the origin exist (both equal $0$), yet along $y = x$ the function satisfies $f \to 1/2 \ne f(0,0)$ — it is not even **continuous**.

⭐ The root of the problem is an asymmetry: existence of partial derivatives is $n$ **one-dimensional** conditions (for scalar $f$; for $f:\mathbb{R}^{n} \to \mathbb{R}^{m}$ it is $mn$ conditions), whereas differentiability (`def.total-differential`) is **one** $n$-dimensional condition.
