def.etale-sheaf

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a scheme.

Write XetX_{\text{et}}Xet​ for the small étale site of XXX: objects are étale maps U→XU \to XU→X (usually restricted to the affine, locally of finite presentation ones, so that the category is essentially small), morphisms are XXX-morphisms, and coverings are jointly surjective étale families {Ui→U}\{U_{i} \to U\}{Ui​→U}.

A presheaf on XetX_{\text{et}}Xet​ is a contravariant functor

F:(Xet)op⟶Ab.\mathcal{F} : \bigl(X_{\text{et}}\bigr)^{\mathrm{op}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ab} .F:(Xet​)op⟶Ab.

It is an étale sheaf if for every étale covering {Ui→U}\{U_{i} \to U\}{Ui​→U} the sequence is exact:

0⟶F(U)⟶∏iF(Ui)⟶∏i,jF(Ui×UUj).0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}(U) \longrightarrow \prod_{i} \mathcal{F}(U_{i}) \longrightarrow \prod_{i,j} \mathcal{F}\bigl(U_{i} \times_{U} U_{j}\bigr).0⟶F(U)⟶i∏​F(Ui​)⟶i,j∏​F(Ui​×U​Uj​).

The category of étale sheaves is written Sh(Xet,Ab)\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{et}}, \mathbf{Ab})Sh(Xet​,Ab).

Notes

How the name "étale" relates in the two places: an étale map of schemes is a morphism simultaneously satisfying:

  • locally of finite presentation;
  • flat;
  • unramified (that is, ΩU/X1=0\Omega^{1}_{U/X} = 0ΩU/X1​=0).

These three are the algebraic counterparts of "local homeomorphism" in topology, but they additionally allow finite coverings (for example Spec⁡C→Spec⁡R\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C} \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{R}SpecC→SpecR: two points covering one).

Why the topology must change: the Zariski topology is too coarse — on Spec⁡k(t)\operatorname{Spec} k(t)Speck(t) it does not see a Galois covering with group kkk. After passing to the étale topology, coefficients such as Z/n\mathbb{Z}/nZ/n can see geometry (Galois groups, Kummer sequences and the whole Weil-conjecture story become meaningful only after this step).

⭐ Zariski coverings are étale coverings (open immersions are étale, and open coverings are of course jointly surjective). Thus the topology "refines" and sheaves "become fewer":

Sh(Xet,Ab)⟶Sh(XZar,Ab)\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{et}}, \mathbf{Ab}) \longrightarrow \mathbf{Sh}(X_{\mathrm{Zar}}, \mathbf{Ab})Sh(Xet​,Ab)⟶Sh(XZar​,Ab)

is "forgetting the étale structure", not an equivalence. In one sentence: étale sheaves are always Zariski sheaves, but not conversely.

⚠️ The technical condition cannot be dropped: the "small étale site" must be a set (not so large that no set can contain it), so one usually takes "affine étale XXX-schemes of locally finite presentation"; different references choose slightly differently, but the resulting topos is the same. This point is not pursued here.

To do étale cohomology one further needs: Sh(Xet,Ab)\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{et}}, \mathbf{Ab})Sh(Xet​,Ab) is a Grothendieck category, it has enough injectives (again via "enough points", with points taken to be geometric points), and compatibility with the long exact sequences of f∗f_{*}f∗​, Rnf∗R^{n}f_{*}Rnf∗​ — that is another whole apparatus.
