# exact sequence　`def.exact-sequence`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.exact-sequence)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.exact-sequence.md)

**What the $0$ at the two ends do**: $0 \to M'$ at the left end says $\ker(f) = 0$, i.e. $f$ is monic; $M'' \to 0$ at the right end says $\operatorname{im}(g) = M''$, i.e. $g$ is epic. Thus "short exact sequence" packages monic, epic, and exact into one statement.

**Another way to write the isomorphism**: a short exact sequence is equivalent to "$M'$ is a subobject of $M$, and $M'' \cong M/M'$". This is the shortest way to make "extension" precise.

📌 Compare with "split short exact sequence": a general exact sequence is **not guaranteed** to be a direct sum; those that can be written as $M \cong M' \oplus M''$ are special cases.
