## Notes · **draft**
⭐ A homeomorphism means "topologically indistinguishable": every **topological property** (compactness, connectedness, Hausdorff, closure) is preserved.

⚠ The converse also deserves care: **metric properties change under a homeomorphism**.

$\mathbb{R}$ is homeomorphic to the open interval $(0, 1)$ ($x \mapsto \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan x$), yet the former is complete and the latter is not — the classic example that completeness is not a topological property, and the dividing line between the "compact / closed" and the "complete / totally bounded" families of properties in metric spaces.
