又称群·group·阿贝尔群·交换群·Abelian group

陈述

设 GGG 是集合，⋅\cdot⋅ 是 GGG 上的二元运算（即函数 G×G→GG \times G \to GG×G→G，(a,b)↦a⋅b(a, b) \mapsto a \cdot b(a,b)↦a⋅b）。称 (G,⋅)(G, \cdot)(G,⋅) 是一个群，当且仅当：

  • 结合律：(ab)c=a(bc)(a b) c = a (b c)(ab)c=a(bc) 对一切 a,b,c∈Ga, b, c \in Ga,b,c∈G 成立；
  • 单位元：存在 e∈Ge \in Ge∈G 使 ea=ae=ae a = a e = aea=ae=a 对一切 aaa 成立；
  • 逆元：对每个 a∈Ga \in Ga∈G 存在 a−1∈Ga^{-1} \in Ga−1∈G 使 aa−1=a−1a=ea a^{-1} = a^{-1} a = eaa−1=a−1a=e。

再满足交换律 ab=baa b = b aab=ba 的群叫阿贝尔群（Abelian group），也叫交换群。

说明

⭐ 格式还是「集合 + 运算 + 公理」：群不是凭空的对象 —— 一个集合、一个函数、三条等式就定出了一个群。判别一个东西是不是群，就是逐条核对这些等式。

单位元与逆元是唯一的：若 e,e′e, e'e,e′ 都是单位元，则 e=ee′=e′e = e e' = e'e=ee′=e′；若 a−1,a′a^{-1}, a'a−1,a′ 都是 aaa 的逆，则 a′=a′e=a′aa−1=ea−1=a−1a' = a' e = a' a a^{-1} = e a^{-1} = a^{-1}a′=a′e=a′aa−1=ea−1=a−1。所以「eee」与「a−1a^{-1}a−1」这两个记号合法。

例子与反例：(Z,+)(\mathbb{Z}, +)(Z,+) 是阿贝尔群；(Q∖{0},⋅)(\mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}, \cdot)(Q∖{0},⋅) 是阿贝尔群；(Z,⋅)(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)(Z,⋅) 不是群（只有 ±1\pm 1±1 有乘法逆元）；

n×nn \times nn×n 可逆矩阵在乘法下是群但不阿贝尔（n≥2n \ge 2n≥2）。

📌 还有一层更弱的：只要求结合律与单位元、不要求逆元，叫幺半群。（N\mathbb{N}N 在加法下就是幺半群不是群。）

各方向用到的假设（群是公理定义，较短判据须保留环境条件）：

  • 群   ⟺  \iff⟺ 结合律、双侧单位元及双侧逆元：定义展开，不含交换律要求。
  • 群   ⟹  \implies⟹ 单位元与逆元唯一：用结合律和两侧单位、逆元等式作消去，无需选择公理来同时选逆元，因为逆元已经唯一。
  • 幺半群中每个元素可逆   ⟹  \implies⟹ 群：直接展开；只有消去律不够，非负整数加法有消去律但正元素无逆元。
  • 有限且非空的消去半群   ⟹  \implies⟹ 群：左右乘法单射由有限性变成满射，进而构造共同单位及逆元；无限情形的上述反例说明不能省有限性。

Statement draft

Let GGG be a set and ⋅\cdot⋅ a binary operation on GGG (that is, a function G×G→GG \times G \to GG×G→G, (a,b)↦a⋅b(a, b) \mapsto a \cdot b(a,b)↦a⋅b). Then (G,⋅)(G, \cdot)(G,⋅) is a group if and only if:

  • associativity: (ab)c=a(bc)(a b) c = a (b c)(ab)c=a(bc) for all a,b,c∈Ga, b, c \in Ga,b,c∈G;
  • identity: there exists e∈Ge \in Ge∈G such that ea=ae=ae a = a e = aea=ae=a for all aaa;
  • inverses: for every a∈Ga \in Ga∈G there exists a−1∈Ga^{-1} \in Ga−1∈G such that aa−1=a−1a=ea a^{-1} = a^{-1} a = eaa−1=a−1a=e.

A group that also satisfies the commutative law ab=baa b = b aab=ba is called an abelian group, or commutative group.

Notes draft

⭐ The format is again "set + operation + axioms": a group is not an object out of nowhere — one set, one function and three identities determine it. To decide whether something is a group, check these identities one by one.

The identity and inverses are unique: if e,e′e, e'e,e′ are both identities, then e=ee′=e′e = e e' = e'e=ee′=e′; if a−1,a′a^{-1}, a'a−1,a′ are both inverses of aaa, then a′=a′e=a′aa−1=ea−1=a−1a' = a' e = a' a a^{-1} = e a^{-1} = a^{-1}a′=a′e=a′aa−1=ea−1=a−1. Hence the notations "eee" and "a−1a^{-1}a−1" are legitimate.

Examples and counterexamples: (Z,+)(\mathbb{Z}, +)(Z,+) is an abelian group; (Q∖{0},⋅)(\mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0\}, \cdot)(Q∖{0},⋅) is an abelian group; (Z,⋅)(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)(Z,⋅) is not a group (only ±1\pm 1±1 have multiplicative inverses);

n×nn \times nn×n invertible matrices form a group under multiplication but not an abelian one (n≥2n \ge 2n≥2).

📌 There is a weaker layer: requiring only associativity and an identity, but not inverses, gives a monoid. (N\mathbb{N}N under addition is a monoid but not a group.)

Which hypothesis each direction uses (a group is defined by axioms; shorter criteria must keep their ambient hypotheses):

  • group   ⟺  \iff⟺ associativity, two-sided identity and two-sided inverses: unwinding the definition, with no commutativity requirement.
  • group   ⟹  \implies⟹ uniqueness of identity and inverses: cancellation using associativity and the two-sided identity/inverse identities; no axiom of choice is needed to select inverses, since the inverse is already unique.
  • every element invertible in a monoid   ⟹  \implies⟹ group: direct expansion; cancellation alone is not enough, since the non-negative integers under addition satisfy cancellation but positive elements have no inverses.
  • finite non-empty cancellation semigroup   ⟹  \implies⟹ group: left and right multiplication are injective, hence bijective by finiteness, which yields a common identity and inverses; the infinite counterexample above shows finiteness cannot be dropped.
