Statement

What this does: Provides a substitute for an inverse for singular and rectangular matrices. The pseudoinverse A+A^{+}A+ is the unique matrix satisfying the four Penrose conditions.

Notes

Meaning of the four conditions: The first two say that AA+AA^{+}AA+ and A+AA^{+}AA+A are idempotent (projections, Idempotent Operator). The last two say that the projections are self-adjoint (orthogonal projections). Thus A+A^{+}A+ acts by orthogonally projecting bbb onto the column space and setting the kernel component to zero, giving the unique minimum-norm least-squares solution.

⭐ A canonical way to select a solution when uniqueness fails: When Ax=bAx = bAx=b is consistent with infinitely many solutions, A+bA^{+}bA+b gives the one of minimum norm. When Ax=bAx = bAx=b is inconsistent, A+bA^{+}bA+b gives a solution of minimum residual. The pseudoinverse thus handles both minimum-norm and least-squares solutions.

⚠️ The pseudoinverse does not satisfy (AB)+=B+A+(AB)^{+} = B^{+}A^{+}(AB)+=B+A+ in general (it holds only in special cases). This contrasts with the inverse identity (AB)−1=B−1A−1(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}(AB)−1=B−1A−1 and is the easiest point to misremember.

⚠️ Notation: Both A+A^{+}A+ and A†A^{\dagger}A† are used (the latter is common in physics). We use A+A^{+}A+ to avoid confusion with the conjugate transpose A∗A^{*}A∗.
