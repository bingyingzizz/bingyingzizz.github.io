Notes

The difference from termwise integration for nonnegative functions: there no convergence hypothesis is needed (nonnegativity rules out cancellation), whereas here the condition is sufficient, not necessary: on a one-point probability space with fj=(−1)j+1/jf_{j} = (-1)^{j+1}/jfj​=(−1)j+1/j one has ∑∫∣fj∣=∞\sum \int |f_{j}| = \infty∑∫∣fj​∣=∞, yet ∑∫fj\sum \int f_{j}∑∫fj​ converges and termwise integration still holds — integrability buys the convenience of allowing both signs.

This is the key tool in the proof of the Fubini–Tonelli theorem.
