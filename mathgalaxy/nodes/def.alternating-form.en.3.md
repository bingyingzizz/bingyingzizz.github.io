## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The criterion for "alternating" (zero when two arguments coincide) is the easiest to use, while the formula for "skew-symmetric" (with signs) is the easiest to compute with — the two agree when $\operatorname{char} K \ne 2$.

⚠ They differ when $\operatorname{char} K = 2$: then $-1 = 1$, so every skew-symmetric form is automatically symmetric, and alternation is the stronger condition.

The convention here is $\operatorname{char} K = 0$, or at least $\ne 2$ (all of $\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{Q}$ qualify).
