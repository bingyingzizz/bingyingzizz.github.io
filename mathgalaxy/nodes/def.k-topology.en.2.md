## Notes · **draft**
The $k$-topology is **the final (finest) topology making all continuous maps $S \to X$ (with $S$ compact Hausdorff) continuous**. It is **finer** than the original topology — there are more open sets (those originally open are of course still $k$-open).

⭐ **Compactly generated = equal to its own $k$-ification**: $X$ compactly generated $\iff$ $kX = X$ (the same topological space). Hence $k$ is an **idempotent** operation: $k(kX) = kX$.
