def.closed-surface

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A 2-dimensional topological manifold SSS (see topological manifold) is called a closed surface if

  • SSS is compact;
  • SSS is connected;
  • SSS has no boundary (each point has a neighborhood modeled on an open subset of R2\mathbb{R}^{2}R2, with no points of half-plane type).

Equivalently, SSS is a compact, connected, Hausdorff, second-countable space without boundary that is locally homeomorphic to R2\mathbb{R}^{2}R2.

Notes

⭐ Each of the three conditions has a role, and dropping any one changes the classification theorem:

  • Dropping connectedness,   ⟹  \implies⟹ becomes a disjoint union of finitely many closed surfaces, and the classification theorem applies to each component.
  • Dropping compactness,   ⟹  \implies⟹ can have infinite genus or infinitely many holes. The classification becomes much more complicated and is outside the scope of this graph.
  • Dropping the requirement of no boundary,   ⟹  \implies⟹ becomes a compact surface with boundary. Capping the boundary components with disks returns to the closed case.

⭐ Distinctions from three other notions of surface (the reason for this node):

Regular Surface and Gauss Map and Orientable Surface concern subsets of R3\mathbb{R}^{3}R3 covered by parametrizations (extrinsic, with a metric and curvature). A Riemann surface is a one-dimensional complex manifold. This node concerns topology alone, with no metric, complex structure, or presumed embedding in Euclidean space.

⭐ Basic examples: the sphere S2S^{2}S2, the torus T2T^{2}T2 (genus 1), the projective plane RP2\mathbb{RP}^{2}RP2 (nonorientable), and the Klein bottle (nonorientable). ⚠ All four already have nodes: sphere, torus, projective plane, and Klein bottle.

📌 The genus in genus is defined only for orientable closed surfaces. The nonorientable case uses a different notion (χ=2−k\chi = 2 - kχ=2−k).
