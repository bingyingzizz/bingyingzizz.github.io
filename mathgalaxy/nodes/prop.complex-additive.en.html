Statement

Let C\mathcal{C}C be an additive category. Complexes form a full additive subcategory C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C) of it:

  • biproducts are taken termwise: (K⊕L)n:=Kn⊕Ln(K \oplus L)^{n} := K^{n} \oplus L^{n}(K⊕L)n:=Kn⊕Ln, dK⊕L:=dK⊕dLd_{K \oplus L} := d_{K} \oplus d_{L}dK⊕L​:=dK​⊕dL​;
  • kernels and cokernels are also termwise: (ker⁡f)n=ker⁡fn(\ker f)^{n} = \ker f^{n}(kerf)n=kerfn, dker⁡f:=dK∣ker⁡fnd_{\ker f} := d_{K}|_{\ker f^{n}}dkerf​:=dK​∣kerfn​;
  • limits and colimits in C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C) are computed termwise.

Notes

"Termwise" is a general fact about functor categories: C(C)\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C})C(C) is a full subcategory of Hom⁡((Z,≤),C)\operatorname{Hom}((\mathbb{Z},\le), \mathcal{C})Hom((Z,≤),C), every construction is performed on each nnn separately, and then one checks that the differentials are compatible. This is why all later limits and colimits of complexes need no separate proof.
