Statement

Let VVV have ordered basis B=(v1,…,vn)\mathcal{B} = (v_1, \ldots, v_n)B=(v1​,…,vn​) and WWW have ordered basis C=(w1,…,wm)\mathcal{C} = (w_1, \ldots, w_m)C=(w1​,…,wm​), both finite-dimensional KKK-vector spaces.

The m×nm \times nm×n matrix [T]BC[T]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{C}}[T]BC​ is called the matrix of TTT with respect to these two bases, where the jjj-th column is the coordinate vector of T(vj)T(v_j)T(vj​) in C\mathcal{C}C.

Then the map T↦[T]BCT \mapsto [T]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{C}}T↦[T]BC​ is an isomorphism of KKK-vector spaces

Hom⁡K(V,W)  ≅  Mm×n(K)\operatorname{Hom}_K(V, W) \;\cong\; M_{m \times n}(K)HomK​(V,W)≅Mm×n​(K)

and composition corresponds to multiplication: if T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W, S:W→US : W \to US:W→U, then

[S∘T]BE=[S]CE  [T]BC[S \circ T]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{E}} = [S]_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathcal{E}}\; [T]_{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{C}}[S∘T]BE​=[S]CE​[T]BC​

Notes

⭐ This is the birth certificate of all matrix theory: a matrix is not a defined object but the coordinates of a linear map in chosen bases (exactly the same mechanism as the entry on coordinates, only with a basis chosen on each side).

⭐ Thus every matrix operation acquires meaning: addition = sum of maps, multiplication = composition, identity matrix = identity map, invertible = isomorphism.

Every formula about matrices can be translated into the language of maps, and conversely — this is why the graph puts the two in separate clusters yet connects them by edges.

⚠ All of this depends on the choice of bases: change the basis and the matrix changes. The same space V=WV = WV=W with the same basis used on both sides gives similarity P−1APP^{-1}APP−1AP; for a general T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W the two bases change independently and one gets equivalence Q−1APQ^{-1}APQ−1AP (e.g. V=W=RV = W = \mathbb{R}V=W=R, T=idT = \mathrm{id}T=id: changing only the basis of the domain to (2)(2)(2) gives [2][2][2], which is not similar to [1][1][1]).

The linear map itself, however, does not change.
