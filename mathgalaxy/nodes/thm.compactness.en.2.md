## Notes · **draft**
It is a **one-sentence corollary**: by the completeness theorem, having a model $\iff$ being consistent; and inconsistency is witnessed by a **finite** proof, which uses only finitely many formulas of $\Gamma$. So inconsistency is always finitely visible, and taking the contrapositive gives compactness.

**Second proof** (avoiding completeness): use ultraproducts / Łoś's theorem — take the directed family of finite subsets as the index set, choose a model of each finite subset, and the ultraproduct is a model of the whole theory. Ultraproducts are not developed here, so this is only a remark.
