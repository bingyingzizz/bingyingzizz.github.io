Statement

Let MMM be an RRR-module, and suppose there are two short exact sequences

0→K→P→ π M→0,0→K1→P1→ π1 M→0,0 \to K \to P \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } M \to 0, \qquad 0 \to K_{1} \to P_{1} \xrightarrow{\ \pi_{1}\ } M \to 0,0→K→P π ​M→0,0→K1​→P1​ π1​ ​M→0,

in which P,P1P, P_{1}P,P1​ are both projective. Then

P1⊕K  ≅  P⊕K1.P_{1} \oplus K \;\cong\; P \oplus K_{1} .P1​⊕K≅P⊕K1​.

In other words, the kernels of the two projective precovers of MMM become isomorphic after each is summed with the projective module from the other precover.

Notes

⭐ Syzygies agree up to projective direct summands, and this conclusion requires the middle terms of both precovers to be projective. Projectivity keeps appearing throughout the theory of syzygies because it is the hypothesis that makes the splitting argument work.

⚠️ The conclusion is an isomorphism of direct sums. The modules KKK and K1K_{1}K1​ need not be isomorphic. To remove this ambiguity, work in the stable category, where objects that differ by projective direct summands are identified up to isomorphism.

Name. This is Schanuel's lemma. It first appeared in Schanuel's unpublished lecture notes and became widely known through texts by Rotman, Cartan–Eilenberg, and others.
