## Notes · **draft**
⭐ This proposition is the **group-theoretic half of the Abel–Ruffini theorem**: the reason a general equation of degree five or more cannot be solved in radicals is here — the generic Galois group of the equation is $S_n$, and $S_n$ for $n \ge 5$ is not solvable.

⭐ The dividing line falls between $A_4$ and $A_5$, and this is **not** an asymptotic phenomenon but a hard jump: $A_4$ has the normal subgroup $V_4$ (so $S_4$ can be split), while $A_5$ has no nontrivial normal subgroup, and $A_5$ is non-abelian, hence not solvable.
