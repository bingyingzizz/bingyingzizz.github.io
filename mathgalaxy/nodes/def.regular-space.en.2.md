# Regular Space　`def.regular-space`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.regular-space)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.regular-space.md)

**Why $T_{1}$ is indispensable**: the indiscrete topology is $T_{3}$ but not $T_{1}$, and certainly not $T_{2}$. After adding $T_{1}$, the set $\{y\}$ is closed, so "$x$ and $\{y\}$ can be separated" yields two disjoint open sets — exactly $T_{2}$.

**Its place in the chain**: regular space $\implies T_{2} \implies T_{1} \implies T_{0}$ (all reverse implications fail).

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ **Convention**: the **T-number denotes the bare property; only the named notion carries $T_{1}$**.

So T3 means a point and a closed set can be separated (without $T_{1}$), while a regular space means T3 plus $T_{1}$.
