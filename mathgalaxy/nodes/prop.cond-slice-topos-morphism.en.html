Statement

Let XXX be a topological space. There is a topos morphism

cX:Cond/X⇄Open(X),c_{X} : \mathrm{Cond}/X \rightleftarrows \mathrm{Open}(X),cX​:Cond/X⇄Open(X),

where Open(X)\mathrm{Open}(X)Open(X) is the topos corresponding to the topological space of open sets of XXX (open sets ordered by inclusion, with its standard topology).

The inverse image sends Y∈Cond/XY \in \mathrm{Cond}/XY∈Cond/X to U↦Hom⁡/X(U‾,Y)U \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{/X}(\underline{U}, Y)U↦Hom/X​(U​,Y); the direct image sends F∈Open(X)F \in \mathrm{Open}(X)F∈Open(X) to lim→⁡U⊆X, s∈F(U)U‾\varinjlim_{U \subseteq X,\ s \in F(U)} \underline{U}lim​U⊆X, s∈F(U)​U​.

Notes

What this is doing. Cond/X\mathrm{Cond}/XCond/X is the world of "doing condensed mathematics with XXX as basepoint"; Open(X)\mathrm{Open}(X)Open(X) is the world of "the open-set lattice of XXX". This morphism connects the two: cohomology computed in the two worlds can be converted into one another.

How to read the shape of the inverse image: Hom⁡/X(U‾,Y)\operatorname{Hom}_{/X}(\underline{U}, Y)Hom/X​(U​,Y) is "over XXX, a map from UUU to YYY". The smaller UUU is, the more maps it can give — this is exactly the "restriction" flavour of sheaves.

How to read the shape of the direct image: lim→⁡U‾\varinjlim \underline{U}lim​U​ is the colimit of "gluing all open sets together" — the colimit of open sets along inclusions is their union.
