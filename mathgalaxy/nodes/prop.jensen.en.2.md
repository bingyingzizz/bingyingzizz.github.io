Equivalently: the more zeros there are, and the closer they are to the origin, the larger the right-hand side (the average of the logarithm of the modulus on the boundary).

## Notes · **draft**
**Proof**: first do the case **without zeros**: put $g = f$ and use the fact that $\log|g|$ is harmonic (`def.harmonic`); the mean-value property of harmonic functions gives $\log|g(0)| = $ the boundary average.

With zeros, "divide out" each zero: put

$$B(z) := \prod_{j=1}^{n} \frac{R(z - z_{j})}{R^{2} - \bar z_{j} z},$$
