prop.additive-iff-preserves-biproduct

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let C,D\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{D}C,D be additive categories and F:C→DF : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}F:C→D a functor. Then the following three are equivalent:

FFF is additive   ⟺  \iff⟺ FFF preserves the zero object and all direct sums   ⟺  \iff⟺ FFF preserves all finite products   ⟺  \iff⟺ FFF preserves all finite coproducts

Notes

⭐ Practical value: to check that a functor is additive, it suffices to check that it preserves biproducts, without touching the group structure on Hom⁡\operatorname{Hom}Hom.

The equivalence of the four relies entirely on "in an additive category, finite product = finite coproduct = biproduct":

  • Additive   ⟺  \iff⟺ preserves the zero object and all direct sums: (  ⟹  )(\implies)(⟹) an additive functor preserves the biproduct structure (given by the kernel–cokernel duality); (⇐)(\Leftarrow)(⇐) one must use the projection/inclusion morphisms of the direct sum to assemble F(f+g)=F(f)+F(g)F(f+g) = F(f)+F(g)F(f+g)=F(f)+F(g) — this is the only step of the four that requires work.
  • Preserves all direct sums   ⟺  \iff⟺ preserves all finite products: this step depends entirely on the axioms of an additive category (finite products are automatically biproducts, so "preserving products = preserving coproducts"). ⚠️ In a general category this step is false.
  • Preserves biproducts: synonymous with the previous item (a biproduct is both a product and a coproduct).

⚠️ The hypothesis "additive category" cannot be removed: in Set\mathbf{Set}Set a functor preserving finite products (such as X↦X×XX \mapsto X \times XX↦X×X) need not preserve coproducts, let alone be additive. ⚠️ "Preserves direct sums" and "preserves coproducts" agree only in an additive category: so the precise reading of this proposition is "between additive categories, preserving products = preserving coproducts = additive".
