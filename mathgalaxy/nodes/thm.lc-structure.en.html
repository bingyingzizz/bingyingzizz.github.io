thm.lc-structure

Statement

Let MMM be a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group. Then MMM contains an open subgroup isomorphic to

V⊕K,V \oplus K,V⊕K,

where VVV is a finite-dimensional real vector space (viewed as an additive group) and KKK is a compact Hausdorff abelian group.

Equivalently, there is an exact sequence

0⟶V⊕K⟶M⟶D⟶0,0 \longrightarrow V \oplus K \longrightarrow M \longrightarrow D \longrightarrow 0,0⟶V⊕K⟶M⟶D⟶0,

where DDD is a discrete abelian group.

Notes

How to read the theorem. Locally compact Hausdorff abelian groups have three basic building blocks: discrete groups, finite-dimensional real vector spaces, and compact Hausdorff groups. The theorem says that every such group is an extension of V⊕KV \oplus KV⊕K by a discrete group.

Why VVV must be finite-dimensional: The additive group of a real vector space is locally compact   ⟺  \iff⟺ it is finite-dimensional. Infinite-dimensional real Banach spaces (such as ℓ2\ell^{2}ℓ2) fail to be locally compact and fall outside the scope of the structure theorem.

What an open subgroup means: V⊕K⊆MV \oplus K \subseteq MV⊕K⊆M is an open subgroup, so the cokernel D=M/(V⊕K)D = M/(V\oplus K)D=M/(V⊕K) carries the quotient topology. Because V⊕KV \oplus KV⊕K is open, the quotient is discrete. Openness is what makes the quotient discrete.

⭐ This theorem drives Chapter 8. It is used to show that higher Ext groups between locally compact abelian groups vanish. Since every object is built by extensions from three classes (discrete, finite-dimensional real Banach, and compact connected), it suffices to verify Ext vanishing for pairs from those classes. This reduces infinitely many kinds of groups to 3×33 \times 33×3 cases.

Proof (standard approach).

  1. Connected component: M0M^{0}M0 (the connected component of the identity) is a closed subgroup. M0M^0M0 is a connected locally compact abelian group, and such groups are precisely those of the form Rn×K\mathbb{R}^{n}\times KRn×K (with KKK a compact connected abelian group). This is classical theory (the no-small-subgroups property and Lie theory separate the compact subgroup part of M0M^{0}M0 from the vector group part).
  2. Totally disconnected quotient: M/M0M/M^{0}M/M0 is a totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff group. Such a group has a compact open subgroup (take a compact neighborhood of the identity and then the closure of the subgroup it generates).
  3. Putting the pieces together: Take M1M_{1}M1​ to be the preimage of a compact open subgroup. Then M1M_{1}M1​ is open and M1/M0M_{1}/M^{0}M1​/M0 is compact. Thus M1M_{1}M1​ is an extension of the compact group KKK by Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn: 0→K→M1→Rn→00 \to K \to M_{1} \to \mathbb{R}^{n} \to 00→K→M1​→Rn→0.
  4. Splitting the extension: Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn is a vector group, and an extension of a compact group by a vector group splits (average over the compact direction, or use a local section and compactness to globalize it). Hence M1≅Rn×KM_{1} \cong \mathbb{R}^{n}\times KM1​≅Rn×K ✓.
  5. Discrete quotient: M1M_{1}M1​ is open   ⟹  \implies⟹ D:=M/M1D := M/M_{1}D:=M/M1​ is discrete in the quotient topology ✓, so 0→V⊕K→M→D→00\to V\oplus K\to M\to D\to 00→V⊕K→M→D→0 is exact. ∎
