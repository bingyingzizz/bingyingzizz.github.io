Statement

What it does: guarantees that “orthogonal decomposition” cancels — if two forms become isomorphic after adding the same thing, they were already isomorphic. Only with this can one speak of the unique decomposition of a form.

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional vector space, β\betaβ a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form over a field KKK (char⁡K≠2\operatorname{char} K \ne 2charK=2), and U,W⊆VU, W \subseteq VU,W⊆V a nondegenerate subspace. If there is an isometry (a linear isomorphism preserving β\betaβ)

U⊥W ≅ U′⊥W,U \perp W \ \cong\ U' \perp W ,U⊥W ≅ U′⊥W,

then U≅U′U \cong U'U≅U′ (as spaces with forms).

Corollary (unique decomposition): a nondegenerate form always splits orthogonally into several hyperbolic planes plus one anisotropic piece, and both the number of hyperbolic planes (the Witt index) and the anisotropic part are uniquely determined.

Notes

Why the hypothesis “nondegenerate”: in the degenerate case cancellation fails (when β=0\beta = 0β=0 the left side is always 000). This is why the whole theory of quadratic forms assumes nondegeneracy first — not pedantry, but the lifeline of cancellation.

⭐ It is the last brick of “classification”: Sylvester's law of inertia gives “the number of signs in a diagonal form is unique” over the reals; Witt cancellation gives “the number of blocks in a decomposition is unique over any field”. The former works only over R\mathbb{R}R, the latter over any field with char ≠\ne= 2.

⚠ char⁡K≠2\operatorname{char} K \ne 2charK=2 cannot be dropped: in characteristic 222 “symmetric” and “alternating” coincide, 12\frac{1}{2}21​ does not exist, and orthogonalization and Witt theory must be rewritten (that is another subject). It is not expanded here.

⭐ A stronger version is the Witt extension lemma: an isometry between nondegenerate subspaces always extends to an isometry of the whole space. Cancellation follows directly; the proof is on the edge imp.witt-cancellation.

Reading and proof. U′⊥WU'\perp WU′⊥W denotes the orthogonal summand inside the isometric image (U′⊆VU'\subseteq VU′⊆V is not required). The full proof is on the edge: first extend f∣Wf|_Wf∣W​ to an isometry V2V_2V2​ of the whole space, then use F−1∘fF^{-1}\circ fF−1∘f to fix WWW, and finally, from the uniqueness of W⊥W^{\perp}W⊥, conclude U≅U′U\cong U'U≅U′.
