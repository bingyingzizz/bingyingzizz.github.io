Statement

Let 0→K→P→ π M→00 \to K \to P \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } M \to 00→K→P π ​M→0 be a short exact sequence of RRR-modules with PPP projective (equivalently: KKK is a syzygy of MMM). Then for every n≥1n \ge 1n≥1 and every RRR-module NNN:

Ext⁡Rn+1(M,N)  ≅  Ext⁡Rn(K,N),Tor⁡n+1R(M,N)  ≅  Tor⁡nR(K,N).\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}_{R}(M, N) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(K, N), \qquad \operatorname{Tor}_{n+1}^{R}(M, N) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Tor}_{n}^{R}(K, N).ExtRn+1​(M,N)≅ExtRn​(K,N),Torn+1R​(M,N)≅TornR​(K,N).

Iterating: for k≥0k \ge 0k≥0, Ext⁡Rn+k(M,N)≅Ext⁡Rn(ΩkM,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n+k}_{R}(M, N) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(\Omega^{k}M, N)ExtRn+k​(M,N)≅ExtRn​(ΩkM,N) (n≥1n \ge 1n≥1).

Notes

Proof (Ext side: resolve NNN injectively). Take an injective resolution of NNN: a quasi-isomorphism N→I∙N \to I^{\bullet}N→I∙. Since IkI^{k}Ik is injective, Hom⁡(−,Ik)\operatorname{Hom}(-, I^{k})Hom(−,Ik) is exact, so applying it termwise to the short exact sequence yields a short exact sequence of complexes (in the opposite direction)

0→Hom⁡(M,I∙)→Hom⁡(P,I∙)→Hom⁡(K,I∙)→0.0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(M, I^{\bullet}) \to \operatorname{Hom}(P, I^{\bullet}) \to \operatorname{Hom}(K, I^{\bullet}) \to 0 .0→Hom(M,I∙)→Hom(P,I∙)→Hom(K,I∙)→0.

Take the long exact cohomology sequence

⋯→Ext⁡n(M,N)→Ext⁡n(P,N)→Ext⁡n(K,N)→ δ Ext⁡n+1(M,N)→Ext⁡n+1(P,N)→⋯\cdots \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, N) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(P, N) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(K, N) \xrightarrow{\ \delta\ } \operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, N) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(P, N) \to \cdots⋯→Extn(M,N)→Extn(P,N)→Extn(K,N) δ ​Extn+1(M,N)→Extn+1(P,N)→⋯

Since PPP is projective, Ext⁡k(P,N)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(P, N) = 0Extk(P,N)=0 for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1, so every segment degenerates into an isomorphism: δ\deltaδ is bijective, i.e. Ext⁡n+1(M,N)≅Ext⁡n(K,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, N) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(K, N)Extn+1(M,N)≅Extn(K,N) ✓.

Tor side (dual: tensor the short exact sequence with NNN). ⊗\otimes⊗ is right exact, so after tensoring P⊗N→M⊗NP\otimes N \to M\otimes NP⊗N→M⊗N is still surjective; the injectivity at the left end may fail, which is exactly where Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor appears. Take the long exact sequence

⋯→Tor⁡n+1(P,N)→Tor⁡n+1(M,N)→Tor⁡n(K,N)→Tor⁡n(P,N)→⋯\cdots \to \operatorname{Tor}_{n+1}(P, N) \to \operatorname{Tor}_{n+1}(M, N) \to \operatorname{Tor}_{n}(K, N) \to \operatorname{Tor}_{n}(P, N) \to \cdots⋯→Torn+1​(P,N)→Torn+1​(M,N)→Torn​(K,N)→Torn​(P,N)→⋯

PPP projective   ⟹  \implies⟹ flat   ⟹  \implies⟹ Tor⁡k(P,N)=0\operatorname{Tor}_{k}(P, N) = 0Tork​(P,N)=0 for k≥1k \ge 1k≥1, hence Tor⁡n+1(M,N)≅Tor⁡n(K,N)\operatorname{Tor}_{n+1}(M, N) \cong \operatorname{Tor}_{n}(K, N)Torn+1​(M,N)≅Torn​(K,N) ✓. ∎

⭐ Why it is called a shift: it trades "the invariant of MMM in degree n+1n+1n+1" for "the invariant of its syzygy in degree nnn".

Thus any statement about Ext⁡n\operatorname{Ext}^{n}Extn can be lowered step by step until it lands on Ext⁡0=Hom⁡\operatorname{Ext}^{0} = \operatorname{Hom}Ext0=Hom or Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1 — the only levels where elements are visible.

⚠️ The hypothesis "PPP projective" cannot be dropped: the proof of Ext⁡n+1(M,N)≅Ext⁡n(K,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M,N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^n(K,N)Extn+1(M,N)≅Extn(K,N) uses exactly the vanishing of the PPP term. For a general short exact sequence there is only a five-term long exact sequence, not an isomorphism.

Combined with syzygies: Ext⁡n(M,N)≅Ext⁡1(Ωn−1M,N)\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, N) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\Omega^{n-1}M, N)Extn(M,N)≅Ext1(Ωn−1M,N), and with "classification of first-order extensions" this turns higher Ext into "extensions of NNN by Ωn−1M\Omega^{n-1}MΩn−1M".
