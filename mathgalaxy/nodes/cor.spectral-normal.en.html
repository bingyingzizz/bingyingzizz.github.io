Statement

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space and T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V normal (T∗T=TT∗T^{\ast}T = TT^{\ast}T∗T=TT∗). Then there is an orthonormal basis of VVV in which TTT is diagonal:

T=UΛ U†,U†U=I, Λ=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn)T = U \Lambda\, U^{\dagger}, \qquad U^{\dagger}U = I,\ \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n)T=UΛU†,U†U=I, Λ=diag(λ1​,…,λn​)

Moreover:

  • TTT is self-adjoint   ⟺  \iff⟺ all eigenvalues are real;
  • TTT is unitary   ⟺  \iff⟺ all eigenvalues have modulus 1;
  • TTT is an orthogonal projection   ⟺  \iff⟺ the eigenvalues all lie in {0,1}\{0, 1\}{0,1} (under the hypothesis that TTT is normal one does not need to require TTT self-adjoint again: a normal operator whose spectrum lies in R\mathbb{R}R is automatically self-adjoint).

Notes

⭐ These three "property of TTT   ⟺  \iff⟺ property of eigenvalues" statements are the most useful by-product of the spectral theorem: they translate a problem about an operator into a problem about a set of numbers.

⭐ Complex normality is the common generalization of real symmetry and real orthogonality: over the complex field both can be diagonalized; over the reals only the symmetric ones can.

⚠ The projection clause holds only for self-adjoint operators: a general idempotent (a projection) need not be unitarily diagonalizable.

Which hypothesis each direction uses (the numerical criteria for the spectrum rest on the complex normal spectral theorem):

  • Unitary diagonalization   ⟹  \implies⟹ normal: a diagonal matrix commutes with its adjoint, by direct computation; normal   ⟹  \implies⟹ unitary diagonalization uses the finite-dimensional complex spectral theorem and the existence of eigenvalues over the complex numbers.
  • In that diagonalization, self-adjoint   ⟺  \iff⟺ all eigenvalues real, and unitary   ⟺  \iff⟺ all eigenvalues have modulus 111, are both termwise computations with the adjoint; the converses cannot be detached from the normality hypothesis.
  • Orthogonal projection   ⟹  \implies⟹ self-adjoint with spectrum in {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1}, by idempotence; conversely, the self-adjoint spectral decomposition gives an idempotent, hence an orthogonal projection. Once normality is assumed in this node, spectrum in {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1} already suffices.
  • A non-normal Jordan block (1101)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)(10​11​) has spectrum entirely real of modulus 111, yet is neither self-adjoint nor unitary; a nontrivial rotation of the real plane has no real eigenbasis, showing that the complex hypothesis matters.
