def.fine-sheaf · fine

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a paracompact Hausdorff space and F\mathcal{F}F a sheaf of abelian groups on XXX. Call F\mathcal{F}F a fine sheaf if F\mathcal{F}F admits partitions of unity: for every locally finite open cover of XXX, say {Ui}\{U_{i}\}{Ui​}, there exist sheaf endomorphisms ηi:F→F\eta_{i}:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}ηi​:F→F with supp⁡ηi⊆Ui\operatorname{supp}\eta_{i}\subseteq U_{i}suppηi​⊆Ui​ and locally finite sum ∑iηi=id⁡F\sum_{i}\eta_{i}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{F}}∑i​ηi​=idF​.

Notes

Typical example: the sheaf of C∞C^{\infty}C∞-modules (differential forms) on a smooth manifold is fine; a partition of unity directly gives ηi\eta_{i}ηi​ (multiplication by ρi\rho_{i}ρi​).

Properties: a fine sheaf is necessarily soft (apply partitions of unity to extend sections from closed sets); soft sheaves on a paracompact Hausdorff space have no higher cohomology. Hence fine resolutions compute sheaf cohomology.

Relation to flabby sheaves: fine and flabby sheaves do not in general contain one another; the Godement resolution uses flabby sheaves, while the de Rham complex uses fine sheaves, and both lead to acyclicity.

⚠ The partition of unity in the definition needs paracompactness; without it, the usual definition of "fine" uses a C(X)C(X)C(X)-module structure instead.
