Statement

In one sentence: Γijk\Gamma^{k}_{ij}Γijk​ and ωjk\omega^{k}_{j}ωjk​ are the same thing (connection coefficients) as seen in two frames.

Notes

The real relation to ① and Γ\GammaΓ: the coordinate frame ∂i\partial_{i}∂i​ is generally neither orthogonal nor normalized; after orthonormalization eie_{i}ei​ is no longer a coordinate frame ([e1,e2]≠0[e_{1},e_{2}]\ne 0[e1​,e2​]=0), and the extra term ⟨[ei,ej],ek⟩\langle [e_{i},e_{j}],e_{k}\rangle⟨[ei​,ej​],ek​⟩ is precisely the price of this.

So "ωjk(ei)\omega^{k}_{j}(e_{i})ωjk​(ei​) equals some Γ\GammaΓ" holds literally only when the metric has already been flattened by the coordinates (flat Cartesian coordinates).

Two ways of writing along a curve, the most useful comparison:

  • coordinate frame: γ˙=x˙k∂k\dot\gamma = \dot x^{k}\partial_{k}γ˙​=x˙k∂k​, ∇γ˙γ˙=(x¨k+Γijkx˙ix˙j)∂k\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = \left(\ddot x^{k}+\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\dot x^{i}\dot x^{j}\right)\partial_{k}∇γ˙​​γ˙​=(x¨k+Γijk​x˙ix˙j)∂k​;
  • orthonormal frame: γ˙=yjej\dot\gamma = y^{j}e_{j}γ˙​=yjej​, ∇γ˙γ˙=(y˙k+ωjk(γ˙)yj)ek\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = \left(\dot y^{k}+\omega^{k}_{j}(\dot\gamma)y^{j}\right)e_{k}∇γ˙​​γ˙​=(y˙​k+ωjk​(γ˙​)yj)ek​.

The second formula has no quadratic term, because the frame itself is rotating: the quadratic term has been absorbed into y˙\dot yy˙​ and ω\omegaω. "Whether the geodesic equation has a quadratic term" depends on the frame, not on the surface.
