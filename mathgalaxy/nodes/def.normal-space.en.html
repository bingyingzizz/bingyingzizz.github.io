Statement

A topological space XXX is normal if it is T1T_{1}T1​ and any two disjoint closed sets A,BA, BA,B can be separated by open sets: there are open sets U⊇AU \supseteq AU⊇A and V⊇BV \supseteq BV⊇B with U∩V=∅U \cap V = \emptysetU∩V=∅.

Replacing "disjoint closed sets" by "a point and a closed set not containing it" gives a regular space; replacing it by "two points" gives a Hausdorff space.

Notes

The hierarchy of separation axioms (each step strengthens the previous one):

  • T1T_{1}T1​: singletons are closed;
  • Hausdorff: two points can be separated by open sets;
  • regular: a point and a closed set not containing it can be separated   ⟹  \implies⟹ Hausdorff;
  • normal: two disjoint closed sets can be separated   ⟹  \implies⟹ regular.

Compact Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ normal (the most frequently used consequence of compactness: in a compact space two disjoint closed sets can be separated by finitely many pairs of open sets). Hence spaces such as [0,1][0,1][0,1] and βI\beta IβI are normal — so Urysohn's lemma always applies there.

The full proof runs along two steps, "compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ paracompact" plus "paracompact +++ Hausdorff   ⟹  \implies⟹ normal", see 仿紧 Hausdorff 空间正规; ⚠️ normality is not hereditary: a subspace of a normal space need not be normal. This differs from both "compact" and "Hausdorff".
