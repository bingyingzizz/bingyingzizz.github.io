Statement

Let dim⁡V=n<∞\dim V = n < \inftydimV=n<∞ and T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V. Then

  • μT∣χT\mu_T \mid \chi_TμT​∣χT​ (the minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial); in particular deg⁡μT≤n\deg \mu_T \le ndegμT​≤n;
  • μT\mu_TμT​ and χT\chi_TχT​ have exactly the same roots in the algebraic closure of KKK (that is, the same set of eigenvalues);
  • hence TTT is diagonalizable (viewed over the algebraic closure)   ⟺  \iff⟺ μT\mu_TμT​ has no repeated roots.

Notes

⭐ The first of the three is a direct consequence of Cayley–Hamilton plus the ideal property: χT∈IT=(μT)\chi_T \in I_T = (\mu_T)χT​∈IT​=(μT​).

⭐ The proof of the second (same roots) is worth remembering: if λ\lambdaλ is an eigenvalue with eigenvector vvv, then μT(T)v=μT(λ)v\mu_T(T)v = \mu_T(\lambda) vμT​(T)v=μT​(λ)v, while μT(T)=0\mu_T(T) = 0μT​(T)=0 and v≠0v \ne 0v=0, hence μT(λ)=0\mu_T(\lambda) = 0μT​(λ)=0;

the converse follows from μT∣χT\mu_T \mid \chi_TμT​∣χT​.

⚠ The phrase "viewed over the algebraic closure" in the third assertion cannot be omitted: whether μT\mu_TμT​ has repeated roots must be decided in an extension field (the rotation over R\mathbb{R}R: μT=x2+1\mu_T = x^{2} + 1μT​=x2+1 has no repeated roots, is diagonalizable over C\mathbb{C}C, but not over R\mathbb{R}R).

Hypotheses used in each direction (finite dimension guarantees that the polynomial exists; diagonalization also needs splitting and no repeated roots):

  • μT∣χT\mu_T\mid\chi_TμT​∣χT​: uses the finite-dimensional Cayley–Hamilton theorem and the fact that the annihilating ideal is generated by μT\mu_TμT​; it is not an expansion of the definition of divisibility. The infinite-dimensional shift operator may have no nonzero annihilating polynomial.
  • eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ   ⟹  \implies⟹ μT(λ)=0\mu_T(\lambda)=0μT​(λ)=0: apply μT(T)\mu_T(T)μT​(T) to a nonzero eigenvector; the converse follows from divisibility, giving χT(λ)=0\chi_T(\lambda)=0χT​(λ)=0, and then the finite-dimensional determinant criterion.
  • diagonalizable   ⟹  \implies⟹ the minimal polynomial splits and has no repeated roots: compute coordinatewise in an eigenbasis and use the annihilating ideal; it is not a literal rewriting of the definition of diagonalizability.
  • minimal polynomial splits with no repeated roots   ⟹  \implies⟹ diagonalizable: needs the Bezout identity for coprime factors, or polynomial interpolation to construct the spectral projections; no spectral theorem and no characteristic zero.
  • If one only says "no repeated roots", diagonalization must be discussed over the algebraic closure; the real plane rotation with x2+1x^2+1x2+1 has no repeated roots yet is not diagonalizable over the reals. Nor can "no repeated roots" be dropped: the minimal polynomial of a nonzero 2×2 nilpotent Jordan block is x2x^2x2.
