陈述

实数集 R\mathbb{R}R 不可数：不存在从 N\mathbb{N}N 到 R\mathbb{R}R 的满射，即 R\mathbb{R}R 不能排成一个序列。

更强的结论：

∣R∣=∣P(N)∣=2ℵ0>ℵ0|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0∣R∣=∣P(N)∣=2ℵ0​>ℵ0​

说明

⭐ 证明走闭区间套 + 对角线（见边）：假设 R\mathbb{R}R 可以排成 x1,x2,…x_1, x_2, \ldotsx1​,x2​,…，就用二分法造一串套起来的闭区间，把 xnx_nxn​ 逐个排除出去，再用完备性取出它们唯一的公共点 —— 它不等于任何一个 xnx_nxn​。

⚠ 整段证明只用到 R\mathbb{R}R 的完备性（确界原理），不需要基数理论；∣R∣=2ℵ0|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}∣R∣=2ℵ0​ 那一半是拿二进制展开与 P(N)\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})P(N) 配对，再引用 Cantor 定理。

这条定理最反直觉的地方：Q\mathbb{Q}Q 与 R\mathbb{R}R 都稠密、都可以逼近彼此，但 Q\mathbb{Q}Q 可数（排得成一个序列）、R\mathbb{R}R 不可数。「稠密」与「一样多」是两回事 —— 这也正是分析必须做实数、不能只做有理数的根本原因。

Abstract reviewed

R\mathbb{R}R is uncountable — there is no surjection N→R\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}N→R — and in fact ∣R∣=∣P(N)∣=2ℵ0>ℵ0|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0∣R∣=∣P(N)∣=2ℵ0​>ℵ0​. Uncountability is proved by nested closed intervals and completeness alone, with no cardinal theory; the cardinality statement pairs binary expansions with P(N)\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})P(N) and quotes Cantor. The counterintuitive point: Q\mathbb{Q}Q and R\mathbb{R}R are each dense in the other, yet Q\mathbb{Q}Q is countable — density and cardinality are different things, which is why analysis needs R\mathbb{R}R.

Statement reviewed

The real line R\mathbb{R}R is uncountable: there is no surjection from N\mathbb{N}N onto R\mathbb{R}R, that is, R\mathbb{R}R cannot be arranged as a sequence.

A stronger conclusion:

∣R∣=∣P(N)∣=2ℵ0>ℵ0|\mathbb{R}| = |\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})| = 2^{\aleph_0} > \aleph_0∣R∣=∣P(N)∣=2ℵ0​>ℵ0​

Notes reviewed

⭐ The proof goes by nested closed intervals + diagonalisation (see the edge): assume R\mathbb{R}R can be arranged as x1,x2,…x_1, x_2, \ldotsx1​,x2​,…, then bisect to build a nested sequence of closed intervals, ruling out the xnx_nxn​ one by one, and use completeness to extract their unique common point — which differs from every xnx_nxn​.

⚠ The whole proof uses only the completeness of R\mathbb{R}R (the least-upper-bound principle); cardinal theory is not needed. The half saying ∣R∣=2ℵ0|\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}∣R∣=2ℵ0​ matches binary expansions against P(N)\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})P(N) and then quotes Cantor's theorem.

The most counter-intuitive point of this theorem: Q\mathbb{Q}Q and R\mathbb{R}R are both dense and each can be approximated by the other, yet Q\mathbb{Q}Q is countable (it can be arranged as a sequence) while R\mathbb{R}R is not. "Dense" and "equally many" are two different things — and this is precisely why analysis has to be done over the reals rather than over the rationals.
