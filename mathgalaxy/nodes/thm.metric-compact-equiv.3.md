## English abstract · **draft** · machine-drafted, pending author review; Chinese is authoritative（续）
For a metric space $X$, the following are equivalent: $X$ is compact (every open cover has a finite subcover), $X$ is sequentially compact (every sequence has a subsequence converging in $X$), and $X$ is complete and totally bounded. The proof is assembled on the edge: compactness yields sequential compactness by a neighbourhood argument, sequential compactness gives completeness and total boundedness, and the remaining step uses $\varepsilon$-nets and Lebesgue numbers.
