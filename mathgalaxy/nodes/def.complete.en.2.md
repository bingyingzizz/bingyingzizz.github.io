## Notes · **draft**
“Convergent $\implies Cauchy$” always holds; **“$Cauchy \implies$ converges” is the content of completeness**.

Examples: $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, $\mathbb{C}^{n}$, and every Banach space are complete; $\mathbb{Q}$ is not (3, 3.1, 3.14, …, a Cauchy sequence converging to $\sqrt 2$ that does not converge in $\mathbb{Q}$).

**Closed subsets inherit completeness**: when $X$ is complete, $A \subseteq X$ is complete as a subspace $\iff A$ closed in $X$. This will be used below in the characterisation of compactness of subsets.
