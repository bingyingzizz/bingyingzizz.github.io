# the disk does not retract onto the circle　`thm.no-retraction-disk`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=thm.no-retraction-disk)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.no-retraction-disk.md)

with $r(z) = z$ for all $z \in S^{1}$. Such an $r$, if it existed, would be called a **retraction** of $S^{1}$ in $D^{2}$, and $S^{1}$ a **retract** of $D^{2}$. Equivalently: the inclusion $i : S^{1} \hookrightarrow D^{2}$ has no left inverse.

## Notes · **draft**
**What the proof actually does**: apply $\pi_{1}$ to $r \circ i = \operatorname{id}_{S^{1}}$ to obtain $r_{*} \circ i_{*} = \operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{Z}}$.

But $\pi_{1}(D^{2}) = 1$ (the disc is convex $\implies$ contractible), so $i_{*}$ can only be the zero map, and the composite must be zero — a contradiction.
