**Example 3** is the counterexample — it is precisely the source of "products behave badly": $\mathbb{R}^{I}$ is too fine, so fine that there are not enough compact Hausdorff spaces to detect its topology. $k$-ification replaces it by the coarser $k(\mathbb{R}^{I})$, which behaves well.
