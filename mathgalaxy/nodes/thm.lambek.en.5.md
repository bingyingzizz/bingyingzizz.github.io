**Step 3 (induct on $n$)**: When $n = 1$, the submodule $K$ is a finitely generated right ideal, so the hypothesis applies. When $n > 1$, write $R^{n} = R^{n-1} \oplus R$ and define $K_{1} := K \cap R^{n-1}$, with $K_{2}$ the projection of $K$ onto the second component (a finitely generated **right** ideal of $R$, on the same side as the ideals in the criterion. Regard $K \subseteq R^{n}$ as a right submodule). By **Noetherianity**, $K_{1}$ is **finitely generated** as a submodule of $R^{n-1}$, so the induction hypothesis applies directly (the non-Noetherian case requires a separate filtered colimit argument, omitted here).
