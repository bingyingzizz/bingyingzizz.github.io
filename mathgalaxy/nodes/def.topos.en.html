def.topos

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A category T\mathcal{T}T is called a topos if

  1. there is a small set of generators;
  2. finite limits exist;
  3. coproducts exist and are disjoint and universal (equivalently: all colimits exist);
  4. equivalence relations are effective and universal.

Notes

The only difference from a pretopos is item 1: a topos = a pretopos with a small set of generators. So "a topos is a pretopos shrunk until a set can control it".

Examples: Set\mathbf{Set}Set, G-SetG\text{-}\mathbf{Set}G-Set are topoi; the presheaf category C^\widehat{\mathcal{C}}C is a topos; sheaf categories of sites are topoi; sheaves on a topological space and its étalé space also form a topos. Top\mathbf{Top}Top is not a topos.

⭐ The category of condensed sets is a topos — this is the foundation of the Cond section later.

Which of the four is unique to topoi (the difference from pretopoi is all in item 1 and the strengthening of item 3):

  • ① Small set of generators: this is the "smallness" condition, which pretopoi lack. It guarantees that a topos behaves well enough (supports internal language, admits sheafification), and it is the hypothesis for "T≃Sh⁡(C,J)\mathcal{T} \simeq \operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J)T≃Sh(C,J) with C\mathcal{C}C small". ⚠️ Without it, the category may be "too large" to be a sheaf category.
  • ② Finite limits: same as pretopos, a purely structural requirement.
  • ③ Coproducts exist and are disjoint and universal (equivalently: all colimits exist): one notch stronger than a pretopos — a pretopos requires only finite coproducts. Together with ② this gives "all colimits can be computed", which is what makes "sheafification" possible (sheafification takes colimits along sieves).
  • ④ Equivalence relations are effective and universal: same as pretopos.

Together with pretopos: topos === pretopos +++ "small generator +++ arbitrary colimits". So a "pretopos" is "a topos lacking smallness and large colimits"; many constructions (internal equivalence relations, effective epimorphisms, kernel pairs) already work at that level.

⚠️ The four items cannot be dropped at will: drop ①, and Sh⁡(C)\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal{C})Sh(C) (with C\mathcal{C}C large) still satisfies ②③④ but is not a topos (no small generator); keep only "coproducts exist" from ③ without "disjoint universal", and things like Grpop\mathbf{Grp}^{\mathrm{op}}Grpop creep in; drop ④, and "quotient = equivalence relation" fails, so the "set" semantics of the internal language collapses.

⚠️ "Disjoint and universal" in ② is the most often ignored: merely saying "coproducts exist" does not make a topos — coproducts in a topos must be stable under pullback (universal); this is the source of the behaviour of "disjoint unions" in the internal language.
