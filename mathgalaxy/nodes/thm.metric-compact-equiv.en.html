thm.metric-compact-equiv

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Abstract

For a metric space XXX, the following are equivalent: XXX is compact (every open cover has a finite subcover), XXX is sequentially compact (every sequence has a subsequence converging in XXX), and XXX is complete and totally bounded. The proof is assembled on the edge: compactness yields sequential compactness by a neighbourhood argument, sequential compactness gives completeness and total boundedness, and the remaining step uses ε\varepsilonε-nets and Lebesgue numbers. The equivalence is a metric-space phenomenon; in general topological spaces the implications fail.

Statement

Let XXX be a metric space with metric ddd. Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

  • (a) XXX is compact: every open cover has a finite subcover;
  • (b) XXX is sequentially compact: every sequence has a subsequence converging in XXX;
  • (c) XXX is complete and totally bounded.

Notes

The equivalence of compactness, sequential compactness, and completeness plus total boundedness is proved on the edge imp.metric-compact-equiv-proof: compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ sequentially compact uses the contradiction that a neighbourhood of every point meets only finitely many terms; sequentially compact   ⟹  \implies⟹ complete/totally bounded uses Cauchy subsequences and ε\varepsilonε-nets; totally bounded   ⟹  \implies⟹ compact uses Lebesgue numbers.
