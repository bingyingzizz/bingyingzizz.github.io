Statement

Let RRR be a ring with identity. A left RRR-module III is injective if for every injection A↣BA \rightarrowtail BA↣B and every RRR-linear map A→IA \to IA→I, there exists (in general not unique) an RRR-linear map B→IB \to IB→I making the diagram commute:

A⊆Bf↓↓∃f~I=I\begin{array}{ccc} A & \subseteq & B \\[2pt] {\scriptstyle f}\downarrow & & \downarrow{\scriptstyle \exists\, \widetilde{f}} \\[2pt] I & = & I \end{array}Af↓I​⊆=​B↓∃f​I​

Equivalent formulation (dual to projective objects): the functor Hom⁡R(−,I)\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-, I)HomR​(−,I) sends injections to surjections, that is, it is exact.

Notes

Reading: injective means "extensions can always be made". Note that the extension is not unique, so this is not a universal property (unlike the tensor product) but only an existence condition — which makes injectivity hard to characterize pointwise and forces one to use criteria (内射的理想判据).

Dual to projective: reversing all arrows gives the projective objects in 投射 / 内射对象. In R-ModR\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}R-Mod, projective = direct summand of a free module, injective = cannot be embedded as a proper submodule of a larger module (see 内射的单射可裂).

Examples: over a field every module is injective (vector spaces are free, free implies projective, and over a field projective and injective coincide); Z\mathbb{Z}Z is not injective: the inclusion Z↣Q\mathbb{Z} \rightarrowtail \mathbb{Q}Z↣Q has no retraction, because Hom⁡Z(Q,Z)=0\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{Z}) = 0HomZ​(Q,Z)=0.

📌 Reduction route for the criterion: it suffices to test "AAA is an ideal of BBB" (内射的理想判据); for Z\mathbb{Z}Z-modules this further reduces to "divisible" (内射等价于可除).
