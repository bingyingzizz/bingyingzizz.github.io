Statement

A sequence {fn}\{f_n\}{fn​} of measurable functions is Cauchy in measure iff

∀ε>0,μ({x:∣fn(x)−fm(x)∣≥ε})→0(m,n→∞)\forall\varepsilon > 0, \mu(\{x : |f_n(x) - f_m(x)| \ge \varepsilon\}) \to 0\quad (m, n \to \infty)∀ε>0,μ({x:∣fn​(x)−fm​(x)∣≥ε})→0(m,n→∞)

that is: in the sense of measure, once the indices are large enough the terms get closer and closer to each other.

Notes

Cauchy in measure Cauchy  ⟺  Cauchy \iffCauchy⟺ convergence in measure: both directions can be proved (convergence   ⟹  Cauchy\implies Cauchy⟹Cauchy by the triangle inequality; Cauchy  ⟹  Cauchy \impliesCauchy⟹ convergence is the next theorem).

This is one virtue of the “in measure” topology: it is complete. By contrast, a.e. convergence is neither strong nor complete, while L1L^{1}L1 convergence is complete (Riesz–Fischer).
