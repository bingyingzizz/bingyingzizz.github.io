# algebraically closed field　`def.algebraically-closed`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.algebraically-closed)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.algebraically-closed.md)

⚠ **Algebraic closures are unique up to isomorphism** (all are algebraic over $F$ and algebraically closed), but **not unique up to a unique isomorphism** — for example the two embeddings of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ can land in two different places inside $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

📌 Relation to Galois theory: $\overline{F}/F$ is in general **not** a finite extension, hence not a Galois extension (the fundamental theorem requires finite). Its “Galois group” is the profinite group $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)$, which calls for the infinite version of the theory.
