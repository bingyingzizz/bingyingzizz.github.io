# Isotropic Vector　`def.isotropic-vector`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.isotropic-vector)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.isotropic-vector.md)

⚠️ The existence of isotropic vectors does not depend on the **basis** ($q(v) = 0$ is a coordinate-free statement), but whether they exist depends on the **form**: a real symmetric form is governed by its inertia indices — as soon as $q$ is indefinite (both $p, q$ nonzero) there are isotropic vectors; if it is positive or negative definite there are none.

⭐ In relativity this is exactly "light-like": take $\beta$ to be the Minkowski form, and isotropic vectors are the directions of light speed. Mathematically the same thing is called "null vector / isotropic vector", just under different names.
