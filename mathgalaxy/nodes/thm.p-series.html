Statement draft

Let p∈Rp \in \mathbb{R}p∈R. Then the series

∑n=1∞1np\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{p}}n=1∑∞​np1​

converges if and only if p>1p > 1p>1.

Notes draft

⭐ p=1p = 1p=1 is the harmonic series (divergent), p=2p = 2p=2 is ∑1/n2=π2/6\sum 1/n^{2} = \pi^{2}/6∑1/n2=π2/6 (convergent) — the two are standard examples where "tests fail on the boundary": the root test gives α=1\alpha = 1α=1 for both.

⭐ The proof is the most beautiful application of Cauchy 凝聚判别法 (Cauchy's condensation test): when an=n−pa_n = n^{-p}an​=n−p is monotonically decreasing, the original series converges   ⟺  \iff⟺ ∑2ka2k=∑(21−p)k\sum 2^{k} a_{2^{k}} = \sum (2^{1-p})^{k}∑2ka2k​=∑(21−p)k converges, and the latter is a geometric series with ratio 21−p2^{1-p}21−p, which converges   ⟺  \iff⟺ 21−p<1  ⟺  p>12^{1-p} < 1 \iff p > 121−p<1⟺p>1.
