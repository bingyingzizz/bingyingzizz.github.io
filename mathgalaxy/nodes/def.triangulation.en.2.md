# triangulation of a space　`def.triangulation`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.triangulation)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.triangulation.md)

If such a $(K, h)$ exists, then $X$ is **triangulable**. The image of each simplex under $h$ is called a **face** of $X$
(a vertex, edge, or triangle). Thus $X$ is expressed as a union of triangles, with any two triangles that meet intersecting along a **whole edge** or at a vertex.

## Notes · **draft**
⚠️ A triangulation consists of **combinatorial data and a homeomorphism placing those data in the space**. Both are part of the triangulation.

Thus triangulability of $X$ is a topological property, whereas the form of $K$ is combinatorial data. The two must be distinguished.
