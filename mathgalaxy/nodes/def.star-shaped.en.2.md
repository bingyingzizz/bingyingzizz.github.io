## Notes · **draft**
⭐ A **nonempty** convex set is always star-shaped: choose any $x_{0} \in C$, convexity gives the whole segment. The empty set is convex but has no point at all, hence is not star-shaped — the hypothesis "nonempty" cannot be dropped. The converse fails: a cross in the plane is star-shaped about its centre but not convex.

⚠ A star centre need not be unique; the Poincaré lemma only requires that the region be contractible to a point, so star-shapedness is weaker than convexity (see lem.poincare-lemma).
