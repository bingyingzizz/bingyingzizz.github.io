Statement

Let KKK be algebraically closed, T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V, dim⁡V<∞\dim V < \inftydimV<∞. Then there is a basis of VVV in which the matrix of TTT is block diagonal

J=(J1⋱Js),Jk=(λk1⋱⋱⋱1λk)J = \begin{pmatrix} J_{1} & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & J_{s} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad J_{k} = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda_{k} & 1 & & \\ & \ddots & \ddots & \\ & & \ddots & 1 \\ & & & \lambda_{k} \end{pmatrix}J=​J1​​⋱​Js​​​,Jk​=​λk​​1⋱​⋱⋱​1λk​​​

each JkJ_kJk​ is a Jordan block of the form λkI+Nk\lambda_k I + N_kλk​I+Nk​ (NkN_kNk​ being the nilpotent "up-shift" matrix).

Equivalent formulation: TTT is diagonalizable   ⟺  \iff⟺ all Jordan blocks are 1×11 \times 11×1.

Notes

⭐ This is the only complete classification theorem in finite-dimensional linear algebra: over an algebraically closed field, "similarity" is completely determined by the Jordan type (block sizes and the distribution of eigenvalues).

It also gives a complete list of "similarity invariants".

⭐ The two halves of the proof: primary decomposition (split by eigenvalue) plus, on each root subspace, taking "the chain ker⁡Nk\ker N^{k}kerNk" of the nilpotent operator N=T−λIN = T - \lambda IN=T−λI and extending the basis layer by layer (this step uses 商空间 and induction).

⚠ All three hypotheses are needed: algebraically closed (otherwise there is no λ\lambdaλ), finite-dimensional (otherwise there may be no eigenvalue), and KKK a field.

Over R\mathbb{R}R one can only use the "real Jordan form" or switch to the rational canonical form (有理标准形).
