Statement

Assume that the characteristic polynomial of AAA splits over KKK (otherwise not even a Jordan form exists: over R\mathbb{R}R the rotation (0−110)\begin{pmatrix}0 & -1\cr 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}(01​−10​) is a counterexample). Under this hypothesis the Jordan normal form is unique up to "the order of the Jordan blocks", that is, A∼BA \sim BA∼B if and only if AAA and BBB have the same Jordan type (blocks disregarded as to order):

A∼B  ⟺  μA(λ)=μB(λ)  (∀λ)A \sim B \iff \mu_A(\lambda) = \mu_B(\lambda) \ \ (\forall \lambda)A∼B⟺μA​(λ)=μB​(λ)  (∀λ)

More quantitatively: for each eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ, the sizes of the Jordan blocks form a partition μ(λ)\mu(\lambda)μ(λ); two matrices are similar if and only if, for every λ\lambdaλ, μA(λ)=μB(λ)\mu_A(\lambda) = \mu_B(\lambda)μA​(λ)=μB​(λ).

Notes

⭐ The practical form of uniqueness: the number of blocks = geometric multiplicity (dim⁡Eλ\dim E_\lambdadimEλ​), the largest block size = the multiplicity of λ\lambdaλ in the minimal polynomial, the sum of the sizes = algebraic multiplicity.

Thus "classification up to similarity" is compressed into a set of integer partitions.

⭐ This yields a nice corollary: AAA is nilpotent (Ak=0A^{k} = 0Ak=0 for some kkk)   ⟺  \iff⟺ its only eigenvalue is 000   ⟺  \iff⟺ its characteristic polynomial is xnx^{n}xn.

⚠ "Order of blocks disregarded" is necessary: the Jordan form is written according to an ordering of eigenvalues and blocks, and permuting blocks gives another description of the same operator (just another basis).
