Statement

Suppose fff is holomorphic and nonconstant on a domain Ω\OmegaΩ. Then fff is an open map: every open subset of Ω\OmegaΩ has open image.

Corollaries:

  1. Another formulation of the maximum modulus principle: ∣f∣|f|∣f∣ cannot attain a maximum in the interior of Ω\OmegaΩ, since otherwise the image would fail to be open.
  2. A holomorphic injection is biholomorphic onto its image (its inverse is also holomorphic).
  3. If the image of fff is contained in an open set, then “Im⁡f\operatorname{Im} fImf has empty interior” is equivalent to “fff is constant.”

⭐ A more intuitive way to read the theorem: A nonconstant holomorphic function cannot collapse an open set to a line or a point. The maximum modulus principle, Schwarz's lemma, and the implication univalent   ⟹  \implies⟹ biholomorphic are different expressions of this idea.

⚠️ Nonconstancy is essential, since a constant function maps an open set to a point. ⚠️ The theorem requires holomorphicity. Continuous real-valued functions lack this property.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the open mapping theorem is an analytic result, and the equivalence between constancy and empty interior is a corollary):

  • Being an open map   ⟺  \iff⟺ having open image for every open set follows in both directions by definition. The implication nonconstant and holomorphic   ⟹  \implies⟹ open uses isolated zeros and Rouché's theorem, comparing f−f(z0)f-f(z_0)f−f(z0​) with f−f(z0)−wf-f(z_0)-wf−f(z0​)−w.
  • Constant   ⟹  \implies⟹ image with empty interior follows directly. Image with empty interior   ⟹  \implies⟹ constant follows by contraposition of the open mapping theorem and requires the domain to be connected. Here the image refers to the set f(Ω)f(\Omega)f(Ω) rather than the imaginary part of the function.
  • Open map   ⟹  \implies⟹ nonconstant follows because a constant map on a nonempty domain has a singleton image. Continuity alone is insufficient. For example, the image of f(z)=Re⁡zf(z)=\operatorname{Re}zf(z)=Rez has empty interior in the complex plane.
  • Holomorphic injection   ⟹  \implies⟹ biholomorphism: The open mapping theorem first gives continuity of the inverse. The local zero-multiplicity argument then gives f′≠0f'\ne0f′=0, and the inverse function theorem gives holomorphicity of the inverse. This uses more than the definition of injectivity.
  • Without connectedness, global nonconstancy is insufficient. A function taking different constant values on two disjoint disks is holomorphic and nonconstant but fails to be open. The interior maximum modulus principle follows from openness of the image and the geometry of disks.
