def.hilbert-space

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let (H,⟨⋅,⋅⟩)(H, \langle\cdot, \cdot\rangle)(H,⟨⋅,⋅⟩) be an inner product space over K\mathbb{K}K (linear in the first variable, conjugate-linear in the second), with norm ∥x∥:=⟨x,x⟩\|x\| := \sqrt{\langle x, x\rangle}∥x∥:=⟨x,x⟩​.

If (H,∥⋅∥)(H, \|\cdot\|)(H,∥⋅∥) is complete, it is called a Hilbert space.

Notes

Prototype: L2(Ω,μ)L^2(\Omega, \mu)L2(Ω,μ) (completeness from Riesz–Fischer), and ℓ2(I)\ell^2(I)ℓ2(I). Finite-dimensional inner product spaces are automatically complete, so they are degenerate Hilbert spaces.

⚠️ Convention clash: mathematical literature usually takes "linear in the first variable", physics (Dirac) takes "linear in the second". The difference is one conjugation, but changing the convention also changes the conjugate-linearity in the Riesz representation (whether y↦⟨⋅,y⟩y \mapsto \langle \cdot , y\rangley↦⟨⋅,y⟩ is conjugate-linear or linear), so a single document must be consistent.

⭐ Hilbert space = "a self-dual Banach space" (see "Riesz representation theorem (Hilbert version)"), which is the root of its ability to treat linear functionals as vectors.
