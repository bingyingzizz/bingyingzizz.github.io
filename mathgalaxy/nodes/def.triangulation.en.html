def.triangulation

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a topological space. A triangulation of XXX is a pair (K,h)(K, h)(K,h):

  • KKK is a simplicial complex (simplicial complex), and ∣K∣|K|∣K∣ is its geometric realization;
  • h:∣K∣→Xh : |K| \to Xh:∣K∣→X is a homeomorphism.

If such a (K,h)(K, h)(K,h) exists, then XXX is triangulable. The image of each simplex under hhh is called a face of XXX (a vertex, edge, or triangle). Thus XXX is expressed as a union of triangles, with any two triangles that meet intersecting along a whole edge or at a vertex.

Notes

⚠️ A triangulation consists of combinatorial data and a homeomorphism placing those data in the space. Both are part of the triangulation.

Thus triangulability of XXX is a topological property, whereas the form of KKK is combinatorial data. The two must be distinguished.

⚠️ Triangulations are highly nonunique. The same S2S^{2}S2 admits infinitely many genuinely different triangulations (a tetrahedron, an octahedron, arbitrary subdivisions, and so on).

Any quantity defined using a triangulation therefore requires a proof of independence from the triangulation. That is what the result on χ\chiχ establishes (Euler characteristic together with simplicial approximation).

⭐ One-way implication: The realization of a simplicial complex is always triangulable (take the identity), but some topological spaces are not triangulable, as discussed in the next section.

📌 The definition does not require KKK to be finite. A triangulation of a compact space is automatically finite, while noncompact spaces (such as open surfaces) require locally finite triangulations.
