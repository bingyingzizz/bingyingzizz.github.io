## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The inverse of a continuous strictly monotone function is continuous and monotone (see "continuity of the inverse"), and its derivative comes from the inverse function theorem.

⚠ The domain of $\log$ is $(0, +\infty)$ — on $\mathbb{R}$, $\exp$ never takes non-positive values, so $\log$ has no negative inputs either (the complex logarithm needs a separate branch, where branch cuts appear).

⭐ The two familiar faces of $e$ are unified here: $e = \exp 1 = \sum 1/n!$ and $e = \lim_n (1 + 1/n)^{n}$ imply each other, but the former is the definition and the latter a theorem.
