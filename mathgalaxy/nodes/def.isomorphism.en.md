## Statement · **draft**
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and $f : X \to Y$ a morphism in it. We say that $f$ is an **isomorphism** if there exists a morphism $g : Y \to X$ with

$$g \circ f = \mathrm{id}_{X}, \qquad f \circ g = \mathrm{id}_{Y}.$$

Such a $g$ is unique (if $g, g'$ both work, then $g = g' f g = g'$); we write $g = f^{-1}$. In this case $X$ and $Y$ are called **isomorphic**, written $X \cong Y$.
