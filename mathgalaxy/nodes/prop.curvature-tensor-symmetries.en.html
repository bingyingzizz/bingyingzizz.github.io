Statement

What this does: it takes stock of how many independent components the curvature tensor really has — four symmetries cut n4n^{4}n4 down to n2(n2−1)/12n^{2}(n^{2}-1)/12n2(n2−1)/12. This is the arithmetic source of "curvature in dimension two is one number, in dimension four it is two tensors".

Let (M,g)(M,g)(M,g) be a Riemannian manifold, with curvature operator R(X,Y)Z=∇X∇YZ−∇Y∇XZ−∇[X,Y]ZR(X,Y)Z = \nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}ZR(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z and components Rijkℓ=⟨R(∂i,∂j)∂k,∂ℓ⟩R_{ijk\ell} = \langle R(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k},\partial_{\ell}\rangleRijkℓ​=⟨R(∂i​,∂j​)∂k​,∂ℓ​⟩ (conventions as in Riemann 曲率张量 and Gauss 曲率).

Then

  1. Antisymmetric in the first two indices: Rijkℓ=−RjikℓR_{ijk\ell} = -R_{jik\ell}Rijkℓ​=−Rjikℓ​;
  2. antisymmetric in the last two: Rijkℓ=−RijℓkR_{ijk\ell} = -R_{ij\ell k}Rijkℓ​=−Rijℓk​;
  3. the two pairs can be exchanged: Rijkℓ=RkℓijR_{ijk\ell} = R_{k\ell ij}Rijkℓ​=Rkℓij​;
  4. first Bianchi identity: Rijkℓ+Rjkiℓ+Rkijℓ=0R_{ijk\ell}+R_{jki\ell}+R_{kij\ell} = 0Rijkℓ​+Rjkiℓ​+Rkijℓ​=0.

Notes

The first two have different sources: antisymmetry in X,YX,YX,Y is immediate from [∇X,∇Y][\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}][∇X​,∇Y​] in the definition; antisymmetry in Z,WZ,WZ,W uses metric compatibility (∇g=0\nabla g = 0∇g=0) — first prove ⟨R(X,Y)Z,Z⟩=0\langle R(X,Y)Z,Z\rangle = 0⟨R(X,Y)Z,Z⟩=0 and then polarize.

The fourth comes from torsion-freeness: R(X,Y)Z+R(Y,Z)X+R(Z,X)Y=0R(X,Y)Z+R(Y,Z)X+R(Z,X)Y = 0R(X,Y)Z+R(Y,Z)X+R(Z,X)Y=0.

An elegant equivalent proof: when torsion vanishes, ∇2Z(X,Y):=∇X∇YZ−∇∇XYZ\nabla^{2}Z(X,Y) := \nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}Z∇2Z(X,Y):=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇∇X​Y​Z is symmetric in X,YX,YX,Y, and R(X,Y)Z=∇2Z(X,Y)−∇2Z(Y,X)R(X,Y)Z = \nabla^{2}Z(X,Y)-\nabla^{2}Z(Y,X)R(X,Y)Z=∇2Z(X,Y)−∇2Z(Y,X);

summing cyclically over the three arguments, the two symmetric terms trade places and cancel term by term.

The third is pure algebraic bookkeeping: write the first Bianchi identity for several cyclic choices of the four arguments, move signs using the first two, and add; what remains is exactly Rijkℓ−RkℓijR_{ijk\ell}-R_{k\ell ij}Rijkℓ​−Rkℓij​. It carries no geometry, so we do not reproduce it term by term.

Why count components: for n=2n = 2n=2 only R1212R_{1212}R1212​ remains, so two-dimensional curvature is one number (the Gauss curvature); for n=3n = 3n=3 all 666 components are determined by Ricci (the Weyl tensor vanishes); for n=4n = 4n=4, 20=10+1020 = 10+1020=10+10, the second half being the Weyl tensor — that is where gravitational waves live.
