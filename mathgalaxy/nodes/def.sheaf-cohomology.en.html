Statement

Let M∙M^{\bullet}M∙ be an abelian sheaf complex on a site C\mathcal{C}C and let X∈CX \in \mathcal{C}X∈C. For an object XXX, the nnn-th cohomology group is

Hn(X,M∙):=RnΓ(X,M∙),H^{n}(X, M^{\bullet}) := R^{n}\Gamma(X, M^{\bullet}),Hn(X,M∙):=RnΓ(X,M∙),

where Γ(X,−)\Gamma(X, -)Γ(X,−) is the section functor at XXX.

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: sheaf cohomology is the right derived functor of the section functor. Γ(X,−)\Gamma(X,-)Γ(X,−) is left exact but not right exact (sheafification interferes), and RnΓR^{n}\GammaRnΓ is exactly the half that repairs the break.

Why it is needed: H0(X,M)=Γ(X,M)=M(X)H^{0}(X, M) = \Gamma(X, M) = M(X)H0(X,M)=Γ(X,M)=M(X) alone sees only global sections; from H1H^{1}H1 on it records the information that "sheaves fail to glue" (H1H^{1}H1 classifies principal bundles / torsors of sheaves).

📌 Example: H0(X;Z)H^{0}(X;\mathbb{Z})H0(X;Z) is the group of global sections; H1(S1;Z)≅ZH^{1}(S^{1};\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}H1(S1;Z)≅Z (agreeing with singular homology, see @@ex.h1-circle@@); on fine sheaves (such as the sheaf of C∞C^{\infty}C∞ functions) higher sheaf cohomology vanishes.
