Statement

What this does: Glues locally defined objects into a global one. Construct an object on each open set and take a weighted sum, using the functions of a smooth partition of unity as weights.

Why local finiteness is essential: Without it, ∑ρα\sum\rho_{\alpha}∑ρα​ may have infinitely many nonzero terms near a point, and the sum need not converge or be smooth. Local finiteness gives only finitely many terms near each point, so smoothness, integrability, and linearity carry over from finite sums.

What it is used to prove: Existence of Riemannian metrics (Riemannian Metric), existence of fiber metrics on vector bundles (Fiber Metric on a Vector Bundle), integration of top-degree forms (Integral of a Differential Form), and characterization of orientability by a nowhere-vanishing nnn-form (Orientation and Volume Forms). Each proof constructs the objects locally and glues them with a partition of unity.

Second countability is essential: The long line is locally Euclidean and Hausdorff but is neither separable nor paracompact, so partitions of unity cannot in general be constructed on it. The technical hypotheses in the manifold definition matter.

Continuous and smooth versions: The continuous version only requires paracompactness (paracompactness and partitions of unity). The smooth version uses bump functions to obtain smoothness. On manifolds, these are two regularity levels of the same construction.
