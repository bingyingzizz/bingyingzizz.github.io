rm and Cauchy–Schwarz use non-negative quadratic expressions and do not require finite dimension or completeness; recovering an inner product from an arbitrary norm requires the parallelogram identity.
- Positive definiteness cannot be dropped: $\mathbb R^2$ carries the symmetric bilinear form $x_1y_1-x_2y_2$, which pairs a non-zero vector with itself to zero, so it cannot define a norm.
