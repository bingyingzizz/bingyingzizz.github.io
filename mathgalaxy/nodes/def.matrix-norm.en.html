Statement

What this does: Measures the size of a matrix. Two distinct approaches are to measure the matrix as a collection of entries (the Frobenius norm) or as an operator (the operator norm).

Operator norm (induced norm): On Rn\mathbb{R}^{n}Rn, given a vector norm ∥⋅∥\lVert\cdot\rVert∥⋅∥, define

∥A∥:=sup⁡x≠0∥Ax∥∥x∥=sup⁡∥x∥=1∥Ax∥.\lVert A\rVert := \sup_{x \ne 0}\frac{\lVert Ax\rVert}{\lVert x\rVert} = \sup_{\lVert x\rVert = 1}\lVert Ax\rVert .∥A∥:=x=0sup​∥x∥∥Ax∥​=∥x∥=1sup​∥Ax∥.

Frobenius norm: ∥A∥F:=(∑i,j∣aij∣2)1/2\lVert A\rVert_{F} := \bigl(\sum_{i,j}\lvert a_{ij}\rvert^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}∥A∥F​:=(∑i,j​∣aij​∣2)1/2 (the length of the matrix viewed as a mnmnmn-dimensional vector).

Basic properties: Both are norms and satisfy ∥AB∥≤∥A∥ ∥B∥\lVert AB\rVert \le \lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert∥AB∥≤∥A∥∥B∥ (submultiplicativity). The operator norm also satisfies ∥Ax∥≤∥A∥ ∥x∥\lVert Ax\rVert \le \lVert A\rVert\,\lVert x\rVert∥Ax∥≤∥A∥∥x∥.

Relation to the spectrum: ∥A∥2=σmax⁡(A)=λmax⁡(A∗A)\lVert A\rVert_{2} = \sigma_{\max}(A) = \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(A^{*}A)}∥A∥2​=σmax​(A)=λmax​(A∗A)​ (the spectral norm is the largest singular value), and ∥A∥F=(∑σi2)1/2\lVert A\rVert_{F} = \bigl(\sum \sigma_{i}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}∥A∥F​=(∑σi2​)1/2.

Notes

Why both norms are useful: The operator norm measures the maximum amplification factor and is directly related to spectral radius and convergence (ρ(A)≤∥A∥\rho(A) \le \lVert A\rVertρ(A)≤∥A∥, Spectral Radius). The Frobenius norm measures the overall size of the entries, is easy to compute, and is naturally suited to low-rank approximation (as in the statement of Eckart–Young).

⚠️ The Frobenius norm is not an operator norm (except in dimension one). It is not induced by a vector norm, as shown by ∥I∥F=n\lVert I\rVert_{F} = \sqrt{n}∥I∥F​=n​, while every operator norm satisfies ∥I∥=1\lVert I\rVert = 1∥I∥=1. This gives the distinguishing test.

⚠️ The spectral norm uses singular values rather than eigenvalues: ∥A∥2=σmax⁡\lVert A\rVert_2 = \sigma_{\max}∥A∥2​=σmax​, whereas ρ(A)=max⁡∣λ∣\rho(A) = \max\lvert\lambda\rvertρ(A)=max∣λ∣. The two agree only for normal matrices (Normal Matrix).
