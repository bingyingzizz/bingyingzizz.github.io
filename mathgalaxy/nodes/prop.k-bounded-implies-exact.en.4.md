# K-Bounded Exactness Implies Exactness　`prop.k-bounded-implies-exact`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.k-bounded-implies-exact)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.k-bounded-implies-exact.md)

By **the assumption that $\operatorname{im} d^{n+1}$ is closed**, it equals its closure, so $s \in \operatorname{im} d^{n+1}$. The reverse inclusion $\operatorname{im} d^{n+1} \subseteq \ker d^{n}$ follows from $d \circ d = 0$. Thus $\ker d^{n} = \operatorname{im} d^{n+1}$, which is exactness. ∎

⚠️ **The closed-range hypothesis is essential**: On $\ell^{2}$, take $d(x_{j}) := (x_{j}/j)$. Then $\operatorname{im} d$ is dense and nonclosed, and bounded exactness with bound $K$ can hold even when exactness fails. **Dense exactness $\ne$ exactness**.
