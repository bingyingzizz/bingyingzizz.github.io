## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The only content of this theorem is **well-definedness**: there may be many bases, but their cardinality is the same number — that is what makes the word "dimension" meaningful.

The proof is entirely Steinitz: two bases are each independent and generating for the other, so two applications of the exchange lemma give $\#\mathcal{B} \le \#\mathcal{B}'$ and the reverse.

⚠ In the finite-dimensional case the exchange lemma suffices;

the infinite-dimensional case requires proving "infinite bases are equipotent" (using the cardinal arithmetic of $\#(A \times \mathbb{N}) = \#A$), which is much more involved than the finite case.
