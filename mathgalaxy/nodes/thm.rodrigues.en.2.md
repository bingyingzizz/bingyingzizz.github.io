## Notes · **draft**
**Why it is elegant**: $W(u) = ku$ is linear algebra, $\mathrm{d}n(v) = -k\,\mathrm{d}r(v)$ is geometry — it says that "along a principal direction the rotation of the normal is **proportional** to the motion of the tangent, with the principal curvature as the factor" — same or opposite direction depending on the sign of $k$ (for the sphere with outward normal $n = r$, $k = -1$, hence $\mathrm{d}n = \mathrm{d}r$ points the **same way**).

**Coordinate form**: in a curvature-line parametrization $n_{u} = -k_{1}r_{u}$, $n_{v} = -k_{2}r_{v}$. Written with $\mathrm{d}r, \mathrm{d}n$, no curvature-line net is required.
