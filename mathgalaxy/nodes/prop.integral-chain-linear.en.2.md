# Linearity of the Integral on Chains　`prop.integral-chain-linear`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.integral-chain-linear)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.integral-chain-linear.md)

Let $c$ be, on $M$, a chain of degree $k$; let $\omega,\eta$ be $k$-forms, and let $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$. Then

$$\int_{c}(a\,\omega+b\,\eta) = a\int_{c}\omega+b\int_{c}\eta ,\qquad \int_{a\,c+b\,c'}\omega = a\int_{c}\omega+b\int_{c'}\omega .$$

In particular $\int_{-c}\omega = -\int_{c}\omega$ (a chain with coefficient $-1$ is "walking backwards").

Thus integration is a bilinear pairing

$$C_{k}(M)\times\Omega^{k}(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},$$

with the geometric object (cubes and their sums) on the left and the dual object (forms and their sums) on the right.
