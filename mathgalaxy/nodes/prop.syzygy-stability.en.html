Statement

Let MMM be an RRR-module, and let P∙P_{\bullet}P∙​ and Q∙Q_{\bullet}Q∙​ be two projective resolutions of it, yielding the nnnth syzygies ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM and Ω1nM\Omega_{1}^{n}MΩ1n​M, respectively. Then for every n≥0n \ge 0n≥0 there are projective modules F,F1F, F_{1}F,F1​ and an isomorphism

ΩnM⊕F  ≅  Ω1nM⊕F1.\Omega^{n}M \oplus F \;\cong\; \Omega_{1}^{n}M \oplus F_{1} .ΩnM⊕F≅Ω1n​M⊕F1​.

In particular, ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM is projective   ⟺  \iff⟺ Ω1nM\Omega_{1}^{n}MΩ1n​M is projective. Thus whether the nnnth syzygy is projective is independent of the choice of resolution, which is why projective dimension is well defined.

Notes

Proof (induct on nnn, applying Schanuel's lemma at each step). For n=0n = 0n=0, we have M≅MM \cong MM≅M and take F=F1=0F = F_{1} = 0F=F1​=0 ✓. For n=1n = 1n=1, the two precovers 0→ΩM→P0→M→00 \to \Omega M \to P_{0} \to M \to 00→ΩM→P0​→M→0 and 0→Ω1M→Q0→M→00 \to \Omega_{1}M \to Q_{0} \to M \to 00→Ω1​M→Q0​→M→0 both have projective middle terms, so Schanuel's lemma gives P0⊕Ω1M≅Q0⊕ΩMP_{0}\oplus\Omega_{1}M \cong Q_{0}\oplus\Omega MP0​⊕Ω1​M≅Q0​⊕ΩM directly ✓. For the inductive step, suppose ΩnM⊕F≅Ω1nM⊕F1\Omega^{n}M \oplus F \cong \Omega_{1}^{n}M \oplus F_{1}ΩnM⊕F≅Ω1n​M⊕F1​.

The two resolutions give two precovers in degree n+1n+1n+1 with projective middle terms PnP_{n}Pn​ and QnQ_{n}Qn​. Their targets are ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM and Ω1nM\Omega_{1}^{n}MΩ1n​M, respectively. Since the targets are different modules, Schanuel's lemma cannot be applied directly.

To obtain a common target, incorporate the direct summands.

Take

Pn⊕Qn↠ΩnM⊕F,Pn⊕Qn↠Ω1nM⊕F1,P_{n} \oplus Q_{n} \twoheadrightarrow \Omega^{n}M \oplus F, \qquad P_{n} \oplus Q_{n} \twoheadrightarrow \Omega_{1}^{n}M \oplus F_{1},Pn​⊕Qn​↠ΩnM⊕F,Pn​⊕Qn​↠Ω1n​M⊕F1​,

(using the isomorphism from the induction hypothesis to transport the target of the second map). Both precovers have the projective middle term Pn⊕QnP_{n}\oplus Q_{n}Pn​⊕Qn​ ✓. Applying Schanuel's lemma again gives

Ωn+1M⊕(Pn⊕Qn)  ≅  Ω1n+1M⊕(Pn⊕Qn),\Omega^{n+1}M \oplus \bigl(P_{n}\oplus Q_{n}\bigr) \;\cong\; \Omega_{1}^{n+1}M \oplus \bigl(P_{n}\oplus Q_{n}\bigr),Ωn+1M⊕(Pn​⊕Qn​)≅Ω1n+1​M⊕(Pn​⊕Qn​),

which has the required form, since the added summands are projective ✓. ∎

⚠️ In the inductive step, the distinction between ΩnM\Omega^{n}MΩnM and Ω1nM\Omega_{1}^{n}MΩ1n​M matters. The induction hypothesis gives an isomorphism after adding direct summands, so at every step the precovers must be constructed again after taking direct sums. This is what “stable” means here.

⭐ Making the construction canonical. Once differences by projective direct summands are quotiented out, Ωn\Omega^{n}Ωn becomes a genuine functor. The resulting quotient category is the stable module category, where syzygies are well defined up to isomorphism. This also explains the role of stability in K0K_{0}K0​, stable homotopy theory, and KKK-theory.
