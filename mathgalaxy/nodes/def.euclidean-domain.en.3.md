# Euclidean domain　`def.euclidean-domain`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.euclidean-domain)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.euclidean-domain.md)

The key step in proving that a Euclidean domain $\implies$ PID: take a nonzero ideal $I \ne 0$ and among its elements choose one with minimal $N$, call it $b$; then any $a \in I$, divided by $b$, has remainder $r = a - qb \in I$ satisfying $N(r) < N(b)$, forcing $r = 0$, hence $I = (b)$.
