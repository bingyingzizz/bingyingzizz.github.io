Statement

The category of sheaves is a reflective subcategory of the category of presheaves, with reflector

♯:C^⟶C^,T↦T♯\sharp : \widehat{\mathcal{C}} \longrightarrow \widehat{\mathcal{C}}, \qquad T \mapsto T^{\sharp}♯:C⟶C,T↦T♯

called sheafification: for every sheaf FFF,

Hom⁡C^(T♯, F)  ≅  Hom⁡C^(T, F)\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(T^{\sharp},\ F\bigr) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(T,\ F\bigr)HomC​(T♯, F)≅HomC​(T, F)

and it is idempotent: (T♯)♯≅T♯(T^{\sharp})^{\sharp} \cong T^{\sharp}(T♯)♯≅T♯. Concretely, T♯T^{\sharp}T♯ is obtained by "applying the Čech construction to a presheaf twice".

Notes

Corollaries:

  1. Limits in the category of sheaves are computed in the category of presheaves (a reflective subcategory is closed under limits);
  2. T↦T♯T \mapsto T^{\sharp}T↦T♯ preserves all colimits and finite limits.

Examples:

  • For the coarsest topology (J(X)={hX}J(X) = \{h_{X}\}J(X)={hX​}) every presheaf is a sheaf and ♯\sharp♯ is the identity; for the finest topology (J(X)=J(X) = J(X)= all sieves) the category of sheaves has only one object, the constant presheaf 1\mathbf{1}1.
  • The sheafification of a constant presheaf (the same set EEE on every open set) is called the constant sheaf.
  • Taking XXX and C=Open⁡(X)\mathcal{C} = \operatorname{Open}(X)C=Open(X) on a topological space gives the usual category of sheaves, and ♯\sharp♯ is the usual sheafification functor.
