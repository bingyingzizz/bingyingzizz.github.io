陈述

设 XXX 是集合，A⊆P(X)\mathfrak{A} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)A⊆P(X)。A\mathfrak{A}A 是 XXX 上的一个 σ\sigmaσ代数，当且仅当它同时满足三条：

  1. 含全集：X∈AX \in \mathfrak{A}X∈A
  2. 对补封闭：A∈A  ⟹  Ac:=X∖A∈AA \in \mathfrak{A} \implies A^c := X \setminus A \in \mathfrak{A}A∈A⟹Ac:=X∖A∈A
  3. 对可数并封闭：A1,A2,…∈A  ⟹  ⋃n=1∞An∈AA_1, A_2, \ldots \in \mathfrak{A} \implies \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \in \mathfrak{A}A1​,A2​,…∈A⟹⋃n=1∞​An​∈A

二元组 (X,A)(X, \mathfrak{A})(X,A) 称为可测空间（measurable space），A\mathfrak{A}A 的元素称为可测集（measurable set）。

说明

三条是配套的，缺一不可：只留 (2)(3) 就是σ\sigmaσ环；把 (3) 降成「有限并」就是代数。

由 (2)(3) 用 De Morgan 立得对可数交封闭：(⋂nAn)c=⋃nAnc∈A\Big( \bigcap_n A_n \Big)^c = \bigcup_n A_n^c \in \mathfrak{A}(⋂n​An​)c=⋃n​Anc​∈A。于是有限并、有限交、差、对称差全都可用 —— 真正超出「代数」的，只有可数这一件事。

⚠ 方向不能反：σ\sigmaσ代数一定是代数，代数不一定是 σ\sigmaσ代数。

反例：X=RX = \mathbb{R}X=R 上「有限集与余有限集」全体是代数，而 N={1}∪{2}∪{3}∪⋯\mathbb{N} = \{1\} \cup \{2\} \cup \{3\} \cup \cdotsN={1}∪{2}∪{3}∪⋯ 是其中可数多个元素的并、本身既非有限也非余有限，所以那个代数不封闭于可数并。

记号里的「σ\sigmaσ」表示可数（取自德文 Summe / 法文 somme 的首字母），和标准差那个 σ\sigmaσ 无关。

为什么非要有它：测度的可数可加性 μ(⋃An)=∑μ(An)\mu (\bigcup A_{n}) = \sum \mu (A_{n})μ(⋃An​)=∑μ(An​) 需要一个对可数运算封闭的定义域，否则等式两边根本不在同一个集合族里。整条测度构造就是从这里起步的。

三条公理之间其实只有一条“硬”的（其余两条几乎可以互相换）：

  • 含全集   ⟺  \iff⟺ 含空集：由对补封闭即得（∅=Xc\emptyset = X^{c}∅=Xc）。所以写成「含 ∅\emptyset∅」或「含 XXX」等价，只要同时有对补封闭。
  • 对可数并封闭   ⟺  \iff⟺ 对可数交封闭：用两次 de Morgan：⋂nAn=(⋃nAnc)c\bigcap_{n} A_{n} = \bigl(\bigcup_{n} A_{n}^{c}\bigr)^{c}⋂n​An​=(⋃n​Anc​)c。所以「可数并对」与「可数交」两条只留一条即可 —— 但必须配「对补封闭」，缺了它两条都推不出来。
  • 「可数并」不能弱化成「有限并」：只有有限并 + 对补 得到的是代数（Boolean algebra），不是 σ\sigmaσ-代数。差别是实质的：有理数集在 2R2^{\mathbb{R}}2R 的有限并代数里可以被包含，但 {q}\{q\}{q} 的可数并（=Q= \mathbb{Q}=Q）就不一定 —— 而测度论的全部内容都要数可数并。
  • 「可数」不能加强成「任意」：σ\sigmaσ-代数不对任意并封闭。反例：R\mathbb{R}R 上的 Borel σ\sigmaσ-代数对所有开集取并（开集全体是任意并封闭的）是对的，但对任意子集取并会跑出去（每个单点是 Borel，但其任意并可以是不可测集，前提是 2ℵ02^{\aleph_{0}}2ℵ0​ 个并——这需要选择公理构造 Vitali 集）。

⚠️ 一句话记法：σ\sigmaσ-代数 = 「含全集 + 对补封闭 + 对可数并封闭」，其余全是这两条的推论；而「对补 + 有限并」只是代数，弱的不是一点。

Statement draft

Let XXX be a set and A⊆P(X)\mathfrak{A} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)A⊆P(X). Then A\mathfrak{A}A is a σ\sigmaσ-algebra on XXX if and only if it satisfies all three of the following:

  1. Contains the whole set: X∈AX \in \mathfrak{A}X∈A
  2. Closed under complements: A∈A  ⟹  Ac:=X∖A∈AA \in \mathfrak{A} \implies A^c := X \setminus A \in \mathfrak{A}A∈A⟹Ac:=X∖A∈A
  3. Closed under countable unions: A1,A2,…∈A  ⟹  ⋃n=1∞An∈AA_1, A_2, \ldots \in \mathfrak{A} \implies \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \in \mathfrak{A}A1​,A2​,…∈A⟹⋃n=1∞​An​∈A

The pair (X,A)(X, \mathfrak{A})(X,A) is called a measurable space, and the elements of A\mathfrak{A}A are called measurable sets.

Notes draft

The three conditions come as a package and none can be dropped: keeping only (2) and (3) gives a σ\sigmaσ-ring; weakening (3) to "finite unions" gives an algebra.

From (2) and (3), De Morgan gives closure under countable intersections at once: (⋂nAn)c=⋃nAnc∈A\Big( \bigcap_n A_n \Big)^c = \bigcup_n A_n^c \in \mathfrak{A}(⋂n​An​)c=⋃n​Anc​∈A. Hence finite unions, finite intersections, differences and symmetric differences are all available — the only thing that genuinely goes beyond an algebra is countability.

⚠ The direction cannot be reversed: every σ\sigmaσ-algebra is an algebra, but an algebra need not be a σ\sigmaσ-algebra.

Counterexample: on X=RX = \mathbb{R}X=R, the family of all finite and cofinite sets is an algebra, while N={1}∪{2}∪{3}∪⋯\mathbb{N} = \{1\} \cup \{2\} \cup \{3\} \cup \cdotsN={1}∪{2}∪{3}∪⋯ is a union of countably many of its elements and is itself neither finite nor cofinite, so that algebra is not closed under countable unions.

The "σ\sigmaσ" in the notation means countable (from the initial letter of German Summe / French somme); it has nothing to do with the σ\sigmaσ of standard deviation.

Why it is needed: countable additivity of a measure μ(⋃An)=∑μ(An)\mu (\bigcup A_{n}) = \sum \mu (A_{n})μ(⋃An​)=∑μ(An​) requires a domain closed under countable operations, otherwise the two sides of the identity do not even live in the same family of sets. The whole construction of measure starts here.

Of the three axioms only one is really "hard" (the other two can almost be exchanged for each other):

  • Contains the whole set   ⟺  \iff⟺ contains the empty set: immediate from closure under complements (∅=Xc\emptyset = X^{c}∅=Xc). So writing "contains ∅\emptyset∅" or "contains XXX" is equivalent, provided closure under complements is also present.
  • Closed under countable unions   ⟺  \iff⟺ closed under countable intersections: use De Morgan twice: ⋂nAn=(⋃nAnc)c\bigcap_{n} A_{n} = \bigl(\bigcup_{n} A_{n}^{c}\bigr)^{c}⋂n​An​=(⋃n​Anc​)c. So of "countable unions" and "countable intersections" only one needs to be assumed — but it must be paired with closure under complements; without that neither follows.
  • "Countable unions" cannot be weakened to "finite unions": finite unions plus complements give an algebra (Boolean algebra), not a σ\sigmaσ-algebra. The difference is substantial: the rationals can be covered in the finite-union algebra of 2R2^{\mathbb{R}}2R, but the countable union of the singletons {q}\{q\}{q} (=Q= \mathbb{Q}=Q) need not be — and all of measure theory needs countable unions.
  • "Countable" cannot be strengthened to "arbitrary": a σ\sigmaσ-algebra is not closed under arbitrary unions. Counterexample: on R\mathbb{R}R the Borel σ\sigmaσ-algebra is closed under unions of all open sets (the open sets themselves are closed under arbitrary unions), but taking unions over arbitrary subfamilies escapes it (every singleton is Borel, yet an arbitrary union of them can be non-measurable, given 2ℵ02^{\aleph_{0}}2ℵ0​ of them — constructing a Vitali set needs the axiom of choice).

⚠️ One-line mnemonic: a σ\sigmaσ-algebra = "contains the whole set + closed under complements + closed under countable unions", and everything else is a consequence of the last two; while "complements + finite unions" is only an algebra, and the gap is not small.
