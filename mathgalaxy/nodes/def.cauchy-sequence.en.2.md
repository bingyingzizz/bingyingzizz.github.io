## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The difference from convergence is the **direction of information**: to check convergence one needs $x$ first in order to compute $|x_n - x|$, whereas a Cauchy sequence lets the terms **compare among themselves** — someone who does not know the limit can still verify it.

⭐ In $\mathbb{R}$ the two are **equivalent** (the Cauchy criterion);

in $\mathbb{Q}$ they are not ($1, 1.4, 1.41, \ldots$ is Cauchy but has no rational limit).

$\mathbb{R}$ is constructed precisely to fill this gap.
