Statement

A morphism f:X→Yf : X \to Yf:X→Y is a regular epimorphism if it is the coequalizer of some parallel pair: there exist u,v:W⇉Xu, v : W \rightrightarrows Xu,v:W⇉X with f=coker⁡(u,v)f = \operatorname{coker}(u, v)f=coker(u,v).

Notes

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set every surjection is regular: take W=X×YXW = X \times_{Y} XW=X×Y​X with the two projections; their coequalizer is exactly fff. In a general category this fails — "epi" (right cancellable) is much weaker than "regular epi", and the gap is precisely whether YYY is really a quotient of XXX.
