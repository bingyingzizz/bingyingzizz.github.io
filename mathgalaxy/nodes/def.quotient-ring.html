Statement draft

Let AAA be a ring and I⊆AI \subseteq AI⊆A an ideal. On the additive quotient group A/IA/IA/I define

(a+I)+(b+I):=(a+b)+I,(a+I)(b+I):=ab+I.(a+I)+(b+I) := (a+b)+I, \qquad (a+I)(b+I) := ab+I .(a+I)+(b+I):=(a+b)+I,(a+I)(b+I):=ab+I.

These operations are well defined and make A/IA/IA/I a ring, the quotient ring; the quotient map π:A→A/I\pi : A \to A/Iπ:A→A/I, π(a)=a+I\pi(a) = a+Iπ(a)=a+I, is a ring homomorphism with ker⁡π=I\ker \pi = Ikerπ=I.

Universal property: if f:A→Bf : A \to Bf:A→B is a ring homomorphism with f(I)=0f(I) = 0f(I)=0, then there is a unique ring homomorphism fˉ:A/I→B\bar f : A/I \to Bfˉ​:A/I→B with fˉ∘π=f\bar f \circ \pi = ffˉ​∘π=f.

⭐ Why an ideal is necessary: for (a+x)(b+y)≡ab(modI)(a+x)(b+y) \equiv ab \pmod I(a+x)(b+y)≡ab(modI) one needs ay,xb,xy∈Iay, xb, xy \in Iay,xb,xy∈I; since x,y∈Ix, y \in Ix,y∈I while a,ba, ba,b are arbitrary, III must absorb multiplication from both sides.
