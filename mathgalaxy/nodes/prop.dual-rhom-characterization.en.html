Statement

Let MMM be a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group and M∨M^{\vee}M∨ its Pontryagin dual. Then

  • in general M∨≅RHom⁡Z(M,T)M^{\vee} \cong \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, T)M∨≅RHomZ​(M,T);
  • if MMM is compact, then M∨≅RHom⁡Z(M,Z)[1]M^{\vee} \cong \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{Z})[1]M∨≅RHomZ​(M,Z)[1];
  • if MMM is a (finite-dimensional real) Banach space, then M∨≅RHom⁡Z(M,R)M^{\vee} \cong \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{R})M∨≅RHomZ​(M,R).

Here T=R/ZT = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}T=R/Z.

Notes

What is being said. Classical Pontryagin duality M∨=Hom⁡cts(M,T)M^{\vee} = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(M, T)M∨=Homcts​(M,T) speaks of "continuous homomorphisms into the circle". This item gives three equivalent ways of writing it at the derived level — so that "taking the dual" can be performed in the derived world and can then enter RHom / tensor computations.

Why the three are mutually consistent.

Use 0→Z→R→T→00 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to T \to 00→Z→R→T→0: for compact MMM, RHom⁡(M,R)=0\operatorname{RHom}(M, \mathbb{R}) = 0RHom(M,R)=0 (compactly →\to→ Banach is all zero), hence RHom⁡(M,T)≅RHom⁡(M,Z)[1]\operatorname{RHom}(M, T) \cong \operatorname{RHom}(M, \mathbb{Z})[1]RHom(M,T)≅RHom(M,Z)[1], and that is where the second item comes from.

Note that the three cases have different "shifts": the compact case gains a [1][1][1], the Banach case changes the target to R\mathbb{R}R. This shows that "duality" is not a single uniform operation at the derived level — which one applies depends on which class MMM belongs to.

📌 Use: reduce "computing the Pontryagin dual" to "computing RHom", for which the Breen–Deligne spectral sequence and the classwise vanishing theorems are available.

Proof (reduce to three classes by the structure theorem). Every locally compact Hausdorff abelian group is an extension of a "discrete group" by "a finite-dimensional real Banach space ⊕\oplus⊕ a connected compact Hausdorff abelian group", so it suffices to verify the three classes.

  • MMM discrete: take two stages of a free resolution 0→F′→F→M→00 \to F' \to F \to M \to 00→F′→F→M→0 (F,F′F, F'F,F′ free). From "the RHom⁡\operatorname{RHom}RHom of a discrete free group has only degree 000" one gets the long exact sequence
0→M∨→F∨→F′∨→Ext⁡Z1(M,T)→0,0 \to M^{\vee} \to F^{\vee} \to F'^{\vee} \to \operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, T) \to 0,0→M∨→F∨→F′∨→ExtZ1​(M,T)→0,

This both gives M∨≅RHom⁡Z(M,T)M^{\vee} \cong \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, T)M∨≅RHomZ​(M,T) and incidentally locates Ext⁡1\operatorname{Ext}^{1}Ext1.

  • MMM compact: first apply the previous paragraph to M∨M^{\vee}M∨ (whose dual is again compact), obtaining RHom⁡Z(M∨,T)≅M\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M^{\vee}, T) \cong MRHomZ​(M∨,T)≅M;

then use 8.3.7 (substituting M∨M^{\vee}M∨ and Z\mathbb{Z}Z) together with "compact →\to→ real Banach is all zero" to get RHom⁡Z(M,Z)≅M∨[−1]\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{Z}) \cong M^{\vee}[-1]RHomZ​(M,Z)≅M∨[−1], i.e. M∨≅RHom⁡Z(M,Z)[1]M^{\vee} \cong \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{Z})[1]M∨≅RHomZ​(M,Z)[1].

  • MMM finite-dimensional Banach: "finite-dimensional Banach to a discrete group is all zero" cuts the three classes down to the target R\mathbb{R}R; comparing term by term gives M∨≅RHom⁡Z(M,R)M^{\vee} \cong \operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M, \mathbb{R})M∨≅RHomZ​(M,R). ∎
