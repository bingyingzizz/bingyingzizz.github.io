Statement

A linear map T:V→WT : V \to WT:V→W is a linear isomorphism iff TTT is a bijection. One then writes V≅WV \cong WV≅W and says VVV and WWW are isomorphic.

Basic fact: when dim⁡KV=n<∞\dim_K V = n < \inftydimK​V=n<∞, V≅KnV \cong K^{n}V≅Kn (choose a basis; v↦[v]Bv \mapsto [v]_{\mathcal{B}}v↦[v]B​ is the isomorphism). More generally,

V≅W  ⟺  dim⁡V=dim⁡WV \cong W \iff \dim V = \dim WV≅W⟺dimV=dimW

(the right-to-left half of “  ⟺  \iff⟺” holds in finite dimension; in infinite dimension it still holds, provided “dimension” is read as the cardinality of a basis).

Notes

⭐ This “basic fact” is the cancellation theory of all finite-dimensional linear algebra: isomorphism classes are determined by dimension alone, so there is only one “nnn-dimensional vector space” up to isomorphism.

Thus studying all finite-dimensional vector spaces amounts to studying all KnK^{n}Kn.

⚠ In infinite dimension both directions hold (equipotent bases give an isomorphism, and an isomorphism gives equipotent bases); one only has to read “dimension” as the cardinality of a basis.

⭐ In category language: V↦dim⁡VV \mapsto \dim VV↦dimV matches the finite-dimensional objects of K-VectK\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}K-Vect with cardinals, so the structure of the additive category reduces to concrete linear algebra.
