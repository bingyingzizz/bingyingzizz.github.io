## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The minimal polynomial is the finest of the **similarity invariants** (together with the characteristic polynomial): the Jordan form is determined by them, and two matrices are similar $\iff$ they have the same **invariant factors** (equivalently, the same Jordan form).

⭐ The fact that $I_T$ is an ideal matters: $p(T) = 0$, $q(T) = 0 \implies (p + q)(T) = 0$, and $r\,p$ lies in it too — so $T$ becomes a **module** over $K[x]$ (with $x$ acting as $T$), and the later proof of the Jordan form is carried out entirely in this module.
