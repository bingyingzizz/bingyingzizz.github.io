# Grothendieck universe axiom　`ax.grothendieck-universe`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=ax.grothendieck-universe)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./ax.grothendieck-universe.md)

It is **stronger** than "there exists a universe": it says universes are unbounded. That is exactly what category theory needs — $\mathbf{Set}$ is a small category relative to $U$, while $U$ itself lies in a larger universe $U^{+}$, so "the category of $U$-small categories" is again small in $U^{+}$. **One always has room to go up a level.**

Weak form: when only finitely many fixed categories are involved, assuming "there exists a universe" (equivalently, "there exists a strongly inaccessible cardinal") suffices; unboundedness is not needed.
