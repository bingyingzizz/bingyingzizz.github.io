Statement

The eigenvalues of WWW (equivalently, of the symmetric matrix (hij)(h_{ij})(hij​)) are called the principal curvatures, denoted k1≥k2k_{1} \ge k_{2}k1​≥k2​; the corresponding unit eigen-directions are called principal directions. They are given by the characteristic equation:

det⁡(hij−k gij)=0,det⁡(W−k id)=0.\det(h_{ij}-k\,g_{ij}) = 0,\qquad \det(W-k\,\mathrm{id}) = 0 .det(hij​−kgij​)=0,det(W−kid)=0.

In an orthonormal frame gij=δijg_{ij} = \delta_{ij}gij​=δij​, the characteristic polynomial is k2−2Hk+Kk^{2}-2Hk+Kk2−2Hk+K, where H=12tr⁡WH = \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}WH=21​trW and K=det⁡WK = \det WK=detW.

Notes

Why eigenvalues: knk_{n}kn​ is the Rayleigh quotient of WWW; extremal points on the unit circle satisfy II(v,⋅)=k I(v,⋅)II(v,\cdot) = k\,I(v,\cdot)II(v,⋅)=kI(v,⋅), i.e. W(dr(v))=k dr(v)W(\mathrm{d}r(v)) = k\,\mathrm{d}r(v)W(dr(v))=kdr(v) (Lagrange multipliers), and the extremal values are the eigenvalues — this is exactly the statement that "the directions of extremal value are eigen-directions of WWW".

Principal directions are orthogonal: eigenvectors of a real symmetric matrix belonging to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal (spectral theorem). The same conclusion follows from the Rayleigh quotient together with variations; the treatment here uses the spectral theorem directly.

Two characteristic equations: det⁡(W−kI)=0\det(W-kI) = 0det(W−kI)=0 and det⁡(h−kg)=0\det(h-kg) = 0det(h−kg)=0 agree in an orthonormal frame; in a parameter net {ru,rv}\{r_{u}, r_{v}\}{ru​,rv​} one must carry g=(EFFG)g = \begin{pmatrix} E & F \\ F & G \end{pmatrix}g=(EF​FG​), so ggg cannot be omitted.
