Statement

On Z=(N×N)/∼\mathbb{Z} = (\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N})/{\sim}Z=(N×N)/∼, take the operations and order defined in 整数 ℤ. Then:

(i) the two operations are well defined (independent of the choice of representatives);

(ii) (Z,+,⋅)(\mathbb{Z}, +, \cdot)(Z,+,⋅) is a commutative ring with unity;

(iii) Z\mathbb{Z}Z has no zero divisors (it is an integral domain): xy=0  ⟹  x=0xy = 0 \implies x = 0xy=0⟹x=0 or y=0y = 0y=0;

(iv) ≤\le≤ is a total order compatible with the operations: x≤y  ⟹  x+z≤y+zx \le y \implies x + z \le y + zx≤y⟹x+z≤y+z, x≤y, 0≤z  ⟹  xz≤yzx \le y,\ 0 \le z \implies xz \le yzx≤y, 0≤z⟹xz≤yz;

(v) n↦[n,0]n \mapsto [n, 0]n↦[n,0] is an injection from N\mathbb{N}N to Z\mathbb{Z}Z, preserving addition, multiplication, and order.

Notes

⭐ Each item is "move to representatives and use the arithmetic laws of N\mathbb{N}N". There is no subtraction in N\mathbb{N}N, so addition in Z\mathbb{Z}Z is written [a,b]+[c,d]=[a+c,b+d][a,b] + [c,d] = [a+c, b+d][a,b]+[c,d]=[a+c,b+d] — the +++ inside the representatives is the +++ of 自然数的运算律.

⭐ No zero divisors is the most interesting item: let x=[a,b]x = [a,b]x=[a,b] and y=[c,d]y = [c,d]y=[c,d] both be positive, that is, a>ba > ba>b and c>dc > dc>d, and write a=b+pa = b + pa=b+p and c=d+qc = d + qc=d+q (p,q≥1p, q \ge 1p,q≥1). Then xy=[ac+bd, ad+bc]xy = [ac + bd,\ ad + bc]xy=[ac+bd, ad+bc], and

(ac+bd)−(ad+bc)=pq>0,(ac + bd) - (ad + bc) = pq > 0,(ac+bd)−(ad+bc)=pq>0,

so xy>0xy > 0xy>0, and it cannot be 000. When the signs are opposite the product is negative, by the same argument.

⚠ Only after this step does "hence Q\mathbb{Q}Q is an ordered field" in 有理数 ℚ have a basis — the denominators of rational numbers come from Z∖{0}\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}Z∖{0}, and the premise that "000 cannot be a denominator" is exactly "bd=0bd = 0bd=0 has only the trivial solution in an integral domain".
