Statement

What it does: normality is a relation between closed sets. It is not inherited by subspaces — so requiring that every subspace be normal is a genuine strengthening, and that is T5T_{5}T5​.

A topological space XXX is completely normal (also hereditarily normal) if every subspace of XXX is normal (正规空间).

Equivalent criterion (Urysohn's separation theorem; the most usable form): XXX is completely normal   ⟺  \iff⟺ for any two separated sets A,BA, BA,B (that is, A‾∩B=A∩B‾=∅\overline{A} \cap B = A \cap \overline{B} = \emptysetA∩B=A∩B=∅) there exist disjoint open sets U⊇AU \supseteq AU⊇A and V⊇BV \supseteq BV⊇B.

Position in the chain: T6  ⟹  T5  ⟹  T4T_{6} \implies T_{5} \implies T_{4}T6​⟹T5​⟹T4​.

⚠️ One does not need to add "plus T1T_{1}T1​": the definition of a normal space already contains T1T_{1}T1​, and a subspace inherits T1T_{1}T1​ from the ambient space, so complete normality automatically gives T1T_{1}T1​.

Notes

⭐ Why "separated sets" is the natural strengthening: two closed sets are automatically separated (A‾=A\overline{A} = AA=A, A∩B=∅A \cap B = \emptysetA∩B=∅   ⟹  \implies⟹ A∩B‾⊆A∩B=∅A \cap \overline{B} \subseteq A \cap B = \emptysetA∩B⊆A∩B=∅).

Thus separated sets form a wider class than closed sets, and being able to separate a wider class is equivalent to a stronger space.

This criterion replaces the convoluted "hereditary" definition with an operational set-theoretic condition.

⚠️ Normality is not hereditary — this is the sole reason T5T_{5}T5​ exists: the classical example is the Tychonoff plank, or a variant of the Sorgenfrey plane — a subspace of a normal space need not be normal. So T5T_{5}T5​ is not a rewording of T4T_{4}T4​ but genuinely adds an axiom.

⚠️ Every metrizable space is T5T_{5}T5​: every subspace of a metric space is again a metric space and hence normal. This is why analysis rarely needs T5T_{5}T5​: it holds automatically (the stronger property is T6T_{6}T6​, see 完美正规空间).
