Notes

⭐ The induction principle governs proofs, recursive definition governs constructions — two sides of the same fact, both coming from "ω\omegaω is the smallest inductive set".

Addition, multiplication and order on N\mathbb{N}N, and later the operations on Z\mathbb{Z}Z and Q\mathbb{Q}Q, are all defined recursively and their properties verified by induction.

⚠ The induction principle holds only for ω\omegaω;

for a general well-ordered set one needs transfinite induction / transfinite recursion.

⚠ Recursive definition must use "a function FFF", not "a sentence", otherwise one falls into the trap of defining something in terms of itself.
