Notes

⭐ Three equivalent formulations; any one may be used:

  • a normal chain with abelian quotients (the definition);
  • the derived series terminates after finitely many steps (easiest to compute);
  • there is a composition series all of whose factors are cyclic of prime order (easiest to test).

⭐ The origin of the name and its real value: the main theorem of Galois theory translates "a polynomial equation is solvable by radicals" into "its Galois group is solvable" — that is where the Abel–Ruffini theorem comes from.

The other half of this story (field extensions) is in cluster 7.

⚠ "Solvable" is not "abelian": S3S_3S3​ and S4S_4S4​ are solvable but non-abelian.

"Solvable" says "can be peeled apart layer by layer into abelian quotients", i.e. "finitely many steps away from abelian".

⭐ Simple + solvable   ⟹  \implies⟹ abelian (the composition series can only be {e}⊴G\{e\} \trianglelefteq G{e}⊴G, and the factor GGG is simple and must be abelian).

Hence every non-abelian simple group is non-solvable; A5A_5A5​ is the first example.

⭐ Two classes to know: all abelian groups are solvable (a one-step chain);

all ppp-groups are solvable (induct on the class equation; a non-trivial centre   ⟹  \implies⟹ allows induction on Z(G)Z(G)Z(G) and G/Z(G)G/Z(G)G/Z(G)).
