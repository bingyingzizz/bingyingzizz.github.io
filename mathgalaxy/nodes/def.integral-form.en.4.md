## Notes · **draft**
**Why $n$-forms specifically**: under a change of variables the coefficient of $\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{n}$ transforms by $\det$, which matches the $\det$ in the change-of-variables formula exactly (no absolute value when orientation is preserved).

To integrate a form of degree $k<n$, one must first choose a $k$-dimensional submanifold and pull it back — that is "integration of forms on submanifolds".
