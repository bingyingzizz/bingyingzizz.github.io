Statement

Let μ∗\mu^{*}μ∗ be an outer measure on XXX, and M\mathcal{M}M the family of all μ∗\mu^{*}μ∗-measurable sets. Then

  1. M\mathcal{M}M is a σ\sigmaσ-algebra;
  2. μ∗∣M\mu^*|_\mathcal{M}μ∗∣M​ is a measure on (X,M)(X, \mathcal{M})(X,M) (that is, μ∗\mu^{*}μ∗ is genuinely additive on M\mathcal{M}M);
  3. this measure is complete.

Notes

This is the hinge of the whole construction of measure theory: an outer measure need not be additive, but on its "splittable sets" it becomes additive.

The proof of 1 is a lengthy verification: finite unions, complements and countable disjoint unions, one by one.

Completeness of 3 comes from the definition itself: if μ∗(A)=0\mu^{*}(A) = 0μ∗(A)=0 and B⊆AB \subseteq AB⊆A, then for every EEE one has μ∗(E∩B)≤μ∗(B)=0\mu^{*}(E \cap B) \le \mu^{*}(B) = 0μ∗(E∩B)≤μ∗(B)=0 and μ∗(E∩Bc)≤μ∗\mu^{*}(E \cap B^{c}) \le \mu^{*}μ∗(E∩Bc)≤μ∗(E); squeezing both sides shows that BBB satisfies the measurability condition.
