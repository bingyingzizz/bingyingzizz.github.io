Statement

Let XXX be a locally compact Hausdorff space and MMM a discrete abelian group (regarded as a constant condensed abelian group). Then for all n≥0n \ge 0n≥0

Hcondn(X, M)≅Hsheafn(X, M).H^{n}_{\mathrm{cond}}(X,\ M) \cong H^{n}_{\mathrm{sheaf}}(X,\ M).Hcondn​(X, M)≅Hsheafn​(X, M).

Notes

⭐ This is the bridge joining the two cohomology theories. Condensed cohomology is computed from "sheaves on XXX"; sheaf cohomology is computed from "sheaves on the open sets of XXX". This says: as long as XXX is locally compact and the coefficients are discrete, the two sides agree.

How to prove it.

Using the topos morphism cX:Cond/X⇄Open(X)c_{X} : \mathrm{Cond}/X \rightleftarrows \mathrm{Open}(X)cX​:Cond/X⇄Open(X) and its derived functors gives a spectral sequence whose E2E_{2}E2​ terms are Rqc∗R^{q}c_{*}Rqc∗​ applied to MMM.

The key computation is Rqc∗M‾=0R^{q}c_{*}\underline{M} = 0Rqc∗​M​=0 (q>0q > 0q>0), given by a computation on stalks: after passing to stalks, MMM is discrete and carries no higher information.

The spectral sequence degenerates and the two sides are isomorphic.

Why "coefficients discrete" is required. Once the coefficients carry a topology (say a real Banach space), Rqc∗R^{q}c_{*}Rqc∗​ no longer vanishes in general and the two sides fail to agree — this is precisely the situation §8.2 has to handle.

📌 Use: condensed cohomology on any locally compact space can be computed directly with classical sheaf cohomology (open covers, Čech, fine resolutions — the whole old toolkit applies).
