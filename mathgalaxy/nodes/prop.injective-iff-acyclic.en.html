prop.injective-iff-acyclic

Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category and I∈AI \in \mathcal{A}I∈A. Then the following are equivalent:

  1. III is an injective object;
  2. III is acyclic with respect to F:A→A′F : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}'F:A→A′ for every left exact additive functor (here FFF is that functor);
  3. III, for every left exact functor F=Hom⁡A(M,−)F = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M, -)F=HomA​(M,−) of that form, is acyclic for FFF.

Notes

Proof. (1)  ⟹  (2)(1) \implies (2)(1)⟹(2): when III is injective, III together with the identity map is itself an injective resolution of length 000, 0→I→ 1 I→00 \to I \xrightarrow{\ 1\ } I \to 00→I 1 ​I→0 ✓.

Using it to compute the right derived functor: RF(I)=F(I)RF(I) = F(I)RF(I)=F(I) is concentrated in degree 000, so RnF(I)=0R^{n}F(I) = 0RnF(I)=0 (n>0n > 0n>0) ✓, that is, III is FFF-acyclic. (2)  ⟹  (3)(2) \implies (3)(2)⟹(3): obvious (Hom⁡(M,−)\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)Hom(M,−) is left exact). (3)  ⟹  (1)(3) \implies (1)(3)⟹(1): for every MMM, III being acyclic for Hom⁡(M,−)\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)Hom(M,−) means RnHom⁡(M,I)=Ext⁡n(M,I)=0R^{n}\operatorname{Hom}(M, I) = \operatorname{Ext}^{n}(M, I) = 0RnHom(M,I)=Extn(M,I)=0 (n>0n > 0n>0); in particular Ext⁡A1(M,I)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M, I) = 0ExtA1​(M,I)=0 holds for all MMM ✓.

And by the injective criterion, Ext⁡A1(−,I)=0\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(-, I) = 0ExtA1​(−,I)=0 is exactly the equivalent characterization of "III injective" (in an abelian category it is equivalent to "every monomorphism I↣NI \rightarrowtail NI↣N has a retraction") ✓.

Hence III is injective ✓.

∎

⭐ Use: to verify "injective" one may start from the side of any left exact functor — and in the condensed / sheaf setting the most convenient left exact functor is taking sections Γ(S,−)\Gamma(S, -)Γ(S,−).

Hypotheses used in each direction (one must first ensure the right derived functors used exist):

  • Acyclic   ⟺  \iff⟺ all positive right derived functors vanish is a definition; in the usual injective-resolution framework one should add that A\mathcal AA has enough injectives, or otherwise explain the mechanism by which the derived functors exist; one cannot simply assume it from being abelian.
  • (1)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): compute the derived functor using an injective resolution of length zero; (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3) is the special case of the functor, together with the basic fact that Hom⁡(M,−)\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)Hom(M,−) is left exact.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (1): it suffices that for all MMM one has Ext⁡1(M,I)=0\operatorname{Ext}^1(M,I)=0Ext1(M,I)=0; use the classification of extensions by first Ext to split every 0→I→E→M→00\to I\to E\to M\to00→I→E→M→0, then obtain the injective extension property from the pushout diagram. This step is the injective criterion, not an unfolding of the definition.
  • "All functors" cannot be weakened to an arbitrary single functor: the identity functor is exact and acyclic on every object, but Z\mathbb ZZ as an abelian group is not injective; so the original remark that injectivity may be tested from any functor must be read as carrying additional sufficient hypotheses.
