Statement

Let T:V→VT : V \to VT:V→V be linear with V≠{0}V \ne \{0\}V={0}. A scalar λ∈K\lambda \in Kλ∈K is called an eigenvalue of TTT if and only if there is a nonzero vector v∈Vv \in Vv∈V such that

T(v)=λ vT(v) = \lambda\, vT(v)=λv

Such a vvv is called an eigenvector belonging to λ\lambdaλ. The eigenvectors belonging to λ\lambdaλ, together with 000, form the subspace

Eλ:=ker⁡(T−λ I)⊆VE_{\lambda} := \ker(T - \lambda\,I) \subseteq VEλ​:=ker(T−λI)⊆V

Notes

⭐ In one sentence: eigenvectors are the vectors that TTT only stretches, without changing their direction, and the eigenvalue is the stretch factor.

Equivalently: λ\lambdaλ is an eigenvalue   ⟺  \iff⟺ T−λIT - \lambda IT−λI is not invertible   ⟺  \iff⟺ det⁡(λI−T)=0\det(\lambda I - T) = 0det(λI−T)=0.
