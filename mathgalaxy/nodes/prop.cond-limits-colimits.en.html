Statement

Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond has all (small) limits and colimits. Limits are computed pointwise: for every compact Hausdorff space SSS,

(lim←⁡iXi)(S)=lim←⁡iXi(S).\Bigl(\varprojlim_{i} X_{i}\Bigr)(S) = \varprojlim_{i} X_{i}(S).(ilim​​Xi​)(S)=ilim​​Xi​(S).

Colimits cannot be computed this way — one first takes the colimit pointwise in presheaves and then sheafifies:

(lim→⁡iXi)(S)=(sh⁡(S′↦lim→⁡iXi(S′)))(S).\Bigl(\varinjlim_{i} X_{i}\Bigr)(S) = \Bigl(\operatorname{sh}\bigl(S^{\prime} \mapsto \varinjlim_{i} X_{i}(S^{\prime})\bigr)\Bigr)(S).(ilim​​Xi​)(S)=(sh(S′↦ilim​​Xi​(S′)))(S).

Notes

Why colimits fail. The inclusion functor i:Cond↪CHaus^i:\mathrm{Cond}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{CHaus}}i:Cond↪CHaus is a right adjoint and does not preserve colimits; sheafification aaa is its left adjoint and only repairs the presheaf colimit into a sheaf, so a colimit must first be taken pointwise in presheaves and then sheafified once.

A concrete counterexample. Take a surjection F↠SF \twoheadrightarrow SF↠S of compact Hausdorff spaces, for example S=S1S = S^{1}S=S1, F=S1F = S^{1}F=S1, z↦z2z \mapsto z^{2}z↦z2. In Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond, surjections are effective epimorphisms, so

coeq⁡(F×SF⇉F)=hS;\operatorname{coeq}\bigl(F \times_{S} F \rightrightarrows F\bigr) = h_{S};coeq(F×S​F⇉F)=hS​;

but pointwise T=S1T = S^{1}T=S1: F×SF=S1×{±1}F \times_{S} F = S^{1} \times \{\pm 1\}F×S​F=S1×{±1}, the two projections on Hom(S1,F)=Hom(S1,S1)\mathrm{Hom}(S^{1}, F) = \mathrm{Hom}(S^{1}, S^{1})Hom(S1,F)=Hom(S1,S1) are g↦gg \mapsto gg↦g and g↦−gg \mapsto -gg↦−g, so the pointwise coequalizer glues id\mathrm{id}id and −id-\mathrm{id}−id and is a proper quotient of Hom(S1,S1)\mathrm{Hom}(S^{1}, S^{1})Hom(S1,S1);

whereas in hS(S1)=Hom(S1,S1)h_{S}(S^{1}) = \mathrm{Hom}(S^{1}, S^{1})hS​(S1)=Hom(S1,S1) these two elements are not equal.

The two differ, so the colimit is not computed pointwise.

What "pointwise" means: to compute a condensed set one must know its value at every SSS. Limits can be taken all the way down to each SSS; colimits cannot, with one extra sheafification step.

⭐ This is the technical basis of all later computations: every limit appearing in Cond\mathrm{Cond}Cond can be computed at each SSS, and limits of sets are easy.

⚠️ Finite coproducts are the exception (they are pointwise): hS1∐hS2=hS1∐S2h_{S_{1}} \coprod h_{S_{2}} = h_{S_{1} \coprod S_{2}}hS1​​∐hS2​​=hS1​∐S2​​ — because ∐\coprod∐ is precisely a cover of the prestandard topology, and the sheaf condition "finite coproducts become finite products" gives it directly.
