# Pontryagin Dual as a Derived Hom　`prop.dual-rhom-characterization`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=prop.dual-rhom-characterization)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./prop.dual-rhom-characterization.md)

Use $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R} \to T \to 0$: for **compact** $M$, $\operatorname{RHom}(M, \mathbb{R}) = 0$ (compactly $\to$ Banach is all zero), hence $\operatorname{RHom}(M, T) \cong \operatorname{RHom}(M, \mathbb{Z})[1]$, and that is where the second item comes from.

**Note that the three cases have different "shifts"**: the compact case gains a $[1]$, the Banach case changes the target to $\mathbb{R}$. This shows that "duality" is not a single uniform operation at the derived level — which one applies depends on which class $M$ belongs to.
