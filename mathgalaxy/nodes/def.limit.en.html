Statement

The limit of the diagram D:I→CD : I \to \mathcal{C}D:I→C is the cone over DDD with the universal property: a cone (lim⁡D, pi)(\lim D,\ p_i)(limD, pi​) such that for every cone (Y, gi)(Y,\ g_i)(Y, gi​) there is a unique morphism g:Y→lim⁡Dg : Y \to \lim Dg:Y→limD with

pi∘g=gi(i∈I)p_i \circ g = g_i \qquad (i \in I)pi​∘g=gi​(i∈I)

Equivalently, the cone functor is represented by lim⁡D\lim DlimD:

Hom⁡CI(ΔY,D)  ≅  Hom⁡C(Y,lim⁡D)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{I}}(\Delta Y, D) \;\cong\; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y, \lim D)HomCI​(ΔY,D)≅HomC​(Y,limD)

Dually, the colimit colim⁡D\operatorname{colim} DcolimD of DDD satisfies Hom⁡CI(D,ΔY)≅Hom⁡C(colim⁡D,Y)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{I}}(D, \Delta Y) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\operatorname{colim} D, Y)HomCI​(D,ΔY)≅HomC​(colimD,Y).

When III is finite it is called a finite limit.

Notes

The universal property says just one thing: every other cone factors through it uniquely. Hence a limit, if it exists, is unique up to isomorphism — there is no need to choose a particular construction; any one will do.

Two equivalent formulations: "a universal cone" and "Hom⁡CI(Δ−,D)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}^{I}}(\Delta -, D)HomCI​(Δ−,D) is representable". The latter turns the limit into a computable isomorphism.

"Universal" can be pushed further: a limit is a terminal object in the category of cones (see "element category"). This sentence recurs repeatedly later.
