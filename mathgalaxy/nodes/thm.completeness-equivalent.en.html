Statement

On R\mathbb{R}R the following six statements are mutually equivalent:

(1) Supremum principle: every nonempty subset of R\mathbb{R}R that is bounded above has a supremum.

(2) Monotone bounded theorem: every increasing sequence bounded above converges.

(3) Nested interval theorem: nested closed intervals whose lengths tend to zero have an intersection consisting of exactly one point.

(4) Bolzano–Weierstrass: every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence.

(5) Cauchy criterion: every Cauchy sequence converges.

(6) Metric completeness: (R,∣x−y∣)(\mathbb{R}, |x - y|)(R,∣x−y∣) is a complete metric space.

The six form a strongly connected block: any one of them implies the other five.

Notes

⭐ This is the second example in the graph of “several formulations of the same thing forming a strongly connected block” — the first being the choice principles (the axiom of choice   ⟺  \iff⟺ the well-ordering theorem   ⟺  \iff⟺ Zorn's lemma   ⟺  \iff⟺ the Hausdorff maximal principle   ⟺  \iff⟺ Tukey's lemma).

Their treatment in the graph is the same: they always stay on the same layer.

⭐ Why it is worth a separate node: which one is chosen as the premise determines the style of every later proof — proving via the supremum principle (order), via nested intervals (geometry), or via the Cauchy criterion (sequences) are three different routes.

This block is precisely that “transfer map”.

⚠ “Complete” here has two contexts: the supremum-principle one is order completeness (bounded above implies a supremum), the metric-space one is metric completeness (Cauchy sequences converge).

On R\mathbb{R}R they are equivalent, but do not carry this equivalence outside as a general fact.
