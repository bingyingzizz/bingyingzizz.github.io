Statement

Compact Hausdorff spaces are the topological spaces that are both compact and Hausdorff. Taking them as objects and continuous maps as morphisms gives the category CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus. The inclusion functor is denoted CHaus↪Top\mathbf{CHaus} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Top}CHaus↪Top.

Notes

Putting compactness and Hausdorffness together is a good bargain: a compact Hausdorff space is regular and normal, and a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is automatically a homeomorphism. Many things that require extra hypotheses in Top\mathbf{Top}Top come for free here.

Arbitrary limits exist in CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus, and they are exactly the result computed in Top\mathbf{Top}Top (see the entry "CHaus is a reflective subcategory").
