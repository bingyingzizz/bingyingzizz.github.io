Statement

A morphism of complexes f:K→Lf : K \to Lf:K→L is called a quasi-isomorphism if for every nnn, Hn(f)H^{n}(f)Hn(f) is an isomorphism. A complex KKK is called acyclic if Hn(K)=0H^{n}(K) = 0Hn(K)=0 for all nnn.

Notes

Note: K→LK \to LK→L quasi-isomorphism   ⟺  \iff⟺ L→KL \to KL→K quasi-isomorphism ("quasi-isomorphism" is a symmetric phrase, although the map has only one direction); KKK acyclic   ⟺  \iff⟺ [0]→K[0] \to K[0]→K quasi-isomorphic   ⟺  \iff⟺ K→[0]K \to [0]K→[0] quasi-isomorphic.

Two propositions: (1) if K→L→M→K \to L \to M \toK→L→M→ is a distinguished triangle, then fff is a quasi-isomorphism   ⟺  \iff⟺ MMM is acyclic; (2) for a morphism of triangles (u,v,w)(u, v, w)(u,v,w), u,vu, vu,v is a quasi-isomorphism   ⟺  \iff⟺ www is a quasi-isomorphism.

A quasi-isomorphism is a map that "is not an isomorphism in K(C)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})K(C), but is indistinguishable on homology" — formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms gives the derived category.
