Statement

Let {an}⊆R\{a_n\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}{an​}⊆R (or C\mathbb{C}C). The series ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ is said to converge absolutely iff the series of positive terms

∑n=1∞∣an∣\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} |a_n|n=1∑∞​∣an​∣

converges.

Notes

⭐ Absolute convergence is the watershed of this pack: an absolutely convergent series can be treated as a finite sum — it may be reordered arbitrarily (rearrangement-invariant), multiplied term by term (Cauchy product), and grouped arbitrarily.

A conditionally convergent series has none of these properties.

⭐ It reduces the problem of a general series to one of positive terms: once ∑∣an∣\sum |a_n|∑∣an​∣ is known to converge, the whole family of tests (comparison, Kummer, root, integral, condensation) becomes available.

⚠ The converse fails (see "conditional convergence"), and the name is misleading: it is not "a stronger kind of convergence", but "convergence plus indifference to signs".
