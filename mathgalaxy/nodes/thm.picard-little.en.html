Statement

Let fff be entire and not constant. Then fff takes every value in C\mathbb{C}C except at most one point.

That is: there is at most one w0∈Cw_{0} \in \mathbb{C}w0​∈C such that the equation f(z)=wf(z) = wf(z)=w has a solution for every w≠w0w \ne w_{0}w=w0​.

Example: eze^{z}ez omits 000 (and indeed 000 is not attained), exactly one.

Notes

⭐ How much stronger it is than Liouville: Liouville (Liouville's Theorem) says “a bounded entire function is constant” and only rules out a bounded image; Picard says “the image cannot omit even two points” — even when the image is unbounded.

The proof given in this graph covers only the finite-order case (this is an honest label): if fff has finite order and omits two values a,ba, ba,b, then g:=(f−a)/(f−b)g := (f - a)/(f - b)g:=(f−a)/(f−b) is an entire function omitting 000 and 111.

By the Hadamard factorization (Hadamard factorization theorem), g=ePg = e^{P}g=eP;

if 111 is omitted, then eP−1e^{P} - 1eP−1 has no zeros   ⟹  \implies⟹ it also has a factorization   ⟹  \implies⟹; comparing growth orders successively forces PPP to be constant, contradicting that fff is nonconstant.

⚠️ The general case requires Bloch's theorem or the modular function λ\lambdaλ (the universal cover from the upper half-plane onto C∖{0,1}\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}C∖{0,1}); this graph has neither, so it is recorded as an external input. This is stated honestly in the node; it does not pretend to be a complete proof.

📌 Relation to Casorati–Weierstrass: Casorati says the image is dense near an essential singularity; Picard strengthens this to “omits at most one point”. The little theorem is its version for “entire functions + singularity at infinity”.
