Statement

What it says: the most self-referential class of fiber bundles — the fiber is not an arbitrary manifold but the structure group GGG itself, and GGG acts freely on the bundle from the right. Every vector bundle can be built from a principal bundle (by taking an associated bundle).

Let GGG be a Lie group, P,BP, BP,B smooth manifolds and π:P→B\pi : P\to Bπ:P→B smooth. A principal GGG-bundle over PPP is the fiber bundle π\piπ together with a right action of GGG on PPP

P×G⟶P,(u,g)⟼u⋅g,P\times G\longrightarrow P,\qquad (u,g)\longmapsto u\cdot g ,P×G⟶P,(u,g)⟼u⋅g,

such that

  1. fibers are preserved: π(u⋅g)=π(u)\pi(u\cdot g) = \pi(u)π(u⋅g)=π(u);
  2. the action is free and transitive on each fiber: u⋅g=u  ⟺  g=eu\cdot g = u \iff g = eu⋅g=u⟺g=e, and any two points of the same fiber PbP_{b}Pb​ differ by a right multiplication (so PbP_{b}Pb​ is homeomorphic to GGG);
  3. it is locally trivial and GGG-equivariant: every point has a neighbourhood UUU and a diffeomorphism Φ:π−1(U)→U×G\Phi : \pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times GΦ:π−1(U)→U×G, written Φ(u)=(π(u),φ(u))\Phi(u) = \bigl(\pi(u),\varphi(u)\bigr)Φ(u)=(π(u),φ(u)), satisfying
Φ(u⋅g)=(π(u), φ(u) g).\Phi(u\cdot g) = \bigl(\pi(u),\ \varphi(u)\,g\bigr).Φ(u⋅g)=(π(u), φ(u)g).

The fiber is then GGG (right multiplication g↦hgg\mapsto hgg↦hg carries it into itself), and so is the structure group GGG. The frame bundle gives the most used examples: for a vector bundle E→BE\to BE→B of rank kkk, the bundle of frames is a principal GL⁡(k,R)\operatorname{GL}(k,\mathbb{R})GL(k,R)-bundle — this is the bridge between vector bundles and principal bundles.
