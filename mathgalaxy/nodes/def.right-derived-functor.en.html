Statement

Let F:A→A′F : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}'F:A→A′ be an additive functor and let A\mathcal{A}A have enough injective objects. The right derived functor of FFF, written RFRFRF, is the (essentially unique) functor making the diagram commute

K+(A)→  F  K+(A′)↓K+(I)→  RF  \begin{array}{ccc} K^{+}(\mathcal{A}) & \xrightarrow{\;F\;} & K^{+}(\mathcal{A}') \\[2pt] \big\downarrow & & \\[2pt] K^{+}(\mathcal{I}) & \xrightarrow[\;RF\;]{} & \end{array}K+(A)↓⏐​K+(I)​F​RF​​K+(A′)​

in which the vertical arrow means replacing an object by an injective complex (taking an injective resolution).

Notes

How to read it: FFF itself is not exact (so it cannot be sent directly to the derived category), but first replacing the complex by an injective one, then applying FFF, and finally returning to the derived category makes this step exact — that composite is RFRFRF.

Component form: RnF(M):=Hn(RF(M))R^{n}F(M) := H^{n}(RF(M))RnF(M):=Hn(RF(M)), where MMM is regarded as a complex concentrated in degree 000. R0F=FR^{0}F = FR0F=F (for a left exact functor), RnF=0R^{n}F = 0RnF=0 (n<0n < 0n<0).

⭐ Motivation: when FFF is left exact, a short exact sequence 0→M′→M→M′′→00 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 00→M′→M→M′′→0 yields only 0→FM′→FM→FM′′0 \to FM' \to FM \to FM''0→FM′→FM→FM′′ — the right end breaks off. The right derived functor R1F(M′′)R^{1}F(M'')R1F(M′′) repairs exactly that break and splices the long exact sequence back together.

Dual: the left derived functor LFLFLF uses projective resolutions and repairs the case in which the "right half breaks off" (M⊗A−M \otimes_{A} -M⊗A​−, Tor⁡\operatorname{Tor}Tor).
