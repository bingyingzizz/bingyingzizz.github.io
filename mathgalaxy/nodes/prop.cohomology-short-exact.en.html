Statement

Let A\mathcal{A}A be an abelian category and K∙K^{\bullet}K∙ a cochain complex in A\mathcal{A}A.

Write Zk:=ker⁡dkZ^{k} := \ker d^{k}Zk:=kerdk (cocycles) and Bk:=im⁡dk−1B^{k} := \operatorname{im} d^{k-1}Bk:=imdk−1 (coboundaries), so that Hk=Zk/BkH^{k} = Z^{k}/B^{k}Hk=Zk/Bk.

Then for every kkk there is a pointwise exact sequence

0⟶Hk⟶coker⁡(dk−1)⟶Zk+1⟶Hk+1⟶0.0 \longrightarrow H^{k} \longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}\bigl(d^{k-1}\bigr) \longrightarrow Z^{k+1} \longrightarrow H^{k+1} \longrightarrow 0.0⟶Hk⟶coker(dk−1)⟶Zk+1⟶Hk+1⟶0.

Notes

⭐ This sequence is often written as two short exact sequences spliced together:

0→Hk→Kk/Bk→Bk+1→0,0→Bk+1→Zk+1→Hk+1→0.0 \to H^{k} \to K^{k}/B^{k} \to B^{k+1} \to 0, \qquad 0 \to B^{k+1} \to Z^{k+1} \to H^{k+1} \to 0.0→Hk→Kk/Bk→Bk+1→0,0→Bk+1→Zk+1→Hk+1→0.

these two are exactly what is used later when constructing spectral sequences.

⭐ Use: it splits "homology" into two short exact sequences that only involve cocycles and coboundaries. The entire technical content of spectral sequences is a step-by-step comparison between these short exact sequences.
