Notes

⭐ This is the main theorem of §8.2 and the central result of the chapter. It extends acyclicity from Stone spaces to general compact Hausdorff spaces, with coefficients in a real Banach space.

Proof strategy.

(1) Map a Stonean space S0S_{0}S0​ onto SSS (using filtered colimits of compact Hausdorff spaces along closed inclusions and Stonean covers). (2) On S0S_{0}S0​, acyclicity for Banach coefficients on Stone spaces gives acyclicity of the Čech complex;

(3) Compare the Čech complex of S0→SS_{0} \to SS0​→S with the cohomology of SSS itself. The discrepancy term lies in C(K,V)C(K, V)C(K,V);

(4) Use Banach-valued Tietze extension to eliminate the discrepancy, obtaining bounded acyclicity on SSS;

(5) Use “KKK-bounded acyclicity   ⟹  \implies⟹ acyclicity” to obtain ordinary acyclicity.

⚠️ The result fails for complex Banach spaces. This is one direct motivation for the theory of solid modules and solid quasi-coherent sheaves. In the real case one can use the order structure behind Tietze extension (suprema on R\mathbb{R}R and partitions) to eliminate the discrepancy. The complex case lacks these tools and requires different machinery.

That result concerns discrete coefficients (the space need only be locally compact), whereas this one concerns Banach coefficients (the space need only be compact). Together they cover most computations in Chapter 8.
