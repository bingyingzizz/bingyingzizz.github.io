## Notes · **draft**
**What it says.** In a general category, "epimorphism" (right-cancellative) and "coequalizer" (the quotient of some pair) are different notions. In a topos the two **coincide** — which is why one may freely "take quotients" in a topos: every epimorphism really does quotient out an equivalence relation.

**What $X \times_{Y} X \rightrightarrows X$ is**: $X \times_{Y} X = \{\, (x, x') : f(x) = f(x') \,\}$ is the **kernel pair** of $f$;

its two projections are that pair.

The coequalizer glues together "points identified by $f$" — and the result is exactly $Y$.
