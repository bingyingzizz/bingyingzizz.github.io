# orthogonality　`def.orthogonality`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.orthogonality)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.orthogonality.md)

**The hypothesis of Pythagoras' theorem**: $u \perp v$ suffices (no finite dimension, no completeness): $\lVert u+v\rVert^{2} = \lVert u\rVert^{2} + 2\operatorname{Re}\langle u,v\rangle + \lVert v\rVert^{2}$, and in the real case $\langle u,v\rangle = 0$ can simply be cancelled; **the complex case needs $\operatorname{Re}\langle u,v\rangle = 0$** (in general $\langle u,v\rangle$ is a complex number, and orthogonality only says that its real part vanishes — orthogonality itself is defined by $\langle u,v\rangle = 0$, so there is no problem, but in the proof one cannot simply throw one term away).
