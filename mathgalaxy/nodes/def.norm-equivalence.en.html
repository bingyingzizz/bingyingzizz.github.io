Statement

Let p,qp, qp,q be two norms on EEE. Call ppp stronger than qqq (written q⪯pq \preceq pq⪯p) if there exists C>0C > 0C>0 such that

q(x)≤C p(x)∀x∈E.q(x) \le C\,p(x) \qquad \forall x \in E .q(x)≤Cp(x)∀x∈E.

If each is stronger than the other, call ppp and qqq equivalent, written p∼qp \sim qp∼q.

Notes

  • (a) equivalence - (b) the same induced topology - (c) the same Cauchy sequences - (d) the same convergent sequences.

⚠️ The relation is asymmetric, and its direction runs against intuition: a strong norm (larger ppp) has smaller balls, more open sets, harder convergence, and fewer Cauchy sequences. So "passing to a stronger norm" removes convergent objects — which is exactly why one chooses the weakest norm that suffices (e.g. the weak topology).

⚠️ Equivalence preserves completeness and very little else: two equivalent norms give the same completeness (the same Cauchy sequences), but extremal problems can have different answers (the balls differ).
