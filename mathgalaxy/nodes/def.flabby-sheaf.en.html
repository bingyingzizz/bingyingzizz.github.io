def.flabby-sheaf · flabby

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a topological space and F\mathcal{F}F an abelian sheaf on XXX. F\mathcal{F}F is called a flabby sheaf if for every open set U⊆XU\subseteq XU⊆X the restriction map Γ(X,F)→Γ(U,F)\Gamma(X,\mathcal{F})\to\Gamma(U,\mathcal{F})Γ(X,F)→Γ(U,F) is surjective: every section over an open set extends to the whole space.

Notes

Difference from soft sheaves: flabby sheaves require extension from open sets; soft sheaves require extension from closed sets. Neither generally contains the other, but on a paracompact Hausdorff space flabby sheaves are soft (a theorem, not proved here).

Why it matters: flabby sheaves have no higher cohomology (Godement) — not proved in this graph; read together with the skeleton status of @@thm.soft-sheaf-acyclic@@; hence flabby resolutions compute sheaf cohomology; every term of the Godement resolution is flabby (see @@def.godement-resolution@@).

Examples: Cq(F)\mathcal{C}^{q}(\mathcal{F})Cq(F) in the Godement resolution; the sheaf of discontinuous sections U↦∏x∈UFxU\mapsto\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal{F}_{x}U↦∏x∈U​Fx​. Constant sheaves are in general not flabby.

⚠ Flabby sheaves differ from injective sheaves: injective sheaves are always flabby, but not conversely.
