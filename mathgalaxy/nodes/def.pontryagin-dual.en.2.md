## Notes · **draft**
**Why $\mathbb{T}$**: It is a **compact** $T_{1}$ group and the simplest group that can accommodate all characters. The dual group is therefore automatically compact (when $M$ is discrete). This is how duality reverses size.

**Two basic examples** (both can be computed directly): $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \mathbb{T}$, $\widehat{\mathbb{T}} \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Duality exchanges the discrete group of integers and the compact circle group.

The third example $\widehat{\mathbb{R}} \cong \mathbb{R}$ comes from the Fourier transform: every continuous character of $y \mapsto e^{2\pi i xy}$ has this form.
