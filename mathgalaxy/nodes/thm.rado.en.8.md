# Rado theorem: every compact surface is triangulable　`thm.rado`

[in graph](../#n=thm.rado)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./thm.rado.md)

enters in the interiors of the simplices and makes them vertices, at which point the involution acts simplicially on the barycentric subdivision (see Illman for a more general theorem). The resulting quotient complex $\widetilde{S}/(\mathbb{Z}/2)$ is a triangulation of $S$. The quotient map is bijective on each open simplex, and the simplicial automorphism determines the gluings. The previous notes said to use the local model $z\mapsto z^{2}$ at branch points. **This is incorrect**. The orientation double cover has no branch points because the action is free. The model $z\mapsto z^{2}$ applies to the branched covering from a meromorphic function in Approach A. Confusi
