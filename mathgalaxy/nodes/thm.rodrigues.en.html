Notes

Why it is elegant: W(u)=kuW(u) = kuW(u)=ku is linear algebra, dn(v)=−k dr(v)\mathrm{d}n(v) = -k\,\mathrm{d}r(v)dn(v)=−kdr(v) is geometry — it says that "along a principal direction the rotation of the normal is proportional to the motion of the tangent, with the principal curvature as the factor" — same or opposite direction depending on the sign of kkk (for the sphere with outward normal n=rn = rn=r, k=−1k = -1k=−1, hence dn=dr\mathrm{d}n = \mathrm{d}rdn=dr points the same way).

Coordinate form: in a curvature-line parametrization nu=−k1run_{u} = -k_{1}r_{u}nu​=−k1​ru​, nv=−k2rvn_{v} = -k_{2}r_{v}nv​=−k2​rv​. Written with dr,dn\mathrm{d}r, \mathrm{d}ndr,dn, no curvature-line net is required.

⚠️ The minus sign comes from W=−dnW = -\mathrm{d}nW=−dn. If a book takes W=dnW = \mathrm{d}nW=dn, its Rodrigues formula has no minus sign — before copying a formula, check whether the sign is placed in WWW or in IIIIII.

Hypotheses used in each direction (the geometric formula rests on definitions, the stationary criterion on self-adjointness of the shape operator):

  • (1)   ⟺  \iff⟺ (2): principal directions are defined as nonzero eigenvectors of the shape operator, and together with W∘dr=−dnW\circ\mathrm dr=-\mathrm dnW∘dr=−dn this is a definitional unfolding; the sign depends on the conventions for the normal and the shape operator.
  • (2)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (3): the normal curvature is the Rayleigh quotient of a self-adjoint operator; differentiating on the unit tangent circle, an eigenvector makes the first derivative vanish — this is not merely terminology.
  • (3)   ⟹  \implies⟹ (2): the derivative vanishes for all unit tangent variations perpendicular to that direction, so Wu−kuWu-kuWu−ku is parallel to uuu, and then k=⟨Wu,u⟩/⟨u,u⟩k=\langle Wu,u\rangle/\langle u,u\ranglek=⟨Wu,u⟩/⟨u,u⟩ gives zero.
  • One needs a sufficiently smooth regular surface, a unit normal and v≠0v\ne0v=0, so that the first fundamental form is positive definite and WWW is self-adjoint; the zero vector has no direction or normal curvature. A general non-self-adjoint operator fails the criterion, e.g. a nonzero antisymmetric plane rotation has identically zero Rayleigh quotient but no real eigen-direction.
