Statement

A Banach abelian group is a seminormed abelian group MMM satisfying

  • ∥s∥=0  ⟹  s=0\|s\| = 0 \implies s = 0∥s∥=0⟹s=0 (separation, meaning that the seminorm is a norm);
  • MMM is complete in the metric induced by the norm.

Every seminormed abelian group MMM has a Banach completion obtained by first taking the Hausdorff quotient and then completing,

M⟼M/M0^,M0={ s:∥s∥=0 }.M \longmapsto \widehat{M/M_{0}},\qquad M_{0} = \{\, s : \|s\| = 0 \,\}.M⟼M/M0​​,M0​={s:∥s∥=0}.

Notes

What this looks like in condensed mathematics. For a Banach abelian group MMM, the sections Hom⁡(Z[S],M)\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}[S], M)Hom(Z[S],M) carry a supremum seminorm, so MMM carries norm information as a condensed abelian group. All quantitative statements in §8.2 use this seminorm.

⚠️ The norm here is on an abelian group. Every real Banach space is a Banach abelian group after forgetting scalar multiplication, and the converse fails. Many theorems in Chapter 8 use only the group structure.

⭐ Taking the Hausdorff quotient and then completing is a reflection from the category of seminormed abelian groups onto the subcategory of Banach abelian groups. Quantitative properties such as KKK-bounded exactness pass through this reflection with the same bounds.
