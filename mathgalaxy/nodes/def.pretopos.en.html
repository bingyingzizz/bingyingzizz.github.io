def.pretopos

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

A category C\mathcal{C}C is called a pretopos if

  1. finite limits exist;
  2. finite coproducts exist and are disjoint and universal;
  3. equivalence relations are effective and universal;
  4. epimorphisms are regular and universal.

Notes

Item 4 is not independent — it can be deduced from the first three, so some textbooks write only three. The advantage of writing it is to recall one thing: here epimorphisms are automatically regular, so "epimorphism" and "quotient map" are the same thing.

Standard fact: monomorphisms can be defined by limits (iii is a monomorphism   ⟺  \iff⟺ that square is cartesian), and pullbacks and limits preserve monomorphisms; this guarantees that pushouts also preserve monomorphisms — which is what makes the "disjoint" and "effective" conditions in items 2 and 3 meaningful.

Examples: Set\mathbf{Set}Set, FinSet\mathbf{FinSet}FinSet are pretopoi; CHaus\mathbf{CHaus}CHaus is a topos; Top\mathbf{Top}Top is not a pretopos (epimorphisms need not be quotient maps). The sheaf category of any site is a pretopos.

The four axioms do not carry equal weight (this is where the difference between a pretopos and a topos hides):

  • ① Finite limits exist: a purely structural requirement, guaranteeing only that one can speak of fibred products and equalizers (hence of "relations"). It involves neither colimits nor epi/mono.
  • ② Finite coproducts are disjoint and universal: this is a real condition and cannot be dropped. "Disjoint" = the pullback A×A⊔BB≃∅A \times_{A\sqcup B} B \simeq \emptysetA×A⊔B​B≃∅ is empty (one requires an initial object and that it is "really empty"); "universal" = coproducts are stable under pullback. Merely requiring "finite coproducts exist" is far from enough — coproducts in Set\mathbf{Set}Set are disjoint and universal, but coproducts in Grp\mathbf{Grp}Grp (free products) are not universal, so Grp\mathbf{Grp}Grp is not a pretopos.
  • ③ Equivalence relations are effective and universal: this item guarantees that "quotients" correspond to "relations" (effective = every equivalence relation is the kernel pair of some epimorphism; universal = preserved under pullback). ⚠️ Without it, "quotient objects" and "equivalence relations" do not match, and the first isomorphism theorem cannot even be stated.
  • ④ Epimorphisms are regular and universal: this is the most technical item (regular = the first projection of some kernel pair). ⚠️ It overlaps with ③ but they do not imply each other: ③ says equivalence relation →\to→ epimorphism, ④ says epimorphism →\to→ equivalence relation.

Pretopos vs topos: the missing item. A topos = a pretopos + (effective epimorphisms + local representability, or equivalently Giraud's axioms). So the whole meaning of the four letters "pretopos" is: it already has all the apparatus for quotients, fibred products, and equivalence relations, but not yet the layer that makes "sheaves". This is also why many constructions (internal equivalence relations, stability of effective epimorphisms) already work for pretopoi.

⚠️ The most common comparison: Set\mathbf{Set}Set is a topos; Grp\mathbf{Grp}Grp, Ring\mathbf{Ring}Ring, Cat\mathbf{Cat}Cat are all not pretopoi (coproducts are not universal). To decide whether a concrete category is a pretopos, check ② first — it is the item that fails most easily.
