## Notes · **draft**
⭐ Similarity is an **equivalence relation** (reflexive / symmetric / transitive), so $M_n(K)$ splits into similarity classes; **the classification problem is to describe all similarity classes**.

⭐ Two worlds, two ways of saying the same thing: on the matrix side "$B = P^{-1}AP$", on the map side "the same $T$, two bases".

The two edges `thm.change-of-basis` and `dep.changeofbasis-similarity` on the graph say exactly this.

⚠ Similarity is stricter than **equivalence** ($B = PAQ$): equivalence only requires equal rank, while similarity also requires equal characteristic polynomial.
