## Notes · **draft**
The difference from **termwise integration for nonnegative functions**: there no convergence hypothesis is needed (nonnegativity rules out cancellation), whereas here the condition is **sufficient**, not necessary: on a one-point probability space with $f_{j} = (-1)^{j+1}/j$ one has $\sum \int |f_{j}| = \infty$, yet $\sum \int f_{j}$ converges and termwise integration still holds — integrability buys the convenience of allowing both signs.

This is the key tool in the proof of the Fubini–Tonelli theorem.
