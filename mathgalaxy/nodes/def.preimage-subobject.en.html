Statement

Let f:X′→Xf : X^{\prime} \to Xf:X′→X be a morphism and Y↣XY \rightarrowtail XY↣X a subobject of XXX (see @@def.subobject@@). The preimage of YYY along fff is the fibred product

In Set\mathbf{Set}Set, f−1(Y)={ x∈X′:f(x)∈Y }f^{-1}(Y) = \{\, x \in X^{\prime} : f(x) \in Y \,\}f−1(Y)={x∈X′:f(x)∈Y}, that is, the usual set-theoretic preimage.

Notes

Intuition: the preimage is "pulling YYY along fff back to X′X^{\prime}X′" — it solves f(x)∈Yf(x) \in Yf(x)∈Y.

It is the same construction as intersection: the intersection Y∩Z=Y×XZY \cap Z = Y \times_{X} ZY∩Z=Y×X​Z is the pullback along two monomorphisms, while the preimage is the pullback along an arbitrary morphism fff; both are fibred products, only the morphisms pulled back along differ.

It is still a subobject: the pullback of a monomorphism along any morphism is still a monomorphism (pullbacks preserve monomorphisms), so f−1(Y)→X′f^{-1}(Y) \to X^{\prime}f−1(Y)→X′ really is a subobject.

⚠ One needs the fibred product along fff to exist; in a category without fibred products the preimage may not be defined.
