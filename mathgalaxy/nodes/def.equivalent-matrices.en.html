Statement

Let A,B∈Mm×n(K)A, B \in M_{m \times n}(K)A,B∈Mm×n​(K). If there exist invertible matrices P∈GLm(K)P \in GL_m(K)P∈GLm​(K) and Q∈GLn(K)Q \in GL_n(K)Q∈GLn​(K) such that

B=PAQ,B = P A Q,B=PAQ,

then AAA and BBB are called equivalent. This is the same as saying that AAA can be transformed into BBB by finitely many elementary row and column operations.

Equivalence is an equivalence relation on Mm×n(K)M_{m \times n}(K)Mm×n​(K), and it allows m≠nm \ne nm=n. Over a field KKK, the equivalence classes are completely classified by rank: every AAA is equivalent to the unique normal form

diag⁡(Ir,0),r=rank⁡A.\operatorname{diag}(I_r, 0), \qquad r = \operatorname{rank} A.diag(Ir​,0),r=rankA.

Equivalence is weaker than both similarity (B=P−1APB = P^{-1} A PB=P−1AP) and congruence (B=PTAPB = P^{T} A PB=PTAP): the latter two are stated only for square matrices of the same order, and each of them preserves more invariants.

Notes

⭐ Equivalence remembers only the rank: multiplying on the left changes the basis of the row space, multiplying on the right changes the basis of the column space, so rows and columns can be eliminated independently.

⭐ Normal form: use elementary row operations to reach reduced row echelon form, then use elementary column operations to turn the pivot columns into unit vectors and kill the other columns, obtaining diag⁡(Ir,0)\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)diag(Ir​,0). Each step is left or right multiplication by an elementary matrix; multiplying these elementary matrices together gives P,QP,QP,Q.

⚠ Similarity and congruence are special cases of equivalence (square matrices of the same order; take Q=P−1Q=P^{-1}Q=P−1 or Q=PTQ=P^{T}Q=PT), but neither contains the other.

⚠ Over Z\mathbb{Z}Z one uses the Smith normal form instead.
