# partial isometry　`def.partial-isometry`
English entry · root `../`
[in graph](../#n=def.partial-isometry)　·　[Chinese page (authoritative)](./def.partial-isometry.md)

Skeleton of the proof: if (3) holds, put $P = u^*u$. Then $\|u x\|^2 = \langle u^*u x, x\rangle = \|P x\|^2$. The image of the orthogonal projection $P$ is $(\ker P)^\perp$, and $\ker P = \ker u$, so on $(\ker u)^\perp$ one has $Px = x$, giving (1). From (1), $u$ and $uP$ agree on $\ker u$ as $0$ and on $(\ker u)^\perp$ as $u$, giving (2). Taking adjoints on both sides of (2) gives $u\,u^* = (u\,u^*)^2$, and $u\,u^*$ is self-adjoint, hence (4). With $u^*$, repeating (3)$\implies$(4) gives (4)$\implies$(3), so the four are equivalent.
