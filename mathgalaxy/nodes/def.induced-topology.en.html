def.induced-topology

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let C′\mathcal{C}'C′ be a site and f−1:C→C′f^{-1} : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'f−1:C→C′ any functor. The induced topology on C\mathcal{C}C is the finest topology making f−1f^{-1}f−1 continuous.

Notes

What it says: given any functor f−1:C→C′f^{-1} : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}'f−1:C→C′, take on C\mathcal{C}C "the finest Grothendieck topology making f−1f^{-1}f−1 continuous".

⭐ Use: upgrade an arbitrary pullback at the presheaf level to a morphism of sites (the standard SGA4 construction); the price is that coverings must be recomputed, and the resulting topology is generally finer than the original.

⛔ Same name, different objects: the "induced topology" of topological spaces (subspace topology, @@def.subspace-topology@@) has nothing to do with the topology on a site discussed here; they merely share the Chinese name.
