Statement

Let ν\nuν be a σ\sigmaσ-finite signed measure and μ\muμ a σ\sigmaσ-finite positive measure, both on (X,M)(X, \mathcal{M})(X,M). Then there is a unique pair of σ\sigmaσ-finite signed measures λ\lambdaλ, ρ\rhoρ such that

λ⊥μ,ρ≪μ,ν=λ+ρ\lambda \perp \mu, \quad \rho \ll \mu, \quad \nu = \lambda + \rhoλ⊥μ,ρ≪μ,ν=λ+ρ

and ρ\rhoρ can be written as an integral: there is a generalized μ\muμ-integrable function fff with

dρ=fdμd\rho = f d\mudρ=fdμ

Any two such fff agree μ−a.e.\mu -\text{a.e.}μ−a.e..

Notes

⭐ This is the structure theorem of the whole theory of signed measures: every ν\nuν splits uniquely into "a part singular with respect to μ\muμ" plus "a part absolutely continuous with respect to μ\muμ".

In particular, when ν≪μ\nu \ll \muν≪μ, λ=0\lambda = 0λ=0, hence dν=fdμd\nu = f d\mudν=fdμ — this is what is usually called the Radon–Nikodym theorem.
