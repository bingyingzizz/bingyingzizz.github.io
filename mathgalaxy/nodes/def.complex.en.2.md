Thus every $z \ne 0$ has an inverse $z^{-1} = \bar{z}/|z|^2$: $\mathbb{C}$ is a field.

## Notes · **draft**
⭐ The difference between $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{R}^2$ is only the multiplication — and this multiplication is what makes $\mathbb{C}$ an **algebraically closed field** (every polynomial has a root) and what makes the integral theory of complex-valued functions cleaner than the real case: the formulation of $|f|$ and of $L^p$ theory no longer has to split positive and negative parts.

⚠ **$\mathbb{C}$ lies outside the ordered fields**: it carries **no** total order compatible with the operations.
