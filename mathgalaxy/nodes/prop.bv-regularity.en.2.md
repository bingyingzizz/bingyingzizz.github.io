**(e)** set $G(x) = F(x+)$; then $F'$ and $G'$ exist and are equal **almost everywhere**. ⚠️ One cannot say “everywhere”: $F \in BV$ only guarantees that $F^{\prime}$ exists a.e. (which is what (d) says).

## Notes · **draft**
Everything is reduced to increasing functions via “$F = G - H$”: one-sided limits of an increasing function clearly exist; countably many discontinuities is the monotone-function theorem; a.e. differentiability is the same theorem (the derivative of a difference $=$ the difference of the derivatives).
