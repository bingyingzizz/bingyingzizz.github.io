def.soft-sheaf

Machine-drafted and pending author review. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

Let XXX be a topological space and F\mathcal{F}F an abelian sheaf on XXX. F\mathcal{F}F is called a soft sheaf if for every closed set A⊆XA\subseteq XA⊆X the restriction map Γ(X,F)→Γ(A,F)\Gamma(X,\mathcal{F})\to\Gamma(A,\mathcal{F})Γ(X,F)→Γ(A,F) is surjective: that is, every section over a closed set extends to a section over the whole space.

Notes

Relation to fine sheaves: fine sheaves (sheaves admitting a partition-of-unity action) are always soft; on a paracompact Hausdorff space, sheaves of C∞C^{\infty}C∞-modules (such as the sheaf of differential forms) are fine and hence soft. Constant sheaves are in general not soft.

Why it matters: on a paracompact Hausdorff space soft sheaves have no higher cohomology (Godement's theorem), so soft resolutions can be used to compute sheaf cohomology; see @@thm.soft-sheaf-acyclic@@ (skeleton: steps 0–2 complete, step 3 conditional on "flabby sheaves are acyclic").

⚠ Soft sheaves differ from injective sheaves: injective sheaves are always soft, but not conversely; softness is a "section extension" property, injectivity a categorical one.
