Statement

In plain words: on a surface "curvature" is really only one number, the Gaussian curvature KKK. Here it is computed from one component of the curvature tensor; the next node (the Theorema Egregium) then shows that it is the same number as k1k2k_{1}k_{2}k1​k2​.

The Gaussian curvature of a surface SSS at a point ppp is defined by the curvature tensor as

K(p)=⟨R(∂1,∂2)∂2,∂1⟩det⁡(gij)=R1221g11g22−g122,K(p) = \frac{\langle R(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})\partial_{2},\partial_{1}\rangle}{\det(g_{ij})} = \frac{R_{1221}}{g_{11}g_{22}-g_{12}^{2}},K(p)=det(gij​)⟨R(∂1​,∂2​)∂2​,∂1​⟩​=g11​g22​−g122​R1221​​,

(1,21,21,2 are local coordinate indices). In an orthonormal frame e1,e2e_{1},e_{2}e1​,e2​ it reduces to

K=⟨R(e1,e2)e2,e1⟩.K = \langle R(e_{1},e_{2})e_{2},e_{1}\rangle .K=⟨R(e1​,e2​)e2​,e1​⟩.

On the other hand, if hijh_{ij}hij​ is the matrix of coefficients of the second fundamental form in the same frame, then K=det⁡(hij)=k1k2K = \det(h_{ij}) = k_{1}k_{2}K=det(hij​)=k1​k2​.

Notes

⚠️ Signs and index order (uniform throughout this package): Rijkℓ=⟨R(∂i,∂j)∂k,∂ℓ⟩R_{ijk\ell} = \langle R(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\partial_{k},\partial_{\ell}\rangleRijkℓ​=⟨R(∂i​,∂j​)∂k​,∂ℓ​⟩, so on the unit sphere (K=1K = 1K=1, gij=δijg_{ij} = \delta_{ij}gij​=δij​) one has R1221=1R_{1221} = 1R1221​=1, R1212=−1R_{1212} = -1R1212​=−1.

Many books write "K=R1212/det⁡gK = R_{1212}/\det gK=R1212​/detg", which uses another index order (or another sign for RRR); mathematically it is the same thing.

The only check that a convention is right: the unit sphere must give K=1K = 1K=1.

Relation to the contraction 12gikgjℓRijkℓ\frac{1}{2}g^{ik}g^{j\ell}R_{ijk\ell}21​gikgjℓRijkℓ​: in dimension two there is only one independent component, and enumerating the four indices gives K=−12gikgjℓRijkℓK = -\frac{1}{2}g^{ik}g^{j\ell}R_{ijk\ell}K=−21​gikgjℓRijkℓ​ (the sign under the convention of this package). The two expressions give the same number; the difference is only the sign coming from the index order.

Two routes lead to it:

  • Intrinsic: RRR involves only ggg and its first and second derivatives (Γ\GammaΓ contains first derivatives, RRR contains ∂Γ\partial\Gamma∂Γ), so knowing III is enough to compute KKK;
  • Extrinsic: K=k1k2=det⁡(hij)K = k_{1}k_{2} = \det(h_{ij})K=k1​k2​=det(hij​), using the two principal curvatures.

Both routes give the same number — this is the content of the Theorema Egregium.

⚠️ KKK is a quantity at a point, with no direct size relation to "the curvature of a curve": on a surface with K=1K = 1K=1 there can be curves of curvature 000 (geodesics), and on a cylinder with K=0K = 0K=0 there can be curves of nonzero curvature.

📌 Examples: the sphere of radius RRR has K=1/R2K = 1/R^{2}K=1/R2 (see 球面的曲率); the cylinder has K=0K = 0K=0 (k1=1/Rk_{1} = 1/Rk1​=1/R, k2=0k_{2} = 0k2​=0, see 圆柱的第一基本形式); the plane has K=0K = 0K=0. The three examples cover the three signs of KKK.
