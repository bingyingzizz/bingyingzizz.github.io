prop.lc-ab-preabelian

Translated for an earlier content version and not updated since. The Chinese text is authoritative.

Statement

The category of locally compact Hausdorff abelian groups is preabelian but not abelian.

Explicitly, the kernel is the usual kernel with the subspace topology. The cokernel is the quotient group N/f(M)‾N/\overline{f(M)}N/f(M)​ with the quotient topology (one must quotient by the closure to obtain a Hausdorff quotient). Morphisms need not be strict. For example,

id:Rdisc⟶R\mathrm{id} : \mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{disc}} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}id:Rdisc⟶R

is not strict, with the discrete topology on one copy of the group and the usual topology on the other.

Notes

⭐ This shows that the category of topological abelian groups lacks the structure needed for homological algebra. This motivates passing to condensed abelian groups, which form a Grothendieck category with the requisite structure.

Why having kernels and cokernels is insufficient. In an abelian category, every monomorphism is the kernel of some morphism. Equivalently, it is isomorphic to the kernel of its cokernel, or strict.

Rdisc→R\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{disc}} \to \mathbb{R}Rdisc→R is a monomorphism (it is isomorphic to its image), but its cokernel is 000. The entire image is quotiented out in the cokernel.

The kernel of the cokernel is therefore different from the original object, so strictness fails.

Proof.

  • Preabelian: Finite products and finite coproducts exist (a product is the product group with the product topology, and a coproduct is the direct sum with the coproduct topology), as do kernels and cokernels. The kernel is the usual kernel with the subspace topology. The cokernel is N/f(M)‾N/\overline{f(M)}N/f(M)​ with the quotient topology (f(M)f(M)f(M) need not be closed, so one must quotient by the closure. A quotient by a closed subgroup with the quotient topology is locally compact Hausdorff) ✓.
  • Failure to be abelian: Take id:Rdisc→R\mathrm{id} : \mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{disc}} \to \mathbb{R}id:Rdisc→R.

It is continuous because the discrete topology is finer than the Euclidean topology, and it is injective, hence a monomorphism.

Its cokernel is R/R=0\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R} = 0R/R=0.

In an abelian category every monomorphism is strict, meaning f=ker⁡(coker⁡f)f = \ker(\operatorname{coker} f)f=ker(cokerf). Here ker⁡(0)=R\ker(0) = \mathbb{R}ker(0)=R, but ker⁡(0)\ker(0)ker(0) carries the Euclidean topology and Rdisc\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{disc}}Rdisc does not. The two are isomorphic but unequal (they are not isomorphic as morphisms of topological groups) ✗.

∎ (Equivalently, id\mathrm{id}id is isomorphic to its image but is not the kernel of its cokernel, so the mono-epi factorization is not strict.)
