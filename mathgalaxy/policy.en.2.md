# MathGalaxy · Content policy · continuation (page 2)

> page 1 `policy.en.md`

- Defining an object by axioms, properties or a universal property is legitimate, but
  **a definition does not by itself establish existence.** Existence must come from
  accepted foundational axioms, an explicit construction or a valid existence theorem
  (non-constructive ones are fine). The test: does the sentence *define a new object*,
  or does it *state a property of an object that already exists*? The former needs an
  existence justification.
- Example: ℝ can be built as rational Cauchy sequences modulo null sequences, after which
  the field, order and supremum axioms are theorems; merely asserting "ℝ is a complete
  ordered field" with no construction or existence theorem leaves the existence
  obligation unresolved.
- The first-order language and its formulas (`def.first-order-language`, `def.formula`)
  are **meta-level** objects, more primitive than ZFC itself, and are outside this rule
